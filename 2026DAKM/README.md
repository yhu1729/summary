## Claude (September 2026)

### Summary

The authors propose a numerical method for the invariance equation of nonlinear moment matching, a model-reduction approach in which a high-dimensional system $\dot x=f(x,u)$, $y=h(x)$ is driven by a low-dimensional signal generator $\dot\omega=s(\omega)$, $u=\ell(\omega)$. In the background theory, if the generator is neutrally stable and the system is locally exponentially stable, a mapping $x=\pi(\omega)$ solving the $n$ coupled first-order PDEs $\frac{\partial\pi}{\partial\omega}s(\omega)=f(\pi(\omega),\ell(\omega))$, $\pi(0)=0$, describes the steady-state response, and $h(\pi(r))$ gives the output of a reduced-order model (ROM) of dimension $d$. Each component of $\pi$ is expanded in monomials of total degree $1$ to $M$ on a box $\Omega$, and the Galerkin residual equations are solved by Newton's method, in the least-squares sense when the Jacobian is singular. The scheme recovers the exact solution of a four-state cart-pendulum problem with residuals near $10^{-9}$. For a nonlinear resistor-inductor ladder with $n=1000$ driven by a linear or a Van der Pol oscillator ($d=2$), residuals decrease with $M$, and two-state ROMs reproduce the steady-state output with relative RMS errors of $4.5\times10^{-3}$ and $4.1\times10^{-2}$ at $M=8$. The authors note that accuracy degrades on larger domains and with stronger generator nonlinearity, that tensorized quadrature cost grows exponentially in $d$, and that higher-dimensional generators remain future work.

### Contributions

1. Exploited the ladder structure by precomputing the exact tensors $[\mathbf N]_{ijk}=\int_\Omega\phi_i\phi_j\phi_k$ and $[\mathbf O]_{ijkl}=\int_\Omega\phi_i\phi_j\phi_k\phi_l$, giving a block-tridiagonal Jacobian with diagonal blocks $\mathbf A+2\kappa\mathbf M+\mathcal N(\mathbf c_i)+\mathcal O(\mathbf c_i)$ and off-diagonal blocks $-\mathbf M$, so that switching from the linear to the Van der Pol generator only requires reassembling $\mathbf A$.
2. Chose the free ROM input maps $\bar g(r,u)=(0,c)^\top u$ for the linear oscillator and $(0,\mu(1-r_1^2)+c)^\top u$ for the Van der Pol generator, whose linearizations $\begin{bmatrix}0&a\\-a&-c\end{bmatrix}$ and $\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\-1&-c\end{bmatrix}$ are Hurwitz for every $c>0$.
3. Reported, for $n=1000$ on $\Omega=[-0.6,0.6]^2$ and $M=2$ to $8$ ($N=5$ to $44$ basis functions per component), residuals falling from $3.4\times10^{-4}$ to $6.5\times10^{-10}$ for the linear oscillator and from $7.4\times10^{-3}$ to $8.1\times10^{-7}$ for the Van der Pol generator, with three Newton iterations from $\mathbf c^0=\mathbf 0$ in every Van der Pol case.
4. Showed that at $M=6$ enlarging $\Omega=[-a,a]^2$ from $a=0.6$ to $1.2$ raises the residual on $[-0.6,0.6]^2$ for $\mu=0.25$, $1$, and $2$, most strongly for $\mu=2$ (from about $4\times10^{-5}$ to about $50$), which the authors attribute to the cubic term $\mu\omega_1^2\omega_2$ amplifying coefficient errors near the boundary.
5. At reduced dimension 2, the Van der Pol ROM outperforms proper orthogonal decomposition ($\epsilon_{\rm rel}=5.7\times10^{-2}$) and balanced truncation of the linearization ($1.2\times10^{-1}$), and simulates to $t=50$ in 0.0120 s against 0.4243 s for the full model, at an offline cost of 8423 s for $M=8$.

### Comments

- Section 3.2, Eqs. (3.19)–(3.20): the basis is defined as the tensor product of the one-dimensional basis $\varphi_M(\omega)=(\omega,\omega^2,\dots,\omega^M)$, which has no constant term, so every product in (3.20) contains each $\omega_j$ at least to the first power and pure powers such as $\omega_1$ are excluded (for $d=2$, $M=2$ only $\omega_1\omega_2$ remains); this conflicts with the multi-index set $\Lambda_M=\{\nu:1\le|\nu|\le M\}$, the count $N=\binom{M+d}{M}-1$, Table 3 ($N=5,14,27,44$), and the Test 1 solution $(\omega_1,k\omega_1,\omega_2,k\omega_2)^\top$, so the evident intended reading includes the constant in the one-dimensional factors and removes only the constant product.
- Sections 4.3 and 5, Eq. (4.15): Section 2.1 states "In the rest of the paper, we shall assume that assumptions (A1) and (A2) hold", and Section 5.1 relies on the resulting guarantee that $\pi$ exists near the origin, but the Van der Pol generator with $\mu>0$ linearizes at the origin to $S=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\-1&\mu\end{bmatrix}$, whose eigenvalues have real part $\mu/2>0$, so its origin is unstable and (A1) fails (Section 2 notes that neutral stability implies purely imaginary eigenvalues of $S$), leaving Proposition 2.7 without its hypotheses; unresolved.
- Section 4.1: the statement in Section 2.1 that (A1) and (A2) hold in the rest of the paper conflicts with the cart-pendulum dynamics, whose Jacobian $\partial f/\partial x$ at the origin has $\partial f_3/\partial x_1=a_1>0$ and eigenvalues $\pm\sqrt{a_1}$ and $0$ (double), so (A2), which requires all eigenvalues to have negative real part, fails; unresolved.
- Section 5.4, Table 3: the text states that "The Jacobian $JF$ is stored sparsely, so the time complexity scales linearly in $n$ and quadratically in $N$", but the total times in Table 3 rise from 1.92 s at $N=5$ to 8423 s at $N=44$, a factor of about 4400 for an 8.8-fold increase in $N$ (quadratic scaling would give about 77), roughly as $N^4$, in line with the $O(N^4)$ tensor $\mathbf O$ named later in the same section; unresolved.
