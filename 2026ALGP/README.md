### Summary

Experiments with the 9 fs, multiterawatt LUCID laser provide the first evidence that carrier-envelope-phase evolution can bunch laser-wakefield electrons into a comb of narrow energy peaks. Ionization injection from nitrogen K-shell electrons in a helium-nitrogen target occurs near threshold within a narrow density window around $1.5\times10^{19}\,\mathrm{cm}^{-3}$. As the carrier-envelope phase slips during propagation, successive optical half-cycles enter and leave the trapping window at discrete positions. Their different acceleration lengths generate peaks separated by about 3 MeV, corresponding to an effective field near $70\,\mathrm{GV\,m^{-1}}$. Neighboring peaks have alternating angular chirps, as expected from half-cycle injection. Particle-in-cell simulations reproduce discrete injection, the comb, alternating angles, and approximately 6% relative spacing. The mechanism offers waveform-synchronized, potentially attosecond injection and could be scaled toward higher energies and repetition rates.
