### Summary

Gerard and coauthors propose a common language for describing flux-surface shape in both tokamaks and stellarators. The method analyzes cross-sections using Fourier shaping modes, extending familiar axisymmetric notions such as elongation, triangularity, and squareness to non-planar and non-axisymmetric surfaces. In stellarators, each shaping mode gains an additional degree of freedom: rotation about the magnetic axis. Applying the method to precisely quasi-axisymmetric and quasi-helical equilibria, and to selected QUASR database configurations, the authors find that quasi-symmetry correlates with a spatial resonance between shape complexity and axial rotation of the shaping modes. The slopes of the shaping-distribution lines track rotational transform and field-period number. The paper frames flux-surface shaping as a quantitative diagnostic for comparing stellarator geometries and for studying how quasi-symmetry, transform, and higher-order geometric content interact.

### Contributions

1. Defined Fourier-based shaping modes for general ideal-MHD flux surfaces.
2. Extended tokamak-style shape descriptors to non-axisymmetric stellarator geometries.
3. Identified axial rotation of shaping modes as the extra stellarator shape degree of freedom.
4. Linked quasi-symmetry to resonant distributions of shape complexity and rotation.
5. Connected shaping spectra to rotational transform and field-period dependence across configurations.
