### Summary

This theoretical study examines small-amplitude ion-acoustic shocks in a magnetized electron–ion plasma with anisotropic warm ions and a generalized $(r,q)$ electron distribution. Two spectral parameters describe nonthermal electron velocity profiles, while Chew–Goldberger–Low double-adiabatic relations distinguish ion pressures parallel and perpendicular to the magnetic field. Reductive perturbation yields a Korteweg–de Vries–Burgers equation balancing nonlinearity, dispersion, and viscous dissipation; a hyperbolic-tangent construction supplies monotonic travelling shock profiles. The derived phase speed increases with the parallel ion-to-electron temperature ratio and the field-aligned propagation direction cosine, but is independent of perpendicular ion temperature at this order. In the reported parameter scans, shock amplitude decreases with increasing spectral indices, either ion temperature ratio, or the parallel direction cosine, and increases with magnetic-field strength or viscosity. These trends follow the results section and figures; the conclusion contains conflicting statements about the spectral-index dependence. The findings characterize sensitivity within a reduced fluid model and have no experimental or observational validation.

### Contributions

1. Combines viscous ion-fluid dynamics, anisotropic double-adiabatic pressure laws, and a generalized electron density response with admissibility conditions $q>1$ and $q(r+1)>5/2$.
2. Derives the phase-speed relation $\lambda=\pm l_z\sqrt{1/a+3\sigma_\parallel}$, where $a$ is the linear electron-density response coefficient, $l_z$ is the field-aligned direction cosine, and $\sigma_\parallel$ is the parallel ion-to-electron temperature ratio.
3. Obtains explicit nonlinear, dispersive, and dissipative coefficients for the Korteweg–de Vries–Burgers equation and constructs a quadratic hyperbolic-tangent travelling-shock solution.
4. Quantifies decreasing shock amplitudes with increasing spectral parameters and either ion temperature ratio in the reported scans, including comparisons across both signs of temperature anisotropy.
5. Characterizes the competing influences of propagation geometry, magnetic-field strength, and viscosity: the scanned shocks weaken with increasing $l_z$ and strengthen with increasing ion-cyclotron-to-ion-plasma frequency ratio or viscosity.
