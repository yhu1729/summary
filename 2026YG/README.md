### Summary

This study asks whether SPARC, whose vessel, divertor, and poloidal-field coils were designed for positive triangularity, can support negative-triangularity plasmas. More than 600 FreeGS free-boundary Grad--Shafranov calculations scan triangularity $-0.7\leq\delta\leq0.7$ and elongation $1.0\leq\kappa\leq2.1$ under coil-current and wall-clearance constraints. At a reduced toroidal field of $8\,\mathrm{T}$, a feasible double-null case has $\delta=-0.35$, $\kappa=1.68$, plasma current $2.1\,\mathrm{MA}$, and major radius $1.75\,\mathrm{m}$. Relative to the primary-reference design, its plasma volume is about 42--43% smaller and its connection length is roughly 40% shorter, limiting fusion performance and divertor compatibility. Central-solenoid demand falls by 54% relative to an $8\,\mathrm{T}$ positive-triangularity comparison, but the PF3 shaping-coil current grows by about a factor of 5.5. The computed equilibria satisfy Troyon-beta and kink-safety-factor criteria, although vertical stability, transport, and integrated discharge evolution remain for future analysis.
