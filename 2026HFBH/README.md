### Summary

Using Gkeyll, the authors run 256 concurrent, unsupervised full-$f$ global electrostatic gyrokinetic simulations of a TCV-inspired boundary spanning the closed-field-line edge and open-field-line scrape-off layer. The scan combines eight triangularities, eight elongations, and four injected powers and evolves each case beyond turbulence relaxation. Shaping effects depend strongly on power: at low power, positive triangularity produces a relatively hot scrape-off layer, whereas at high power negative triangularity develops stronger edge ion-temperature gradients and better confinement. The authors attribute the low-power hot scrape-off layer to trapped-ion banana geometry: triangularity changes the field-line distance between turning points and the high-field-side limiter, altering exposure to cold recycled neutrals. Transport fingerprints place most cases in the ITG/TEM regime, while local linear Gyacomo calculations identify trapped-electron modes as the most unstable electrostatic modes. The approximately $75$ TB open ensemble supports transport-model benchmarking and data-driven modeling. The model omits electromagnetic fluctuations, higher-order finite-Larmor-radius effects, and detailed neutral and sheath physics.

### Contributions

1. Demonstrated an unattended, concurrent 256-case full-$f$ boundary gyrokinetic parameter scan.
2. Quantified how triangularity affects scrape-off-layer temperature and edge gradients differently across heating powers.
3. Proposed a trapped-ion arc-length mechanism for the hot low-power positive-triangularity scrape-off layer.
4. Combined nonlinear transport fingerprints with local linear calculations to diagnose the ITG/TEM regime.
5. Released an approximately $75$ TB simulation ensemble for transport benchmarks and data-driven modeling.
