### Summary

Krylov complexity measures how far a quantum state spreads in the basis generated by its dynamics. This work applies it to two quantum kicked-rotor models and four localization mechanisms: quantum antiresonance, localization induced by regular classical phase-space structures, dynamical localization from quantum interference, and power-law localization from a singular kicking potential. Arnoldi iteration constructs the Floquet Krylov basis, where probability profiles and inverse participation ratios reflect localization strength. Each mechanism produces a distinct pattern of complexity growth, oscillation, or saturation. Long-time averaged complexity and fluctuations of the Arnoldi coefficients further separate classical-induced localization from quantum localization: the former lacks clear scaling with effective Planck's constant, whereas dynamical localization shows systematic power-law scaling. Arnoldi-coefficient variance also detects the transition from integrability to classical chaos even when quantum dynamics remains localized. Conventional level-spacing statistics identify localization but do not distinguish its type. The authors additionally show that the conclusions depend strongly on the initial state; random delocalized states can erase differences among localization mechanisms, limiting Krylov complexity as a standalone chaos diagnostic.

### Contributions

1. Characterized four distinct localization mechanisms of quantum kicked rotors in a Floquet Krylov basis.
2. Connected Krylov-space probability profiles and participation ratios to the degree of localization.
3. Identified mechanism-specific growth, oscillation, and saturation signatures in Krylov complexity.
4. Distinguished classical-induced and interference-driven localization through effective-Planck-constant scaling of complexity and Arnoldi fluctuations.
5. Showed that Arnoldi-coefficient variance detects chaos onset while exposing the strong initial-state dependence of Krylov diagnostics.
