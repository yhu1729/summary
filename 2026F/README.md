### Summary

The paper derives plasma similarity transformations from kinetic equations coupled to Maxwell's equations and tests them with particle-in-cell and fluid simulations. For a system enlarged by a factor $k$, the collisionless scaling multiplies lengths and times by $k$, fields by $k^{-1}$, and charged-particle distribution functions and densities by $k^{-2}$, while leaving velocities, species masses, and charges unchanged. The same transformation accommodates electromagnetic and relativistic dynamics. In weakly ionized plasmas, neutral density scales as $k^{-1}$ so that charge--neutral collisions remain compatible. Simulations compare two-stream instabilities, electron diodes, and radio-frequency discharges with standing-wave effects under matched scaling conditions. Highly ionized, quasineutral plasmas require a different density scaling, $k^{-1}$, when charge--charge collisions dominate and an ambipolar field relation replaces Poisson's equation. Fluid microdischarge calculations show violations between the weakly and strongly ionized limits due to gas heating and chemistry. A temperature-dependent correction reduces these discrepancies but is presented as less rigorous than the limiting scalings. Thus, the results establish conditional similarity relations, rather than a single exact scaling across arbitrary plasma regimes.

### Contributions

1. Derives a common space--time, field, and distribution-function scaling for collisionless kinetic equations coupled to the full Maxwell system.
2. Shows compatibility with relativistic electron dynamics because the velocity and Lorentz factor remain invariant.
3. Demonstrates scaled agreement in particle-in-cell simulations of two-stream evolution, electron diodes, and electromagnetic radio-frequency discharges.
4. Distinguishes charge--neutral-dominated density scaling from the charge--charge-dominated scaling obtained under a quasineutral ambipolar approximation.
5. Proposes and tests a neutral-temperature correction that reduces microdischarge similarity violations during the transition between weak and strong ionization.
