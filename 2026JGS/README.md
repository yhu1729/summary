### Summary

Long-duration two-dimensional OSIRIS particle-in-cell simulations map how magnetization controls quasi-parallel, trans-relativistic electron--ion shocks with Lorentz factor about two. Across $\sigma=10^{-4.5}$--$10^{-3}$, the upstream precursor changes from Weibel or filamentation dominated at $\sigma\lesssim10^{-4}$ to Bell dominated at $\sigma\gtrsim10^{-3}$. Bell turbulence reaches larger magnetic-energy fractions and supports Bohm-like ion acceleration, $E_{\max,i}\propto t$; Weibel-scale turbulence yields $E_{\max}\propto t^{1/2}$. Ions retain substantial nonthermal energy fractions, roughly 0.1--0.2, throughout. Electron behavior changes sharply: Bell-generated transverse fields suppress injection, whereas Weibel-dominated shocks heat electrons toward ion equipartition and place comparable fractions of flow energy into nonthermal ions and electrons. Supplementary one-dimensional simulations explain why high-current Bell amplification is capped by the ambient field at low magnetization, allowing Weibel modes to win, while a feedback model identifies low-current Bell and electron--ion Weibel states as steady configurations. The results connect shock microphysics to cosmic-ray acceleration and synchrotron emission in several astrophysical transients.
