### Summary

Hall magnetohydrodynamics modifies ideal MHD below the ion skin depth, but its complete wave classification and topology had remained disputed. The authors linearize homogeneous, barotropic Hall MHD and cast it as a Hermitian matrix Schrödinger system. Cardano's formula gives exact eigenfrequencies, while explicit eigenvectors and symmetry analysis organize the spectrum. They prove that the positive-frequency spectrum has exactly three branches: slow magnetosonic-Hall, shear Alfvén-Hall, and fast magnetosonic-Hall waves. Each branch continuously approaches its ideal-MHD counterpart as the Hall parameter vanishes, so the Hall term changes dispersion and polarization without creating additional branches. Previously proposed circularly polarized Beltrami modes are identified as special parallel-propagation forms of existing branches. The decisive qualitative change is topological. Two branches meet at an isolated Weyl point whose local dispersion forms a Dirac cone. Eigenmode line bundles over a sphere enclosing that point have nonzero Chern numbers, whereas the Weyl point moves to infinity in the ideal-MHD limit. The study isolates bulk topology in a uniform equilibrium and connects these invariants to the possibility of protected boundary waves in inhomogeneous Hall-MHD plasmas.

### Contributions

1. Derived a Hermitian Hamiltonian, exact dispersion relations, and eigenvectors for the full linear Hall-MHD wave system.
2. Proved a one-to-one homotopy between the three Hall-MHD and ideal-MHD wave branches.
3. Resolved a literature disagreement by showing that Hall MHD introduces no additional wave branches.
4. Located an isolated Weyl degeneracy and characterized its surrounding Dirac-cone dispersion.
5. Computed nonzero Chern numbers for the eigenmode bundles, establishing topology absent from ideal MHD.
