## Claude (October 2026)

### Summary

The authors extend their kinetic theory of parametric instabilities driven by a high-harmonic fast wave (HHFW) from a uniform scrape-off layer (SOL) to the inhomogeneous near-SOL pedestal with density and temperature gradients. In a slab with uniform $B$ and a pump of frequency $\omega_0 = 30.5\,\omega_{ci}$, the ion Vlasov equation is solved in a frame oscillating with the ions, and the electron response, seen there as harmonics shifted by $k_{0z}$ and $\omega_0$, couples the potential's Fourier components. A renormalized linearization includes nonlinear ion scattering by the resulting ion-cyclotron (IC) turbulence through $\hat\omega = \omega - iC_i$. The dispersion relation, truncated to the high-harmonic IC mode and two satellites, is solved with the complex Halley method for deuterium, $n_0 = 2 \times 10^{11}$ cm$^{-3}$, $B_0 = 1$ T, $T_i = T_e$, and pump amplitude $\hat E_{0x} = 0.22$. Unstable harmonics $n = 17$ to $27$ appear only for finite $k_{0z}$; growth rates peak at steep density gradients, are governed by inverse ion Landau damping, rise strongly with $\eta_i$, and respond weakly to $\eta_e$. The saturated turbulence level follows from $\gamma_{Lin} = C_i$, and quasilinear moments give perpendicular ion heating exceeding parallel heating by $1/(k_z\rho_i)$, consistent with NSTX edge-ion observations.

### Contributions

1. Formulated the problem in ion-oscillation-frame variables in which the pump enters only through terms of order $k_{i0}\xi_i$, neglected for HHFW, so the ion Vlasov equation (4) takes its field-free form; introduced guiding-center variables (5)–(7) and the renormalized linearization (8)–(13), which turns second-order nonlinearities into third-order ones and adds the Gaussian orbit-scattering factor $e^{-C_i(t - t_1)}$ (18) with $C_i$ given by (31)–(32); expressed the electron density and potential in the ion frame through Bessel series in $a_{ei} = |k_i\xi_{ie}|$ with phase $\delta$ (21)–(24), yielding the coupled functional equation (25)–(26), the infinite system (33) with coefficients $b_{mq}$ (34), and the determinant condition (35), which reduces to the current-driven instability equation as $\omega_0 \to 0$.
2. Gave the renormalized ion permittivity (29) and linear electron permittivity (30) for Maxwellian species with inhomogeneous density and temperature in terms of $\xi_\alpha = k_yv_{d\alpha}/(\sqrt{2}k_zv_{T\alpha})$, $\eta_\alpha = d\ln T_\alpha/d\ln n_\alpha$, $A_{\alpha p} = I_p(k_\perp^2\rho_\alpha^2)e^{-k_\perp^2\rho_\alpha^2}$, $\hat A_{ip}$, and $z_{ip} = (\hat\omega - p\omega_{ci})/(\sqrt{2}k_{iz}v_{Ti})$, noting that replacing $\omega$ by $\hat\omega$ in the electron term is negligible because $|z_e| \ll 1$ and $|C_i| \ll |\omega|$.
3. Found numerically that a spatially uniform pump ($k_{0z} = 0$) suppresses the inverse-electron-Landau-damping instability of the uniform SOL over $\omega_0 \sim (30$–$50)\omega_{ci}$, so finite $k_{0z}$ is essential; with $(k_{0z}\rho_i)^{-1} = 16.763$, $(k_z\rho_i)^{-1} = 14.92$, $k_x\rho_i = 0.612$, $k_y\rho_i = 4.65$, $m_i/m_e = 3680$, and harmonic sums over $-50 \le n, r \le 50$, Figs. 1–7 ($\eta_i = 0, 0.7, 1.2, 1.5$; $\eta_e = 0, 0.7$) show growth-rate maxima at small $L_n/\rho_i$, largest for $n = 19$–$25$ and about three times smaller for $n = 17$ and $27$, instability confined to $n = 17$–$27$, $|z_{in}| < 3.5$ with $|z_{en}| \ll 1$, maxima for $\eta_i = 1.5$ about twice those for $\eta_i = 0.7$, ETG-only growth rates near $10^{-3}\omega_{ci}$, and, for $\eta_i = 1.2$, peaks of $\gamma/\omega_{ci} \approx 0.17$–$0.19$ near $L_n/\rho_i \approx 30$–$60$ against about $2 \times 10^{-3}$ for $\eta_i = \eta_e = 0$.
4. Developed the renormalized ion-kinetic quasimode decay theory: zeroth-order solution $\hat\omega = \Omega(k_i + q_0k_{0z}) - q_0\omega_0$ (38), the growth rate (39) valid for $\gamma < |\delta\omega|$, saturation at $\gamma_{Nl} = \gamma_{Lin} - C_i = 0$ (41), the steady-state integral equation (44) whose resonance-broadening term exceeds the quasilinear term by about an order of magnitude, and the estimate $e|\bar\varphi_i|/T_i \sim |\delta\Omega|/(\omega_{ci}\hat k_{i\perp}^2\rho_i^2F_i^{1/2})$ (47) for the turbulence level.
5. Derived from the renormalized quasilinear equation (48)–(53), with resonance function $R_i$ (54) composed of a Lorentzian broadening part and a resonant delta part, the perpendicular heating equation (56) with rate $\nu_{i\perp} \sim \gamma(\hat k_i)W/(n_{0i}T_{i\perp})$ (57) and anomalous conductivity $\chi_{i\perp} \sim (\gamma/\hat k_{i\perp}^2)(\omega_{ci}/\delta\Omega)^2W/(n_{0i}T_{i\perp})$ (58), the parallel heating rate $\sim k_z\rho_i\gamma W/(n_{0i}T_{iz})$ (60), hence the anisotropy ratio $k_z\rho_i \ll 1$ (61), and concluded that near-SOL growth and heating rates about an order of magnitude above uniform-SOL values make pedestal formation a likely enhancer of edge RF absorption.

### Comments

- Section III: the harmonic sums are said to run "over $n$ in Eq. (34) for $\varepsilon_i$" and "over $r$ in Eq. (35) for $b_{mq}$", but $\varepsilon_i$ with its harmonic sum is Eq. (29), the sum over $r$ defining $b_{mq}$ is Eq. (34), and Eq. (35) is the determinant condition; (29) and (34) are evidently intended.
- Section III: "$\hat E_{0x} = 0.22$, which corresponds to $E_{0x} = 400$ V/cm for the values of $n_{0i}$ and $T_i$ specified above", but only the ratio $T_i/T_e = 1$ is specified; Eq. (36) with $n_0 = 2 \times 10^{11}$ cm$^{-3}$ and $E_{0x} = 400$ V/cm implies $T_i \approx 9$ eV, which is not stated.
- Sections III and IV: Section III states that for $\omega_0 = 30.5\,\omega_{ci}$ "unstable solutions are found only for the high-harmonic IC modes with $n = 17$–$27$", whereas Section IV refers to "all unstable IC harmonics with $n = 21, 23, 25$, and $27$" in Figs. 1–4, omitting $n = 17$ and $19$, which those figures show to be unstable; unresolved.
- Section II, after Eq. (15): the nonlinear phase shift $k_i\delta r_i(t)$ "is included in Eq. (15) without terms of second order", but (15) is the definition of the phase shift itself and it enters the expansion (14); Eq. (14) is evidently intended.
