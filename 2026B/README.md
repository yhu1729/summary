### Summary

Ion-temperature clamping in stellarators denotes the observed saturation of ion temperature despite increased heating power. The paper proposes a minimal explanation based on Anderson localization of ion-temperature-gradient (ITG) modes. Within a reduced fluid closure, aperiodic stellarator curvature converts the field-line mode equation into a quasiperiodic Hill equation. A controlled tight-binding approximation then maps it to the Aubry--André--Harper model, whose eigenstates undergo a localization transition. For the continuous equation, a positive Lyapunov exponent identifies a geometry-dependent localization threshold. The resulting ordering places the linear ITG instability threshold below the localization threshold and the observed clamp above both. The author conjectures that localized eigenfunctions cannot sustain coherent cross-field heat transport, creating a low-transport regime whose lower gradient bound depends on magnetic geometry rather than heating power. This transport interpretation remains a prediction: nonlinear gyrokinetic simulations and experiments are required, and the reduced model omits electromagnetic, trapped-electron, and finite-Larmor-radius effects.

### Contributions

1. Derived a quasiperiodic Hill equation for ITG mode structure in aperiodic stellarator geometry within a reduced fluid model.
2. Established a controlled tight-binding connection between the ITG equation and the Aubry--André--Harper localization model.
3. Used the Lyapunov exponent to define a geometry-dependent localization threshold for the continuous mode equation.
4. Identified a three-threshold ordering separating linear instability, Anderson localization, and the observed temperature clamp.
5. Formulated testable predictions linking magnetic-spectrum aperiodicity to a power-independent gradient bound while clearly separating the conjectured transport consequence from the derived localization result.
