## Codex/ChatGPT (September 2026)

### Summary

A flux-driven global simulation with the electromagnetic, drift-reduced Braginskii code GDB develops a centrally peaked density profile from an initially flat profile with particle fueling localized near the last closed flux surface. The $8.4$ ms calculation uses a collisional, C-Mod-like, inner-wall-limited tokamak edge with freely evolving density boundaries. Two inward-transport mechanisms emerge. Initially, opposing density and temperature gradients support drift-wave turbulence whose electron thermal-diffusion response drives inward particle transport despite outward heat transport. Linear phase analysis and a control run suppressing electron-temperature evolution support this interpretation. Later, poloidally asymmetric equilibrium density and potential produce an inward $\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{B}$ flux. The measured poloidal phase offset, approximately $-0.08\pi$, is attributed to nonideal corrections to electron force balance; the leading-order Boltzmann response would cancel this flux. Removing transverse heat fluxes erases the up-down asymmetry and relaxes density peaking. Magnetic flutter also carries inward particles. The late-time particle budget does not close, so its numerical channel fractions are indicative rather than definitive. The demonstrated mechanism concerns a collisional edge model; extension to hotter, less collisional plasmas requires improved closures.

### Contributions

1. Demonstrates transport-timescale density peaking with edge-localized fueling, without prescribed core density or an imposed pinch velocity.
2. Uses linear cross-phase analysis and an electron-temperature control simulation to identify thermal diffusion as an early inward turbulent-transport mechanism.
3. Identifies a late inward equilibrium $\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{B}$ channel associated with poloidal density--potential phase shifts beyond the leading-order Boltzmann response.
4. Shows that suppressing transverse heat fluxes removes the up-down asymmetry and relaxes the centrally peaked density profile.
5. Decomposes particle transport into turbulent, equilibrium, and magnetic-flutter contributions while exposing limitations of the late-time accumulation budget.
