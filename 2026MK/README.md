### Summary

Misra and Krishan study gravitational, Rayleigh-Taylor-type instability in partially ionized magnetoplasmas using a two-fluid model. Electrons and neutrals are treated as a combined fluid with effective charge and mass, while singly charged ions form the second fluid; electron-neutral and ion-neutral collisions couple the fluids. The resulting low-frequency, long-wavelength electrostatic dispersion relation modifies the classical fully ionized result by introducing a plasma-density inhomogeneity length scale and collision-dependent changes to the Brunt-Vaisala frequency. The instability growth rate depends on the transverse wave-number ratio $\kappa = k_x/k_y$ and normalized ion-neutral collision frequency $f=\nu_{\rm in}/\Omega_{\rm ci}$. The paper estimates minute-to-hour instability timescales relevant to the ionospheric E region, solar chromosphere, and prominence threads. It remains a linear theory, but it provides parameter trends for future nonlinear modeling and observational comparison.
