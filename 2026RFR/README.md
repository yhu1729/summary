### Summary

This study uses one-dimensional azimuthal and two-dimensional radial-azimuthal particle-in-cell simulations to determine how an oscillatory axial electric field changes instability-driven electron transport in idealized $E\times B$ plasmas. The response depends strongly on forcing frequency and amplitude. Modulation below $150$ MHz, especially near $40$ MHz, weakens the electron cyclotron drift instability, shifts spectral energy toward lower frequencies and longer wavelengths, and reduces axial electron transport by up to $30\%$. Near the electron cyclotron frequency, forcing broadens the spectrum and can greatly enhance transport; at $560$ MHz the response is non-monotonic in amplitude, with high-amplitude cases increasing axial current by more than two orders of magnitude. Fourier and bicoherence analyses identify modulation sidebands, harmonics, and coherent three-wave coupling with a low-frequency mode near $2$ MHz. Transport changes are further traced to the phase correlation between electron-density and azimuthal-electric-field fluctuations. Within the local slab model, forcing near $40$ MHz and amplitudes of approximately $0.1$-$0.3E_y$ give the strongest suppression.
