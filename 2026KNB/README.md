### Summary

This review presents real-time time-dependent density functional theory (TDDFT) as a unified first-principles tool for electronic response in warm dense and high-energy-density matter. Propagating finite-temperature Kohn–Sham orbitals after a controlled perturbation captures collective and single-particle behavior, including free-free, bound-free, and bound-bound processes without an imposed state decomposition. The paper gives practical workflows for extracting dynamic structure factors relevant to x-ray Thomson scattering, frequency-dependent conductivity and optical properties, and nonlinear electronic stopping power. It emphasizes convergence requirements involving supercell size, time step, trajectories, pseudopotentials, and spectral broadening. The same versatility creates substantial cost and uncertainty: exchange-correlation approximations, frozen cores, finite cells, and extreme frequencies can bias predictions, while high temperature and high atomic number demand many states and fine resolution. The review positions TDDFT both as a predictive method and as a benchmark for cheaper response models.

### Contributions

1. Unified linear and nonlinear high-energy-density response calculations within one real-time TDDFT framework.
2. Provided practical procedures for dynamic structure factors, conductivity, optical properties, and stopping power.
3. Explained how the method captures collective, free-free, bound-free, and bound-bound dynamics self-consistently.
4. Catalogued numerical sensitivities from cells, trajectories, time propagation, pseudopotentials, and frequency resolution.
5. Identified accuracy and cost limits that motivate improved functionals, heterogeneous computing, and reduced models.
