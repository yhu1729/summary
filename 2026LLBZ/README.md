### Summary

The authors couple relativistic guiding-center, full-$f$ particle-in-cell runaway electrons to JOREK's reduced-MHD background plasma, creating an efficient hybrid fluid-kinetic model for slowly evolving resistive instabilities. Axisymmetric force balance, runaway drift-orbit shifts, and linear $(2,1)$ tearing-mode behavior agree with analytical theory, full-orbit JOREK, M3D-C1, and eigenvalue results. Runaway electrons create a narrow current sublayer, and their current gradient adds a mode-frequency component absent from Ohmic plasmas. Nonlinearly, runaway-driven magnetic islands saturate wider than Ohmic islands: the width ratio is about 1.7 for $\Delta'\approx0.2$ and exceeds 2 for $\Delta'\approx2$, where topology becomes more complex. Magnetic energy grows strongly while bulk-plasma kinetic energy and reconnection flows remain weaker because perturbed runaway current drives reconnection without the usual Lorentz-force acceleration of the fluid. The framework targets disruption and benign runaway-beam termination studies.

### Contributions

1. Implemented relativistic guiding-center full-$f$ runaway electrons coupled self-consistently to JOREK reduced MHD.
2. Verified equilibrium and drift-orbit shifts against analytical predictions and full-orbit calculations.
3. Reproduced linear tearing-mode growth and frequency benchmarks, including the narrow runaway-current sublayer.
4. Quantified magnetic-island saturation substantially wider than in otherwise identical Ohmic cases.
5. Demonstrated magnetic-fluid decoupling that produces stronger islands but weaker reconnection flows.
