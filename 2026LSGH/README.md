# 2026LSGH

## Claude (September 2026)

### Summary

The authors report the first seeded free electron laser (FEL) driven by a laser-plasma accelerator (LPA) in the high-gain regime, where radiation grows exponentially along the undulator. As background, LPA-driven FELs had lased in self-amplified spontaneous emission, which amplifies the beam's own undulator radiation, and, seeded by an external coherent pulse, only in the low-gain regime. At HZDR, a 188.8 MeV, 400 pC beam with 7% RMS energy spread and peak spectral charge density above 10 pC/MeV is energy-chirped by a decompressing chicane and enters a 1.98 m undulator with a 268.2 nm third-harmonic seed, whose wavelength chirp, set by fused-silica rods, gives durations of 295, 473, and 1186 fs FWHM at constant energy. The FEL, red-shifted near 273 nm, interferes with the seed. GENESIS simulations matching the measured spatiospectral images show exponential growth over the last 0.6 m with gain lengths of 0.55–0.68 m, deemed independent of seed duration, and about threefold amplification; shortening the seed fourfold raises exit peak power and measured FEL spectral intensity about fourfold. The authors report pulse-energy fluctuations up to 100% and measurement uncertainties above 30%, and note that saturation, predicted at 360 MW after 6 m, lies beyond the single undulator module.

### Contributions

1. Drove the LPA with the 150-TW arm of DRACO in a tailored self-truncated ionization-induced injection scheme, whose energy, divergence, and charge varied shot to shot by up to 35% while their means stayed within 2% without drift over four eight-hour days, and transported the beam through a quadrupole triplet against chromatic emittance growth, a four-dipole chicane with $R_{56}=2.0$ mm lowering the slice energy spread at the cost of peak current, and four quadrupoles for chromatic sliding focusing into a 99-period undulator with $\lambda_u=20$ mm and $K_u=2.31$, with the 2.55 µm RMS bunch length and (2.0, 1.5) mm mrad emittances obtained by matching simulated to measured spatiospectral distributions.
2. Modeled the seed as $\lambda_\mathrm{seed}(t)=\lambda_{\mathrm{seed}-0}+(t-\tau)/D_\lambda$ from a Fourier-limited 29.3 fs pulse dispersed by at least 9.9 mm of transmissive optics plus fused-silica rods of 10 or 50 mm with group-velocity dispersion $-4945.5$ ps/(nm km) at 270 nm, and, because the resulting $D_\lambda=-0.049$, $-0.098$, and $-0.296$ ps/nm poorly reproduced the FEL wavelength versus delay, added a residual $-0.025$ ps/nm attributed to the infrared driver laser, giving $-0.074$, $-0.123$, and $-0.321$ ps/nm and the three seed durations.
3. Simulated the FEL by transporting a 6D Gaussian distribution of $10^6$ particles with BETA through a chromatic matching lattice for 188.8 MeV into GENESIS with a seed described in amplitude and phase, Fourier transforming the exit field at $x=0$ into a $(y,\lambda)$ distribution back-propagated over 10 cm, and adjusting the seed–beam delay to the measured spectral centroid, which reproduced the interference-fringe spacing and curvature; fits of $\hat P_\mathrm{FEL}\propto e^{z/L_g}$ between $z=1.4$ and 2.0 m gave 0.68, 0.61, and 0.55 m for the 1186, 473, and 295 fs seeds.
4. Calibrated absolute pulse energies with the LPA off by splitting the transported seed 60%/40% between a power meter and the spectrometer over two series of ten shots, giving $9\times10^{-5}$ pJ per count, opened the entrance slit to 2 mm, integrated the seed in an ellipse of 100 by 50 pixel radii and the FEL above a manually set wavelength within ±15 pixels of its vertical position, rescaled each shot to the mean seed energy of 33 nJ assuming linear scaling, and found a double-Gaussian fit within 30% overall and 6% for the shortest seed, attributing the larger model deviation for the longest seed to mistuned transport on that day.
5. Scanned the seed–beam delay by ±800, ±350, and ±230 fs for the 1186, 473, and 295 fs seeds, taking the FEL central wavelength and intensity from Gaussian fits to Fourier-filtered spectral line profiles beyond a threshold wavelength; the wavelength rose linearly with delay at a rate set by the seed chirp, as predicted from the single resonance point $\lambda_R(t_0)=\lambda_\mathrm{seed}(t_0)$ plus the undulator red shift with $L_\mathrm{eff}=1.0$ m, and the intensity peaked at an optimal delay within a window that narrowed for shorter seeds, which also widened the seed–FEL interference fringes.

### Comments

- Fig. 1: the caption assigns "(green) $D_{\lambda1}$, (blue) $D_{\lambda2}$ and (red) $D_{\lambda3}$", with $D_{\lambda1}=-0.321$, $D_{\lambda2}=-0.123$, and $D_{\lambda3}=-0.074$ ps/nm, but the lines in panel (b) are labeled $\lambda_\mathrm{seed}(t,D_{\lambda0})$ (green), $\lambda_\mathrm{seed}(t,D_{\lambda1})$ (blue), and $\lambda_\mathrm{seed}(t,D_{\lambda2})$ (red); their slopes, shallowest for green and steepest for red, follow the caption's values, so the panel labels are evidently shifted down by one index.
- Abstract, main text, and Fig. 3(b): the normalized energies $E^*_\mathrm{FEL}$ (dots) of Fig. 3(b) are said to exhibit "a maximum of 45 nJ using the shortest seed duration", and the abstract states that 45 nJ pulse energies were achieved "in good agreement with quantitative numerical simulations", but in the logarithmic Fig. 3(b) the 295 fs measurement lies near 13 nJ, the top of its RMS error bar near 24 nJ, and the simulated star near 12 nJ, so neither the plotted measurements nor the simulations reach 45 nJ; whether 45 nJ is an unplotted single-shot value is unresolved.
- Table I and Fig. 3: Table I lists the seed energy $E_\mathrm{seed}=250$ nJ among the FEL experiment parameters, but the numerical modeling of Fig. 3 uses "an input seed energy of 33 nJ", and the measured seed energies $E_\mathrm{seed}$ extracted from the spectrometer images have mean $\langle E_\mathrm{seed}\rangle=33$ nJ, while the captions of Figs. 2 and 3 refer to Table I for the other parameters; where in the beamline 250 nJ applies is not stated; unresolved.
- Appendix A: "$D_\lambda=\mathrm{GVD}^*\times d\times10^6$", with $D_\lambda$ in ps/nm, $d$ in mm, and $\mathrm{GVD}^*=-4945.5$ ps/(nm.km), gives about $-4.9\times10^{10}$ ps/nm for $d=9.9$ mm, whereas Table II lists $-0.049$ ps/nm $=-4945.5\times9.9\times10^{-6}$, and likewise $-0.098$ and $-0.296$ ps/nm for 19.9 and 59.9 mm; the evident intended factor is $10^{-6}$, the mm-to-km conversion.
- Appendix A, Eq. (A1): after dispersion "the seed pulse duration is increased up to" $\Delta T_\mathrm{seed}=\Delta T_{\mathrm{seed}-i}+d\times\Delta\lambda\times\mathrm{GVD}^*$, but the stated $\mathrm{GVD}^*=-4945.5$ ps/(nm.km) makes the increment negative, giving about $-147$ fs from the Fourier-limited 29.3 fs for $d=9.9$ mm and the 3.6 nm seed bandwidth of Table I, whereas the Table II durations 205.56, 383.60, and 1095.75 fs equal $29.3\ \mathrm{fs}+d\,\Delta\lambda\,|\mathrm{GVD}^*|$ for $d=9.9$, 19.9, and 59.9 mm; the evident intended increment uses $|\mathrm{GVD}^*|$.
- Table II caption: the seed properties are a "Calculation using Eq. (B1)", but Appendix B contains no equation, and Appendix A states that "According to those analytical calculations", which culminate in Eq. (A1), "the seed should exhibit the parameters summarized in Table II"; the evident intended reference is Eq. (A1).
