### Summary

This paper uses global nonlinear gyrokinetic simulations to study how energetic ions modify ion-scale turbulence near safety-factor rational surfaces. The authors separate several competing mechanisms: dilution of thermal profiles, quasi-resonant fast-ion interaction, and turbulence self-interaction that generates zonal shearing layers. They find that fast ions reduce the destabilization threshold for zonal modes, strengthening shearing structures near the $q=1$ surface and producing regions where turbulent transport can fall by about 90%. Tests with fast ions treated as a dilution species confirm that dilution is central to this threshold reduction, while quasi-resonant effects compete with it. At higher fast-ion temperature, the simulations excite an $n=m=1$ fishbone at beta-induced Alfven eigenmode frequency. The fishbone can further reduce turbulence through beat-driven zonal structures when it does not strongly flatten thermal profiles; otherwise, redistribution increases thermal fluxes. The results show that rational surfaces and energetic particles can jointly support turbulence suppression in high-confinement tokamak scenarios.

### Contributions

1. Simulated fast-ion effects on core turbulence with global nonlinear gyrokinetics.
2. Identified fast-ion dilution as a mechanism that lowers the zonal-mode destabilization threshold.
3. Quantified strong local transport suppression near rational surfaces from enhanced shearing layers.
4. Separated dilution, quasi-resonant interaction, and turbulence self-interaction effects.
5. Analyzed how an energetic-particle-driven fishbone can either suppress or enhance thermal transport.
