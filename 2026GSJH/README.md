### Summary

This work combines single-stage stellarator design, which optimizes plasma and coils together, with stochastic coil optimization over ensembles of randomly perturbed coils. A fixed-boundary VMEC equilibrium is coupled to the coils through squared magnetic flux, allowing the objective to favor broad optima that tolerate manufacturing and assembly errors. Warm-started quasi-axisymmetric and quasi-helically symmetric configurations are compared with deterministic single-stage and stochastic stage-II designs using field error, quasisymmetry, engineering constraints, and guiding-center particle loss. The stochastic single-stage method escapes local minima that stall deterministic optimization and attains robustness comparable to stochastic stage II while improving optimized equilibrium quasisymmetry and squared flux. Gains are modest for the quasi-axisymmetric case, but the quasi-helical design has about threefold lower quasisymmetry error at $1.5\,\mathrm{mm}$ perturbations; its perturbed particle loss rises by $44\%$, compared with $430\%$ for the deterministic design. Free-boundary optimization remains a proposed improvement.

### Contributions

1. Unified stochastic coil perturbations with simultaneous fixed-boundary plasma-and-coil optimization.
2. Showed that perturbation-amplitude increases need not degrade optimization of the other physics objectives.
3. Benchmarked the method on quasi-axisymmetric and quasi-helically symmetric warm-start configurations.
4. Quantified tolerance using perturbed quasisymmetry, squared flux, and ensembles of guiding-center particle-loss simulations.
5. Demonstrated that optimizing robustness can be more valuable than pursuing vanishing unperturbed field error.
