**Summary**

Classical resistive magnetohydrodynamics predicts that the fastest linear tearing mode is independent of plasma beta, the ratio of thermal to magnetic pressure. This work relaxes strict pressure isotropy using a nonideal, energy-conserving gyrotropic MHD model. Boundary-layer analysis and pseudospectral eigenvalue calculations show that dynamically generated pressure-anisotropy fluctuations suppress tearing even when the equilibrium is isotropic. At high $\beta$, the maximum growth rate follows $\sigma_{\max}\tau_a\propto\beta^{-1/4}$, while the fastest-growing mode shifts to longer wavelengths. The full scaling retains the classical Lundquist-number dependence but acquires beta corrections in both growth rate and wavenumber. An imposed anisotropy changes the outcome: $p_{\parallel,0}>p_{\perp,0}$ strengthens suppression, whereas $p_{\parallel,0}<p_{\perp,0}$ can offset it and, at low beta, produce faster growth than isotropic MHD predicts. Viscous effects remain consistent with classical magnetic-Prandtl-number behavior. The result narrows the conditions under which plasmoid-mediated reconnection may begin in high-beta astrophysical plasmas. Its scope is the linear, macroscopic, single-fluid regime; Hall, two-fluid, kinetic, turbulent, and nonlinear reconnection physics remain outside the model.

**Five most significant contributions**

1. Derived a linear tearing theory for an energy-conserving nonideal gyrotropic MHD model with self-consistent pressure dynamics.
2. Established the high-beta scaling $\sigma_{\max}\tau_a\propto\beta^{-1/4}$ for initially isotropic equilibria.
3. Generalized the classical fastest-mode scalings to include coupled Lundquist-number and beta dependence in both growth rate and wavenumber.
4. Verified the analytical predictions by solving the complete linear eigenvalue problem with a converged pseudospectral method.
5. Quantified how the sign of equilibrium pressure anisotropy can either suppress tearing or overcome beta stabilization in low-beta plasmas.
