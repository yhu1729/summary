### Summary

Global gyrokinetic simulations investigate ion-temperature-gradient instability and internal-transport-barrier formation close to a tokamak's magnetic axis, where conventional ballooning representations fail. A mean radial-electric-field well raises the critical temperature gradient for linear instability when central magnetic shear is weak, but not when it is strong. The distinction follows from geometric curvature: near the axis it nearly cancels the $E\times B$ shear in the inner half of the well and roughly doubles it in the outer half. Consequently, the electric-field well stabilizes high-toroidal-mode-number turbulence outside, while weak magnetic shear must suppress the low-mode-number instability inside. Nonlinear simulations including ion heating and ion-ion collisions produce an internal transport barrier near $r=0.13a$ only for weak shear and support approximate radial force balance for the mean electric field. The authors therefore propose a complementary mechanism in which weak shear and curvature-modified $E\times B$ stabilization act in different radial regions. Electron neoclassical fluxes and the electric-field generation mechanism are outside the model's scope.
