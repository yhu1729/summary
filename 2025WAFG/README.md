### Summary

This work uses PICNIC, a fully kinetic, exactly energy-conserving particle-in-cell Monte Carlo collision code, to simulate the burn phase of National Ignition Facility shot N210808 in one-dimensional spherical geometry. Initialized from HYDRA profiles about 150 ps before bang time, the model evolves the hot spot, deuterium--tritium ice, and carbon liner while treating radiation, fusion, Coulomb collisions, and nuclear elastic scattering. Large-angle Rutherford and nuclear elastic collisions with fusion $\alpha$ particles generate suprathermal fuel ions and reproduce the measured extent and relative yield of the 15.5--18 MeV $\alpha$ knock-on neutron signal. Adding these processes, however, neither materially changes total yield nor produces the experiment's anomalous greater-than-50-keV primary-neutron spectral shift. PICNIC qualitatively captures HYDRA's burn-wave dynamics but predicts a delayed bang time and higher peak hot-spot temperature; its 2.35--2.45 MJ yields also exceed both HYDRA's 2.1 MJ result and the experimental 1.37 MJ yield. The authors therefore limit the negative conclusion to the modeled one-dimensional collision physics and identify multidimensional magnetic effects, impurity kinetics, neutron scattering, and transport-model differences as unresolved directions.

### Contributions

1. Applies a fully kinetic, energy-conserving PIC-MCC model to the complete burn phase of an ignition-regime inertial-confinement-fusion capsule.
2. Integrates large-angle Rutherford scattering, nuclear elastic scattering, radiation transport, and anisotropic fusion within a self-consistent spherical simulation.
3. Reproduces the experimentally identified extent and relative yield of the 15.5--18 MeV $\alpha$ knock-on neutron spectrum.
4. Shows that the modeled large-angle collision physics does not explain N210808's anomalously large primary-neutron spectral shift.
5. Benchmarks PICNIC against HYDRA's burn evolution and quantifies yield, bang-time, and peak-temperature discrepancies requiring further transport and multidimensional study.
