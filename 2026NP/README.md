### Summary

This paper generalizes gyrokinetic secondary-instability theory to retain toroidal geometry, especially the radial magnetic drift and associated Stringer--Winsor force. The resulting dispersion relations unify three branches of zonal-flow-generating secondary modes: the conventional Rogers--Dorland--Kotschenreuther mode, localized ion-temperature-gradient secondary modes, and the toroidal secondary mode (TSM). The TSM is a growing zonal flow that propagates radially because zonal shearing and magnetic-drift advection create an up--down pressure asymmetry. Gyrokinetic simulations support the predicted TSM frequencies, growth, propagation, and radial-wavenumber threshold. In strongly driven tokamak ion-temperature-gradient turbulence, parallel streaming sets this threshold near $k_x\rho_i\simeq 1/(2q)$, while the TSM dominates localized secondary modes at sufficiently strong drive. The theory applies to arbitrary magnetic geometry and helps explain the short-radial-wavelength zonal structures that contribute to turbulence saturation.

### Contributions

1. Generalized gyrokinetic secondary-mode theory to include toroidal radial magnetic drifts and the Stringer--Winsor force.
2. Derived dispersion relations connecting toroidal, ion-temperature-gradient, and Rogers--Dorland--Kotschenreuther secondary modes.
3. Identified how shearing and drift advection generate the pressure asymmetry that drives propagating toroidal secondary modes.
4. Quantified parameter regimes and radial-wavenumber thresholds for competing secondary instabilities.
5. Validated the toroidal-secondary-mode predictions against gyrokinetic simulations of tokamak turbulence.
