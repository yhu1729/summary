### Summary

This paper studies how plasma-ion motion affects resonantly driven wakefield accelerators, especially schemes that use a train of drivers or self-modulation of a long beam to excite a plasma wave over many periods. Because these concepts rely on persistent electron plasma waves, ion motion can disrupt the beam--plasma resonance and limit wakefield growth. Simulations show two related suppression mechanisms. First, ion motion shifts the plasma response so the drive beam loses resonance with the wave it excites. Second, transverse wavebreaking causes phase mixing, degrading coherent wake excitation. Earlier work emphasized the phase-mixing mechanism; this study shows that resonance loss is a distinct contribution and that both effects share the same scaling with ion mass. The results are directly relevant to recent self-modulation experiments and to the choice of plasma species in resonant wakefield accelerator design. The paper clarifies why heavier ions can be advantageous and how ion dynamics constrain long interaction lengths in multi-period plasma-wave excitation.

### Contributions

1. Simulated ion-motion effects in resonantly driven plasma wakefield acceleration and long-beam self-modulation.
2. Identified resonance loss between the drive beam and plasma wave as a wakefield-suppression mechanism.
3. Distinguished resonance loss from transverse-wavebreaking phase mixing.
4. Demonstrated that both suppression mechanisms scale similarly with ion mass.
5. Connected ion-motion limits to plasma-species choices in recent and future wakefield acceleration experiments.
