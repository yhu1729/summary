### Summary

This study uses direct numerical simulations to determine when fully three-dimensional rotating, stably stratified turbulence develops an inverse energy cascade in a thin periodic domain. Thirty $6144\times6144\times192$ simulations at $Re=500$ vary Rossby and Froude numbers while fixing the aspect ratio and forcing scale. The resulting phase diagram shows that rotation promotes upscale transfer whereas increasing stratification suppresses it; when both effects are strong, the transition occurs near $Ro/Fr\approx80$ for the chosen geometry. Every detected inverse cascade is bidirectional, with some injected energy still transferred to dissipative small scales. Spectral projections distinguish quasi-geostrophic modes from gravity-inertial waves and show how rotation channels energy toward vertically invariant modes that support upscale transfer, while waves contribute to the forward cascade. Several inverse-cascading cases occupy parameter ranges relevant to planetary atmospheres, demonstrating physical plausibility without claiming that the simplified simulations reproduce atmospheric dynamics.

### Contributions

1. Mapped inverse-cascade behavior across 30 high-resolution simulations spanning a broad Rossby-Froude parameter grid.
2. Established how rotation, stratification, and their ratio control the inverse-transfer boundary for the selected geometry.
3. Showed that every observed inverse cascade is split, retaining simultaneous transfer toward dissipative scales.
4. Identified vertically invariant rotational modes as the upscale channel and gravity-inertial modes as an important forward-transfer channel.
5. Demonstrated inverse cascades in non-quasi-geostrophic regimes compatible with selected planetary-atmosphere parameters.
