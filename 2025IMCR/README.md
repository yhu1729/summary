### Summary

Two-dimensional particle-in-cell simulations investigate relativistic turbulence and reconnection in plasmas containing electrons, positrons, and protons at a realistic proton-to-electron mass ratio. Varying the proton fraction changes the hierarchy of magnetic islands, current sheets, and plasmoids generated by the turbulent cascade. Proton-rich mixtures suppress the smallest-scale structures but strengthen global dissipation, showing that composition controls both the number of active reconnection sites and their energy-conversion efficiency. To diagnose the mechanism, the authors derive a relativistic multispecies generalization of Ohm's law. Its term balance indicates that divergence of the electron and positron pressure tensors supplies the principal nonideal electric field governing the reconnection rate. The work extends kinetic reconnection studies beyond pair or electron–ion plasmas and connects composition-dependent microphysics to possible particle acceleration and high-energy emission near black holes and neutron stars.

4. Derived a relativistic multispecies Ohm's law for reconnection diagnostics.
5. Identified electron and positron pressure-tensor divergence as the main reconnection driver.
