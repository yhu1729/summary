### Summary

In DIII-D tokamak measurements, the authors examine the edge harmonic oscillation (EHO), a phase-locked set of magnetic harmonics associated with quiescent high-confinement operation without damaging edge-localized bursts. Bispectral analysis of internal polarimetry, magnetic coils, and edge density fluctuations detects significant nonlinear coupling among EHO harmonics during sustained quiescent operation, supporting a saturation mechanism predicted by magnetohydrodynamic simulations. After core-resonant magnetic tearing modes appear, an intermittent EHO-like edge fluctuation couples to those core modes rather than strongly to its own harmonics, while edge-localized modes persist. Internal interferometer-polarimeter measurements reveal this core--edge coupling more clearly than external coils. The observations suggest that tearing-mode interaction can interrupt coherent EHO formation; the proposed momentum-transfer mechanism and the benefit of tearing-mode control remain hypotheses for further modeling and experiments.

### Contributions

1. Provided direct experimental evidence of nonlinear coupling among EHO harmonics in quiescent H-mode.
2. Detected coupling between a transient EHO-like fluctuation and core-resonant tearing modes.
3. Distinguished the coupled modes using internal magnetic, external coil, and edge-density diagnostics.
4. Associated harmonic decoupling with the return of edge-localized bursts in the measured discharge.
5. Identified core--edge mode interaction as a possible constraint on sustained quiescent confinement.
