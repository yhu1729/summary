### Summary

This review and perspective compares thermal, inertial, beam-driven,
plasmon-assisted, and muon-catalyzed fusion through nuclear reaction networks,
fuel supply, stopping, radiation losses, and neutron production. The authors
argue that deuterium--tritium systems must be assessed together with tritium
breeding, activation, shielding, and the energy carried by 14 MeV neutrons.
They show that deuterium--deuterium burn produces tritium and helium-3 whose
secondary reactions materially change heating and neutron balances, and they
quantify how helium-3 additions alter thermal power balance. For nonthermal
fusion, the paper estimates beam interaction lengths, reviews nonlinear
electron screening near heavy nuclei, and examines proton--boron, boron
nitride, and proposed beryllium--helium-3 reaction cycles. It also evaluates
reported nanostructured-target signatures and routes to improve muon-catalyzed
fusion. Several proposals remain conceptual: sustaining the necessary beams,
separating fusion products from other signals, and achieving sufficient
fusions per muon require experimental and system-level validation. One author
discloses a related patent interest.

### Contributions

1. Integrated reaction-network, neutron, tritium, and fuel-cycle constraints into comparisons of fusion concepts.
2. Quantified secondary burn channels in deuterium fuel and the thermal effect of helium-3 admixture.
3. Analyzed interaction lengths and electron screening for beam-driven fusion in dense targets.
4. Assessed proton--boron and nanostructured-target evidence and proposed coupled boron-nitride and beryllium--helium-3 cycles.
5. Revisited muon-catalyzed fusion thresholds and possible muon-production and reactivation routes.
