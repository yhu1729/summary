### Summary

This paper studies electrostatic instabilities of one-dimensional Bernstein--Greene--Kruskal equilibria using high-precision Vlasov--Poisson simulations with controlled collisionality and resolution. The initial state is produced by driving initially thermalized electrons with an external electric field until high-amplitude electron acoustic waves create a stationary two-hole phase-space configuration. After the driver is removed, large-scale density perturbations trigger an instability in which vortices merge into a one-hole state. By varying the short-range collision rate and grid resolution, the simulations separate physical collisional effects from numerical phase-space dissipation. The growth rate follows the Landau-theory expectation that it is controlled by the distribution-function slope in the resonant region, while the instability onset time is delayed by collisions. Long-time simulations also characterize the saturation stage through Hermite spectral analysis. Collisionless, well-resolved runs exhibit a power-law Hermite spectrum associated with constant enstrophy flux, whereas significant collisional effects introduce a high-mode cutoff and decay. The work clarifies how BGK equilibria lose stability and how collisions modify the timing and spectral structure without simply changing the resonant growth mechanism.

### Contributions

1. Constructed unstable BGK equilibria through self-consistent Vlasov--Poisson simulations driven by high-amplitude electron acoustic waves.
2. Quantified the transition from a two-hole to one-hole phase-space state under large-scale density perturbations.
3. Separated effects of physical collisionality and grid resolution on instability onset and growth.
4. Verified that the growth rate is governed by the resonant distribution-function slope, consistent with weakly inhomogeneous Landau theory.
5. Used Hermite spectra to distinguish collisionless enstrophy-flux behavior from collisional high-mode cutoff and decay.
