### Summary

Weibel-generated fields must merge before seeding large-scale cosmic magnetism, but magnetohydrodynamics omits particle-scale kinetics. Using fully kinetic, 2.5-dimensional particle-in-cell simulations of decaying electron--positron plasmas, the paper follows reconnection-driven coalescence of magnetic islands at moderate to high plasma $\beta$. Mergers initially transfer magnetic energy to larger scales. They also create pressure anisotropy, however, and falling instability thresholds excite mirror and especially firehose fluctuations. Near firehose marginality, effective magnetic tension vanishes; reconnection and contraction then slow or stop, leaving elongated structures near the particle Larmor scale. Simulations with guide fields show that the outcome is nonmonotonic: an intermediate field produces a frozen, worm-like state, whereas a stronger field prevents firehose onset and permits inverse transfer. Greater separation between island size and Larmor radius also prevents complete arrest because firehose activity saturates below the reconnection scale. A Chew--Goldberger--Low model yields a criterion in initial $\beta$ and normalized guide-field strength for crossing the firehose boundary, agrees with simulated anisotropy trajectories, and predicts limited scale growth after crossing. The mechanism may confine Weibel seeds to microscopic scales in high-$\beta$ astrophysical plasmas. Extrapolation remains limited by the simulations' two-dimensional geometry and pair-plasma composition.

### Contributions

1. Identified pressure-anisotropy-driven microinstabilities as a kinetic mechanism that can suppress reconnection-mediated inverse magnetic-energy transfer.
2. Showed that firehose marginality removes magnetic tension at the merger scale and produces arrested, elongated magnetic structures in marginally magnetized plasmas.
3. Demonstrated that stronger guide fields or greater separation between island and Larmor scales restore continued coalescence.
4. Derived and validated an analytical criterion relating initial plasma $\beta$ and guide-field strength to firehose onset and cascade suppression.
5. Connected the suppression mechanism to the viability of Weibel-generated seed fields for cosmic magnetogenesis while identifying dimensional and plasma-composition limitations.
