### Summary

The paper presents the vacuum-vessel design and three-dimensional finite-element assessment for PRAGYA, a privately developed low-aspect-ratio tokamak with major radius about $0.4\,\mathrm{m}$, minor radius above $0.18\,\mathrm{m}$, plasma current up to $25\,\mathrm{kA}$, and toroidal field $0.1\,\mathrm{T}$. The SS304L vessel is split by a toroidal electrical break to reduce eddy currents and uses double O-ring seals, multiple diagnostic and pumping ports, internal stiffeners, and supported pumping to reach approximately $10^{-6}\,\mathrm{mbar}$. A mesh-refinement study motivates a roughly $1.012$-million-element model and selection of a $6\,\mathrm{mm}$ wall. Under atmospheric pressure and self-weight, the model predicts about $110\,\mathrm{MPa}$ maximum von Mises stress and $0.5\,\mathrm{mm}$ maximum deformation. Baking at $150\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ produces about $280\,\mathrm{MPa}$ peak secondary stress, judged acceptable under the adopted criteria. Linear buckling analyses find the support leg and vessel assembly stable for the modeled loads. Electromagnetic loading during normal operation and disruptions is deferred.

### Contributions

1. Specified an integrated vacuum-vessel design for the compact PRAGYA tokamak and its operational geometry.
2. Incorporated a toroidal electrical break, double O-ring seals, ports, stiffeners, supports, and pumping interfaces.
3. Used mesh refinement and thickness studies to select a converged model and a $6\,\mathrm{mm}$ SS304L wall.
4. Quantified stress and deformation under atmospheric pressure, self-weight, and $150\,^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ baking.
5. Assessed linear buckling margins for the support leg and complete vessel assembly while defining the electromagnetic-load limitation.
