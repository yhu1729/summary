### Summary

This study applies the STRIPE framework to carbon erosion and impurity transport around DIII-D's high-power helicon antenna. STRIPE combines SOLPS-ITER edge-plasma backgrounds, COMSOL radio-frequency sheath fields, RustBCA sputtering yields, and GITR/GITRm full-orbit transport. Two H-mode discharges compare antenna--plasma gaps of roughly $7\,\mathrm{cm}$ and $4\,\mathrm{cm}$. Modeled rectified sheath potentials reach $1$--$5\,\mathrm{kV}$ and localize near the antenna bottom, where field lines meet surfaces at grazing incidence. Carbon self-sputtering dominates; sheath-accelerated deuterium contributes about 1% of gross erosion. The small-gap case produces $7.2\times10^{20}$ eroded particles per second, redeposits about 12.3%, and sends 58% into the confined plasma, whereas the large-gap case yields much less erosion, about 4% redeposition, and 35% core penetration. Total helicon-sourced carbon remains small relative to the background, consistent with present graphite-wall observations. Quantitative sheath amplitudes remain uncertain because current models underresolve short-wavelength slow waves and are not validated for nearly tangential fields, a concern for future high-$Z$ walls.

### Contributions

1. Coupled edge plasma, full-wave sheath, sputtering, and full-orbit impurity models in one antenna analysis.
2. Predicted kilovolt rectified sheaths localized by antenna geometry and grazing magnetic incidence.
3. Quantified carbon self-sputtering and the smaller sheath-accelerated deuterium contribution.
4. Compared erosion, redeposition, and core penetration for two antenna--plasma gaps.
5. Identified sheath-model and slow-wave-resolution limitations relevant to high-$Z$ plasma-facing materials.
