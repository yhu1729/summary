# 2026CH2

## Claude (September 2026)

### Summary

Finite element Hessian, elasticity, and divdiv complexes in three dimensions have so far been built case by case. Arnold and Hu derive the continuous complexes with the Bernstein–Gelfand–Gelfand (BGG) construction from stacked de Rham complexes whose Sobolev smoothness matches, but finite element spaces conforming only in $H(\mathrm{curl})$ or $H(\mathrm{div})$ lack the tangential or normal continuity that the connecting maps require. The authors introduce two reduction operations on short exact sequences, a tilde operation that restricts the target space and takes its preimage and a hat operation that restricts the domain through an auxiliary operator, plus an inverse-hat augmentation, each preserving exactness under stated conditions. Applied to their smooth finite element de Rham complexes on tetrahedral meshes, whose vertex, edge, and face smoothness is set by vectors $r=(r^v,r^e,r^f)$, the BGG construction yields exact finite element Hessian, elasticity, and divdiv complexes for families of smoothness vectors and degrees, with bubble counterparts. Degrees of freedom for the new tensor spaces come from tangential–normal decompositions, face trace complexes, and two-dimensional analogues. Special cases recover several earlier complexes, but those on Alfeld splits, which correspond to $r^0=(2,1,1)$, lie outside the framework, and no discretization of a specific PDE is analysed.

### Contributions

1. Formulated tilde, hat, and inverse-hat operations on short exact sequences, with dimension-count criteria for exactness, that make the BGG construction applicable to finite element spaces with mismatched continuity.
2. Derived exact finite element Hessian complexes, with a variant allowing inequality constraints between smoothness vectors, that recover an earlier finite element Hessian complex for $r^0=(4,2,1)$ and $k\ge7$.
3. Derived exact finite element elasticity complexes for $r^0\ge(2,1,0)$, including a smoother variant beginning in $H^2$, by combining BGG with inverse hat operations.
4. Derived exact finite element divdiv complexes and relaxed variants for $r^0\ge(1,0,0)$, recovering two earlier finite element divdiv complexes.
5. Constructed unisolvent degrees of freedom for $H(\mathrm{curl};\mathbb S)$, $H(\mathrm{inc};\mathbb S)$, and $H(\mathrm{sym}\,\mathrm{curl};\mathbb T)$ elements from tangential–normal decompositions, trace complexes, and polynomial bubble complexes.

### Comments

- §4.2, proof of Lemma 4.6, Eq. (35): the identity is printed as $\dim\mathbb V^{\mathrm{div}}_{k-1}(\boldsymbol r;\mathbb M)-\dim\mathbb V^{\mathrm{div}}_{k-1}(\boldsymbol r;\mathbb T)=\dim\mathbb V^{L^2}_k(\boldsymbol r)$, but the difference between $\mathbb M$- and $\mathbb T$-valued spaces of degree $k-1$ is the trace part of degree $k-1$, and the proof of Lemma 6.2 uses (35) with right-hand side $\dim\mathbb V^{L^2}_{k-1}(\boldsymbol r_2)$; the intended subscript is $k-1$.
- §5.3, Lemma 5.20: the lemma assumes only that $(\boldsymbol r_2,\boldsymbol r_2\ominus1,k)$ is div stable, but its proof states "We do need the div stability $\operatorname{div}\mathbb V^{\mathrm{div}}_{k+2}(\boldsymbol r_0)=\mathbb V^{L^2}_{k+1}(\boldsymbol r_1)$", which appears neither in the lemma nor in (55) or Theorem 5.21; whether it follows from the stated hypotheses is unresolved.
- §6.2, DoF (67i), and the proof of Lemma 6.5: the face moments of $\Pi_f(\operatorname{inc}\boldsymbol\tau)\boldsymbol n$ are taken against $\mathbb B^{\mathrm{div}}_{k-1}(f;\boldsymbol r_2)/\mathrm{RT}(f)$, and the proof claims that these moments vanish for $\boldsymbol q\in\mathrm{RT}(f)$ when $\boldsymbol\tau|_e=0$ and $(\operatorname{curl}\boldsymbol\tau)^\top|_e=0$, but the analogous $H(\mathrm{div};\mathbb S)$ DoF (32d) uses $\mathrm{RM}(f)$, and for $\boldsymbol\tau=(xy(1-x-y-z))^3\,\boldsymbol e_1\otimes\boldsymbol e_1$, which satisfies both edge conditions on the face $z=0$, the moment against the dilation field in $\mathrm{RT}(f)$ equals $1/16800$ while those against constants and rotations vanish; the intended quotient is evidently $\mathrm{RM}(f)$.
- §6.3, after Eq. (82): the count is justified by "div div $\mathbb B^{\mathrm{div}\,\mathrm{div}^+}_k(\boldsymbol r_2;\mathbb T)=\mathbb B_{k-2}(\boldsymbol r_3)/\mathrm{RT}$", whereas (82) itself uses $\mathbb B^{\mathrm{div}\,\mathrm{div}^+}_k(\boldsymbol r_2;\mathbb S)$ and Lemma 4.18 states the result for $\mathbb S$-valued bubbles with quotient $\mathbb P_1(T)$; the count is unaffected because both quotients have dimension 4.
