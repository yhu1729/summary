### Summary

This paper develops a unified kinematic theory separating rigid rotation from spin or shear contributions to vorticity in three-dimensional flow. Direction-dependent decompositions are derived from the motion of material line and surface elements, whose angular velocities contain complementary strain-induced terms around the mean $\boldsymbol{\omega}/2$. The surface-element spin is shown to equal the relative vorticity in the generalized Caswell formula, linking it directly to surface shear stress. Analogous decompositions are constructed for streamlines and streamsurfaces. In the real-Schur representation of the velocity-gradient tensor, two intrinsic vorticity decompositions distinguish positive- and negative-spin cases associated with Liutex--shear and complementary anti-KKB mechanisms. The authors prove that direction-dependent modes on the rotation-axis-normal plane are bounded by these intrinsic extrema. Simple shear, point and Burgers vortices, vortex-sheet instability, triaxial strain, and Saturn's polar flow illustrate how the framework distinguishes orbital rotation, vortex structure, and shear.

### Contributions

1. Derived direction-dependent vorticity decompositions from material line- and surface-element rotation.
2. Proved complementary relations between orthogonal line and surface angular velocities.
3. Identified surface-element spin with relative vorticity and surface shear stress.
4. Formulated two invariant decompositions describing KKB and anti-KKB rotational mechanisms.
5. Established bounds linking streamline-based rotation modes to intrinsic vorticity extrema.
