### Summary

This paper analyzes plasma wakefield acceleration driven by profiled trains of triangular electron bunches. The central result is that the conditions for forming plateau-like accelerating or decelerating wakefields coincide with the conditions for placing bunches in their most stable focusing regions. Analytical formulas determine bunch spacing and peak-density ratios for triangular and rectangular precursors, and show that the density ratio becomes universal from the third bunch onward. The authors also analyze driver-witness pairs, finding that the accelerating field can be restored after each pair, which theoretically permits indefinitely repeated bunch trains. Quasi-static 2.5D PIC simulations with LCODE verify the predicted bunch parameters and plateau fields. Ion mobility breaks the ideal infinite-train picture by shifting ions and eventually introducing inhomogeneity, but the simulations indicate that short stable trains with uniform acceleration remain feasible. The work provides design rules for stable low-charge bunch-train plasma accelerators.

### Contributions

1. Derived plateau-formation conditions for triangular bunch trains with different precursor shapes.
2. Showed that plateau conditions coincide with high-stability focusing-force placement.
3. Identified a universal peak-density ratio for bunches beginning with the third bunch.
4. Verified the analytical design rules using quasi-static 2.5D PIC simulations.
5. Quantified how ion mobility limits infinite trains while preserving feasible short stable trains.
