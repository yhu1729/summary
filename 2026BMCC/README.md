### Summary

This paper develops a unified description of transverse electron-beam dynamics across a laser-plasma accelerator's density up-ramp, plateau, down-ramp, and neighboring transport lines. The transverse focusing gradient $K$ controls the evolution of the Twiss parameters and emittance. For a constant-$K$ plateau, the authors derive formulas for chromatic betatron decoherence, the distance to saturation, and the saturated emittance; particle-in-cell and transfer-matrix simulations validate these predictions. Matching $\alpha=0$ and $\beta=1/\sqrt{K}$ at a plasma-section entrance suppresses emittance growth, and a matched beam can remain matched as $K$ subsequently varies without requiring an adiabatic ramp. Density ramps chiefly ease coupling to conventional optics by reducing the Twiss parameter $\gamma=(1+\alpha^2)/\beta$ before the beam reaches a transport line, where chromatic growth can otherwise exceed that inside the plasma by orders of magnitude. Simulations of linear, exponential, and Gaussian ramps show that ramp length matters more than detailed shape and quantify practical mismatch tolerances. The results support joint optimization of ramps, plasma plateaus, and transport optics in multistage accelerators.

### Contributions

1. Identified the focusing gradient $K$ as the organizing parameter for transverse dynamics throughout a realistic plasma stage.
2. Derived consistent expressions for betatron decoherence length and saturated emittance from energy spread and initial Twiss mismatch.
3. Validated the analytical model with FBPIC, WAKE-T, and TRACEWIN simulations.
4. Showed that entrance matching preserves emittance under subsequent variations of $K$ independently of adiabaticity.
5. Established ramp-design guidelines and mismatch tolerances that reduce $\gamma$ and limit chromatic emittance growth in adjoining transport lines.
