# 2025RGHL

## Claude (October 2026)

### Summary

The authors accelerate $s$-stage implicit Runge–Kutta (IRK) time stepping, mainly Gauss–Legendre, for the linear wave equation $u_{tt} - c^2\Delta u = b$ with Neumann conditions, written as a first-order system and discretized by $P_1$ finite elements with mass and stiffness matrices $M$ and $E$. After factoring out $A \otimes I$ and using the eigendecomposition $A^{-T} = WDW^{-1}$, the $2Ns \times 2Ns$ stage system becomes a Sylvester equation equivalent to $s$ shifted systems $(\bar{M} - (h_t/d_i)B)\bar{K}_i = (FW)_i/d_i$, of which conjugate symmetry leaves $\lceil s/2\rceil$ to solve. Each is solved by GMRES with a block LU preconditioner needing one solve with $M$ (two Gauss–Seidel sweeps) and one with $M + (h_t/d_i)^2E$, where the complex $d_i$ is replaced by the real average $d_{avg}$ so that one real AMG setup and V-cycle serve all shifts. The systems are solved separately ($\mathcal{P}^I_{Syl}$) or stacked ($\mathcal{P}^{II}_{Syl}$) to batch sparse products. Against block triangular preconditioners $\mathcal{P}_{LD}$, $\mathcal{P}_\kappa$, and a new $\mathcal{P}_{TAI}$ minimizing $\|L^{-1}A - I\|_2$, the Sylvester approach is about twice as fast for $s = 5$ on $h = 2^{-9}$ meshes with fewer V-cycles and scales as $O(sN)$ apart from the $W$ transform. Results cover two-dimensional linear problems; nonlinear and time-dependent coefficients are deferred.

### Contributions

1. Derived the Sylvester reformulation (3.1)–(3.3): with $\hat{B} = -h_t\bar{M}^{-1}B$ and $\hat{k} = (A \otimes I_{2N})k$, the system becomes $\hat{B}\hat{K} + \hat{K}A^{-T} = \hat{F}$, which $A^{-T} = WDW^{-1}$ turns into shifted systems $(\bar{M} - (h_t/d_i)B)(\bar{K})_i = (FW)_i/d_i$ with stages recovered by $k = (A^{-1} \otimes I_{2N})\,\mathrm{vec}(\bar{K}DW^{-1})$ (3.5); showed $(FW)_{2i} = \overline{(FW)_{2i-1}}$, so solutions for eigenvalues with negative imaginary part follow by conjugation.
2. Replaced $M + (h_t/d_i)^2E$ by $M + (h_t/d_{avg})^2E$ with $d_{avg} = (d_1 + \cdots + d_s)/s$, which is real because the eigenvalues pair, minimizes $\|D - dI\|_F$, and was found numerically close to the best real shift for the slowest system; the resulting exact preconditioner (4.4) has $(2,2)$ block $h_t^2(d_i^2 - d_{avg}^2)/(d_i^2d_{avg}^2)\,E + M$, and one AMG setup replaces $\lceil s/2\rceil$ complex ones.
3. Introduced $\mathcal{P}_{TAI}$, the block lower triangular preconditioner $I_s \otimes \bar{M} - h_tL \otimes B$ with $L$ minimizing $\|L^{-1}A - I\|_2$ over lower triangular matrices, computed from $s$ small least-squares problems instead of the Nelder–Mead minimization of $\kappa(L^{-1}A)$ in $\mathcal{P}_\kappa$; for $h = 2^{-3}$ and $s = 3, 5$ its exactly constructed version had the eigenvalues best separated from the origin among $\mathcal{P}_{LD}$, $\mathcal{P}_\kappa$, and $\mathcal{P}_{TAI}$ (Fig. 4.1), although the fields of values for $s = 5$ all contain $(-2, 4) \times (-3i, 3i)$.
4. Reported in Table 4.1 eigenbasis condition numbers of 3.5 to 3.9 for the $\mathcal{P}^I_{Syl}$ systems and $\mathcal{P}^{II}_{Syl}$ with $s = 2$ to 5, against $5.8 \times 10^7$ to $1.6 \times 10^8$ for the block preconditioners, which fall to $1.1 \times 10^2$ to $5.4 \times 10^2$ once the single zero-eigenvalue mode of the singular Neumann stiffness matrix is removed, noting that naive numpy.linalg routines give inaccurate values because of the dense eigenvalue clusters.
5. At $h = 2^{-9}$, $\mathcal{P}^I_{Syl}$ needed fewer AMG V-cycles for $s \ge 4$ and about half the runtime of $\mathcal{P}_{TAI}$ at $s = 5$ and throughout mesh refinement (Figs. 4.4, 4.5), with runtime linear in $N$ and, after division by $s$, overlapping across stage counts, whereas $\mathcal{P}_{TAI}$ shifted as $O(s^2N)$ (Fig. 4.6); $\mathcal{P}^{II}_{Syl}$ outperformed the others with SuperLU multiple right-hand sides but only for $s \ge 4$ with pyAMG (Fig. 4.7); the space-variable wave and Klein–Gordon variants with $\kappa = 1 + 0.75\cos(\sqrt{2}x_1 + x_2/\sqrt{3})$ and $\beta = 0.75 + 0.375\sin(x_1/\sqrt{6} - \sqrt{5}x_2)$ showed $O(h_x^2)$ errors without order reduction (Fig. 4.9).

### Comments

- Sections 4.1 and 4.3: Section 4.1 states that $\mathcal{P}^I_{Syl}$ and $\mathcal{P}^{II}_{Syl}$ need "$\lceil s/2\rceil$ V-cycles and $2s$ Gauss–Seidel iterations per application", whereas Section 4.3 counts for $\mathcal{P}^I_{Syl}$ "two V-cycles" and "two iterations of Gauss–Seidel" per GMRES iteration of each of the $\lceil s/2\rceil$ systems, that is, $2\lceil s/2\rceil$ of each; unresolved.
- Section 4.3, paragraph on $\mathcal{P}^{II}_{Syl}$: the batched solves are with "$M - (h_t/d_{avg})^2E$" and the compared system matrices are "$M - (h_t/d_{avg})^2E$ and $M - h_t^2l_{ii}^2E$", whereas (3.6), Section 3.1, and Section 4.1 define these matrices as $M + (h_t/d_{avg})^2E$ and $M + h_t^2l_{ii}^2E$; the plus sign is evidently intended.
