### Summary

This paper analyzes neoclassical transport in large-aspect-ratio tokamak barriers where density, temperature, and potential vary over the ion poloidal gyroradius $\rho_p$, yet the banana-orbit width remains smaller by $\sqrt{\epsilon}$. This intermediate ordering retains an analytic drift-kinetic treatment while excluding finite-orbit-width loss. Strong radial electric fields shift the trapped-particle region, and small poloidal variations of density, potential, parallel flow, and ion temperature alter transport at leading order. The ion parallel-momentum balance couples momentum and particle flux: without a parallel-momentum source, neoclassical particle transport is small, whereas external or turbulence-mediated momentum input can drive a much larger ion particle flux while preserving ambipolarity. Coupling quasineutrality to the transport equations makes profile prediction nonlinear. Examples yield up to three density-gradient solutions for a prescribed particle flux and four radial-electric-field solutions for fixed local profiles; the latter have energy fluxes differing by 56%. The authors suggest that transitions among coexisting roots could relate to low- and high-transport states or H--L back-transitions, but this interpretation remains conjectural and the theory is restricted to the stated ordering.

### Contributions

1. Clarified the strong-gradient neoclassical ordering between local and finite-orbit-width regimes.
2. Showed that poloidal profile variation modifies trapping and neoclassical fluxes at leading order.
3. Derived the coupling between parallel-momentum sources and ion particle transport in barriers.
4. Formulated nonlinear profile prediction through quasineutrality-dependent transport coefficients.
5. Demonstrated multiple density-gradient and electric-field solutions with distinct energy transport.
