### Summary

This paper identifies a kinetic mechanism for rapid ion-electron energy exchange in moderate-Alfvénic-Mach-number, quasiparallel magnetized collisionless shocks. Two-dimensional OSIRIS particle-in-cell simulations model counter-streaming ion populations in an electron background, while a linear multifluid dispersion solver identifies unstable modes. Magnetized runs transfer ion drift energy to electrons much faster than Coulomb collisions; in a representative shock-transition case electrons reach about $450\,\mathrm{eV}$ and $T_e/T_i>0.2$, whereas unmagnetized runs exchange much less energy. The mechanism has two stages: resonances between electron-whistler and drifting-ion Alfvén or magnetosonic waves extract ion drift energy, then Landau resonance transfers wave-mediated energy to ions and electrons. Oblique-field and physical-mass-ratio simulations retain the predicted modal structure. The authors propose that the resulting coupling could inform two-temperature magnetohydrodynamic or hybrid models of astrophysical and fusion plasmas.

### Contributions

1. Reproduced faster-than-Coulomb ion-electron energy exchange in a simplified kinetic shock-transition model.
2. Demonstrated that an imposed magnetic field is essential for strong coupling in the studied regime.
3. Identified electron-whistler resonances with ion magnetosonic and Alfvén waves using multifluid theory.
4. Showed that Landau resonance mediates subsequent ion and electron thermalization.
5. Verified the mode picture for oblique fields and the physical ion-electron mass ratio.
