### Summary

This paper uses global gyrokinetic particle-in-cell simulations with ORB5 to determine how turbulence generates radially extended, frequency-coherent zonal structures in realistic TCV and ASDEX Upgrade magnetic configurations. Self-consistent simulations show that ion-temperature-gradient turbulence produces these global structures and identify high toroidal mode numbers as the effective drivers. The authors then extract the spatial structure of selected turbulent modes and impose it through a nonzonal electrostatic antenna, isolating the nonlinear coupling from the full turbulent system. A single $n=80$ antenna reproduces a global zonal structure in the studied TCV case, demonstrating that generation does not require a statistical superposition of many modes. Frequency scans find a zonal response at twice the antenna frequency, consistent with three-wave coupling to an $n=-80$ sideband. Because lower-$n$ ion-temperature-gradient frequencies approach zero, the mechanism also implies a threshold below which modes cannot drive a response in the geodesic-acoustic-mode frequency range.

### Contributions

1. Produced global zonal structures self-consistently in realistic TCV and ASDEX Upgrade gyrokinetic simulations.
2. Identified the high-$n$ ion-temperature-gradient spectrum as the source of the nonlinear excitation.
3. Implemented a realistic nonzonal antenna in ORB5 to isolate the generation mechanism.
4. Reproduced a global zonal response using a single toroidal antenna mode.
5. Connected frequency doubling and the low-$n$ drive threshold to three-wave coupling.
