### Summary

This paper combines an Alcator C-Mod discharge database, high-resolution Thomson scattering, neutral measurements, phase-contrast imaging, and pedestal models to study the transition between ELMy and enhanced-$D_\alpha$ H-modes. In ELMy operation, pedestal density responds to neutral sources; in EDA operation it is comparatively insensitive, consistent with stronger particle transport. Quasi-coherent fluctuations strengthen and become more coherent across the transition before weakening at the highest densities. The Saarelma--Connor neutral-transport model reproduces ELMy pedestal densities up to about $2.0\times10^{20}\,\mathrm{m}^{-3}$. Adding a resistive-ballooning-mode transport term improves agreement for EDA cases up to about $3.0\times10^{20}\,\mathrm{m}^{-3}$. EPED scans show that the separatrix-to-pedestal density ratio shifts the peeling--ballooning branch transition and changes predicted pressure. Applied to SPARC scenarios, added edge transport weakens the near-separatrix density gradient and lowers predicted pedestal density by roughly $20\%$. Model coefficients and boundary inputs remain important uncertainties.

### Contributions

1. Linked neutral-source sensitivity to the ELMy-to-EDA change in pedestal particle transport.
2. Mapped quasi-coherent fluctuation evolution across density and confinement regimes.
3. Tested the Saarelma--Connor pedestal-density model against high-density C-Mod data.
4. Introduced a resistive-ballooning transport term that improves EDA density predictions.
5. Quantified separatrix-density effects in EPED and propagated them to SPARC pedestal scenarios.
