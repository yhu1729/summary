### Summary

Beam-driven plasma wakefield accelerators must extract most of a high-energy drive bunch while transferring tens to hundreds of joules per collider stage. This experiment uses a laser-ionized, all-optical hydrogen plasma source to push both depletion and transferred energy toward that regime. A $1.52\,\mathrm{nC}$, $10\,\mathrm{GeV}$ electron bunch transfers at least $5.6\,\mathrm{J}$ to a plasma with density $4.5\times10^{16}\,\mathrm{cm}^{-3}$, corresponding to a measured drive-to-wake efficiency of at least $37\pm3\%$. Some driver electrons decelerate below $0.93\,\mathrm{GeV}$, losing more than 90% of their initial energy, while as much as 90% of the bunch charge participates in the interaction. The results demonstrate multi-joule wake excitation with deep depletion in one stage, although the efficiency remains below the greater-than-70% target envisioned for collider operation.

### Contributions

1. Demonstrated at least $5.6\,\mathrm{J}$ of drive-beam energy transfer to a plasma wake.
2. Measured a drive-to-wake energy-transfer efficiency of at least $37\pm3\%$.
3. Decelerated some $10\,\mathrm{GeV}$ electrons to below $0.93\,\mathrm{GeV}$.
4. Achieved wakefield participation by up to 90% of the drive-bunch charge.
5. Established an all-optical plasma source as a route toward collider-scale depletion experiments.
