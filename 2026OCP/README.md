# 2026OCP

## Claude (September 2026)

### Summary

Electrostatic particle-in-cell simulations solve a Poisson equation each time step, where sheaths create localized steep gradients that make uniformly high-order discretizations wasteful. The authors extend the hybridizable discontinuous Galerkin spectral element (HDG-SEM) Poisson solver of PICLas from one polynomial degree to element-local degrees on curved hexahedra. In this background method, eliminating the element unknowns (static condensation) of a first-order system for the potential $\varphi$ and displacement $\mathbf{D}=\varepsilon\mathbf{E}$ leaves a global system for the face potential $\lambda$. Here each face takes the larger degree of its two elements, and particle charges are projected onto the basis without shape functions. Between runs, degrees are updated by Legendre modal analysis of time-averaged potential and charge densities, with density thresholds scaled by an estimated sampling variance. Prescribed distributions match uniform-degree errors with fewer unknowns for a dielectric sphere and a plasma sheath; in an axisymmetric ion optic, indicator-driven degrees up to 5 between the grids reproduce the uniform-degree-5 axial ion-density profile to 4.6% (relative $L^2$) in a final run 1.276 times faster. The authors note that the threshold multipliers are empirical, that sparse particles near the axis inflate the variance estimate and block refinement there, and that conditioning under nonuniform degrees is unexamined.

### Contributions

1. Following Cockburn et al., took the numerical traces $(\mathbf D^*\cdot\mathbf n)|_K=(\mathbf D\cdot\mathbf n)|_K+\tau_K(\varphi-\varphi^*)$ with $\tau_K=2|K|^{-1/3}$, multiplied the weak form of $\nabla\cdot\mathbf D=\rho$ by $-1$ to obtain symmetric element matrices, and condensed them into trace blocks $\mathcal S^K_{F,F'}=\tilde{\mathcal E}_{F,K}\tilde{\mathcal D}_K^{-1}\tilde{\mathcal E}_{F',K}^T+\mathcal C_{F,K}\mathcal A_K^{-1}\mathcal C_{F',K}^T-\delta_{F,F'}\mathcal F^K_{F,F}$ with $\tilde{\mathcal D}_K=\mathcal D_K-\mathcal B_K\mathcal A_K^{-1}\mathcal B_K^T$, whose sizes and entries but not coupling pattern change with the local degrees, stating that for $\varepsilon>0$, a non-degenerate mesh, and $\tau_K>0$ the element systems stay nonsingular for any degree distribution and the trace matrix is symmetric positive definite for a nonempty Dirichlet boundary.
2. Evaluated integrals by Gauss quadrature at the Legendre–Gauss interpolation nodes, making the $\mathbf D$ mass matrix block diagonal with a $3\times3$ block $\omega_i\omega_j\omega_k\mathcal J_{ijk}^T\mathcal J_{ijk}/(\varepsilon J_{ijk})$ per node and, since $p(F)\ge p(K)$, adding no face quadrature error, and implemented variable degrees with derived element and face types stored in one-dimensional arrays, a global side index and rotation (one of five orientations) per local face, only $\tilde{\mathcal E}_K$ and $\tilde{\mathcal D}_K$ precomputed, and PETSc solves by Cholesky factorization or BoomerAMG-preconditioned conjugate gradients.
3. Nested the leapfrog PIC time loop in an outer loop of runs (Algorithm 1), each run using fixed degrees and time-averaging $\varphi$ and species densities, after which the indicator is evaluated separately along each coordinate and an element's degree may rise by one, stay, or fall to any lower value; a density mode counts as noise when $\hat\rho_k^2<\varepsilon\sigma_k^2$, with $\sigma_k^2=|K|^2\Sigma_{kk}/N_p$ from the single-particle covariance $\boldsymbol\Sigma=\mathbf M^{-1}\big(\int_{-1}^{1}\boldsymbol\psi\boldsymbol\psi^T\bar f\,d\xi\big)\mathbf M^{-T}-\boldsymbol\mu\boldsymbol\mu^T$ of the modal coefficients evaluated for a uniform $f=1/|K|$, and with the radial Jacobian $J_r=\iint2\pi rJ\,d\eta\,d\zeta$ in axisymmetry.
4. For the dielectric sphere ($\varepsilon_r=10$, $R=1$ m, analytic Dirichlet data at radius $2R$, $p_{\max}=2$ to 5 on three meshes), prescribed degree 2 in elements inside the sphere not touching its surface, because the nonconstant Jacobian of the trilinear interior elements causes integration errors at degree 1; for the sheath ($L=0.03$ m, particle weight $w_p=10^6$, $\Delta t=10^{-8}$ s), degree 4 in the two wall elements and 1 elsewhere (14 DOFs) gave a relative $L^2$ error of $4.520\times10^{-3}$, against $2.775\times10^{-1}$ for uniform degree 1 (8 DOFs), $5.308\times10^{-3}$ for uniform degree 4 (20 DOFs), and $6.106\times10^{-3}$ for 32 linear elements (64 DOFs).
5. In the ion optic ($10^{17}$ m$^{-3}$ xenon plasma, grids at 1300 V and $-300$ V, $\Delta t=10^{-11}$ s $\approx0.2/\omega_e$, averages over $10^5$ steps), set $\varepsilon_\varphi=10$ for the potential and $200^2\sigma_k^2$ and $60^2\sigma_k^2$ for the ion and electron densities instead of the theoretical $(3\sigma_k)^2$, offsetting the temporal correlation of particles sampled repeatedly in one element; the relative Xe$^+$ density difference from uniform degree 5 along $r=0.2$ mm was $2.254\times10^{-1}$ at $p_{\max}=1$, $2.734\times10^{-1}$ at $p_{\max}=2$, and $4.629\times10^{-2}$ at $p_{\max}=5$, with wall-clock times over the averaging interval of 2458 to 2870 s on 40 AMD EPYC Milan cores against 3663 s for uniform degree 5.
