### Summary

Global trans-collisional fluid simulations with GRILLIX produce three pedestal relaxation event (PRE)-like bursts over more than $5\,\mathrm{ms}$ of an ASDEX Upgrade I-mode discharge. Tearing parity, electromagnetic radial electron-heat transport, electron-diamagnetic propagation, and a dispersion relation consistent with a kinetic estimate identify microtearing modes as the trigger. The trajectory of density and electron-temperature gradient lengths near $\rho_{\mathrm{pol}}=0.95$ agrees with a simplified slab Landau-fluid growth-rate estimate and motivates a qualitative PRE cycle. A Landau-fluid closure permits the instability and bursts, whereas a Braginskii closure with heat-flux limiters makes the microtearing mode linearly stable and produces no PREs. Simulated events reproduce magnetic activity, predominant electron-temperature relaxation, and approximately $0.3\,\mathrm{ms}$ duration seen experimentally. Important discrepancies remain: the modeled stored energy rises after a burst because of the boundary conditions, and the reference discharge did not exhibit PREs at the simulated time. The authors attribute premature onset partly to classical Spitzer resistivity underestimating damping at high electron temperature, exposing limits of trans-collisional fluid models at low collisionality.

### Contributions

1. Produced multiple PRE-like bursts in a global, experimentally parameterized GRILLIX simulation spanning more than $5\,\mathrm{ms}$.
2. Identified microtearing modes as the trigger using mode parity, transport, propagation, phase relations, and dispersion-relation fingerprints.
3. Matched the nonlinear evolution of pedestal gradients to a simplified linear Landau-fluid growth-rate model and proposed a full PRE cycle.
4. Demonstrated that the Landau-fluid closure destabilizes the relevant mode, while the Braginskii closure with heat-flux limiters suppresses it and the PREs.
5. Compared simulated and experimental event characteristics and isolated boundary conditions and low-collisionality resistivity as important model limitations.
