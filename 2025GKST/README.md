### Summary

This experiment demonstrates a single-beam, all-optical inverse-Compton gamma-ray source driven by a petawatt laser. The laser first accelerates a GeV electron beam in a plasma wakefield; after reaching a plasma mirror, its reflected pulse collides head-on with those electrons and upshifts optical photons to multi-MeV energies. The source produced an average of $(1.2\pm0.6)\times10^9$ photons above 1 MeV per shot, about one hundred times the yield of earlier all-optical sources, and reached a peak spectral brightness of $(3.9\pm1.5)\times10^{22}$ photons/mm$^2$/mrad$^2$/s/0.1% bandwidth near 11 MeV. Measurements of the electron beam, laser evolution, and gamma spectrum support a compact model in which the drive pulse remains guided to the mirror. The model reproduces the measured gamma energy and identifies routes to higher conversion efficiency, including plasma-density tailoring and focusing plasma mirrors. Although its average flux remains below conventional facilities, the source's ultrashort duration and high peak brightness create complementary capabilities for nuclear resonance fluorescence, transmutation, imaging, and fundamental-physics experiments.
