### Summary

This paper analyzes global, non-axisymmetric instabilities of differentially rotating cylindrical plasmas in incompressible Hall magnetohydrodynamics. The Hall term splits the ideal-MHD wave degeneracy into whistler and ion-cyclotron branches, both of which can extract energy from shear. Eigenvalue calculations with CYL_SPEC and initial-value simulations with NIMROD agree on growth rates and radial structures. At modest normalized ion skin depth, whistler modes can grow much faster than corresponding MHD modes; at stronger fields, long-wavelength ion-cyclotron modes remain unstable after ideal-MHD modes are stabilized because Hall physics weakens ion field-line bending. In the electron-MHD limit, the authors derive local and global stability conditions. For an azimuthal field, a zero-axial-wavenumber subset is driven by a global corotation-amplifier mechanism that local WKB dispersion relations miss. The identified parameter ranges may be relevant to weakly ionized accretion disks, although equilibrium stratification and nonlinear momentum transport are not included.

### Contributions

1. Identified distinct global whistler and ion-cyclotron instability branches in rotating Hall-MHD plasma.
2. Showed that non-axisymmetric Hall modes can outgrow and outlast ideal-MHD instabilities.
3. Verified eigenvalue results with independent NIMROD initial-value calculations.
4. Derived electron-MHD local and global stability criteria including cylindrical curvature.
5. Discovered a global corotation-amplified whistler mode absent from local dispersion analysis.
