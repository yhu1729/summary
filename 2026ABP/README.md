### Summary

This paper extends Galley's doubled-variable initial-value action into a canonical Hamiltonian framework for smooth nonconservative systems. Two copies of each coordinate are coupled through an antisymmetric term $K$; identical initial data and final-time equality conditions make the variational problem well posed, while uniqueness places both on-shell paths on the physical-limit submanifold. A Legendre transform produces Hamiltonian dynamics on a doubled, $4N$-dimensional symplectic phase space. Liouville volume is preserved globally even when the physical $2N$-dimensional slice contracts, as for a damped oscillator. Terms beyond first order in virtual variables do not affect physical motion, yielding the exact linear Lie-form Hamiltonian
$$
A=\pi_-\!\cdot V(q_+,\pi_+,t)-q_-\!\cdot G(q_+,\pi_+,t).
$$
A canonical gauge freedom shifts physical momentum by an arbitrary regular function without changing trajectories. Consequently, a smooth second-order initial-value problem $\ddot q=U(q,\dot q,t)$ can be embedded by choosing any locally invertible momentum map $P(q,\dot q,t)$. Earth--Moon tides, an adiabatic piston, and a charged particle illustrate the construction.

### Contributions

1. Clarified the boundary conditions and uniqueness assumptions that produce the physical-limit trajectory.
2. Derived the doubled Hamiltonian system and its extended Poisson bracket through an ordinary Legendre transform.
3. Proved that higher-order virtual terms are dynamically irrelevant and obtained equivalent linear Lie forms.
4. Identified canonical momentum-gauge transformations that preserve physical trajectories.
5. Constructed explicit Lagrangian and Hamiltonian embeddings for general smooth second-order initial-value problems and applied them to three physical examples.
