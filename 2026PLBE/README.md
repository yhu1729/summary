### Summary

This study investigates the origin and divertor energy loads of small edge-localised modes (ELMs), intermittent edge-plasma instabilities, in high-density tokamak operation. DIII-D measurements show that these events remove approximately $0.7\%$ of stored plasma energy while weakly disturbing the edge pressure pedestal. Conditional averages, scrape-off-layer probes, and BOUT++ fluid simulations place their origin near the pedestal foot and separatrix, the boundary between closed and open magnetic field lines. Replacing pedestal-top pressure with pressure from this region in the Eich energy-fluence model brings predictions into agreement with DIII-D and ASDEX Upgrade measurements to approximately $40\%$ on average. First-order extrapolations give peak parallel energy fluences of $0.7\,\mathrm{MJ\,m^{-2}}$ for SPARC and $0.43$–$2.3\,\mathrm{MJ\,m^{-2}}$ for ITER, depending on the assumed origin. These estimates lie below the nominal tungsten-surface melting threshold used in the study. They do not establish divertor survival, which also depends on magnetic incidence angle, exposed leading edges, background heat loads, and extrapolation uncertainty.

### Contributions

1. Quantifies average small-ELM stored-energy losses of approximately $0.7\%$ in DIII-D, with roughly $80\%$ of the released energy accounted for at the divertor targets.
2. Combines pedestal-profile changes, near-separatrix density fluctuations, and extrapolated probe pressures to locate small-ELM filament origins at normalized poloidal flux $\Psi_n\approx0.99$–$1.0$.
3. Identifies high-toroidal-mode-number resistive ballooning activity in BOUT++ simulations, with pressure perturbations near $\Psi_n=0.98$–$1.01$ and nonlinear saturation that only weakly perturbs the pedestal.
4. Modifies the Eich fluence model to use pedestal-foot pressure, correcting the factor-eight-or-greater overestimate for DIII-D small ELMs and obtaining approximately $40\%$ average agreement across the studied measurements.
5. Provides first-order SPARC and ITER peak parallel energy-fluence projections below a nominal $12\,\mathrm{MJ\,m^{-2}}$ tungsten-surface melting threshold, while identifying geometric and material limits on that comparison.
