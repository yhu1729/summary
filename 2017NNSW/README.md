### Summary

This paper identifies a mechanism by which heavier hydrogen isotopes can reduce trapped-electron-mode (TEM) turbulent transport, contrary to conventional gyro-Bohm mass scaling. Linear and nonlinear multi-species gyrokinetic simulations use real-mass kinetic electrons, finite collisions, and realistic helical and tokamak geometries. The ratio of electron-ion collision frequency to ion transit frequency grows with ion mass, so collisions stabilize TEMs more strongly in deuterium and tritium than in hydrogen. Near marginal stability, the reduced TEM drive also makes steady zonal-flow shear more effective. In the Large Helical Device geometry, deuterium lowers the nonlinear heat flux to 48% of the hydrogen value and raises the relative zonal-flow energy; in a Cyclone-base-case-like tokamak, the corresponding heat-flux ratio is 43% at the examined collisionality. Suppressing zonal components increases tokamak TEM transport by more than fourfold, confirming their nonlinear role. The mechanism appears across axisymmetric and non-axisymmetric toroidal configurations and is relevant to density- or electron-temperature-gradient-driven outer-core and pedestal-top plasmas.

### Contributions

1. Performed the first nonlinear LHD TEM simulations with hydrogen isotopes and real-mass kinetic electrons.
2. Identified ion-mass-dependent collisional TEM stabilization through the electron-ion-collision to ion-transit frequency ratio.
3. Demonstrated transport scaling opposite to gyro-Bohm expectations in finite-collisionality TEM turbulence.
4. Quantified how near-marginal steady zonal flows amplify the deuterium transport reduction.
5. Reproduced the mechanism in both helical LHD and axisymmetric tokamak configurations.
