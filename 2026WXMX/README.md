### Summary

Quasi-three-dimensional particle-in-cell simulations propose an all-optical route to nanometer-scale pre-bunched electron beams for compact X-ray free-electron lasers. Two low-intensity counter-propagating lasers establish a plasma-density grating before an intense drive laser arrives. The grating periodically modulates the phase velocity of the expanding laser wake, switching self-injection on and off; longitudinal mapping from injection time to wake phase converts this gating into beam microbunching. Simulations produce multi-$10\,\mathrm{kA}$ beams whose main body has approximately $30\,\mathrm{nm}$ slice emittance and $0.5\,\mathrm{MeV}$ slice energy spread. Polarization matters: equal-frequency parallel drive and grating fields disrupt the wake modulation, while perpendicular polarization or a sufficiently different colliding-laser wavelength restores bunching. A $1600\,\mathrm{nm}$ case yields a $16\,\mathrm{nm}$ period and bunching factor $0.025$. Adjusting laser chirp, duration, and delay generates chirped-period or partially bunched structures. These are simulation predictions; beam transport, preservation of nanometer structure, and X-ray production remain to be demonstrated experimentally.
