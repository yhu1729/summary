### Summary

The paper shows that commonly used temporal Drude models can violate momentum and energy conservation when plasma carriers are removed. A hydrodynamic current equation with an explicit carrier creation/removal momentum term is used to compare no-source, rise-and-collapse-source, and collapse-only-source models. Assuming electrons are created with zero mean velocity but removed carrying the ensemble-average momentum leads to the collapse-only-source model. For a temporally triangular plasma-density pulse, this model satisfies the wavelength-averaged energy balance to numerical precision and returns both current and magnetic field consistently toward vacuum. The other models predict apparent gain without an identified energy source and leave nonphysical residual current or magnetostatic field after the carriers vanish. The analysis supplies a physically consistent basis for interpreting frequency conversion and scattering in pulsed plasmas and other time-varying polarizable media.

4. Derived a wavelength-averaged electromagnetic energy balance for time-modulated plasma fields.
5. Showed that only the collapse-only-source model avoids unexplained gain, residual current, and residual magnetostatic field.
