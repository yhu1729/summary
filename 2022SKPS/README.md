## Claude (October 2026)

### Summary

The authors solve the stage equations of $s$-stage fully implicit Runge–Kutta (IRK) methods, and of equivalent discontinuous Galerkin (DG) in time discretizations, for linear method-of-lines systems $Mu' = \mathcal{L}u + f$. Pulling $A_0 \otimes I$ out of the stage system leaves $A_0^{-1} \otimes I - I \otimes \hat{\mathcal{L}}$, $\hat{\mathcal{L}} = \delta t M^{-1}\mathcal{L}$, whose adjugate-based inverse reduces the update to inverting the characteristic polynomial $P_s(\hat{\mathcal{L}})$ of $A_0^{-1}$. Conjugate eigenvalue pairs $\eta \pm i\beta$ of $A_0^{-1}$ give real quadratic factors $(\eta I - \hat{\mathcal{L}})^2 + \beta^2 I$, each preconditioned by $(\gamma_* I - \hat{\mathcal{L}})^{-2}$ with $\gamma_* = \sqrt{\eta^2 + \beta^2}$. If $A_0$ has eigenvalues with positive real part and the field of values of $\mathcal{L}$ lies in the closed left half-plane, the preconditioned condition number is at most $\sqrt{1 + \beta^2/\eta^2}$, tight, optimal in $\gamma_*$, independent of mesh and time step, and below 2.5 for Gauss, Radau IIA, and Lobatto IIIC methods with up to five stages. Backward-Euler-type preconditioners suffice, and CG or MINRES applies for symmetric operators. MFEM tests on finite-difference and DG advection–diffusion and matrix-free diffusion reach tenth order; fourth-order Gauss needs about half the preconditioner applications of SDIRK schemes, and diffusion-dominated cases need extra inner iterations.

### Contributions

1. Showed (Lemma 5, Remark 6) that $\mathcal{M}_s = A_0^{-1} \otimes I - I \otimes \hat{\mathcal{L}}$ has inverse $(I_s \otimes P_s(\hat{\mathcal{L}})^{-1})\,\mathrm{adj}(\mathcal{M}_s)$, with diagonal adjugate entries monic of degree $s-1$ and off-diagonal entries of degree $s-2$, so that stiffly accurate methods, for which $b_0^TA_0^{-1} = (0,\dots,0,1)$, need only the last adjugate row at $(s-1)^2$ matrix-vector products, against $(s-1)s$ for Gauss methods, and Algorithm 1 forms the right-hand side by Horner evaluation of precomputed polynomials $R_i(\hat{\mathcal{L}})$ applied to $M^{-1}f_i$.
2. Proved Theorem 11: for the factored preconditioner $(\delta I - \hat{\mathcal{L}})^{-1}(\gamma I - \hat{\mathcal{L}})^{-1}$ of $(\eta I - \hat{\mathcal{L}})^2 + \beta^2 I$, the choice $\gamma_* = (\eta^2 + \beta^2)/\delta$ gives $\kappa \le (\delta + (\eta^2 + \beta^2)/\delta)/(2\eta)$, attained by operators with eigenvalues $\{0, \pm i\sqrt{\delta\gamma_*}\}$ and minimal over $\gamma$, by bounding the Rayleigh-type ratio (25) termwise through the constants $c_0 = 1 \ge c_1 = c_2 = c_3 = 2\eta/(\delta + \gamma_*)$; Table 1 lists the resulting bounds, 1.83 for five-stage Gauss and 2.42 for five-stage Lobatto IIIC, while the naive $\gamma = \eta$ squares the bound for SPD operators and raises it to the power $3/2$ for skew-symmetric ones.
3. Iterated on $M\mathcal{Q}_\eta = (\eta M - \delta t\mathcal{L})M^{-1}(\eta M - \delta t\mathcal{L}) + \beta^2 M$ with preconditioner $\mathcal{P}M^{-1}\mathcal{P}$, $\mathcal{P} \approx (\gamma_* M - \delta t\mathcal{L})^{-1}$ (Remark 9, Algorithm 1), halving the mass-inverse applications per iteration and keeping operator and preconditioner SPD for Hermitian $M$ and $\mathcal{L}$, so that three-term Krylov recursions apply (Remark 8); each conjugate pair is resolved before the next to avoid compounding condition numbers, and inexact inner solves are wrapped in flexible outer Krylov iterations (Remark 10).
4. On the periodic finite-difference advection–diffusion problem (18) with $\delta t = 2h$, fourth- and eighth-order stencils and one hypre AMG V-cycle per factor, observed orders 3, 4, 7, and 8 with AMG iterations per step roughly constant under refinement, fourth-order Gauss needing about half the AMG iterations of the five-stage L-stable SDIRK(4), and the block preconditioners GSL [51] and LD [42] taking 1.16 to 2.97 times the runtime of the new method (Table 2); on DG advection–diffusion with weakly imposed time-dependent Dirichlet data, L-stable SDIRK(4) reduced to order 2.7 in $\ell^2$ and 2 in broken $\mathcal{H}^1$, whereas Gauss(4) kept fourth order (Fig. 2).
5. For the purely advective DG problem on 288 cores with AIR, $\gamma_*$ instead of $\eta$ cut inner iterations by up to 5.8 times (five-stage Lobatto IIIC, 175 to 30) and never increased them (Table 3); for $\varepsilon = 0.01$ one AIR iteration per preconditioner application failed to converge in 1000 FGMRES iterations, whereas three gave 150 (fixed-point) or 112 (inner GMRES) total AIR iterations per step (Fig. 5); on 256 cores the new solver had the smallest wallclock time in 11 of 18 cases, e.g. 4.40 s against 24.9 (GSL), 11.5 (LD), and 12.7 (ILU) for Gauss(10) at $\varepsilon = 0$, while LD was faster at $\varepsilon = 0.01$ (Table 4); for matrix-free diffusion with low-order-refined preconditioning, inner CG reduced preconditioner applications by about 1.5 times, and second- and fourth-order Gauss needed 1.3 to 2 times fewer than equal-order DIRK (Fig. 7).

### Comments

- Section 3.4, discussion of line 4 of Algorithm 1: "$R_i$ is a polynomial of degree $s$" whose Horner evaluation "only requires computing the action of $\hat{\mathcal{L}}$ $s$ times", but Remark 6 states that each element of $(b_0^TA_0^{-1} \otimes I)\,\mathrm{adj}(\mathcal{M}_s)$ is a polynomial in $\hat{\mathcal{L}}$ of degree $s-1$, as the cofactors of an $s \times s$ matrix are; the evident intended degree is $s-1$, with $s-1$ applications of $\hat{\mathcal{L}}$.
- Section 4.2.4 and Table 4: the text states that the method "is faster than ILU in all cases but one (in which the times are very close) while offering as much as a $7\times$ speedup in one example (Radau(9) $\epsilon = 0.01$)", but Table 4 lists 113 s for ILU against 5.41 s for IRK with Lobatto(8) at $\epsilon = 0$, a ratio of about 21, and 220 s against 33.4 s for Radau(9) at $\epsilon = 0.01$, a ratio of about 6.6; unresolved.
- Section 4.2.4: "2,359,296 DOFs" on "256 cores" is said to give "9,360 DOFs/core", but $2{,}359{,}296/256 = 9{,}216$.
- Section 2.1 and Table 4: "the block ILU approach in [40]" and the column heading "ILU [40]" cite Pazner's low-order refined preconditioners paper, whereas Section 2.1 ("block ILU preconditioners were successfully applied to a transformed version of (3) in [41]") and Section 4.2.4 ("a block ILU preconditioner as in [41]") cite Pazner and Persson; the evident intended reference is [41].
