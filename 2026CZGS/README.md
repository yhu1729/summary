### Summary

The paper shows that weak-shear regions at extrema of zonal $E\times B$ flow can help confine turbulence rather than merely weaken shear suppression. Global total-$f$ XGC gyrokinetic particle-in-cell simulations exhibit zonal jets with nonzero flow curvature, steep ion gyrocenter-density gradients, and converging avalanche activity. The authors isolate quasi-coherent fluctuations near a jet and reduce gyrokinetic test-particle motion to a planar nontwist map. Poincar\'e sections identify shearless invariant tori, while signatures in the simulated ion distribution connect the reduced model to the self-consistent turbulence. A transmissivity analysis finds that these structures reduce radial transport by about a factor of two in trapped-particle phase space. Avalanches can nevertheless cross the partial barrier through eddy-detachment events that create warm- and cold-core-ring-like structures without fully destroying it. The results extend shearless-transport-barrier concepts from idealized maps to high-fidelity gyrokinetic turbulence and suggest relevance to staircase, pedestal, and turbulence-spreading dynamics.

### Contributions

1. Identified robust shearless transport barriers around zonal jets in global XGC turbulence.
2. Derived a planar nontwist map from gyrokinetic test-particle dynamics and measured fluctuations.
3. Connected shearless invariant tori in the map to structures in the simulated ion distribution.
4. Measured roughly twofold transport reduction in trapped-particle phase space.
5. Explained intermittent barrier crossing through avalanche-driven eddy detachment and ring formation.
