## Claude (September 2026)

### Summary

Suprathermal tails beyond a Maxwellian core are common in weakly collisional space plasmas, but how time-dependent bulk flows shape them is less studied. The authors solve a one-dimensional, spatially periodic Fokker–Planck equation with spatial advection, velocity-space diffusion representing wave–particle interactions, Bhatnagar–Gross–Krook (BGK) collisional relaxation, and a phenomenological drift $A_{\rm eff}=(v-v_{st})/\tau_0$ relative to a streaming velocity $v_{st}$, an anti-drag representing energization that outpaces relaxation toward the flow frame. The streaming velocity is constant or grows or decays exponentially over $5\tau_0$, and the suprathermal fraction $\zeta$ counts particles with $|v|\ge5v_{th}$. For constant $v_{st}\le0.1v_{th}$, broadening is nearly isotropic, driven by advection and diffusion, whereas for $v_{st}\gtrsim0.5v_{th}$ streaming-driven acceleration dominates and the distributions become asymmetric; $\zeta$ grows on a timescale of order $\tau_0$ and saturates at a level that increases with $v_{st}$. For time-dependent flows, early acceleration follows the instantaneous streaming speed, but saturated fractions barely differ from the constant flow with the same time average, so the authors conclude that the time-averaged streaming velocity controls the suprathermal yield. They regard the drift as a coarse-grained closure rather than a microscopic force, use BGK instead of a Coulomb collision operator, and call the description simplified, one-dimensional, and isotropic.

### Contributions

1. Wrote the drift coefficient as the difference of an energization rate and a rate of relaxation toward the flow frame, $\tau_0^{-1}=\tau_{acc}^{-1}-\tau_{rel}^{-1}$, so that it acts as anti-drag for $\tau_{acc}<\tau_{rel}$ and as drag otherwise, and justified its linear dependence on $v-v_{st}$ as the leading term of an expansion about the streaming frame.
2. Modeled stochastic acceleration with $D(v)\propto v^{(s-1)/3}\exp(-v/v_{max})$, taking $s=2$ as baseline and $s=3/2$ for comparison from whistler-turbulence transport models with a cutoff at $v_{max}=10v_{th}$, and chose $\tau_{coll}=10\tau_0$, with $100\tau_0$ as a check, from a solar-wind comparison of dynamical and electron–electron collision times.
3. Discretized the equation with Crank–Nicolson time stepping and second-order central differences, evaluating the drift as the central difference of the flux $J_j=A_{{\rm eff},j}f_j$ and the diffusion term in expanded product form averaged over two time levels, with steps of $10^{-3}$ times the domain size, $v_{max}$, and $\tau_0$.
4. Found for constant streaming velocities from 0 to $2v_{th}$ that the suprathermal fraction at $t=5\tau_0$ increases approximately linearly with $v_{st}$ and is nearly unchanged for $s=3/2$ or $\tau_{coll}=100\tau_0$, with small parameter-dependent deviations only for $v_{st}\lesssim0.5v_{th}$, where diffusion matters more early on.
5. Compared flows that change tenfold over $T=5\tau_0$ with $\Gamma_{st}=\ln10/T$, finding that the decelerating flow produces suprathermal particles fastest while the accelerating flow first broadens isotropically before the trend reverses, and argued that static-force treatments suffice for long-term yields whereas transient bursts require time-dependent flows.

### Comments

- §2.2, Eq. (6): the diffusion term enters as $+\partial_v(D\,\partial_vf/\tau_0^2)$ on the left-hand side of an equation set to zero, so that $\partial_tf=-\partial_v(D\,\partial_vf)/\tau_0^2+\dots$, which for a positive diffusion coefficient is backward diffusion, whereas the conservation form used for the drift, $+\partial_v(A_{\rm eff}f)$, requires the diffusive flux $-D\,\partial_vf/\tau_0^2$ and the text describes this term as stochastic acceleration that forms suprathermal tails; the intended sign is evidently negative.
- §2.2, Eq. (6): the collision term is printed as $\partial_v[v(f-f_{eq})/\tau_{coll}]$, a velocity-space divergence, on which the text relies when it states that on integration over velocity the "collisional relaxation terms reduce to boundary contributions", but the same section defines the BGK term as $-(f-f_{eq})/\tau_{coll}$, which is not a divergence; which form was solved is unresolved.
- §2.2, Eq. (7): $D(v)$ is written in terms of $v$, but the velocity domain is $[-v_{max},v_{max}]$ (Eq. (23)); for $v<0$, $(v/v_{th})^{(s-1)/3}$ is not real for $s=3/2$ and $\exp(-v/v_{max})$ exceeds unity, so the cutoff enhances rather than suppresses diffusion at large negative velocities, contrary to the stated role of $v_{max}$ as the velocity "beyond which the efficiency of resonant wave-particle interactions is suppressed"; whether $|v|$ is intended is unresolved.
- §2.2, Eq. (9): evaluating the printed expression in Gaussian units with the stated $n_e=5$ cm$^{-3}$, $T_e=10^5$ K, and $\ln\Lambda=10$ gives $\tau_{ee}\approx1.7\times10^5$ s rather than $3\times10^4$ s, and hence $\tau_{ee}/\tau_{dyn}\approx87$ rather than 15 with Eq. (8); the weak-collisionality conclusion is unaffected, and the source of the discrepancy is unresolved.
- §2.3 and §3: with $\Gamma_{st}T=\ln10$ and the initial speeds shown in Fig. 2, $v_{i,1}=0.1v_{th}$ and $v_{i,2}=v_{th}$, Eqs. (13) and (14) both give $\langle v_{st}\rangle=0.9v_{th}/\ln10\approx0.39v_{th}$, but §3 and the Fig. 5 caption state $\langle v_{st}\rangle\approx0.41v_{th}$, and the Model 3 line in Fig. 2 lies at about $0.41v_{th}$, so the plotted Model 3 differs from the time average that Eq. (12) prescribes; which value was simulated is unresolved.
- §2.1 and Figs. 3 and 5: the drift $A_{\rm eff}=+(v-v_{st})/\tau_0$ pushes particles away from $v_{st}$ ("outward transport in velocity space", Fig. 1(c)), so for $v_{st}=2v_{th}$ it drives the roughly 98% of the initial Maxwellian with $v<v_{st}$ toward negative $v$, and for $v_{st}=0$ it alone would stretch the distribution by $e^{t/\tau_0}\approx20$ by $t=3\tau_0$; yet Figs. 3 and 5 show the distributions shifting toward positive $v$, with tails enhanced at positive and reduced at negative $v$, which §3 attributes to the streaming drift, and Fig. 3(a) shows only modest broadening for $v_{st}=0$; unresolved.
- Figs. 3 and 4: at $t/\tau_0=3$, Fig. 4(a) gives $\zeta\approx0.06$ for $v_{st}=0$ and $\zeta\approx0.21$ for $v_{st}=2v_{th}$, but in Fig. 3(a) the $v_{st}=0$ distribution, whose total is about 5 (initial peak about 2), falls below the $10^{-2}$ axis floor near $|v|=5.5v_{th}$, which bounds $\zeta$ by about 0.02 even if $f=10^{-2}$ out to $v_{max}$, and the $v_{st}=2v_{th}$ curve integrates to $\zeta\approx0.08$; Figs. 5(a) and 6 at $t/\tau_0=2$ disagree similarly, with bounds of 0.02–0.03 from the distributions against 0.03–0.07; unresolved.
