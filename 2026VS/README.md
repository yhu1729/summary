### Summary

The paper proposes $\tau_{LR}=L_i/R$, evaluated at stationary Ohmic flat-top conditions, as a cross-machine reference time for reducing tokamak plasma current to 20% of its initial value. A Poynting-theorem energy balance motivates the metric, and coupled current- and heat-diffusion simulations with RAPTOR test it on TCV, JET, ITER, and DEMO. Ramp-downs lasting $\tau_{LR}$ produce approximately self-similar current-density peaking, avoid substantial reverse edge current, and keep normalized internal inductance near or below 2; the corresponding times are 0.033, 2.87, 63.2, and 166.9 seconds. At $0.6\tau_{LR}$, constant-shape cases develop reverse current carrying roughly 20--40% of total current and internal inductance near 3. Reducing plasma volume and elongation suppresses this reversal in ITER and DEMO and improves vertical-control margin, but may narrow magnetohydrodynamic stability margins. A zero-dimensional engineering model reproduces simulated $\tau_{LR}$ values within about 10% under baseline assumptions and exposes sensitivity to effective charge, density, and confinement. Burning-plasma exit, auxiliary heating, radiation, and full magnetic control remain outside the model.

### Contributions

3. Quantified reverse current and current peaking produced by faster $0.6\tau_{LR}$ terminations.
4. Showed how coordinated volume and elongation reduction can mitigate fast-ramp current reversal and vertical-control risk.
5. Developed a zero-dimensional model for estimating $\tau_{LR}$ and its dependence on engineering and plasma parameters.
