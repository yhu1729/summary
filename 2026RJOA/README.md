## Claude (September 2026)

### Summary

The authors introduce TokaMark, an open benchmark for machine-learning models on real experimental data from the Mega Ampere Spherical Tokamak (MAST), addressing the scarcity of curated, standardized fusion datasets. From the 11,573 shots of the open FAIR-MAST dataset they select 39 signals (magnetics, kinetics, radiation, currents, coil voltages, fueling and control references, and equilibrium reconstructions) sampled at 0.2–500 kHz and kept at native rates without resampling or imputation. Fourteen window-based tasks in four groups cover instantaneous equilibrium reconstruction, short-term magnetic dynamics, kinetic-profile evolution, and long-horizon forecasting of magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) activity and disruption precursors, posed as reconstruction, autoregressive forecasting of the input diagnostics, or forecasting of other signals. Scores are hierarchical normalized root-mean-square errors (NRMSE) masked to available ground truth. Baselines are mean and persistence predictors and a multi-branch convolutional encoder–decoder with a multilayer-perceptron (CNN) or long short-term memory (CNN+LSTM) fusion backbone. Under a random shot-level 80-10-10 split the networks beat the naive predictors, most strongly on equilibrium and magnetic tasks; holding out the last two campaigns degrades them, sometimes below the naive predictors, which the authors suggest may reflect distribution shift such as sensor recalibration. They note that the baselines are generic, without physics priors or task-specific tuning.

### Contributions

1. Organized the signals by category, origin (diagnostic, actuator, or derived from EFIT reconstruction), sampling rate, and modality (time series, profiles, or video as 1D–3D tensors), and separated Markovian tasks with 5 ms input windows from non-Markovian tasks that use long histories with 5–100 ms horizons, including a Fourier-domain Mirnov-coil target in Task 4-5.
2. Quantified structured missingness in the frozen data, 38.2–55.2% for most equilibrium quantities (concentrated at shot start and end), 46.1% for Thomson scattering, and 0–5.4% for coil currents and voltages, plasma current, references, gas puffing, and neutral-beam power, and published per-task shot counts for both splits.
3. Defined the metric so that window RMSEs are combined by a quadratic mean within each shot, normalized by the signal's standard deviation over the evaluation shots, averaged with equal weight over a task's signals, and only then averaged over test shots, and also reported normalized mean absolute errors for all 57 task–signal pairs.
4. Built the baseline from independent 1D, 2D, or 3D convolutional encoders for each input and actuator on non-overlapping 5 ms chunks, with z-scored, zero-filled inputs, a shared backbone projecting to $D=16$, and mirrored transposed-convolution decoders with cropping ($N=3$, $K=3$, $s=3$), giving 0.27–1.26 million parameters per task.
5. Found that under the temporal split the CNN+LSTM kept the Group 2 score at 0.2928 against 0.7068 for the CNN, persistence beat both networks on soft-X-ray forecasting (Task 4-1: 0.1597 against 1.0970 and 1.1788), and on the random split both networks only matched the mean predictor on Mirnov spectral forecasting (0.6507 and 0.6488 against 0.6533).

### Comments

- Section 4.2, Table 3: the temporal-split Group 4 score of CNN+LSTM is given as 0.8062, but Section 3.3 defines the group score as the average of its task scores, and the five Task 4-1 to 4-5 entries (1.1788, 1.1445, 0.8198, 0.4954, 0.7309) average 0.8739; 0.8062 is the average of the four tasks other than Task 4-2, whereas every other group entry in Table 3 agrees with its task average to within 0.0002.
- Section 4.2: "soft x-rays forecasting (Task 4-1 and Task 4-2) is by far the worst and exceeds unity" conflicts with Table 3 under temporal splitting, where the CNN+LSTM scores worse on Task 3-3 (1.4718) than on Tasks 4-1 (1.1788) and 4-2 (1.1445), and the CNN scores worse on Tasks 2-1 (1.7570) and 4-3 (1.3249) than on Task 4-1 (1.0970); only the CNN on Task 4-2 (3.0603) is the worst entry.
- Table D.3: the Mean column for Task 3-3 contains only "—", the mark used for inapplicable models, although Section 4.1.1 states that the mean model predicts all tasks, Table 3 reports a temporal-split Task 3-3 Mean score of 0.9859, and Table D.4 lists its NMAE values for the same five signals.
- Appendix C.1, Eqs. (6)–(7): the decoder output has size $L_d^{(N,v_o)}$ and Eq. (7) uses $L_d^{(0,v_o)}$ as the compressed map that transposed convolutions upsample, but Eq. (6), $L_d^{(\ell,v_o)}=\lceil s(L_d^{(\ell+1,v_o)}-1)-2p+K+o_p\rceil$, gives $L_d^{(\ell,v_o)}=3L_d^{(\ell+1,v_o)}-1$ for the Appendix C.2 values ($K=3$, $s=3$, $p=1$, $o_p=1$), so $L_d^{(0,v_o)}$ would exceed the target size; the standard transposed-convolution relation has $\ell$ and $\ell+1$ interchanged, and whether that or an inverse relation was intended is unresolved.
