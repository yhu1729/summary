# 2026R3

## Claude (September 2026)

### Summary

The author reorganizes the first nonlinear (second-order) Vlasov–Maxwell response of a homogeneous magnetized plasma with gyrotropic equilibrium, usually written as a double cyclotron-harmonic expansion with long Bessel sums and nested resonance denominators. As background, the author's linear theory keeps the Larmor phase unexpanded and evaluates the orbit integral as the incomplete-Bessel function $G_\mu(z,\psi)=\mathrm i\int_0^\infty e^{-\mathrm iz\sin(\psi+\lambda)}e^{-\mathrm i\mu\lambda}d\lambda=\sum_nJ_n(z)e^{-\mathrm in\psi}/(n+\mu)$, with $z$ the normalized Larmor radius, $\psi$ the gyrophase, and $\mu$ the normalized detuning. For a triad $\mathbf k=\mathbf p+\mathbf q$, the source is the Lorentz force of mode $\mathbf p$ on the linear response of mode $\mathbf q$; with that response in incomplete-Bessel form, the outer orbit integral yields a bivariate orbit-resolvent $G^{(r)}_{\mu,\nu}(z,\psi;w,\chi)$, whose shift $r\in\{-2,\dots,2\}$ combines inner and outer gyrophase blocks. The second-order distribution becomes nine blocks of these kernels and their derivatives. The author proves expansion, resolvent, recurrence, and limit identities, recovers an extension of the Liu–Tripathi double-harmonic formula, derived independently, through Graf's addition theorem, and expresses the nonlinear susceptibility through projected kernels. No numerical evaluation is given; the construction keeps the unperturbed characteristics, and its computational benefit is stated to be regime-dependent.

### Contributions

1. Wrote the second-order characteristic solution in closed form (Eq. (6.3)): with inner blocks $K_{\mathbf q,a}=e^{\mathrm iz_\mathbf q\sin\psi_\mathbf q+\mathrm ia\psi_\mathbf q}G_{\mu_\mathbf q-a}(z_\mathbf q,\psi_\mathbf q)$, $a\in\{-1,0,1\}$, and outer force blocks $\mathcal P_{\mathbf p,b}=e^{\mathrm ib(\phi_\mathbf k-\phi_\mathbf p)}[C^{(\phi)}_{\mathbf p,b}\partial_\phi+C^{(\perp)}_{\mathbf p,b}\partial_{v_\perp}+C^{(\parallel)}_{\mathbf p,b}\partial_{v_\parallel}]$, the orbit integral maps $K_{\mathbf q,a}$ to $e^{\mathrm ia\psi_\mathbf q}G^{(a+b)}_{\mu_\mathbf k,\mu_\mathbf q-a}(z_\mathbf k,\psi_\mathbf k;z_\mathbf q,\psi_\mathbf q)$ and its $\psi_\mathbf q$, $z_\mathbf q$, and $\mu_\mathbf q$ derivatives to the $(\partial_{\psi_\mathbf q}+\mathrm ia)$, $\partial_{z_\mathbf q}$, and $\partial_\nu$ derivatives of that kernel, and the result keeps the Larmor factor $e^{\mathrm iz_\mathbf k\sin\psi_\mathbf k}$ of the linear response.
2. Proved the superposition $G^{(r)}_{\mu,\nu}=\sum_\ell T_\ell(w,\nu)e^{-\mathrm i\ell\chi}G_{\mu+\ell-r}(z,\psi)$, where Newberger's Turkin functions $T_\ell(w,\nu)=\sum_nJ_n(w)J_{n-\ell}(w)/(n+\nu)$ are the Fourier coefficients of the dressed inner factor $e^{\mathrm iw\sin\chi}G_\nu(w,\chi)$, the double expansion with denominators $N+\ell+\mu-r$, the resolvent identity $(\mathrm i\partial_\psi+\mathrm i\partial_\chi+\mu-r)G^{(r)}_{\mu,\nu}=e^{-\mathrm iz\sin\psi}e^{\mathrm iw\sin\chi}G_\nu(w,\chi)$, the outer Bessel equation, the recurrence $\partial_zG^{(r)}_{\mu,\nu}=\frac12[e^{-\mathrm i\psi}G^{(r)}_{\mu+1,\nu}-e^{\mathrm i\psi}G^{(r)}_{\mu-1,\nu}]$, and $G^{(r)}_{\mu,\nu}(z,\psi;0,\chi)=G_{\mu-r}(z,\psi)/\nu$ at zero inner Larmor radius.
3. Obtained from $(\mathrm i\partial_\chi+\nu+w\cos\chi)[e^{\mathrm iw\sin\chi}G_\nu(w,\chi)]=1$ the Turkin recurrence $(\nu+\ell)T_\ell+\frac w2(T_{\ell-1}+T_{\ell+1})=\delta_{\ell,0}$ and the bivariate identity $(\mathrm i\partial_\chi+\nu)G^{(r)}_{\mu,\nu}+\frac w2[e^{\mathrm i\chi}G^{(r+1)}_{\mu,\nu}+e^{-\mathrm i\chi}G^{(r-1)}_{\mu,\nu}]=G_{\mu-r}(z,\psi)$, together with phase insertions that shift $r$, inner derivative rules, and a double-characteristic representation of $G^{(r)}_{\mu,\nu}$ as a two-time orbit integral.
4. Derived the double-harmonic formula directly, $\tilde f^{(2)}_{\mathbf k,N}\propto[N\omega-(\Omega_\mathbf k-k_\parallel v_\parallel)]^{-1}\sum_Me^{-\mathrm i[N\phi_\mathbf k-(N-M)\phi_\mathbf p-M\phi_\mathbf q]}L^{(\mathbf p)}_{NM}\tilde f^{(1)}_{\mathbf q,M}$, whose three blocks carry $J_{N-M+b}(z_\mathbf p)$ and turn gyrophase derivatives into multiplication by $-\mathrm iM$, and recovered it from Eq. (6.3) by expanding the kernels into inner harmonics $\sum_aT_{m+a}(z_\mathbf q,\mu_\mathbf q-a)X_{\mathbf q,a}$, moving derivatives through $J_{M-m}(z_\mathbf q)$ with $\Omega_\mathbf p\mathcal G_\mathbf pJ_{M-m}(z_\mathbf q)=-\mu_\mathbf pq_\perp J'_{M-m}(z_\mathbf q)$, and contracting the intermediate harmonic by Graf's theorem $e^{\mathrm il(\phi_\mathbf p-\phi_\mathbf k)}J_l(z_\mathbf p)=\sum_hJ_{l+h}(z_\mathbf k)J_h(z_\mathbf q)e^{\mathrm ih(\phi_\mathbf k-\phi_\mathbf q)}$, the step that produces the $z_\mathbf p$ Bessel factor.
5. Introduced projected kernels $\mathcal K^{(r,a)}_N(z_\mathbf k,z_\mathbf q,\Delta;\mu_\mathbf k,\nu)$, angular Fourier projections at relative angle $\Delta=\phi_\mathbf k-\phi_\mathbf q$ with the single-sum form $\sum_ne^{\mathrm in\Delta}T_{n+a}(z_\mathbf q,\nu)T_{N-n}(z_\mathbf k,\mu_\mathbf k+n+a-r)$, showed that every angular contraction of the nonlinear current reduces to $\mathcal K^{(r,a)}_N$ and its $\partial_\Delta$, $\partial_{z_\mathbf q}$, and $\partial_\nu$ derivatives, wrote the second-order susceptibility $\chi^{(2)}_{\tau\sigma\rho}$ in the circular basis as nine $(a,b)$ blocks with polarization-resolved coefficients followed by a two-dimensional velocity integral, and argued that the $n$th order is governed by $n$-variate orbit kernels.

### Comments

- Section 10: the inner amplitudes are given as $X_{\mathbf q,1;1}=\frac{e^{\mathrm i\phi_\mathbf q}}{\sqrt2}\mathcal G_{\mathbf q,\Omega_\mathbf q}f_0$, $X_{\mathbf q,-1;-1}=\frac{e^{-\mathrm i\phi_\mathbf q}}{\sqrt2}\mathcal G_{\mathbf q,\Omega_\mathbf q}f_0$, and $X_{\mathbf q,\pm1;0}=\frac{q_\perp}{2\Omega_\mathbf q}\mathcal Hf_0$, but neither $\mathcal G_{\mathbf q,\Omega_\mathbf q}$ nor $\mathcal H$ is defined anywhere in the paper; the definitions of $X_{\mathbf q,\pm1}$ in Section 3 use $\mathcal G_\mathbf q$ and $\mathcal G=v_\parallel\partial_{v_\perp}-v_\perp\partial_{v_\parallel}$, so the evident intended operators are $\mathcal G_\mathbf q$ and $\mathcal G$.
