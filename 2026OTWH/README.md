## Claude (September 2026)

### Summary

Kinetic distribution functions are expensive to store, and moment closures require recovering a distribution from finitely many moments. The authors represent a velocity distribution by nodal values on a uniform tensor grid and propose two moment-aware reconstructions. Sparse entropic quadrature (SPEQ) extends entropic quadrature by writing $f=wg$ with a prior weight $w$, either a discrete Maxwellian fitted to low-order moments or known data, and minimizing discrete entropy, or Kullback–Leibler divergence to a known distribution, plus $\lambda\|g\|_1$ subject to the moment constraints; the penalty acts on $g$ because nonnegativity makes $\|f\|_1$ the conserved density. The concave dual is solved by Newton's method, and values of $g$ below a threshold are zeroed. Low-rank moment recovery (LRMR) minimizes the row-scaled moment residual over nonnegative tensors of Tucker rank $r$ by projected gradient descent, without a guarantee of global minimization. On Maxwell–Boltzmann, Druyvesteyn, and bimodal Mott–Smith distributions, LRMR needs about an order of magnitude fewer degrees of freedom than SPEQ, and strong sparsity hollows out the bulk. On NuFI simulations of a Vlasov–Maxwell beam instability, divergence-based SPEQ retains filaments at high sparsity, whereas entropy-based SPEQ and LRMR lose fine detail, and the authors conclude that LRMR needs additional priors.

### Contributions

1. Derived the dual of the weighted, $\ell_1$-regularized divergence problem, with primal recovery $g_i=(\phi_i/w_i)\exp(-1-(\lambda+s_i)/(\Delta v_iw_i))$, $s=\hat A_m^\top y$, a Hessian $\hat A_mH\hat A_m^\top$ with diagonal $H$, a least-squares initial guess aimed at $g\equiv1$, and row normalization of the moment matrix to improve conditioning.
2. Constructed the Maxwellian weight directly on the discrete grid by solving a scalar Newton equation for the temperature that reproduces the discrete energy, because the discretization breaks the continuous relation $e=\frac32T$.
3. Showed that LRMR recovers the Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution to relative $L_2$ errors near $10^{-14}$ at every rank from 1, about 0.2% of the $40^3$ nodal values, as its separable Tucker rank $(1,1,1)$ implies, while both methods keep the odd next-order moments of the symmetric test distributions near zero.
4. Found for the Mott–Smith case that entropy-based SPEQ errors plateau as sparsity decreases, that constraining moments up to order 6 rather than 4 lowers both the distribution and next-moment errors, and that the cutoff $\varepsilon$ changes sparsity at small $\lambda$ but hardly changes the distribution error.
5. Reported on the NuFI data that divergence-based SPEQ errors grow at later times as filamentation develops, that both SPEQ variants give sparser representations at earlier times, and that LRMR errors barely change when moments up to order 10 are constrained.

### Comments

- §3.1, Eqs. (37)–(38): setting $g_i\equiv1$ and $\lambda=0$ in (30)–(32), as the text states, gives $s_i=\Delta v_iw_i(\log(\phi_i/w_i)-1)=r_i$, that is, $\hat A_m^\top y_0=r$, whose normal equations are $\hat A_m\hat A_m^\top y_0=+\hat A_mr$, but (37) and step 6 of Algorithms 1 and 2 print $-\hat A_mr$; in Algorithm 2 ($w=\phi$) the printed system yields $s=-r$ and $g_i=e^{-2}$ rather than $g_i\equiv1$, so the intended sign is $+$.
- §3.1, step 2 of the weighting procedure: the energy is printed as $e=(M_{2,0,0}/M_{0,0,0}-\bar v_x)^2+(M_{0,2,0}/M_{0,0,0}-\bar v_y)^2+(M_{0,0,2}/M_{0,0,0}-\bar v_z)^2$, which subtracts a velocity from a squared velocity, whereas Eq. (27) and step 3 ($T_0=\frac23e$) use $e$ as the mean of $\|v-\bar v\|^2$; the intended terms are $M_{2,0,0}/M_{0,0,0}-\bar v_x^2$ and its $y$ and $z$ analogues.
- §4.1.2, Eqs. (48)–(49): $\alpha=(2\Gamma(5/4)/(3T\Gamma(3/4)))^2$ is exactly the coefficient for which $\exp(-\alpha\|v\|^4)$ has $\langle\|v\|^2\rangle=\frac32T$, the relation that §3.1 uses with $\exp(-\|v\|^2/T)$, but (48) divides the exponent by a further $T$, so it scales as $T^{-3}\|v\|^4$ and gives $\langle\|v\|^2\rangle=\frac32T^{3/2}$; the extra $1/T$ has no effect for the $T=1$ used, and the intended exponent is $-\alpha\|v\|^4$.
- Fig. 1 and Fig. 2: the Fig. 2 panel for $\tilde\lambda=0.1$ ($m_{\max}=6$, "% of D.o.F = 9.6") shows the four central nodes of slice $k=20$ at no more than about 0.02 where the Maxwell–Boltzmann truth is 0.174, which alone implies a relative $L_2$ error of at least 0.10 on the $40^3$ grid, but Fig. 1 plots about 0.018 for the $m_{\max}=6$ point at 9.6%; what the plotted $\|f_{\rm pred}-f_{\rm true}\|_2/\|f_{\rm true}\|_2$ values measure is unresolved.
- Fig. 3 and Fig. 5: Fig. 5 shows the rank-1 LRMR reconstruction ($m_{\max}=6$, 0.2% of D.o.F.) at about 0.17, and at least 0.15, on the four central nodes of slice $k=20$, where the Druyvesteyn truth is 0.090, and §4.1.2 says LRMR "predicts larger values of the distribution", which alone implies a relative $L_2$ error of at least 0.046, but Fig. 3 plots about $5\times10^{-3}$ for this point; unresolved.
- Fig. 9 caption: "reconstructed distributions for various values of $\lambda$", but the panels are labeled "LRMR, $r=1$", "$r=3$", and "$r=5$", and §4.1.3 says Fig. 9 shows "the same slices of the distributions obtained with the LRMR method"; the intended caption is "for various ranks $r$", as in Fig. 5.
- §4.2: Fig. 12 is said to show the Kullback–Leibler variant "for the same values of $\lambda$ and the same slice as shown on Figure 11", but the Fig. 11 panels use $\tilde\lambda=10^{-8},10^{-4},10^{-2}$ and the Fig. 12 panels use $\tilde\lambda=10^{-8},10^{-2},1$; only the slice ($k=40$, $t=300$) and $\tilde\lambda=10^{-8}$, $10^{-2}$ are shared.
- Figs. 16–18 captions: "absolute error in next predicted moments (right)", but the right-hand axes are labeled $E^{\rm rel}_{m_{\max}+1}$ and §4.2 says these figures show "the relative errors in the predicted moments"; the intended word is "relative".
