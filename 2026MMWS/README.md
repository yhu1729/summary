### Summary

Maity and coauthors model a single-stage capillary gas cell for laser wakefield acceleration. A roughly $2$ mm entrance region contains helium and nitrogen to inject electrons released from nitrogen's inner shell; a longer pure-helium region accelerates them while limiting further injection. Hydrodynamic simulations of a three-inlet cell provide gas-density profiles, which the authors pass directly to particle-in-cell simulations driven by a $100$ TW-class laser. Varying inlet spacing and pressures produces different density ramps and changes beam charge, energy, and energy spread. In the reported Case 2, the ionization-injected beam exits with about $41$ pC charge, $1.02$ GeV mean energy, and $2.4\%$ relative full-width-at-half-maximum energy spread. The simulations also track self-injected helium electrons: they trail the principal beam, but spectral overlap can impair the beam observed experimentally. Slowly rising density profiles suppress this secondary population. These are computational predictions for a proposed target, not measurements from an operating cell.

### Contributions

1. Designed a three-inlet capillary with a localized helium--nitrogen injection region and a longer pure-helium acceleration region.
2. Generated realizable longitudinal gas-density ramps by varying the capillary geometry and inlet pressures in hydrodynamic simulations.
3. Coupled those gas profiles to particle-in-cell simulations to resolve injection and acceleration with a $100$ TW-class laser.
4. Quantified the beam-quality trade-off across five gas configurations, including the $41$ pC, $1.02$ GeV, $2.4\%$-spread result for Case 2.
5. Distinguished trailing self-injected helium electrons from the nitrogen-derived beam and identified rising density ramps that suppress them.
