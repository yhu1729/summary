# 2026GXPS

## Claude (September 2026)

### Summary

The authors deploy on an FPGA a recurrent probabilistic neural network (RPNN) that predicts the next DIII-D tokamak plasma state, for model-predictive-control-style plasma control, which needs low, deterministic latency. As background, the RPNN of Char et al. encodes a 27-dimensional state (7 scalars and 20 principal-component coefficients of 6 profiles) and 13 actuators, given as levels and commanded changes, through an MLP, a gated recurrent unit (GRU), and a residual-MLP decoder with mean and log-variance heads, and bootstrap ensembles add epistemic uncertainty. Replacing LayerNorm by BatchNorm for hls4ml compatibility, the authors retrain on 19,648 chronologically split shots at 20 ms cadence, shrink the model by 94.9% to 175,628 parameters (+1.9% one-step test MSE), and apply 16-bit quantization-aware training in QKeras (a further +2.8% MSE and −4.8% explained variance). Synthesized with hls4ml, Vitis HLS, and Vivado for a Xilinx Alveo U50, the kernel uses at most 65% of any resource and takes 276 cycles, 2.64 µs, per inference at 104.7 MHz. Free-running ensembles track a held-out shot, but errors compound in closed loop. Timing comes from post-implementation analysis because no card was available, and training does not penalize rollout error growth.

### Contributions

1. Validated the BatchNorm substitution on retrained checkpoints, with validation MSE 0.02622 against 0.02728 (−3.9%) and explained variance (EV) 0.5166 against 0.4967, and test MSE 0.02375 against 0.02407 and EV 0.4243 against 0.4038, trained by a two-stage curriculum (MSE training of the whole model, then fine-tuning of only the log-variance head and variance-pinning bounds by a heteroscedastic negative log-likelihood with a $10^{-3}$ penalty on wide pinning intervals; AdamW, learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$, batch 512, early-stopping patience 250 epochs, first two timesteps excluded from the loss) on 17,676/982/990 training/validation/test shots.
2. Ablated 25 reductions of the decoder width, residual-block count, GRU width, and backbone width from the 3.45M-parameter model, 74.2% of which sits in the decoder: on the validation split the four smallest backbones worsened MSE monotonically from +12.4% at 73,228 parameters to +35.5% at 22,508, and on the test split removing all residual blocks raised MSE by 26.9–54.4%, whereas the selected hid128 gru64 dec128 b1 cost +1.9% MSE and −2.7% EV and its two- and three-block variants, 19–38% larger, were no better.
3. Quantized weights, activations, and biases to ap_fixed<16,6> after preliminary sweeps in which ap_fixed<8,3> and ap_fixed<6,1> lost substantially more accuracy, showed that naive post-training quantization destroys the model (validation MSE 1.469, EV −13.57), and reached test MSE 0.02487 and EV 0.3932 after quantization-aware fine-tuning, +4.7% MSE and −7.3% EV relative to the full-size floating-point baseline.
4. Made the GRU hidden state explicit kernel I/O, streaming 117 input and 118 output 32-bit words per inference with zeros supplied at shot start, left the normalizer and variance pinning outside the dataflow region (175,414 of 175,628 parameters synthesized), and used reuse factor 64, streaming I/O, ap_fixed<32,20> accumulators, and an ap_fixed<27,17> input type, giving post-route utilization of 35.24% LUT, 17.79% FF, 65.03% DSP, and 48.32% BRAM, a 122-cycle (1.17 µs) initiation interval, and batch-1 latencies about 230 and 411 times below the 608 µs and 1,085 µs of the same model in TensorFlow on an 8-core AMD EPYC 7763 and an NVIDIA A100.
5. Evaluated closed-loop behavior: in a 225-step replay of test shot 193369 with 15-member ensembles, per-channel increment clamping engaged in 1.3% (baseline) and 2.0% (quantized) of updates; over horizons, against a persistence model scoring EV 0.96 at one step and crossing zero near 99 steps, the quantized ensemble trailed the baseline by 0.51 EV after 100 steps in sample mode (−0.41 against +0.10); and of three ensemble-sampling schemes, Sample-TS1 (member resampled and Gaussian sampled every step) kept 90%-interval coverage within 0.81–0.90 (baseline) and 0.84–0.92 (quantized) with miscalibration areas at most 0.08 and 0.10 through 200 steps, against coverage near 0.27–0.30 for Mean-TS1 and over-coverage of 0.97–0.99 for Sample-TSInf.

### Comments

- Section III B: "After fine-tuning, the 16-bit quantized model lands within +2.8% MSE / −4.8% EV of the FP32 reference on the held-out test split (Table VI)", but Table VI is the architecture-size ablation; the quantized results (MSE 0.02487 and EV 0.3932 against 0.02420 and 0.4128) are in Table VIII.
- Section III A: "every configuration down to roughly 93% parameter reduction stays within ±5% of the full-size baseline's MSE", but Table V lists gru64 dec64 b0, at −86.6% parameters, with +6.3% MSE, the value the same sentence gives as the largest loss above 73,228 parameters.
- Section IV B: "We therefore screen each member in both prediction modes. Figure 2 reports results for these ensembles", but Section IV A states for the Fig. 2 replay that "Each ensemble comprises its full set of fifteen members" and "No stability screening was applied", and the Fig. 2 caption lists "all 15 bootstrap members"; only Figs. 3 and 4 use four screened members.
- Section V B: "the deployed FPGA kernel's 2.64 µs is ≈ 240× faster than the same architecture on the CPU", but Table XII gives 608 µs for the ablated architecture on the CPU at batch size 1, and $608/2.64\approx230$; the A100 ratio, $1085/2.64\approx411$, matches the stated "≈ 410×".
