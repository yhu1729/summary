### Summary

High-resolution VPIC simulations follow decaying compressible fast-wave turbulence from magnetohydrodynamic to sub-ion scales in a collisionless plasma. At large scales, angle-dependent transit-time damping transfers compressive-wave energy to particles, creating suprathermal electrons and field-aligned proton beams. The proton resonant velocity grows with plasma beta; realistic-mass-ratio estimates give about $1.3V_A$ at $\beta=0.5$ and $2.4V_A$ at $\beta=2$, offering a mechanism for super-Alfvénic beams observed by Parker Solar Probe. At sub-ion scales, quasi-perpendicular fast-mode fluctuations couple to multiple ion Bernstein branches whose dispersion, quasi-electrostatic polarization, and compressibility agree with a plasma-dispersion solver. These waves provide a channel for perpendicular energization and Kappa-like proton tails. At higher beta, stronger transit-time damping may suppress the cascade, while beam-driven instabilities provide an alternative Bernstein-wave source.
