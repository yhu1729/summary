### Summary

This proceedings paper summarizes theoretical modeling of high-energy particle acceleration in magnetized turbulent plasmas. It reviews recent findings from fully kinetic simulations of large-amplitude, semi-relativistic to fully relativistic turbulence and interprets them through a generalized Fermi picture of stochastic acceleration. In this view, acceleration is not spatially uniform: the strongest contributions arise when particles interact with sharp, time-dependent bends of magnetic field lines and with regions of velocity compression. The acceleration-rate distribution is highly intermittent, following a broken power law that extends to large values, which makes relativistic large-amplitude turbulence an efficient extreme accelerator. The paper also discusses consequences for particle transport and for energy spectra over long times, especially when radiative losses are present. Its main role is synthetic rather than algorithmic: it organizes simulation evidence into a physical model that connects turbulent electromagnetic structures, stochastic acceleration rates, transport, and observable high-energy particle spectra in astrophysical plasmas.

4. Emphasized the spatial intermittency and broken-power-law distribution of acceleration rates.
