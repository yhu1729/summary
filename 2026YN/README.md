### Summary

The authors introduce a basis-independent diagnostic for velocity-space complexity in gyrokinetic turbulence by applying singular value decomposition to the time-dependent perturbed ion distribution at each perpendicular Fourier mode and interpreting normalized singular-value weights through a von Neumann entropy. Nonlinear flux-tube gyrokinetic-Poisson simulations show low entropy at low wavenumber, where a few singular modes reconstruct the distribution, followed by a rapid increase around $k_\perp\rho_{ti}\sim1$, where many modes become dynamically relevant. Linear growth-rate comparisons place the dominant low-wavenumber electrostatic power near the ion-temperature-gradient unstable range. Hermite and Laguerre decompositions separate parallel Landau phase mixing from perpendicular finite-Larmor-radius mixing: both are active, but broadening of the Hermite spectrum correlates more strongly with the high-wavenumber entropy rise. The diagnostic compactly maps scale-dependent kinetic complexity without prescribing a velocity-space basis.

### Contributions

1. Defined an SVD-based von Neumann entropy for time-evolving gyrokinetic distribution functions.
2. Mapped velocity-space complexity across the full simulated perpendicular-wavenumber spectrum.
3. Identified a sharp complexity increase near $k_\perp\rho_{ti}\sim1$.
4. Connected low-wavenumber turbulent power to the linear ion-temperature-gradient unstable range.
5. Used Hermite--Laguerre spectra to attribute the entropy rise mainly to enhanced parallel phase mixing.
