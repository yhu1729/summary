### Summary

This paper develops a bandwidth-based error theory for scaled generalized Hermite approximations on the real line and generalized Laguerre approximations on the half-line. The projection error is decomposed into spatial truncation, frequency truncation, and exponentially small spectral components, making the scale parameter interpretable as a balance between physical-space coverage and Fourier-space resolution. A transformation between Laguerre and Hermite systems transfers the principal estimate to both bases. The framework extends to derivatives, interpolation, Gaussian quadrature, and Galerkin discretization of a half-line elliptic problem. It predicts behavior missed by classical finite-smoothness estimates: suitable scaling can turn subgeometric convergence into geometric convergence, double algebraic order, explain root-exponential pre-asymptotics, and restore competitive unbounded-domain quadrature accuracy. Numerical experiments support these predictions. The comparison also overturns a simple decay-matching intuition: for a class of Gaussian-decaying analytic functions, two spliced half-line Laguerre expansions are at least competitive with, and can converge faster than, one Hermite expansion, though general superiority remains conjectural.

### Contributions

1. Decomposed scaled Hermite and Laguerre projection error into spatial, frequency, and spectral terms analogous to a sampling-bandwidth criterion.
2. Established an equivalence that transfers generalized Hermite estimates to generalized Laguerre approximation.
3. Derived scale-selection rules predicting geometric, root-exponential, and improved algebraic convergence rates.
4. Extended the analysis to derivatives, interpolation, Gaussian quadrature, and a Laguerre--Galerkin half-line model problem.
5. Proved a class-specific comparison in which two spliced Laguerre bases match or outperform one Hermite basis and validated the predicted rates numerically.
