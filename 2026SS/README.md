## Claude (September 2026)

### Summary

The authors propose a multi-resolution (MR) limiter for Runge–Kutta discontinuous Galerkin schemes for hyperbolic conservation laws that assigns each cell a polynomial degree matching local smoothness, rather than classifying cells as good or troubled (flagged for limiting). A baseline $IS^0$ is the minimum, over substencils containing the target cell, of their largest cell-average difference from it. From the DG degree $m=k$ downward, $IS^m$ sums the absolute differences between the averages of the degree-$m$ truncated polynomial over adjacent cells and their cell averages; while $IS^m>C_mIS^0$, the degree-$m$ modes are removed, preserving the cell average, with a minmod slope reconstruction if no degree $m\ge1$ passes. Neighbours enter only through cell averages, so limiting them leaves the indicators unchanged, as do affine coordinate maps, and affine rescaling of the solution leaves the comparisons unchanged. With $C_m\ge2.86$ one-dimensional advection converges optimally for $k=1$ to $6$, and with $C_m=4$ two-dimensional $L^1$ errors do for $k\le3$, and Euler problems with shocks and strong rarefactions, on two-dimensional quadrilateral and triangular meshes, show essentially no oscillations, often at intermediate degrees. The authors report suboptimal two-dimensional $L^\infty$ orders for $k=1$ and round-off-induced polynomial-degree reductions in constant regions, and suggest degree-dependent $C_m$, less dissipative slope limiting, and less diffusive fluxes.

### Contributions

1. Built the triangular-mesh baseline from the substencils $\{K_0,K_l,K_{l1},K_{l2}\}$, $l=1,2,3$, of the ten-cell stencil of edge neighbours $K_l$ and their neighbours, omitting substencils outside the domain, and the fallback linear polynomial from the componentwise minmod of three slopes, each matching the averages of $K_0$ and a pair of edge neighbours, so that only the baseline stencil is non-compact; polygonal and polyhedral meshes are stated to need only a baseline adapted to the number of neighbours.
2. Contrasted the reconstruction, affine-coordinate, and scale invariance of the MR indicators with the KXRCF indicator of Krivodonova et al., an inflow-edge jump divided by $h^{(k+1)/2}|\partial K_0^-|\,\|p_0\|$, which has none of them, and the Fu–Shu indicator, summed deviations of the neighbour polynomials' $K_0$-averages from $\bar p_0$ divided by $\max_j|\bar p_j|$, which is only affine-coordinate invariant, and kept the accuracy and robustness of combined-wave advection with the solution scaled by one factor per $k$ between $10^{-10}$ and $10^{10}$.
3. Used $\Delta t=0.25h^{4/3}$, $0.3h^{5/3}$, $0.5h^2$, and $0.4h^{7/3}$ for $k=3$ to $6$ so that SSP-RK3 does not limit the spatial order, and quadruple precision for $k\ge4$, in one-dimensional advection of $\sin(\pi x)$, where no cell lowers its degree on meshes with $h\le0.1$ once $C_m\ge2.86$, the thresholds for $k=2$ to $5$ being $0.08$, $0.14$, $0.012$, and $3.7\times10^{-4}$.
4. Reported two-dimensional advection $L^\infty$ orders as low as 1.42 on uniform quadrilaterals and 0.56 on quasi-uniform triangles for $k=1$, attributed to $IS^1$, a second-order quantity, possibly exceeding the baseline at extrema and to $C_m=4$ being suboptimal in two dimensions, and orders 3.33, 5.03, and 3.34 for $k=3$ on triangles, where no cell lowered its degree and the unlimited scheme gave identical errors, tentatively attributed to non-uniform refinement of the triangular meshes.
5. Took the density as indicator variable for the Euler equations, applied the minmod fallback to characteristic variables with a single rotated characteristic decomposition along the density gradient, and applied the Zhang–Shu positivity-preserving limiter after the MR limiter; with 250 cells the $k\ge2$ blast-wave solutions almost recover the density peak, and in double Mach reflection small Kelvin–Helmholtz vortices appear for $k\ge2$ on $960\times240$ quadrilaterals and already for $k=1$ on triangles with boundary edge length $1/240$.

### Comments

- Section 3.1, Eq. (8), and Section 3.2, Eq. (13): the indicators are printed as $IS^m=\frac{1}{h_{j-1}}\left|\int_{K_{j-1}}p_j^m(x)dx-\bar p_{j-1}\right|+\frac{1}{h_{j+1}}\left|\int_{K_{j+1}}p_j^m(x)dx-\bar p_{j+1}\right|$ and $IS^m=\sum_{l=1}^3\frac{1}{|K_l|}\left|\int_{K_l}p_0^m(x,y)dxdy-\bar p_l\right|$, with the prefactor also dividing the cell averages, so (8) is not equivalent to the form stated after Algorithm 1, $\frac{1}{h_{j-1}}\left|\int_{K_{j-1}}(p_j^m(x)-p_{j-1}^m(x))dx\right|+\frac{1}{h_{j+1}}\left|\int_{K_{j+1}}(p_j^m(x)-p_{j+1}^m(x))dx\right|$, and (13) satisfies neither (18) for $\sigma\ne0$ nor the invariance under affine coordinate maps claimed in Section 3.3; the evident intended terms are $\left|\frac{1}{h_{j\pm1}}\int_{K_{j\pm1}}p_j^m(x)dx-\bar p_{j\pm1}\right|$ and $\left|\frac{1}{|K_l|}\int_{K_l}p_0^m(x,y)dxdy-\bar p_l\right|$, the form used in the FS indicator (17).
- Section 3.1, first item of the analysis after Algorithm 1: "when the solution has at most one discontinuity on the global stencil $S$, at least one of $S_L$ and $S_R$ is smooth, and we always have $IS^0=\min(IS_L,IS_R)=\mathcal{O}(h_j)$", but by (5)–(6) both substencils contain $K_j$, so a single discontinuity inside $K_j$ lies in both and makes $IS_L$, $IS_R$, and $IS^0$ generally of the order of the jump; for that cell the second conclusion, "$IS^0$ is a higher-order quantity than $IS^m$ ($m\ge1$)", does not follow, and the same applies to the statement for $S^{\triangle}$ in Section 3.2, whose substencils all contain $K_0$; unresolved.
- Section 3.3, after Definition 3.3: the change of variables is written $|K_j'|=\int_{K_j'}dx'dy'=\int_{K_j}\det(A)dxdy=\det(A)|K_j|$ for a general nonsingular $A$, which gives a negative area when $\det(A)<0$; the Jacobian factor is $|\det(A)|$, and the conclusion $\bar p_j'=\bar p_j$ is unaffected.
- Section 4.2, Example 4.5: "For the cases of $k>1$, although some troubled cells are detected, the polynomial order does not reduce to 0", with only "the troubled cells detected outside the expansion region" attributed to truncation errors, but the time histories in Fig. 8(h) and (j)–(l) show cells in the order-0 legend colour at the centre of the expansion region, $x\approx0$, for $t$ between about 0.14 and 0.27, a streak at least one cell wide for $k=4$, 5, and 6, whereas the $t=0.6$ profiles in Fig. 8(b)–(f) have no order-0 cells inside the fans; unresolved.
- Section 4.2, Example 4.7: the initial density for $x\ge-4$ is "$1+0.2\sin(5\pi x)$", but in Fig. 10 the undisturbed entropy wave ahead of the shock has maxima near $x\approx2.8$ and $4.1$ and minima near $3.45$ and $4.7$, a period of about 1.26 that matches $\sin(5x)$ (period $2\pi/5$) rather than $\sin(5\pi x)$ (period 0.4); the evident intended density is $1+0.2\sin(5x)$.
- Section 4.2, Example 4.8: the blast waves are computed "in the computational domain $[-1,1]$", but the initial data are defined only for $0\le x\le1$ and all panels of Fig. 11 span $0\le x\le1$; the evident intended domain is $[0,1]$.
- Section 4.2, Example 4.8: "Fig. 11 shows the distributions of the density and the polynomial order at $t=1.8$", but the Fig. 11 caption states $t=0.038$ and its time-history panels end at about $t=0.038$; the evident intended time is $t=0.038$.
