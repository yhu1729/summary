# 2026SJNB

## Claude (September 2026)

### Summary

Sharp ridges, creases of large principal curvature along which field lines collimate, appear mostly on the inboard side in compact quasiaxisymmetric (QA) stellarators. The authors extend their vacuum ridge theory to ideal magnetohydrostatic equilibria with pressure and current by expanding the strong-quasisymmetry generalized Grad–Shafranov system in the deviation from axisymmetry, using a basis that stays nondegenerate in that limit. The first-order system is linear but overdetermined, and a volumetric solution is deemed unlikely; defining ridges as minima of $|\nabla\psi|$ and imposing $\nabla\psi_0\cdot\nabla\psi_1\approx0$ closes it on a flux surface. For many field periods $N$, local periodic solutions exist only where a discriminant is nonnegative, which confines $\mathcal A_0=\nabla\psi_0\cdot\nabla|\nabla\psi_0|^2/(2|\nabla\psi_0|^2)$ to a band and hence the Gaussian curvature $\mathcal K$ of the axisymmetric background to a mostly negative range whose positive part is smaller by $O(\varepsilon_p^2)$, with $\varepsilon_p=|\nabla\psi_0|/F$; the perturbation therefore vanishes on the outboard side, where $\mathcal K$ is largest. A ballooning-type analysis for arbitrary $N$ gives the same condition. Numerical solutions on a Solov'ev tokamak surface are localized inboard, and in SIMSOPT-optimized QA configurations with $n_{fp}=2$, 3, 4, and 6, ridges lie where $\mathcal K$ is strongly negative and $\nabla\psi_0\cdot\nabla\psi_1$ is small. The analysis is local, linear, and single-surface.

### Contributions

1. Recast the strong-quasisymmetry Grad–Shafranov system in the $(\boldsymbol u,\nabla\psi,\boldsymbol B)$ basis and grouped its first-order expansion so that $\boldsymbol u_1$ follows from $\psi_1$ and $\Lambda_1$ by on-surface relations, the generalized Grad–Shafranov equation fixes the radial gradient of $\Lambda_1$, and a surface-averaged compatibility condition fixes the magnetic shear.
2. Derived the large-$N$ ridge ordering $\Lambda_1^{(0)}=0$ and $\nabla\psi_0\cdot\nabla\psi_1/|\nabla\psi_0|^2=O(N^{-1})$, with radial derivatives of other perturbations of order $N^2$ for irrational and order one for rational rotational transform, the latter requiring $\mathcal C_\psi=0$ and giving X-point ridges along closed field lines on both the inboard and outboard sides.
3. Reduced the lowest-order on-surface problem to a hyperbolic Laplace equation from conjugate-net theory, whose local solutions $\exp iN(k_\theta\theta\pm\phi)$ obey a quadratic dispersion relation for the ridge pitch $k_\theta$, and showed that in the ballooning formulation zeros of $\mathcal C_1$ are regular singular points with indices $0$ and $1+2ix_0$ that act as infinite barriers localizing the solutions.
4. Expressed the Gaussian curvature of an axisymmetric flux surface through $\psi_0$ as $\mathcal K=(\partial_R\psi_0/R)(\partial_R^2\psi_0+\partial_z^2\psi_0-\mathcal A_0)/|\nabla\psi_0|^2$, linking it to the Grad–Shafranov operator and identifying $\mathcal A_0=0$ with the Aronsson equation.
5. Posed the overdetermined ridge equations as minimizing $|\mathbf A\mathbf x|$ at fixed $|\mathbf x|$, solved as the eigenproblem $\mathbf A^\top\mathbf A\mathbf x+\lambda\mathbf x=0$ with a Fourier–Galerkin discretization for $N=8$ and linear plus mapped Zienkiewicz infinite elements for $k_\alpha=4$, with normalized residuals from $6\times10^{-4}$ to $3.8\times10^{-3}$.

### Comments

- §4.3: the text states that "$\delta_J=-(R^2p'+FF')$ from lowest-order force-balance (3.2)", but (4.18) defines $2\delta_J=\Delta^*\psi_0$ and (3.2) gives $\Delta^*\psi_0=-(FF'+R^2p')$, so $\delta_J=-(R^2p'+FF')/2$; the vacuum limit $\delta_J=0$ used afterwards is unaffected.
- §4.3, Eq. (4.22): in the vacuum limit (4.21) gives the bounds $\mathcal A_{00}\pm|\mathcal A_{01}|=\delta_R(1+\varepsilon_p^2)\pm|\delta_R|\sqrt{1+\varepsilon_p^2}$, whose upper value for $\delta_R>0$ expands as $\varepsilon_p^2\delta_R(2/\varepsilon_p^2+3/2+O(\varepsilon_p^2))$, not the printed $\varepsilon_p^2\delta_R(1/\varepsilon_p^2+3/2+O(\varepsilon_p^2))$; through (4.23c) the lower curvature bounds in (4.24) and (4.25) should then read $-(\varepsilon_p\delta_R/F)^2(2/\varepsilon_p^2+1/2+O(\varepsilon_p^2))$ and $-2(R_0\delta_R/F)^2$.
- §4.5, Eq. (4.35): the characteristic variable is printed as $\varphi\equiv\phi-\int(dR/R)(F/|\nabla\psi_0|)\partial_z\psi_0$, but the operator of (4.31), $\partial_R+(F/R)(\partial_z\psi_0/|\nabla\psi_0|^2)\partial_\phi$, has characteristics $d\phi/dR=F\partial_z\psi_0/(R|\nabla\psi_0|^2)$, and only $|\nabla\psi_0|^2$ makes $\varphi$ dimensionless; the intended denominator is $|\nabla\psi_0|^2$.
- §5, Eq. (5.5): eliminating $\psi_1^{(0)}$ between (5.4a) and (5.4b) gives the first-derivative term $2i(\mathcal C_2-\mathcal C_1)k_\alpha^{-1}\partial_\parallel\Lambda_1^{(1)}$, not the printed $2i(\mathcal C_1-\mathcal C_0)k_\alpha^{-1}\partial_\parallel\Lambda_1^{(1)}$; only $2i(\mathcal C_2-\mathcal C_1)$ yields the phase $S_c$ with integrand proportional to $(\mathcal C_2-\mathcal C_1)/\mathcal C_1$ in (5.6), the potential $\mathcal D_B=\mathcal C_2^2-\mathcal C_1\mathcal C_0$ in (5.7), and the phase derivative $(\mathcal C_1-\mathcal C_2\pm\sqrt{\mathcal D_B})/\mathcal C_1$ in (5.9), so $2i(\mathcal C_2-\mathcal C_1)$ is intended.
- §5, Eq. (5.9): (5.4a) with the printed phase $S$ gives $\psi_1^{(0)}=-i(\mathcal C_2\mp\sqrt{\mathcal D_B})\Lambda_1^{(1)}/(F\mathcal C_0\mathcal C_1)$, whereas (5.9) prints $\psi_1\approx-i\Lambda_1/\mathcal C_0$ for the same amplitude $\chi_{1a}e^{ik_\alpha S}\sqrt{\mathcal C_1/\sqrt{\mathcal D_B}}$, without the factor $(\mathcal C_2\mp\sqrt{\mathcal D_B})/(F\mathcal C_1)$; unresolved.

(5.11): for $\mathcal C_1=0$, (5.4) gives $\partial_\parallel S_{\mathcal C_1}=1-\mathcal C_0/(2\mathcal C_2)$ and $\psi_1^{(0)}=-i\Lambda_1^{(1)}/(2F\mathcal C_2)$, and (5.5) as printed gives $\partial_\parallel S_{\mathcal C_1}=1/2-\mathcal C_2/\mathcal C_0$, but the printed $S_{\mathcal C_1}$ has $\partial_\parallel S_{\mathcal C_1}=\mathcal C_2/\mathcal C_0-1/2$, and even for this phase (5.4a) gives $\psi_1^{(0)}=+i\Lambda_1^{(1)}(\mathcal C_2/\mathcal C_0-3/2)/(F\mathcal C_0)$, opposite in sign to the printed expression; unresolved.
