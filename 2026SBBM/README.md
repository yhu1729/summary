### Summary

This study couples radially global GENE gyrokinetic simulations to the Tango transport solver to predict steady electron-temperature, ion-temperature, and density profiles for the 15 MA ITER baseline scenario from near the magnetic axis to $\rho_{\mathrm{tor}}=0.6$. Electromagnetic simulations moderate the strong density peaking found electrostatically and yield a fusion gain of $Q=12.2$. Spectral analysis identifies microtearing, kinetic-ballooning, and Alfvénic ion-temperature-gradient modes at low binormal wavenumber as important transport channels that require high numerical resolution. Local flux-tube calculations recover the dominant modes but respond more stiffly to profile gradients than the global model. Realistic imposed toroidal rotation has little effect, whereas the safety-factor profile is decisive: flat $q\approx1$ regions with weak magnetic shear destabilize core kinetic-ballooning modes and worsen confinement. Electron-temperature-gradient transport is eventually suppressed by slowly evolving zonal flows at ITER-like low collisionality. Fusion-born alpha-particle effects and broader reduced-model validation remain future work.

1. Produced the first radially global gyrokinetic profile prediction for the 15 MA ITER baseline scenario using coupled GENE--Tango simulations.

4. Quantified differences in profile stiffness between global and local flux-tube simulations.
5. Showed that safety-factor shaping and long-timescale zonal-flow dynamics strongly affect confinement predictions.
