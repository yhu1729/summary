### Summary

This study uses axisymmetric JOREK simulations to resolve the formation and vertical dynamics of an X-point radiator (XPR) in ASDEX Upgrade geometry. Reduced-MHD evolution of the deuterium plasma is two-way coupled to full-$f$ kinetic deuterium neutrals and nitrogen impurities. Starting from discharge 38773, the model follows divertor detachment, high-field-side high-density formation, and establishment of a quasi-stationary radiating mantle 6.8 cm above the X-point. Increasing nitrogen seeding raises the radiation and ionization fronts until the XPR becomes a cold, recombining MARFE-like state; removing seeding reverses this sequence, moves the radiator downward, depletes neutrals, and ultimately destroys it. The reference XPR has a 1--2 eV core, electron density near $3\times10^{20}\,\mathrm{m}^{-3}$, and 10--15% impurity concentration. Including impurity--background collisions localizes the radiation poloidally, improving qualitative agreement with experiment and SOLPS-ITER. The simulations establish a mechanistic two-dimensional baseline for future three-dimensional studies of XPR--MHD interaction.

### Contributions

1. Built a wall-extended JOREK model coupling reduced MHD to kinetic deuterium neutrals and nitrogen impurities for an ASDEX Upgrade discharge.
2. Reproduced the progression from attached divertor through detachment and high-field-side high-density formation to an X-point radiator.
3. Characterized a quasi-stationary radiator 6.8 cm above the X-point, including its temperature, density, impurity, and front structure.
4. Resolved upward motion and MARFE formation under increased nitrogen seeding and downward retreat and loss when seeding is removed.
5. Showed that impurity collisions compress nitrogen into a more poloidally localized radiating mantle closer to experimental morphology.
