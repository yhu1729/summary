### Summary

The paper incorporates Shaing's analytic neoclassical toroidal viscosity (NTV) torque into a one-dimensional cylindrical model of nonlinear tokamak response to a single-helicity resonant magnetic perturbation. The model couples toroidal and poloidal momentum balance, magnetic-island evolution through a modified Rutherford equation, and island-phase dynamics. For representative locked and unlocked cases with uniform pressure, NTV is weak near the resonant $q=2$ surface but distributed throughout the plasma core. It therefore modifies core toroidal, poloidal, and parallel rotation without changing whether the resonant surface is locked or rotating. With nonuniform pressure, increasing $\beta$ strengthens the global NTV torque while reducing the resonant electromagnetic torque through changes in parallel flow and island phase; their competition preserves the locked state. A heat-diffusion model for island-induced temperature flattening alters local NTV structure and core flow but again leaves the locked or unlocked classification unchanged. Results are parameter-dependent predictions of a cylindrical single-fluid model rather than a full two-fluid, kinetic, or three-dimensional MHD treatment.

### Contributions

1. Added an energy- and pitch-integrated analytic NTV torque to a nonlinear RMP-response model coupling momentum, island width, and phase.
2. Demonstrated that NTV can rival electromagnetic torque in the core while remaining small at the resonant surface.
3. Showed that NTV changes core rotation profiles without changing locked or unlocked mode states in uniform-pressure examples.
4. Found that increasing nonuniform-pressure $\beta$ raises global NTV torque and lowers electromagnetic torque while retaining locking.
5. Incorporated island-driven temperature flattening and showed that it redistributes NTV without changing the original mode state.
