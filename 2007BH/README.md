### Summary

This review develops a unified foundation for nonlinear gyrokinetics, the reduced kinetic description of low-frequency turbulence in strongly magnetized plasmas. It organizes the theory around three mutually consistent elements: a gyrokinetic Vlasov equation with a gyrocenter Hamiltonian containing quadratic ponderomotive-like terms; gyrokinetic Poisson--Ampere equations containing polarization and magnetization contributions; and an exact energy conservation law retaining the required linear and nonlinear couplings. Phase-space Lagrangian and Hamiltonian Lie-transform methods systematically separate fast gyromotion from slower dynamics through guiding-center and gyrocenter transformations, with push-forward and pull-back operations connecting reduced and particle variables. Simplified equations are presented for electrostatic, shear-Alfvenic, and compressional magnetic fluctuations, and velocity moments connect the kinetic formulation to reduced fluid models. A variational formulation produces the self-consistent Vlasov--Maxwell system and its conserved energy through Noether's method. The framework accommodates nonuniform plasmas and arbitrary magnetic geometry, clarifies the approximations behind commonly used models, and discusses extensions including strong $E\times B$ flow shear and bounce-center kinetics.
