### Summary

This paper analyzes photon acceleration in magnetized electron--ion plasmas. Strong magnetic fields change the electromagnetic dispersion relation and therefore change how radiation interacts with relativistic plasma-wave refractive-index modulations. The authors derive the magnetized wave dynamics, compare ordinary and extraordinary electromagnetic modes, and formulate Hamiltonian descriptions for wave-packet evolution through moving plasma-wave structures. In the photon-acceleration regime, a wave packet can gain frequency and amplitude by interacting with a relativistically moving modulation; Lorentz invariance makes the field amplitude scale with the same frequency-upshift factor. The magnetic field produces both quantitative and qualitative changes, with the strongest enhancement for extraordinary right-circularly polarized waves propagating along the static field. Ordinary and left-polarized modes show weaker changes in the cases considered. The paper connects the mechanism to laboratory high-field laser-plasma experiments and to astrophysical environments where strong magnetization and relativistic plasma waves coexist.

### Contributions

1. Derived magnetized electromagnetic-wave dispersion relevant to photon acceleration.
2. Built Hamiltonian wave-packet descriptions for interaction with relativistic plasma waves.
3. Compared ordinary and extraordinary modes in magnetized electron--ion plasma.
4. Identified extraordinary right-polarized waves as the case with strongest magnetic enhancement.
5. Connected frequency upshift and amplitude gain to laboratory and astrophysical magnetized plasmas.
