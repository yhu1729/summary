### Summary

One-dimensional fully kinetic SMILEI simulations show that linear or parabolic density gradients can replace laser-frequency chirping in autoresonant plasma beat-wave excitation. Spatial variation of the plasma frequency compensates the nonlinear wavelength shift, preserving phase locking and driving waves beyond the Rosenbluth--Liu limit. For normalized laser amplitudes $a_{1,2}=0.1$ and $0.2$, saturation reaches about $0.60E_0$ and $0.95E_0$, respectively; the classical limit is exceeded for $a>0.06$. Saturated amplitude is nearly independent of gradient length, while dephasing length scales linearly with gradient length and drive amplitude. In a steep parabolic profile, two counter-propagating laser pairs create a two-phase quasiperiodic density lattice that persists after the lasers depart; an $800\,\mathrm{nm}$, $7\times10^{17}\,\mathrm{cm}^{-3}$ example lasts about $2.1\,\mathrm{ps}$. The collisionless, cold-electron, one-dimensional model omits multidimensional bowing and filamentation that may degrade the structure.
