# 2026YT

## Claude (September 2026)

### Summary

Alfvén eigenmodes destabilised by energetic ions can carry the ions' energy from the region where the mode is driven to the region where it is damped, a process called spatial channelling; because the mode has toroidal mode number $n$, it also carries toroidal angular momentum, $\Gamma_{J_\phi}=(n/\omega)\Gamma_E$. From resonant motion in action–angle variables, the authors relate the radial current of resonant ions to the power they transfer to the mode, separately for trapped and passing ions. Treating the energetic ions kinetically and the thermal species as fluids, and assuming $v_A/c\ll1$ so that the thermal polarisation current nearly cancels the fast-ion current, they obtain the torque on the thermal plasma. For trapped ions, the thermal plasma receives essentially all the momentum given to the mode; for passing ions, a fraction, small for low-frequency modes with $n\gg1$, stays with the energetic ions. Separated drive and damping regions therefore exert opposite torques that tend to generate sheared toroidal flow. An estimate for illustrative DIII-D-like parameters gives rotation of about 85 km/s and a shearing rate of $2.5\times10^4\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$, below an estimated turbulence growth rate of $5.2\times10^4\,\mathrm{s^{-1}}$. The authors stress that the estimate depends on uncertain parameters and neglects viscosity and mode-induced momentum redistribution.

### Contributions

1. Extended the theory of momentum channelling from well-passing to trapped energetic ions, deriving relations between the radial resonant-ion current and the power transferred to the mode for both orbit classes.
2. Derived toroidal and poloidal acceleration equations with a non-hydrodynamic energetic-ion population, recovering the near cancellation of the fast-ion current by the thermal polarisation current.
3. Showed that the momentum fraction retained by passing energetic ions is bounded by about $2/(nq)$ for low-frequency modes, becoming significant only for $n\sim1$ or high-frequency, cyclotron-resonant modes.
4. Identified momentum channelling as a mechanism for sheared-flow generation and gave a steady-state scaling of the induced rotation with mode number, drive rate, perturbation amplitude, mode width, and confinement time.
5. Contrasted momentum channelling with nonlinear zonal-flow excitation: it needs spatially separated drive and damping and produces flow acceleration proportional to the squared mode amplitude rather than an exponential growth rate.
