### Summary

Zimmermann and coauthors compare benign and non-benign termination of high-current runaway-electron beams in JET and DIII-D after hydrogenic injections. The study combines fast magnetic measurements, EFIT reconstructions, and CASTOR3D linear resistive-MHD modeling for about 40 JET and 20 DIII-D discharges. In JET, unsuccessful high-current cases occur near low edge safety factor, $q_{\rm edge} \approx 2$, and are preceded by intermittent non-terminating MHD activity. Larger internal inductance indicates more peaked runaway-current profiles, which may maintain ionization and prevent effective recombination. Benign JET cases typically terminate at $q_{\rm edge} \geq 3$ with less peaked profiles. DIII-D spans a wider range of edge safety factors but shows the same central role for current-profile peaking. Growth rates alone do not distinguish outcomes; perturbation amplitude and the interplay of ideal and resistive dynamics matter for deconfinement.

### Contributions

1. Compared runaway-electron beam termination behavior across JET and DIII-D datasets.
2. Linked benign termination likelihood to edge safety factor and internal inductance trends.
3. Identified peaked runaway-current profiles as a key factor in non-benign outcomes.
4. Used CASTOR3D modeling to relate observed boundaries to resistive kink modes.
5. Showed that MHD growth rate alone is insufficient to predict efficient runaway deconfinement.
