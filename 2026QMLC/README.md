### Summary

Qiu and coauthors optimize laser-driven proton acceleration in near-critical-density plasma using particle-in-cell simulations and analytic modeling. The study asks whether tighter focusing helps only by increasing intensity, or whether spot size matters even at fixed intensity. Simulations show that reducing the laser spot from a conventional scale to 0.8 $\mu$m increases maximum proton energy despite lower total laser energy. The mechanism is stronger ponderomotive-force-driven electron acceleration at small spot size, producing a faster and stronger charge-separation field. The authors then design a density down-ramp that velocity-matches protons to the accelerating field, enabling phase-stable acceleration and further energy gain. The combined strategy raises proton energy while relaxing the demand for large laser facilities, suggesting that advanced focusing and tailored plasma profiles can make compact laser-plasma proton sources more efficient.

4. Derived a density down-ramp profile for phase-stable proton acceleration.
5. Demonstrated additional energy gain from combining tight focusing with target-profile optimization.
