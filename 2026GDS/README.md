### Summary

This study develops nonlinear theory and particle-in-cell simulations for plasma wakes driven by Compton scattering of an incoherent high-frequency photon burst, rather than by a coherent laser's ponderomotive force. The analysis extends earlier linear theory to asymmetric and nonresonant drivers and derives a relativistic fluid description in which sufficiently energetic bursts approach the cold wave-breaking field. A perfectly collimated photon driver propagates at $c$, enabling trapped electrons to remain phase-locked; practical acceleration is then limited by photon depletion. Angular divergence lowers the driver's ensemble speed and therefore the wake phase velocity, restoring a dephasing limit. One- and two-dimensional OSIRIS simulations validate the predicted wake amplitudes, relativistic wavelength increase, trapping, depletion scaling, and divergence dependence. Finite-width drivers create transverse structures unlike laser wakefields: stochastic Compton momentum transfer produces a persistent current and DC magnetic field that focuses relativistic electrons throughout the wake. Current laboratory photon sources cannot reach the nonlinear regime, whereas intense compact-object radiation propagating through tenuous astrophysical plasma may do so.

### Contributions

1. Extended linear Compton-wake theory to asymmetric and long nonresonant photon bursts.
2. Derived and validated a nonlinear relativistic fluid model reaching the wave-breaking regime.
3. Established electron trapping and phase-locking conditions and depletion- and dephasing-limited energy scalings.
4. Revealed a persistent DC magnetic component that provides transverse focusing in two dimensions.
5. Assessed laboratory and astrophysical regimes for nonlinear Compton-driven wake acceleration.
