### Summary

The paper constructs periodic traveling waves for the one-dimensional two-species Vlasov--Poisson equation as ion and electron patches bounded by four moving velocity profiles. A Hamiltonian reformulation reduces the strip-interface dynamics to a coupled functional equation near homogeneous velocity-layer equilibria. Analytic Crandall--Rabinowitz theory then gives small-amplitude branches at simple Fourier-mode eigenvalues. Depending on the relative geometry of the ion and electron layers, the local diagram has two or four branches; the bifurcations are pitchforks, and symmetric configurations produce a hyperbolic branch geometry. A global analytic bifurcation argument continues each local family until it loops or approaches blow-up, boundary collision, or degeneracy. An affine transformation maps these kinetic patch states to traveling solutions of the two-component Euler--Poisson system, providing both small- and large-amplitude waves. The construction extends earlier electron-only work by allowing both charged species to evolve, at the cost of a higher-dimensional kernel and more intricate branch classification.

5. Transferred the construction through an affine transformation to small- and large-amplitude two-component Euler--Poisson traveling waves.
