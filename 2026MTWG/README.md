# 2026MTWG

## Claude (September 2026)

### Summary

The authors implement, in MATLAB with GPU arrays, a matrix-free Full Approximation Storage (FAS) multigrid solver, a nonlinear multigrid whose coarse grids carry full approximations rather than corrections, for finite differences on cell-centered and staggered grids. Because masked red–black updates are awkward to vectorize there, the smoother is an X-shape multicolor Gauss–Seidel (X-MCGS) sweep over four index-parity subsets in 2D, or eight in 3D, each updated as a strided sub-block, presented as an implementation convenience, not a better smoother than red–black ordering. For $p-\Delta p=f$ the solver converges nearly independently of $h$ with second-order accuracy in 3D, and benchmarks indicate that its speed is governed mainly by memory traffic and smoothing-kernel organization. For incompressible Navier–Stokes, first- and second-order projection schemes are reorganized so that 8 instead of 12 or 15 arrays stay resident, reducing peak memory by 19–30% and run time by 18–31% and fitting $512^3$ on a 24 GB RTX 4090. Lid-driven cavity flow, grain growth with up to 1189 orientations, vesicle phase separation, and two-bubble coalescence are also computed. The authors state that the transfer operators are not optimized, full V-cycles scale worse than the smoothing kernel, mixed precision gained only 5–15%, and multi-GPU execution is future work.

### Contributions

1. Assigned odd–even and even–odd index pairs $(i,j)$ to sets 1 and 2, updated from the previous sweep, and even–even and odd–odd pairs to sets 3 and 4, updated from the new values, each by $p_{i,j}=(p_{i+1,j}+p_{i-1,j}+p_{i,j+1}+p_{i,j-1}+h^2f_{i,j})/(4+h^2)$ on strided index vectors, and found on a $2048^2$ grid that the 1234–1234 X ordering reached $\mathrm{res}<10^{-9}$ in 15 V-cycles, against 21 for 1234–4321 and 31 to 43 for U- and Z-shape orderings.
2. Removed the copies $(\cdot)_{\mathrm{old}}=(\cdot)_{\mathrm{new}}$ of the first-order scheme by writing $u^{n+1}$ into $U_{\mathrm{old}}$ and $\tilde u^{n+1}$ into $U_{\mathrm{new}}$, which leaves the results unchanged, and in the second-order scheme executed $u^{n-1}=u^n$ right after assembling $f_u$, and likewise for $v$ and $w$, so that the $v$ and $w$ momentum equations are advected with $(u^n+\tilde u^{n+1})/2$, and the $w$ equation also with $(v^n+\tilde v^{n+1})/2$, in place of the extrapolations $(3u^n-u^{n-1})/2$ and $(3v^n-v^{n-1})/2$ of the classical implementation.
3. Timed one V-cycle at $16384^2$ and $512^3$ at 0.9396 s and 0.4633 s on the RTX 4090, against 36.6231 s and 21.5990 s on one i9-13900KS core, with both 8 GB GPUs out of memory, and estimated an effective bandwidth of about 217 GB/s, against a 1008 GB/s peak, for an isolated smoothing step from $4096^2$ to $16384^2$ under a minimum-traffic model of 96 bytes per unknown in 2D and 128 in 3D.
4. Reported errors from $1.74\times10^{-3}$ on $32^3$ to $6.75\times10^{-6}$ on $512^3$ for $p_{\mathrm{exact}}=\prod_l\sin(\pi\sin(\pi x_l))$, temporal orders on a $4096^2$ grid at $Re=10$ for $\Delta t=1/10$ to $1/160$ approaching 1.00 for the first-order scheme and 2.01 for the second-order velocity, with the second-order pressure orders falling from 1.47 to 1.13, and a discrete divergence integral below $10^{-19}$ in magnitude in cavity runs on $4096^2$ and $512^3$ grids.
5. Fitted $R_t^m-R_0^m=kt$ to Fan–Chen grain growth with $q$ up to 1189 on $512^2$ and 123 on $128^3$, about $3.12\times10^8$ and $2.58\times10^8$ unknowns, obtaining $m$ near 2 and $k$ leveling off as $q$ grows, showed for vesicle phase separation at $a_{l_d}=0.3$ and 0.7 the realization closest to experiment out of 10 random seeds, and ran the quasi-incompressible Navier–Stokes–Cahn–Hilliard two-bubble case on $256\times256\times1024$ with $\Delta t=2.5\times10^{-5}$ at a peak of 23.46 GB, where the classical implementation exceeded memory.

### Comments

- Section 2.2, Eqs. (9), (11)–(12): the model problem requires "$p=0$ on $\partial\Omega$", but the ghost-point conditions $p_{1,N+1,j}=p_{1,N,j}$ and $p_{1,i,N+1}=p_{1,i,N}$ impose a zero normal derivative on the right and top boundaries (only $p_{1,0,j}=-p_{1,1,j}$ and $p_{1,i,0}=-p_{1,i,1}$ give $p=0$), which the Section 3.1 test solution $\prod_l\sin(\pi\sin(\pi x_l))$, with nonzero normal derivative at $x_l=1$, does not satisfy although Table 2 reports second-order convergence to it; the evident intended conditions are $p_{1,N+1,j}=-p_{1,N,j}$ and $p_{1,i,N+1}=-p_{1,i,N}$.
- Section 2.2: "Step 9 updates $r_0\leftarrow L_{h_0}(p_0^{k,0})+r_0$ without storing $f_0$ separately", but in Algorithm 1 this is Step 8, $f_0:=r_0+L_{h_0}(p_0^{k,0})$ ("Coarse source term"), whereas Step 9 opens the coarse-grid smoothing loop (Steps 9–11 in Remark 2); the evident intended reference is Step 8.
- Section 3.3.2: "Figs. 12 and 11 present the phase separation dynamics on the vesicle membrane for $a_{ld}=0.3$ and $a_{ld}=0.7$, respectively", but the captions assign $a_{l_d}=0.3$ to Fig. 11 and $a_{l_d}=0.7$ to Fig. 12, whose panels show red $l_d$ domains in the blue $l_o$ phase and the reverse; the figure numbers in the text are swapped.
- Section 3.3.2 and Appendix E: the regularized delta function $\delta(\phi)=\epsilon^{-1}f(\phi)+\epsilon|\nabla\phi|^2/2+\eta$ uses the double well $f(c)=c^2(1-c)^2/4$, with minima at 0 and 1, while "$\phi=1$ inside, $\phi=-1$ outside" and Eq. (22) sets $\phi=\tanh\big((r_0-|\boldsymbol{x}|)/(2\sqrt2\epsilon)\big)$, so $f(-1)=1$ makes $\delta\approx\epsilon^{-1}$ throughout the exterior, 100 for $\epsilon=0.01$, above the membrane value $1/(16\epsilon)=6.25$ at $\phi=0$, contrary to "a regularized delta function characterizing the vesicle membrane"; unresolved.
- Table 12: the errors $2.90E-03$ for $u$ at $\Delta t=1/20$, and $7.43E-02$ and $5.73E-03$ for $p$ at $\Delta t=1/20$ and $1/160$, exceed the preceding entries $1.24E-03$, $2.06E-02$, and $1.25E-03$, contrary to the positive orders 2.09, 1.47, and 1.13 in the same rows and to the following orders 2.04 and 1.35; $2.90E-04$, $7.43E-03$, and $5.73E-04$ reproduce all printed orders to rounding and are evidently the intended values.
