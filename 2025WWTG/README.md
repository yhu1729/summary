### Summary

This paper quantitatively compares scrape-off-layer filament properties from real-size GBS fluid turbulence simulations with experiments from the TCV-X21 validation case. The comparison is enabled by extending the open TCV-X21 dataset with two-dimensional gas-puff imaging measurements at the outboard midplane and divertor region. To make the simulation output comparable to the diagnostic, the authors analyze GBS data with a synthetic gas-puff imaging model that includes helium-plasma emission physics and line-integration effects. Filament radial and poloidal velocities agree well between simulation and experiment, while the simulations overpredict filament sizes and underpredict relative fluctuation levels. The simulations indicate that scrape-off-layer filaments are mainly density perturbations rather than temperature perturbations, supporting assumptions used in experimental transport analysis. The paper also examines the relationship between filament poloidal velocity and the time-averaged $E \times B$ direction, finding agreement at the outboard midplane and X-point region but not in the divertor leg.

### Contributions

1. Extended the TCV-X21 validation comparison with two-dimensional gas-puff imaging filament data.
2. Built a synthetic gas-puff imaging diagnostic for GBS turbulence simulations.
3. Compared filament sizes, velocities, and fluctuation levels between simulation and experiment.
4. Identified strong velocity agreement but systematic size and fluctuation discrepancies.
5. Clarified the density-dominated character and $E \times B$ alignment of simulated filaments.
