### Summary

This work incorporates a reduced model of microturbulence-driven electron--ion energy exchange into the one-dimensional steady-state transport solver GOTRESS and evaluates its effect on global tokamak temperature profiles. The exchange is estimated from turbulent particle and heat fluxes, then compared with Coulomb exchange for DIII-D discharge 128913, an electron-heated DIII-D-like case, and ITER and SPARC scenarios. For the measured DIII-D case, turbulent exchange is only a few percent of total power and negligibly changes the profiles. Under strongly unbalanced electron heating, trapped-electron-mode turbulence transfers roughly 1 MW to ions, exceeds collisional transfer by more than a factor of two, and raises the central ion temperature by about 0.55 keV. In ITER and SPARC, ion-to-electron turbulent transfer is largely offset by enhanced collisional transfer, leaving profiles nearly unchanged. The results identify strongly unequal species heating, especially during startup, as the regime where turbulent exchange requires explicit treatment.

### Contributions

1. Integrated a low-cost turbulent electron--ion exchange model into GOTRESS for self-consistent steady-state global profile prediction.
2. Showed that turbulent exchange contributes only a few percent of total power and negligibly changes profiles for DIII-D discharge 128913.
3. Demonstrated that turbulent transfer exceeds collisional transfer by more than twofold and adds about 1 MW to ions in a strongly electron-heated case.
4. Quantified central-temperature shifts of $-0.13$ keV for electrons and $+0.55$ keV for ions under unbalanced heating.
5. Established that compensation by collisional exchange weakens the effect in ITER and SPARC steady states while identifying startup as a priority regime.
