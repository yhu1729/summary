### Summary

Three-dimensional fully kinetic particle-in-cell simulations resolve how weakly magnetized counterstreaming plasmas redistribute flow energy among fields, electrons, and ions. Early electrostatic modes and the filamentary Weibel instability amplify fluctuations but transfer little energy between species. Significant thermalization begins later when drift-kink deformation and filament merging stretch magnetic-field lines, producing a second, dynamo-like amplification stage. Electrons are heated mainly by magnetic pumping through betatron acceleration in the strengthening fields of kinked filaments. This intrinsically three-dimensional mechanism persists across a broad magnetization range. Mass-ratio scans give the final scaling $T_e/T_i\propto(m_i/m_e)^{-1/2}$. Extrapolation to electron--proton plasmas yields $T_e/T_i\approx0.03$, so electrons receive only a few percent of the initial ion kinetic energy, consistent with supernova-remnant observations.
