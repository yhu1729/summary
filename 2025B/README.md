### Summary

This paper revisits magnetic reconnection when electron inertia is the only non-ideal effect. Boozer gives a direct three-dimensional derivation showing that ordinary magnetic-field lines can reconnect, but the lines of the modified field $\vec{\mathcal{B}}=\vec{B}+\nabla\times((c/\omega_{pe})^2\mu_0\vec{j})$ do not. The paper then examines what this invariant field means physically. Guiding-center motion receives electron-inertia corrections proportional to $(c/\omega_{pe})^2$, which are usually smaller than gyroradius-order drift effects unless current density is extremely large. The analysis also emphasizes that two-coordinate reconnection models can be misleading because fully three-dimensional magnetic-line flows are characteristically chaotic. In three dimensions, even weak diffusive non-ideal effects can make $\vec{\mathcal{B}}$ undergo large-scale reconnection within only one or two orders of magnitude of the chaotic-advection timescale. Thus the non-reconnection theorem is mathematically sharp but often practically fragile in realistic three-dimensional plasmas.

4. Assessed when electron-inertia effects are small relative to gyroradius-scale drifts.
5. Explained why chaotic three-dimensional field-line flow limits the practical protection from the invariant field.
