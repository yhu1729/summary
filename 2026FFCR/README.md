### Summary

This study tests an electron-temperature-gradient (ETG) turbulent heat-flux model against JET H-mode pedestals spanning deuterium--tritium isotope mixtures and gas-fuelling rates. Using measured density profiles and separatrix boundary conditions, the model predicts the electron temperature at the density-pedestal top within about 20% across the scans. This supports slab-ETG turbulence, whose threshold depends on $\eta_e=L_{n_e}/L_{T_e}$, as the dominant heat-transport mechanism in the steep-density-gradient region. The model nevertheless underpredicts the fitted temperature-pedestal top by as much as a factor of two because that location lies farther inward, where the density gradient is weak and the adopted heat-flux scaling overpredicts transport. The isotope trend arises indirectly through isotope-dependent density profiles rather than an ion-mass dependence in the ETG scaling. Higher fuelling raises $n_{e,\mathrm{sep}}/n_{e,\mathrm{ped}}$ and lowers the predicted temperature, consistent with experiment. A combined density-pedestal, ETG, and EPED model predicts pedestal density and temperature generally within roughly 10%, although its pedestal-width trend disagrees with measurements.

### Contributions

1. Validated the ETG heat-flux model across JET isotope-mix and gas-fuelling scans rather than a single operating condition.
2. Found density-pedestal-top temperatures within about 20% of measurements throughout the dataset.
3. Showed that applying the steep-gradient scaling inward can underpredict the fitted pedestal-top temperature by more than a factor of two.
4. Explained the modeled isotope dependence through measured density-profile changes and the separatrix-to-pedestal density ratio.
5. Combined density, ETG, and EPED constraints into a full pedestal model with density and temperature predictions generally within about 10%.
