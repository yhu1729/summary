## ChatGPT (July 2026)

### Summary

This perspective asks what would make artificial intelligence effective enough to complement or replace mathematical algorithms in computational fluid dynamics. Rather than cataloguing applications, it derives four pillars of effectiveness from the historical success of mathematics: symmetry and invariance, separation of scales, sparsity, and semantic significance. It maps each pillar to current techniques, including physics-aware losses and equivariant architectures, multiscale and multifidelity learning, neural operators, sparse identification and compressed representations, and methods that embed interpretable scientific structure. Because empirical accuracy alone does not supply guarantees analogous to mathematical proof, the article adds credibility as a practical requirement, emphasizing robustness, uncertainty quantification, representative data, and explicit model doubt. Generative and foundation models may constitute a paradigm shift: they can incorporate some pillars indirectly, but their overparameterization, data demands, and emergent semantics may require a revised epistemological framework. The perspective concludes that physics-structured AI can become integral to hybrid numerical tools, while universality-oriented models require further scrutiny.

### Contributions

1. Framed AI effectiveness in computational fluid dynamics as an epistemological problem beyond benchmark accuracy.
2. Derived four pillars: symmetry, scale separation, sparsity, and semantic significance.
3. Connected each pillar to concrete AI architectures and workflows used in fluid mechanics.
4. Added robustness, uncertainty-aware doubt, and credibility as requirements for scientific adoption.
5. Explained why generative and foundation models may require revised pillars of effectiveness.
