# 2025KM

## Claude (October 2026)

### Summary

This article reports extensions to Irksome, the Firedrake library that converts Unified Form Language descriptions of semidiscrete finite element problems into stage-coupled Runge–Kutta (RK) systems solved with PETSc. Strong Dirichlet conditions are now imposed as algebraic equations on the stage approximations, $u^n + \Delta t\sum_j a_{ij}k_j = g(t_n + c_i\Delta t)$ on $\Gamma$, solved for the stage boundary values when the Butcher matrix $A$ is invertible; this differential-algebraic (DAE) viewpoint retains the stage order and handles incompatible initial and boundary data, which the earlier rule $k_i = g'(t_n + c_i\Delta t)$ misses. Butcher's change of variables $w = Ak$, stage-value formulations that skip the mass solve for stiffly accurate methods, and a DIRK path that builds a single-stage solver are selected by TimeStepper keywords. Preconditioners for the stage-coupled Jacobian are generated by re-deriving the coupled form with an approximate Butcher matrix inside firedrake.AuxiliaryOperatorPC, giving block Jacobi, block Gauss–Seidel, and the Rana LD preconditioner, beside stage-coupled monolithic multigrid. Heat-equation, Navier–Stokes cylinder, and Cahn–Hilliard experiments show RadauIIA methods with stage-independent iteration counts, runtimes comparable to DIRKs of equal stage count, and better accuracy per runtime than Alexander's DIRK and weak-stage-order DIRKs, although stiffness keeps most schemes below their formal order.

### Contributions

1. Derived the DAE-type boundary conditions (2.16)–(2.17), $\sum_j a_{ij}k_j = (g(t_n + c_i\Delta t, \cdot) - u^n)/\Delta t$ on $\Gamma$, noted that $u^{n+1}$ matches $g(t_{n+1})$ up to projection error for stiffly accurate schemes since $u^{n+1} = u_s$, and showed on the 1D heat equation with $u(0, x) = 0$ and boundary values 1 (ten linear elements, three-stage LobattoIIIC, $\Delta t = 0.05$ to $t = 0.5$) that the ODE-type rule, seeing zero boundary time derivative, returns a steady zero solution while the DAE-type rule captures convergence to $u = 1$ (Fig. 2); the keyword bc_method defaults to 'DAE'.
2. Added the formulation $\sum_j (A^{-1})_{ij}w_j - F(t_n + c_i\Delta t, y^n + \Delta t w_i) = 0$ for $w = Ak$, whose nonlinear Jacobian contribution occupies $s$ rather than $s^2$ blocks, selected by splitting = IA or AI (the default); the stage-value form (2.20)–(2.21), stage_type = 'value', with automatic skipping of the mass matrix update for stiffly accurate tableaux; and stage_type = 'dirk', which creates one single-stage variational problem updated between stages, with Alexander's L-stable three-stage DIRK and the weak-stage-order tableaux WSODIRK433 and WSODIRK744 included.
3. Implemented block preconditioners $P_D = D^{-1} \otimes M + \Delta t\,I \otimes K$, $P_L = (L + D)^{-1} \otimes M + \Delta t\,I \otimes K$, and $\tilde P_{LD} = \tilde A_{LD}^{-1} \otimes M + \Delta t\,I \otimes K$ with $A = LDU$ by overriding the form method of firedrake.AuxiliaryOperatorPC to repeat the Irksome transformation with $\tilde A$, so the construction applies to nonlinear problems and exposes the block inverses to further PETSc options; monolithic multigrid uses additive Schwarz on patches $V_i \times \cdots \times V_i$ via PCPatch or ASMPatchPC, with the theory of Kirby [2024] that its iteration matrix's spectral radius does not depend explicitly on the stage count; the Southworth and Leveque preconditioners are not yet supported.
4. For the heat equation with $u = e^{-0.1t}\sin(\pi x)\cos(\pi y)$, serendipity elements of degree 2 and 3, and $\Delta t = M/N$, $M = 1, 4, 8$, found suboptimal $L^2$ accuracy for RadauIIA(1) at all $M$ and for Alexander's DIRK at $M = 8$, an $L^2$ loss for RadauIIA(2) with cubic elements at large steps, and optimal accuracy for RadauIIA($s \ge 3$) and WSODIRK433 (Figs. 3, 4); with FGMRES at tolerance $10^{-8}$ and one BoomerAMG sweep per block, $P_D$ and $P_L$ iterations grew with stages to about 47 and 22 at four stages while $\tilde P_{LD}$ stayed near 10 and DIRK near 8, with comparable runtimes for the latter two (Fig. 5).
5. For flow past a cylinder with Mardal–Tai–Winther velocities, piecewise constant pressures, a three-level netgen hierarchy of 12,800 cells, Newton–Krylov with Eisenstat–Walker forcing, and V(3,3) cycles with elementwise Vanka patches, found the Rana preconditioner faster for RadauIIA(2) and (3) but monolithic multigrid faster for RadauIIA(4) (Fig. 8); over $[0, 8]$ with $\Delta t$ from 0.16 to 0.0025, iteration counts were stable in the stage count, DIRKs ran about as fast as RadauIIA with equal stages, and RadauIIA(3) and (4) gave the best final drag and lift error per runtime, with WSODIRK744 accurate but costliest (Figs. 9–11); for Cahn–Hilliard with Bell elements, $f(c) = 50(c^2 - 1)^2$, $\lambda = 10^{-2}$, Nitsche parameter 250, and $\Delta t$ from $5 \times 10^{-6}$ to $3.125 \times 10^{-7}$, about four Newton iterations per step sufficed, RadauIIA(3) and (4) had the lowest final-energy error, RadauIIA(2) beat Alexander and WSODIRK433 at lower cost, and implicit Euler stayed qualitatively different even at $\Delta t = 6.25 \times 10^{-7}$ (Figs. 13–15).

### Comments

- Section 2, Eq. (2.8): the update is written $u^{n+1} = u^n + \sum_i b_ik_i$ without the factor $\Delta t$ present in (2.3) and in the two-stage LobattoIIIC example $u^{n+1} = u^n + \frac{\Delta t}{2}(k_1 + k_2)$ after (2.10); the factor $\Delta t$ is evidently intended.
- Sections 2 and 4.2: the semidiscrete Navier–Stokes system (2.12) is called a "degree-2 DAE" in Section 2 and "an index 2 DAE" in Section 4.2; the index is evidently intended.
- Section 3.1: "the linear case discussed in Southworth et al. [2022a] could be easily implemented in Irksome, the nonlinear case in Southworth et al. [2022a] is much more involved", but in the reference list [2022a] is Part II, Nonlinearities and DAEs, and [2022b] is Part I, The Linear Setting; [2022b] is evidently intended for the linear case.
- Section 4.1 and Fig. 5: the solver comparison is said to run "on a fixed $256 \times 256$ mesh, integrating over a time interval $[0, 2]$", whereas the caption of Fig. 5 states that the heat equation was integrated "using cubic serendipity elements on a $64 \times 64$ mesh"; unresolved.
