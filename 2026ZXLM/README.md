# 2026ZXLM

## Claude (September 2026)

### Summary

The authors present VEQ (Veloce EQuilibrium), a parametric solver of axisymmetric fixed-boundary tokamak equilibria for repeated geometry queries, and benchmark its open-source realization VEQPy. With the last closed flux surface prescribed, the unknowns are shifted-Chebyshev coefficients of Miller extended harmonic (MXH) shaping profiles and, per input route, of normalized poloidal-flux or toroidal-field profiles. The main solve enforces a Grad–Shafranov residual projected onto test functions induced by constrained flux-surface motion, with moment conditions closing the profile coefficients. Six input routes pair a pressure gradient with toroidal-field, flux-gradient, current, current-density or safety-factor data and map them to one residual. Routes agree for smooth, mutually compatible inputs, deviating most in the near-edge safety factor $q_{95}$. On Solov'ev, CHEASE H-mode and EFIT X-point G-EQDSK files, the last a smoothed diverted boundary, the most accurate Pareto-selected reductions, with 9, 65 and 94 parameters, give RMS flux-surface errors of $1.1$–$1.9\times10^{-3}$ of the minor radius in 1.6–19 ms solve-only time. Sampled pointwise residuals show enrichment mainly improves interior force balance, while H-mode and X-point residuals remain concentrated near the boundary. The authors note that the projected solve does not certify pointwise force balance, timings exclude setup, and only the PF route was applied to G-EQDSK data.

### Contributions

1. Derived the transformed residual $\mathcal{G}=(J/R)(FF_\psi+\mu_0R^2P_\psi)+(g_{\theta\theta}\psi_\rho/JR)_\rho-(g_{\rho\theta}\psi_\rho/JR)_\theta$ from the Euler–Lagrange operator of the axisymmetric energy functional and projected it onto shape test functions $\chi_k=\nabla\psi\cdot\partial\mathbf{r}/\partial p_k$ obtained from the convective-variation identity $\delta_{\mathbf{r}}\psi+\nabla\psi\cdot\delta\mathbf{r}=0$.
2. Separated physical flux and source scales, $\psi_\rho=\alpha_2\hat\psi_\rho$ and $FF_\psi=\alpha_1\widehat{FF}_\psi$, fixed afterward by $I_p$ or $\beta_t$ constraints, and derived route closures from the flux-surface-averaged balance, including a square-root quadrature for $\hat\psi_\rho$ in the PF route and first-order ODEs for $F$ or $F^2$ in the safety-factor (PQ) route.
3. Swept 8022, 9483 and 9478 reduced truncations of the D-shaped, H-mode and X-point cases on a fixed $32\times32$ grid with cold-start Powell-hybrid solves; all returned finite values, all but 299 H-mode candidates met the $10^{-6}$ projected-residual tolerance, and the selected D-shaped High row reached $E_{\mathrm{ref}}/a=6.24\times10^{-5}$ with 9 parameters.
4. Showed that an unanchored point-collocation least-squares polish on the same manifold lowers the global sampled residual of the H-mode and X-point rows (ratios 0.05–0.68) but raises their G-EQDSK shape error 3.3–8.5-fold and the total time 2.8–16.7-fold, which the authors cite for keeping the variational solve.
5. Propagated the geometry factors $V'$ and $V'\langle|\nabla\hat\psi|^2\rangle$ through a fixed-source 1-D flux-coordinate heat-diffusion problem, finding temperature RMS errors of at most $3.1\times10^{-3}$, stored-energy errors of at most $6.6\times10^{-3}$ and beta-proxy errors of at most $1.3\times10^{-2}$ despite $V'$ RMS errors up to $4.7\times10^{-2}$.

### Comments

- Section 4.5, Table 2 and Fig. 5: Table 2 gives PJ2 errors $E_{\mathrm{coeff}}=8.037\times10^{-6}$, $\Delta_{I_p}=7.581\times10^{-6}$ and $\Delta_{\beta_t}=3.648\times10^{-6}$ at $N_\rho=N_\theta=32$, but the PJ2 curves in Fig. 5(a)–(c), plotted against $N_\rho$ at $N_\theta=32$, read about $6\times10^{-4}$, $8\times10^{-4}$ and $3\times10^{-5}$ at $N_\rho=32$ and are the largest of the six routes in panels (a) and (b); the other five rows of Table 2 agree with the figure, so which PJ2 values are correct is unresolved.
- Appendix B, Table B.8: the text states that "The polish reduces the global sampled RMS and maximum residual in every row", but Table B.8 lists $r_{|\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{std}}|_{\max}}=1.007$ for D-shaped (75), which by the caption's convention (values below one indicate reduction) is a slight increase of the maximum; the statement holds for every other row.
