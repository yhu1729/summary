### Summary

This paper reports the first coordinated non-dimensional energy-confinement experiments in closely matched negative-triangularity plasmas on DIII-D and TCV. Lower-single-null discharges varied normalized Larmor radius $\rho_*$ or collisionality $\nu_*$ while approximately holding other dimensionless parameters and radial profiles fixed. TRANSP power-balance analysis finds near-Bohm confinement scaling on DIII-D, driven mainly by the ion channel, whereas TCV shows near-gyro-Bohm scaling for both ions and electrons. Direct cross-machine $\rho_*$ scaling remained infeasible because TCV density peaking and rotation could not be matched to DIII-D. Both devices exhibit only a weak positive collisionality dependence, with $B_T\tau \propto \nu_*^{0.04\pm0.05}$ on DIII-D and $B_T\tau \propto \nu_*^{0.09\pm0.03}$ on TCV. A broader DIII-D engineering regression agrees with the controlled scans in its $\rho_*$ and $\nu_*$ dependencies. The finite scan ranges and residual profile mismatches limit direct reactor extrapolation, but the measurements provide validation targets for first-principles transport models.

### Contributions

1. Conducted the first self-similar, multi-device non-dimensional confinement study of strongly negative-triangularity plasmas.
2. Distinguished near-Bohm DIII-D scaling, dominated by ion transport, from near-gyro-Bohm TCV scaling.
3. Quantified the weakly favorable collisionality dependence of normalized confinement on both tokamaks.
4. Demonstrated agreement between controlled non-dimensional scans and an independently filtered DIII-D engineering regression.
5. Identified the profile-matching obstacles that currently prevent a direct cross-machine normalized-Larmor-radius scaling.
