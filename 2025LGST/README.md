### Summary

This paper reports experimental electron acceleration in a laser wakefield whose velocity can be tuned with a flying-focus pulse. The experiment shapes the beam spatio-temporally and focuses it with an axiparabola, producing a long-depth quasi-Bessel beam that controls the wakefield propagation velocity. This velocity control is aimed at mitigating dephasing, the loss of synchronism that limits electron energy gain in laser wake-field acceleration. The observed electron acceleration demonstrates that the flying-focus wake can preserve the coherent plasma structures needed for relativistic acceleration. Advanced optical and particle-in-cell simulations support the experimental interpretation, and a simplified analytical model aligns with the measured trends. The result is not a full optimization of an accelerator stage, but it is a key proof of principle: a tunable-velocity wakefield can accelerate electrons and therefore can plausibly be used to control the maximum energy achievable in future laser-plasma accelerators.

### Contributions

1. Demonstrated electron acceleration in a tunable-velocity laser wakefield.
2. Used flying-focus beam shaping and an axiparabola to control wakefield velocity.
3. Connected velocity tuning to mitigation of laser wake-field dephasing.
4. Supported the measurements with optical and particle-in-cell simulations.
5. Benchmarked the observed trends against a simplified analytical model.
