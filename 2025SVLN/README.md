### Summary

Particle-in-cell simulations test whether embedded nanoparticles can make electron injection and charge in laser wakefield accelerators tunable. Scanning elements from lithium to gold and diameters from 50 to $200\,\mathrm{nm}$ produces beams spanning roughly 10–$600\,\mathrm{pC}$. Material and size initially control injection through the nanoparticle's ionized electron density and electric field. Above a field-strength saturation threshold, however, the captured charge is governed mainly by the total number of atoms, so different material–size combinations can yield comparable injection. Nanoparticle electrons enter across several plasma periods rather than one localized event; increasing the injected charge consequently broadens the energy distribution. The study converts these trends into experimentally relevant selection rules for nanoparticle composition and size. It establishes a controllable charge-injection mechanism in simulation, while the trade-off between higher charge and larger energy spread remains a central design constraint.

5. Quantified the trade-off between increased beam charge and broader energy spread.
