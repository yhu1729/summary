### Summary

Emdee and coauthors use SOLPS-ITER fluid-kinetic edge simulations to explain inboard-outboard recycling asymmetries in a DIII-D H-mode plasma. The simulations compare cases with and without scrape-off-layer drifts and with different core-boundary rotation conditions derived from charge-exchange rotation measurements. Either drifts or rotation can enhance inner-target particle flux when the ion $B\times\nabla B$ drift points toward the X-point, but their combination produces a much stronger high-field-side/low-field-side asymmetry than either effect alone. With realistic rotation, radial transport of parallel momentum changes upstream scrape-off-layer flows, sending more deuterium flux toward the inboard divertor entrance and improving agreement with measured Lyman-$\alpha$ brightness profiles. The modeled inboard-outboard target recycling ratio rises from 1.9 with low momentum transport to 5.8 with high momentum transport. The Letter argues that rotation, viscous momentum coupling, and drifts should be included in 2D edge modeling for detachment-relevant reactor predictions.

### Contributions

2. Quantified the combined effect of scrape-off-layer drifts and imposed rotation on DIII-D target fluxes.
3. Showed that radial parallel-momentum transport alters upstream scrape-off-layer flow patterns.
4. Improved agreement with experimental inboard-outboard Lyman-$\alpha$ brightness asymmetry.
5. Identified rotation and viscous coupling as important inputs for future divertor and detachment modeling.
