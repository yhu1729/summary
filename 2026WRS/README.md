### Summary

The authors use an ensemble of three-dimensional incompressible magnetohydrodynamic direct numerical simulations to isolate how unstable reconnection creates turbulence without external forcing. An initially weak uniform magnetic field is stretched by an unstable jet until current sheets form. A three-dimensional current-sheet instability then drives a transition from laminar Sweet--Parker-like reconnection through stochastic reconnection to a decaying turbulent state. Reynolds-decomposed kinetic and magnetic energy budgets show that turbulent electromotive-force interaction with the mean magnetic shear is the dominant source of fluctuating magnetic energy. Most energy extracted from the mean magnetic field first enters Alfv\'enic fluctuations and is then exchanged with kinetic fluctuations through a nonlinear, nearly conservative inter-domain cascade. Turbulent production remains localized near reconnection sites, while local negative electromotive work in outflows returns some energy to the mean field. The study supplies a mechanistic energy pathway for reconnection-driven turbulence and distinguishes it from turbulence-triggered reconnection.

### Contributions

1. Demonstrated a self-sustained laminar-to-turbulent reconnection transition in unforced three-dimensional magnetohydrodynamic simulations.
2. Used a five-realization ensemble and Reynolds and phase averaging to separate coherent current sheets from stochastic fluctuations.
3. Derived turbulent kinetic- and magnetic-energy budgets covering production, dissipation, transport, and cross-domain transfer.
4. Identified electromotive-force--mean-magnetic-shear work as the dominant production route from mean magnetic energy to Alfv\'enic fluctuations.
5. Showed linear magnetic-energy-annihilation-rate growth during the current-sheet instability and localized turbulence production to reconnection regions.
