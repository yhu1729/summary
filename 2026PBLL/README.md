### Summary

Fourier coefficients of magnetic perturbations in a tokamak depend on the chosen flux-surface coordinates, which can make estimates of coupling between external fields and resonant plasma responses inconsistent. This paper proves that the coupling matrix has coordinate-invariant singular values when the external vacuum field on the control surface uses a square-root-area Fourier weight and the resonant field on each rational surface uses a full-area weight. Under this pairing, right singular vectors reconstruct the same physical field pattern across coordinate systems. The authors define a dimensionless dominant-mode overlap using the weighted external field and toroidal-field normalization. Generalized Perturbed Equilibrium Code calculations in PEST, Boozer, and Hamada coordinates confirm the analysis. With other weights, combined mode overlaps can differ by a factor of roughly $2$--$3$ for strongly shaped, low-aspect-ratio equilibria; the discrepancy is smaller at high aspect ratio. The result gives a precise weighting prescription for resonant-field metrics used in tokamak coil design and error-field correction.

### Contributions

1. Proved that square-root-area weighting makes the external-field Fourier vector transform unitarily between flux-surface coordinates.
2. Paired that vector with full-area-weighted resonant coefficients to obtain coordinate-invariant coupling-matrix singular values and physical modes.
3. Specified a dimensionless, coordinate-invariant dominant-mode overlap normalized by the toroidal magnetic field.
4. Confirmed the invariance numerically across PEST, Boozer, and Hamada coordinates using GPEC calculations.
5. Quantified errors from alternative weights across an aspect-ratio and shaping scan, reaching roughly a factor of $2$--$3$ in extreme cases.
