### Summary

Stellarator divertors must guide exhaust along three-dimensional magnetic fields, where separatrices often acquire broad stochastic layers. This paper designs an LHD-like helical divertor using optimized modular coils rather than continuous helical coils. The central device is a rotating, lemon-shaped target plasma surface with toroidally continuous sharp corners. Standard coil optimization minimizes the normalized normal magnetic field on this surface, causing its corners to become X-lines of a clean separatrix while also constraining coil length, spacing, and curvature. The resulting modular coils produce the desired topology with substantially less chaos than the Large Helical Device, showing that strong stochasticity is not intrinsic to helical divertors. A weighted squared-flux objective emphasizes accuracy near the sharp corners and improves field-line behavior. The authors also introduce manifold optimization, which directly controls stable and unstable manifolds without minimizing normal field and can further reduce chaos. That configuration is sensitive to coil perturbations, however, and the target boundary was not optimized simultaneously for confinement, stability, quasisymmetry, or quasi-isodynamicity. The work therefore establishes a new divertor-design route while identifying robustness and core-edge co-optimization as unresolved requirements.

### Contributions

1. Produced the first LHD-like helical divertor design based on optimized modular coils.
2. Used toroidally continuous sharp target-surface corners to create X-lines and a clean separatrix.
3. Demonstrated that a helical divertor can have substantially less magnetic chaos than LHD.
4. Added weighted squared-flux optimization to concentrate field accuracy near diverting corners.
5. Introduced manifold optimization as a direct separatrix-control objective and quantified its perturbation sensitivity.
