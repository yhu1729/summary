### Summary

Measurements on Alcator C-Mod show that cross-field particle transport grows rapidly as high-density H-mode plasmas approach loss of confinement and L-mode plasmas approach their density limit. The authors infer particle flux at the separatrix, the boundary between closed and open magnetic field lines, from Lyman-alpha emission, electron profiles, and stationary particle balance. Comparison with the separatrix operational space model shows that the characteristic resistive-ballooning wavenumber organizes the flux more consistently than collisionality alone. Including the cylindrical safety factor accounts for plasma-current variation and yields an empirical operational boundary at $k_{\mathrm{RBM}}^2\hat q_{\mathrm{cyl}}\approx1$. Estimated perpendicular convective heat transport approaches parallel conductive transport near this boundary, consistent with a thermal-collapse model in which equilibrium solutions merge. Under additional geometric and transport assumptions, the threshold implies a density-limit dependence approximately proportional to $P_{\mathrm{sep}}^{4/7}$. These observations connect enhanced transport to power starvation preceding density limits, while the reconstruction and interpretation depend on stationarity, poloidal symmetry, and transport closures. The measurements support resistive-ballooning-related transport without uniquely identifying the instability.

### Contributions

1. Inferred separatrix particle fluxes from Lyman-alpha measurements and electron profiles for L- and H-mode discharges near high-density operational boundaries.
2. Showed that enhanced flux tracks the L-mode density limit and high-density H–L boundary, without corresponding systematic increases near the ideal-ballooning boundary.
3. Identified $k_{\mathrm{RBM}}^2\hat q_{\mathrm{cyl}}$ as a common organizer of particle flux across confinement regimes and plasma-current variation, with an empirical limit near unity.
4. Linked the observed boundary to perpendicular convective heat transport approaching parallel conductive transport, consistent with loss of thermal equilibrium through a fold catastrophe.
5. Derived conditional pressure-gradient scale-length and power-dependent density scalings from the empirical threshold and examined their implications for ITER and SPARC.
