### Summary

Relativistic electron beams counterstreaming through plasma normally develop filamentation that degrades beam uniformity and transport. This paper studies a scissors-shaped geometry of two intersecting beams and a neutralizing return current using linear theory and two-dimensional particle-in-cell simulations. Breaking cylindrical symmetry replaces filamentation with a stratification mode. Collisionless magnetic reconnection rapidly quenches that mode, producing magnetic energy about two orders of magnitude below the one-beam case and maintaining fast-beam transverse nonuniformity near $10\%$ for roughly $8$ ps, compared with more than $300\%$ under filamentation. A later bulk-cavitation stage expels background plasma from magnetic vortices; a reduced model predicts magnetic energy growth proportional to $(t-t_0)^4$, consistent with fitted simulation exponents. The intersecting-beam systems also show much less spectral broadening and energy loss. These results establish geometric configuration as a passive instability-control mechanism, but uniform-density, periodic two-dimensional simulations are only a proof of principle; realistic gradients and full three-dimensional transport remain unresolved.

### Contributions

1. Introduced a canonical scissors-shaped multivelocity beam-plasma configuration.
2. Identified stratification, rather than filamentation, as its dominant initial instability.
3. Showed reconnection-driven suppression of magnetic energy and preservation of beam uniformity.
4. Modeled bulk cavitation and verified its predicted fourth-power magnetic-energy growth.
5. Quantified reduced beam thermalization and stated testable predictions and dimensional limitations.
