# 2026SWLA

## Claude (September 2026)

### Summary

Kilohertz laser-wakefield accelerators (LWFA) driven by few-millijoule pulses reach only about 10 MeV; higher energies need much more laser average power. The authors drive an LWFA with the SYLOS 3 optical parametric chirped-pulse amplification (OPCPA) laser at ELI-ALPS: 70 mJ, 8.1 fs pulses at 850 nm on target, focused to 10 µm FWHM ($a_0=1.5$) into a continuous-flow He(99%)–Ar(1%) gas jet with electron density about $3\times10^{19}$ cm$^{-3}$, where argon enables ionization injection. Operated at 100 Hz because of radioprotection and beam-dump limits, the accelerator produced quasi-monoenergetic 47 MeV beams with 15 pC core charge. Over 10,000 consecutive shots, peak-energy and charge fluctuations were 1.4% and 12% rms, and pointing jitter was 1.2–1.3 mrad rms. Deconvolving the 4.8 MeV spectrometer resolution bounds the intrinsic energy spread below 10% FWHM at 95% confidence. Principal-component analysis of beam profiles separates charge, pointing, and width fluctuations plus one unexplained core–halo mode, and shows variability dominated by drifts below about 1 Hz; recorded laser energy and carrier-envelope phase do not significantly explain it. Particle-in-cell simulations with the measured wavefront reproduce spectrum, charge, and divergence, attributing the narrow spectrum to injection localized by rapid self-focusing, but only with 0.1% instead of 1% argon, an unexplained discrepancy.

### Contributions

1. Demonstrated OPCPA-driven LWFA producing 47 MeV, 15 pC quasi-monoenergetic beams at 100 Hz with a kHz-compatible laser and gas target, which the authors report as a significant energy increase and a 10-fold charge increase over kHz LWFA driven by post-compressed Ti:sapphire lasers.
2. Measured shot-to-shot stability over 10,000 consecutive shots, with pointing jitter about one order of magnitude below the 15.6 and 18.8 mrad FWHM beam divergences.
3. Bounded the intrinsic energy spread by fitting an instrument-response-convolved model to a spectral peak comparable in width to the resolution, obtaining a 3.8 MeV best fit and a 4.6 MeV upper limit from a profile-$\chi^2$ analysis.
4. Validated the principal components against core charge, position, and width (Pearson $r=0.48$–$0.98$) using a circular time-shift test that accounts for slow drifts, and argued that feedback well below the repetition rate could correct the dominant fluctuations.
5. Identified with FBPIC simulations self-truncated ionization injection, mainly from Ar$^{9+}$ to Ar$^{11+}$ and most of the charge within about 100 µm, and explained the small Ar$^{8+}$ contribution through the trapping condition at its early ionization phase.
