## Claude (September 2026)

### Summary

The authors couple predict-first simulations of the 1.5D transport code RAPTOR with the free-boundary inverse equilibrium solver FBT into a Kinetic Equilibrium Prediction (KEP) workflow for preparing full discharges of the TCV tokamak. FBT computes the feedforward poloidal-field (PF) coil currents producing a target shape, but is usually given simple polynomial pressure-gradient $p'(\psi)$ and current-function $TT'(\psi)$ profiles tuned by operators. Here RAPTOR predicts these profiles from the pulse schedule, given an assumed confinement factor $H^{98(y,2)}$ and line-averaged density, using an empirical gradient-based transport model with feedback-controlled pedestal gradients, L–H transitions from the Martin power-threshold scaling, and a separate negative-triangularity (NT) parametrization. FBT takes the RAPTOR profiles, rescaling $p'$ to keep the plasma current, and returns its equilibria to RAPTOR; this loose iteration converges in a few steps. With measured density, electron stored energy is predicted within 20% in essentially all of 211 shots, and ion energy in most of 148. Experimentally, KEP-prepared coil currents improved X-point alignment in a lower-single-null H-mode and held a snowflake-divertor NT L-mode shape near target longer. The authors state that predicting density from gas programming remains delicate, that X-point height and radiation limit H-mode prediction, and that FBT neglects plasma dynamics and vessel currents.

### Contributions

1. Rescaled the Martin threshold by a divertor factor $f_{\mathrm{div}}(s)=1+1/(1+e^{s})$ of the signed normalized separation $s$ of the two X-point flux surfaces, giving 1 for favorable and 2 for unfavorable ion $\nabla B$ drift and 1.5 for a connected double null, with prohibitive values for limited and NT plasmas and an alternative $f^*_{\mathrm{div}}$ that lowers the threshold near $s=0$.
2. Extended the gradient-based model with an NT confinement state, triggered when $\min(\delta_{up},\delta_{bot})<-0.2$ with a 100 ms linear ramp and using fixed parameters selected to match 41 NT shots, and with an ion heat equation whose pedestal $T_i/T_e$ follows an ad hoc density law and which receives 60% of the absorbed NBI power, as an alternative to a $T_i/T_e$ scaling that runs 10–15% faster.
3. Reduced the runtime through schedule-based non-uniform time steps ($dt_{min}\le0.5$ ms in transients, up to 1 ms elsewhere) and C code generation (a factor 2), reaching $2.3\pm0.38$ min per second of discharge, and made FBT robust by freezing the plasma domain after 20 unconverged iterations, so that all FBT equilibria converged given a converged RAPTOR input.
4. For 48 of 51 single-null shots with a classifier-detected H-mode, RAPTOR placed the L–H transition within a 100 ms window, the other 3 being ohmic shots without a clear pedestal; spurious H-mode predictions in 21 ohmic shots were attributed to high X-point height, and three programmed double-null shots transitioned late, showing no clear favorable double-null effect on TCV.
5. Found KEP corrections to feedforward PF coil currents mostly of tens of amperes and occasionally several hundred, as in the H-mode phase of #81882, showed that imposing only RAPTOR's $\beta_{pol}$ and $q_A$ on polynomial profiles leaves differences in plasma and coil flux, and found that the predicted $l_{i3}$ drops at the L–H transition as in an interpretative LIUQE–ASTRA kinetic reconstruction, opposite to the diamagnetic-loop-constrained magnetic reconstruction.

### Comments

- Section 2.1, "Ion temperature": the separatrix condition "$T_i|_{sep}=\gamma T_e|_{ped}$, such that the ion temperature gradient follows $\mu_{T_i}=\gamma\mu_{T_e}$" conflicts with the definition $\gamma=T_i/T_e|_{ped}$, since with the linear pedestal profile of Eq. (2.4), $T_\sigma|_{ped}=T_\sigma|_{sep}+\mu_\sigma(1-\hat\rho_{ped})$, it gives $T_i|_{ped}=\gamma(2T_e|_{ped}-T_e|_{sep})\neq\gamma T_e|_{ped}$; the consistent reading is $T_i|_{sep}=\gamma T_e|_{sep}$.
- Section 3, discussion of #82274: "having a slightly LSN, favorable $s\simeq+0.5$, would have led to a factor 1.25 instead of 1.75" (with $s\simeq-0.5$, as Fig. 10(c) shows), but Eq. (2.8) with $\alpha_f=1$, $\alpha_u=2$, and $G(s)=1/(1+e^{s})$ gives $f_{\mathrm{div}}(0.5)\approx1.38$ and $f_{\mathrm{div}}(-0.5)\approx1.62$, as does the solid curve of Fig. 9; 1.25 and 1.75 correspond to $|s|\approx1.1$; unresolved.
- Section 4.1: the text says the schedule was tested "with the new FBT-RAPTOR result (#83940)", but the Fig. 13 caption and the Fig. 14 caption and legend identify the FBT-RAPTOR-prepared shot as #83740; the evident intended reading is #83740.
- Fig. 16 caption: the initial FBT run is said to be "used for the preparation of shot #83926" and the FBT-RAPTOR result "used to prepare #83946", whereas Section 4.2 and the Fig. 17 caption assign #83946 (with #83947 and #83950) to the initial FBT programming and #83926 (with #83978) to the FBT-RAPTOR correction; the caption's shot numbers appear swapped.
- Fig. 17 caption: #83946, #83947, and #83950 are described as "prepared with an initial FBT equilibrium with high $\beta_{pol}$ and $l_{i3}$", but Section 4.2 calls them "the shots prepared with low-$\beta_{pol}$ and low-$l_{i3}$", the Fig. 16 caption gives the initial FBT run "a low $\beta_{pol}$ and $l_{i3}$", and Figs. 16(a)–(b) show the initial-FBT $\beta_{pol}$ (about 0.05) and $l_{i3}$ (about 0.86) below the FBT-RAPTOR values in the NT phase; "high" evidently should read "low".
- Fig. 16 caption: LIUQE-MER is said to be displayed in (a)–(b), but the legends of panels (a) and (b) label the black curve "LIUQE KER"; unresolved.
