### Summary

This paper analyzes photon dynamics in a beam-driven plasma wakefield accelerator as a mechanism for frequency up-shifting laser light. A tailored plasma density can keep the wake's accelerating phase matched to the group velocity of a witness laser pulse, allowing large frequency shifts limited primarily by the drive beam energy. The authors study photons near this phase-matching condition using a photon kinetic model and validate the calculations against full electromagnetic particle-in-cell simulations. Those computations motivate a linear theory for photon phase-space evolution. The theory predicts scalings for the witness pulse and a self-similar phase-space solution. A notable conclusion is that, within the model, the pulse can be compressed without a lower bound on duration, suggesting a possible route to sub-femtosecond short-wavelength radiation. The work is primarily theoretical and computational, but it identifies design rules for photon acceleration experiments in plasma wakefields.

### Contributions

1. Modeled photon phase-space dynamics near wake-phase matching.
2. Validated photon kinetic calculations against electromagnetic particle-in-cell simulations.
3. Derived linear-theory scalings for the witness pulse.
4. Found a self-similar solution for photon phase-space evolution.
5. Identified photon acceleration as a route to ultrashort short-wavelength radiation.
