### Summary

This paper uses two-dimensional direct numerical simulations to quantify how insoluble surfactants alter nonlinear gravity-capillary waves. A coupled volume-of-fluid and phase-field method resolves surfactant transport, a nonlinear surface-tension equation of state, and Marangoni stresses across wave steepness $ak$, Bond number $Bo$, Reynolds number $Re$, and surfactant strength $\beta$. Surfactants act through two competing mechanisms: reduced local surface tension shifts transitions among parasitic-capillary, gravity-wave, and breaking regimes, while concentration gradients generate Marangoni stresses that smooth the surface. Consequently, roughness, vorticity, and dissipation vary non-monotonically with $\beta$; Marangoni forcing peaks near intermediate concentration, around $\beta\approx0.3$, suppressing capillary ripples but increasing near-surface vorticity and energy loss. A surfactant-dependent effective Bond number predicts regime changes, while the rescaled parameter $BoRe^{-1/2}(ak)^{-1}$ collapses boundaries of the Marangoni-dominated regime. At sufficiently strong surfactant loading, dissipation collapses against a local Marangoni number. Grid and parameter studies support the numerical trends.

### Contributions

1. Simulated surfactant-laden gravity-capillary waves across a broad four-parameter regime space.
2. Separated wave-regime changes caused by surface-tension reduction from smoothing caused by Marangoni stress.
3. Established the non-monotonic surfactant dependence of roughness, vorticity, and energy dissipation.
4. Introduced effective-Bond-number and force-balance scalings that collapse transition boundaries.
5. Linked enhanced intermediate-concentration dissipation to Marangoni-driven near-surface vorticity.
