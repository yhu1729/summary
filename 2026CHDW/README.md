### Summary

This simulation study proposes a hybrid laser-wakefield-acceleration to plasma-wakefield-acceleration stage in which a plasma photocathode converts fluctuating driver beams into lower-charge electron bunches with improved quality and reproducibility. Quasi-three-dimensional particle-in-cell simulations show that witness charge is governed by the photocathode laser and helium density, becoming effectively independent of driver charge once trapping is complete. Relativistic driver velocity makes early witness energy gain weakly sensitive to large changes in driver mean energy and energy spread; suitable extraction before depletion yields approximately $1\%$ energy stability and sub-$1\%$ projected witness energy spread over broad driver variations. Release near the symmetric field-minimum region of the blowout provides tolerance to timing and pointing jitter. When driver charge changes, the trapping position shifts within the wake so that accelerating-gradient variations are partially self-compensated. The stage simultaneously transforms brightness, energy, and stability, producing ultralow-emittance, multi-kA witness beams, but sacrifices charge: the demonstrated driver-to-witness charge ratio is about $1\%$.
