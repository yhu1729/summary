### Summary

The paper reports a proof-of-principle beam-driven plasma-wakefield accelerator in a plasma filament produced by self-guided femtosecond laser propagation through low-pressure nitrogen. The filament supplies a narrow, reproducible plasma channel without a high-current discharge, relativistic ionizing beam, or terawatt-class ionization laser. In a $3$-cm capillary with an approximately $8\times10^{14}\,\mathrm{cm}^{-3}$ plasma plateau, a driver--witness electron-bunch pair generates wakefields that raise the witness energy by about $8$ MeV to $104.5\pm0.4$ MeV, corresponding to an average gradient near $266$ MV/m, while preserving charge and relative energy spread. Across 100 consecutive shots, acceleration occurs in $95\%$ of filament shots with less than $0.5\%$ rms energy jitter. A matched discharge-plasma comparison succeeds in $75\%$ of shots with about $1.3\%$ jitter. Particle-in-cell simulations reproduce the measured energy changes and yield an accelerating field near $260$ MV/m. The approach offers tunable channel geometry and low deposited energy, motivating high-repetition-rate compact accelerator stages.

### Contributions

1. Demonstrated beam-driven wakefield acceleration in a self-generated laser-plasma filament.
2. Measured an approximately $8$ MeV witness gain over $3$ cm, corresponding to about $266$ MV/m.
3. Showed reproducible operation over 100 shots with $95\%$ acceleration success and below $0.5\%$ rms energy jitter.
4. Benchmarked the filament against a discharge plasma, finding higher success and roughly threefold lower jitter.
5. Used particle-in-cell simulations to reproduce the measured wakefield and energy-transfer behavior.
