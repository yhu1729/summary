### Summary

This experimental study investigates harmonic generation in plasma sources driven at $2.45\,\mathrm{GHz}$, where electron density is effectively constant over a microwave cycle. Directional couplers, spectrum analyzers, power meters, and resonance tracking distinguish harmonics generated by the plasma from those supplied by the amplifier. Both inductively coupled plasma (ICP) and capacitively coupled plasma (CCP) sources exhibit stronger nonlinear transport at low pressure. The ICP third harmonic reaches approximately $0.2\%$ of absorbed power, whereas the CCP second harmonic reaches approximately $4\%$. Increasing pressure suppresses harmonic generation in both sources. The authors attribute ICP nonlinearity to a time-dependent effective electron scattering frequency associated with velocity-dependent transport, and CCP nonlinearity to rectification by the sheath, the charged boundary layer next to the wall. Their observations show that nearly constant electron density does not guarantee linear microwave conductivity. The proposed mechanisms interpret the measured harmonic signatures rather than follow from direct time-resolved measurements of scattering frequency.

### Contributions

1. Measures nonlinear microwave response at pressures above $10\,\mathrm{Pa}$ despite electron density remaining effectively constant within the excitation cycle.
2. Extends resonance-based source diagnostics with forward and reflected harmonic measurements, separating amplifier contributions from plasma-generated signals.
3. Finds enhanced third-harmonic generation in the low-pressure ICP source, reaching approximately $0.2\%$ of absorbed power.
4. Observes substantially stronger second-harmonic generation in the low-pressure CCP source, reaching approximately $4\%$ of absorbed power and supporting sheath rectification as its origin.
5. Contrasts time-dependent effective scattering in ICP with sheath rectification in CCP, and relates their reduced nonlinearity at higher pressure to shorter electron mean free paths and thinner sheaths.
