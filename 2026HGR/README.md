# 2026HGR

## Claude (September 2026)

### Summary

Gyromoment (GM) models expand the local $\delta f$ gyrokinetic ion distribution in Hermite–Laguerre moments and need a closure when few moments are kept. For electrostatic flux-tube turbulence with adiabatic electrons, the authors expand the gyroaverage kernels $\hat K_n=(\tau\ell_\perp)^ne^{-\tau\ell_\perp}/n!$ for small ion-to-electron temperature ratio $\tau$, truncate at moment degree $p+2j\le2$ (density, parallel velocity, and parallel and perpendicular temperatures), and keep $\mathcal O(\tau)$ terms only in the density equation, an ad hoc mixed-order truncation. After a gyrocentre-to-particle transformation and rescaling of fields and gradients with $\tau$, this hot-electron-limit (HEL) system reproduces the Z-pinch fluid model of Ivanov et al.; an empirical factor $c_f=4$ matches the Dougherty collisionality to their Landau-based one. Gyacomo runs at $\tau=10^{-3}$ reproduce Ivanov's linear growth rates, two-dimensional heat fluxes with low-collisionality blow-up, and three-dimensional heat-flux plateaus versus parallel box length. In Cyclone Base Case $s$–$\alpha$ geometry, compared with $\tau=1$ gyrokinetic runs, the closure overpredicts peak growth rates (about 0.9 versus 0.25) and heat flux but preserves the temporal dynamics; it shows no Dimits shift and misses gyrokinetic transport suppression. The authors attribute the weak zonal-flow regulation to the missing higher-order moments.

### Contributions

1. Showed that Z-pinch ITG growth rates become independent of $\tau$ for $\tau\lesssim10^{-2}$ and coincide for 4 and 9 GMs, whereas removing $N_i^{20}$ or $N_i^{01}$ changes them, identifying the four-moment set as closed in the hot-electron limit.
2. Traced the residual linear mismatch with Ivanov's growth rates to higher-order $\tau$ terms of Gyacomo's Dougherty operator, using an eigenvalue solver of the HEL–GM system that agrees better with Ivanov at finite collisionality and matches the gyromoment runs exactly without collisions.
3. Found that lowering the collisionality by 20% from $\chi=0.2$ at $\kappa_T=1.2$ destroys the zonal flows and makes the heat flux blow up while barely changing linear growth rates, attributing the blow-up to the nonlinear saturation mechanism and noting that gyrokinetic studies report no such state.
4. Related the three-dimensional heat-flux decrease with parallel domain length to eddy self-interaction through periodic parallel boundaries and parallel decorrelation, and showed that stronger gradients excite slab ITG modes whose short parallel scales make the saturated flux sensitive to parallel resolution.
5. Compared Z-pinch and $s$–$\alpha$ geometries at identical parameters, finding lower transport in the Z-pinch despite larger growth rates for both models, and proposed that uniform curvature drives one coherent zonal-flow mode whereas $z$-dependent tokamak curvature favours competing zonal scales.

### Comments

- §2, after Eq. (2.1): the non-adiabatic part of the distribution function is defined as $h_i=g_i-\langle\phi\rangle$, whose Hermite–Laguerre moments are, by Eq. (2.6), $N_i^{pj}-\hat K_j\phi\,\delta_{p0}$, whereas Eq. (2.11) defines the non-adiabatic GMs as $n_i^{pj}=N_i^{pj}+(q_i/\tau)\hat K_j\phi\,\delta_{p0}$, which the expansions (2.20)–(2.22), including the discussed $\tau^{-1}$ term, use; the sign and the factor $q_i/\tau$ differ, and (2.11) is the form the derivation relies on.
- §2.1: the evolved wavenumbers are given as $k_x=2\pi mN_x/L_x$ and $k_y=2\pi nN_y/L_y$ with $n=1,\dots,N_y/2$; with the §3 values $L_y=150$ and $N_y=256$ the smallest printed $k_y$ would be $2\pi\cdot256/150\approx10.7$, far above the unstable range $k_y\rho_s\lesssim2.5$ of Figs. 1 and 3, and excluding $n=0$ would remove the $k_y=0$ component required by the $\bar\phi_{yz}$ term of Eq. (2.16) and by the zonal flows of Fig. 7(a); the evident intended modes are $k_x=2\pi m/L_x$ and $k_y=2\pi n/L_y$ with $n=0,\dots,N_y/2$.
- §2.2, Eqs. (2.28)–(2.29): applying Eq. (2.10), the rule that yields the printed $-C_\parallel^BT_\perp^*$ in Eq. (2.27), gives the parallel terms $\sqrt\tau[\sqrt3(\hat C_\parallel-C_\parallel^B)q_\parallel^*+\sqrt2\hat C_\parallel u_\parallel^*-\sqrt2C_\parallel^Bq_\perp^*]$ for $(p,j)=(2,0)$ and $\sqrt\tau[(\hat C_\parallel-2C_\parallel^B)q_\perp^*+C_\parallel^Bu_\parallel^*]$ for $(p,j)=(0,1)$, but the printed equations lack $-\sqrt{2\tau}\,C_\parallel^Bq_\perp^*$ and $-2\sqrt\tau\,C_\parallel^Bq_\perp^*$; these terms are discarded in Eqs. (2.32)–(2.33) and vanish in the Z-pinch, so the closed model is unaffected.
- §2.2, Eq. (2.29): Eq. (2.15) gives $\mathcal D_N^{01}=R_Nik_y\hat K_1\phi=\tau R_Nik_y\ell_\perp\phi+\mathcal O(\tau^2)$ and Eq. (2.14) gives $\mathcal D_T^{01}=R_Tik_y(2\hat K_1-2\hat K_2-\hat K_0)\phi=R_Tik_y(-1+3\tau\ell_\perp)\phi+\mathcal O(\tau^2)$, so the $\mathcal O(\tau)$ gradient factor is $(3R_T+R_N)$ rather than the printed $(R_T+3R_N)$; likewise Eq. (2.9) with $L_1^2=L_0-2L_1+2L_2$ and $\hat K_0=1-\tau\ell_\perp+\dots$ gives $\tau\{\ell_\perp\phi,\ n^*-3T_\perp^*+2P_\perp^{\perp*}\}$ rather than the printed $-2T_\perp^*$; both terms are dropped in Eq. (2.33).
- §2.2, Eq. (2.30): the polarization factor is printed as $1-2(\ell_\perp-\tau\ell_\perp^2)$, but Eq. (2.16) with $q_i=1$, $\Gamma_0=\sum_n\hat K_n^2$, and Eqs. (2.17)–(2.18) give $\sum_n\hat K_n^2=1-2\tau\ell_\perp+3\tau^2\ell_\perp^2+\mathcal O(\tau^3)$ and hence $1+2\ell_\perp-3\tau\ell_\perp^2$; the linear term has the wrong sign for $\ell_\perp=k_\perp^2/2$, the convention of the same equation's right-hand side $n^*+\tau\ell_\perp(T_\perp^*-n^*)$, and the printed $\tau\ell_\perp^2$ coefficient corresponds to keeping only $\hat K_0^2$.
- §2.3, Eq. (2.38): the gyroradius vector is defined as $\boldsymbol\rho=\sqrt{2\tau w_\perp}\,\hat{\boldsymbol b}$, parallel to the magnetic field, which contradicts the properties $\langle\boldsymbol\rho\rangle=0$ and $\langle\boldsymbol\rho\boldsymbol\rho\rangle=\tau w_\perp\mathbf I_\perp$ stated in the same paragraph; the intended vector is perpendicular to $\hat{\boldsymbol b}$ and rotates with the gyroangle.
- §3: the Gyacomo gradient is said to be scaled as "$R_T=\tau\kappa_T$", which conflicts with the rescaling $\kappa_T=\tau R_T/2$ of §2.3, with "despite an increasing temperature gradient $R_T$" in the same paragraph as $\tau$ decreases, and with §5, where $\kappa_T=3.5$ "corresponds to $R_T=7$ for $\tau=1$", as does the Fig. 13 axis label "$R_T, 2\times\kappa_T$"; the intended scaling is $R_T=2\kappa_T/\tau$.
- §3 and Fig. 5: §3 states that "Figure 5 compares the heat fluxes obtained in the HEGS with the results from Ivanov et al. (2022)", and §2.3 says $c_f$ was calibrated against Ivanov, Schekochihin & Dorland (2022) "(see figure 5)", but the Fig. 5 caption attributes the reference data to Ivanov et al. (2020), as §6 does for the $c_f=4$ calibration; the source of the two-dimensional reference heat fluxes is unresolved.
- §4 and Fig. 8: the caption gives $L_\parallel=4$ (top) and $L_\parallel=128$ (bottom), whereas the text describing the figure gives "$L_\parallel=8$" for the short domain and "$L_\parallel=32$" for the longer one; the plotted ranges $z/L_B\approx[-2,2)$ and $[-32,32)$, with $L_\parallel=2L_z$ (§3) as confirmed by Fig. 9, whose $L_\parallel=32$ panels span $z/L_B\approx[-8,8)$, correspond to $L_\parallel=8$ and $L_\parallel=128$.
- §4: the weak- and strong-turbulence comparison is introduced as "$\kappa_T=0.38$ and $\kappa_T=0.8$", but the Fig. 9 caption gives $\kappa_T=0.36$ for the weak case, the value used for the weak-turbulence runs in §3 and Fig. 8; the intended value is 0.36.
