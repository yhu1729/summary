## Claude (September 2026)

### Summary

The authors propose a deterministic particle method for the spatially homogeneous Landau equation with per-step cost $O(N+M^d\log M)$ for $N$ particles and $M$ Fourier modes per dimension at fixed transform tolerance. As background, the equation is a continuity equation with a velocity field $U[f]$, which blob methods evaluate pairwise at $O(N^2)$ cost. Writing $U[f]=-J(f,f)/f$ with $J$ the collision flux, they reconstruct the density as the Fourier truncation $P_Mf^N$ of the particle measure on the torus $[-L,L]^d$ by a type-1 nonuniform FFT; flux modes follow by FFT convolution with precomputed Landau kernel modes, and a type-2 transform evaluates flux and floored density at the particles. For smooth $f$ supported in $B(0,R)$, $L>2R$, and Sobolev index $s>d/2+1$, a consistency theorem bounds the velocity-field error on $\{f>\kappa\}$ by $C(\kappa^{-1}M^{-(s-d/2-1)}+\kappa^{-2}\|P_Mf^N-f\|_{C^1})$, separating spectral truncation from density reconstruction. In two-dimensional Maxwellian-molecule tests, relative $L^2$ errors on the BKW solution reach order $10^{-7}$, against about $10^{-2}$ for the blob method, with runtime sub-linear in $N$; errors stagnate in $N$ when $M$ is too small, and too few particles for $M$ cause instability, which Gaussian filtering removes at an accuracy cost. Only mass is conserved exactly; trajectory convergence, low-density regions, and three-dimensional Coulomb interactions remain open.

### Contributions

1. Derived the flux modes $(\hat J_M)_k=\sum_m\hat f_m\hat f_{k-m}\hat\beta(k-m,m)$ with $\hat\beta(l,m)=(l-m)D(m)-(l-m)I(m)$, where $D(m)=\frac{i\pi}{L}\int\Psi(q)e^{-i\pi m\cdot q/L}dq$ and $I_{pq}(m)$ is the same integral weighted by $\mu_p\mu_q$, with $\Psi=|q|^{\gamma+2}$ and $\mu=q/|q|$, the kernel modes of the Pareschi–Russo–Toscani spectral method; identified $P_Mf^N$ as a kernel density estimate with the unit-mass but sign-changing Dirichlet kernel $\psi_M=(2L)^{-d}\sum_ke^{i\pi k\cdot x/L}$, handled by the denominator $\max\{P_Mf^N,\underline f\}$, related it to the Fourier-in-cell approach, and found a drift–diffusion splitting based on a spectral approximation of $\nabla f/f$ less stable.
2. Proved that the periodic flux equals the whole-space flux on $[-L,L]^d$ for data supported in $B(0,R)$ when $L>2R$, the stability bounds $\|J_{\mathrm{per}}(f,g)\|_{C^0}\le C\|g\|_{C^1}\|f\|_{C^1}$ and $\|J_{\mathrm{per}}(f,g)\|_{H^s}\le C\|g\|_{H^{s+1}}\|f\|_{H^{s+1}}$ for $s>d/2$ from the integrability of $|q|^{\gamma+2}$ for $\gamma>-d-2$ and the algebra property of $H^s$, truncation errors of order $M^{-(s-d/2)}$ in $C^0$ and $M^{-(s-1-d/2)}$ in $C^1$, and the flux error $CM^{-(s-d/2-1)}\|f\|_{H^s}^2$ (Proposition 3.3), with the theorem further assuming $\|P_Mf^N-f\|_{C^0}\le\kappa/2$, $\|P_Mf^N-f\|_{H^s}\le1$, and a floor $\underline f\le\kappa/2$, which is then inactive on $\{f>\kappa\}$.
3. On the BKW solution with ring-shaped $f(0,v)=|v|^2e^{-|v|^2}/\pi$ and the collision operator scaled by $1/16$, showed at $t=0$, with $100^2$ particles on $[-10,10]^2$ and $L=10$, the error of $P_Mf^N$ falling to about $10^{-10}$ at $M=64$ and that of $U_M$ worsening as $\kappa$ decreases from $10^{-1}$ to $10^{-5}$, and at $t=8$, with particles on $[-5,5]^2$ and RK4 with $\Delta t=10^{-3}$, errors of order $10^{-5}$ for $M=48$ that do not improve beyond $N=60^2$, errors for $M=32$ that grow from $N=40^2$ to $60^2$, and errors for $M=64$ falling to order $10^{-7}$ at $N=120^2$, while $M=128$ was less accurate than $M=64$ at both $N=100^2$ and $200^2$.
4. Timed MATLAB R2026a runs on an Apple M2 MacBook Air with FINUFFT at tolerance $10^{-13}$ and floor $10^{-13}$, finding log–log runtime slopes in $N$ of 0.42, 0.44, and 0.62 for $M=32$, 48, and 64 against roughly quadratic growth for the blob method (Euler, $\Delta t=10^{-2}$, $\varepsilon=0.64h^2$), which the spectral method undercut from $N>3\cdot10^3$ despite RK4 with $\Delta t=10^{-3}$, and tracked momentum errors near $10^{-8}$ and energy errors below $10^{-5}$ for $M=64$, $N=100^2$, whereas the blob method conserves momentum exactly at the fully discrete level.
5. Stabilized the under-resolved case $M=128$, $N=80^2$, whose unfiltered error grows to about $10^{-1}$ by $t=8$, with the Gaussian filter $(\hat\psi)_k=\exp(-\pi^2\varepsilon|k|^2/(2L^2))$, tested for $\varepsilon\in\{0.1,0.3,0.64\}h^2$, which stabilized the run at a significant loss of accuracy, with $\varepsilon=0.64h^2$ matching the blob accuracy, and reproduced Trubnikov's decay $\Delta T(t)=\Delta T(0)e^{-t/2}$ from $T_1(0)=0.75$, $T_2(0)=0.5$ with final errors $5\times10^{-7}$ ($M=48$, $N=80^2$, $\Delta t=10^{-2}$), $4\times10^{-8}$ ($M=64$, $N=100^2$, $\Delta t=10^{-3}$), and $10^{-2}$ for the blob method, and the relaxation of $0.9\mathcal M(v;(0,0),1)+0.1\mathcal M(v;(3,0),0.1)$ toward the Maxwellian with mean $(0.3,0)$ and temperature 1.315 with $140^2$ particles on $[-6,6]^2$ and $M=64$.

### Comments

- Section 4 and Fig. 3: errors and diagnostics are said to use "a fixed grid made of 120 × 120 points over $[-L,L]^2$", with $L=10$, and "This grid is shared across all simulations and baseline algorithms for better comparison", but the Fig. 3 caption states "The error is computed here on a grid of 200 × 200 points over $[-10,10]^2$", and Fig. 4 reuses these runs ("same as Figure 3a"); unresolved.
- Section 4.1, "Spectral accuracy at $t=0$": the computed quantities are listed as "the truncated Fourier approximations $f^M=\mathcal P_Mf$, $J_M(f,f)$, and $U_M[f^N]$", the projections of the exact density used in the proof of Proposition 3.3, but the Fig. 2 panels are titled $f_M^N$ and $J_M(f_M^N,f_M^N)$, the caption refers to the Fourier KDE $f_M^N$ and to $J_M(\mathcal P_Mf^N,\mathcal P_Mf^N)$, the following sentences to $f_M^N$ and $J_M(f_M^N,f_M^N)$, and the preceding paragraph motivates the test by the accuracy of $\mathcal P_Mf^N$ on the particle initialization grid; the evident intended quantities are $f_M^N=\mathcal P_Mf^N$ and $J_M(f_M^N,f_M^N)$.
