### Summary

The paper tests a radio-emission mechanism in which nonpropagating Alfv\'enic magnetic perturbations carried into a relativistic, magnetized electron--positron shock convert into propagating superluminal ordinary modes downstream. A cold pair-plasma analysis derives frequency and wavenumber matching across the shock and shows that activation requires the shock-frame seed-wave frequency to exceed the downstream plasma frequency. One-dimensional particle-in-cell simulations reproduce the predicted downstream Alfv\'enic and superluminal branches, including their wavenumber jumps, dispersion, and thermally corrected cutoff. Long-wavelength perturbations remain nonpropagating, whereas sufficiently short-wavelength components generate outgoing ordinary modes. Broadband simulations therefore make the shock behave as a plasma-frequency high-pass filter, with the transmitted spectrum retaining the upstream spectral slope. Lorentz transformations connect downstream waves to observer-frame amplitudes and frequencies. The authors discuss magnetar winds and fast radio bursts, but uncertain turbulent energy at sufficiently small scales prevents a firm efficiency estimate; multidimensional, propagating-wave, and turbulence effects remain open.

### Contributions

1. Derived a frequency-matching activation criterion for Alfv\'en-to-superluminal-mode conversion at relativistic magnetized shocks.
2. Predicted downstream wavenumber and amplitude relations for nonpropagating Alfv\'enic and propagating ordinary modes.
3. Validated those relations with one-dimensional relativistic pair-plasma particle-in-cell simulations.
4. Showed that a broadband shock acts as a plasma-frequency high-pass filter while preserving the transmitted spectral slope.
5. Transformed the generated waves to observer frames and identified constraints and uncertainties for magnetar fast-radio-burst applications.
