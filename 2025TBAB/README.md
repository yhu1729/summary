### Summary

This study analyzes how the ion-to-electron temperature ratio $T_i/T_e$ affects core microturbulence and turbulent transport in DIII-D quiescent H-mode plasmas. Using the Gyrokinetic Toroidal Code with a QH-mode equilibrium, the authors perform linear and nonlinear simulations while varying $T_i$ and $T_e$ separately. The results show that reducing $T_i/T_e$ relatively destabilizes trapped-electron modes compared with ion-temperature-gradient modes, with the dominant instability changing across the scanned temperature ratios. Raising $T_e$ at fixed $T_i$ increases saturated transport levels, while lowering $T_i$ at fixed $T_e$ produces a weaker transport increase. The simulations connect these trends to changes in radial correlation length: eddy sizes grow with higher $T_e$ and shrink with lower $T_i$. The paper also tests impurity effects and finds little change in transport quantities or growth rates relative to impurity-free cases. Overall, the work identifies $T_i/T_e$ as an organizing parameter for turbulence-regime balance and transport levels in QH-mode core plasmas.

### Contributions

1. Performed linear and nonlinear gyrokinetic simulations of DIII-D QH-mode core turbulence across controlled $T_i/T_e$ variations.
2. Identified a transition in relative dominance between trapped-electron and ion-temperature-gradient modes as $T_i/T_e$ changes.
3. Distinguished transport effects caused by increasing $T_e$ from those caused by decreasing $T_i$.
4. Connected saturated transport trends to radial correlation-length changes in turbulent eddies.
5. Assessed impurity addition and found that it does not significantly alter growth rates or transport in the studied cases.
