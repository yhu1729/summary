### Summary

This computational study designs a single-stage capillary gas-cell target for laser wakefield acceleration of high-quality GeV-scale electron beams. The proposed target separates injection and acceleration: a short entrance section contains a helium--nitrogen mixture that enables ionization injection, followed by a longer pure-helium region that provides acceleration length while suppressing continuous injection and reducing energy spread. Hydrodynamic simulations optimize the capillary geometry and generate two-section gas-pressure and density profiles, including cases with longitudinal tapering in the acceleration section. These density distributions are then used directly in particle-in-cell simulations to evaluate the wakefield acceleration process and resulting beam properties. The workflow links gas-cell engineering to beam-quality outcomes, showing how tailored capillary profiles influence injection, acceleration, and energy spread. By combining hydrodynamic target modeling with PIC beam dynamics, the paper provides a design route for compact laser-plasma accelerator stages that aim for GeV energy, high brightness, and improved spectral quality.

### Contributions

1. Proposed a two-section capillary gas-cell target that separates ionization injection from the main acceleration region.

3. Coupled simulated gas-density profiles directly into particle-in-cell laser wakefield simulations.
4. Showed how limiting continuous ionization injection can reduce energy spread while maintaining GeV-scale acceleration.
5. Connected capillary geometry, pressure tailoring, and beam-quality metrics in a single computational design workflow.
