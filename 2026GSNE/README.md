### Summary

This paper tests whether nitrogen-vacancy centres in diamond can provide steady-state magnetic diagnostics in the tesla-scale fields expected inside future tokamaks. The authors build a fibre-coupled, millimetre-scale ensemble sensor using continuous-wave optically detected magnetic resonance and microwave excitation up to tens of gigahertz. They demonstrate operation at bias fields up to $1.2\,\mathrm{T}$ and compare non-degenerate orientations, which retain multiple resonances for vector information, with a near-$\langle111\rangle$ alignment optimized for single-axis sensitivity. Across $10$--$150\,\mathrm{Hz}$, measured unshielded sensitivities are approximately $240$--$600\,\mathrm{nT}/\sqrt{\mathrm{Hz}}$ for non-degenerate alignments and $110\pm20\,\mathrm{nT}/\sqrt{\mathrm{Hz}}$ near $\langle111\rangle$. The high-field noise is dominated by technical laser noise rather than an intrinsic nitrogen-vacancy limit. These sensitivities may be sufficient for tokamak control, but deployment still requires resonance tracking, high dynamic range, radiation- and temperature-qualified fibres and microwave lines, and validation under DEMO-level neutron and gamma exposure.

### Contributions

1. Demonstrated fibre-coupled ensemble nitrogen-vacancy magnetometry at fields up to $1.2\,\mathrm{T}$.
2. Measured sub-microtesla sensitivities over $10$--$150\,\mathrm{Hz}$ for two field-alignment regimes.
3. Quantified the high-field sensitivity penalty and identified laser noise as the present limit.
4. Related field alignment to resonance visibility, sensitive-axis geometry, and vector-magnetometry capability.
5. Defined tokamak-engineering requirements for tracking, dynamic range, remote delivery, temperature tolerance, and radiation qualification.
