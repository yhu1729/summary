### Summary

Slendebroek and coauthors compare positive-triangularity and negative-triangularity tokamak power-plant designs using constrained multi-objective optimization in FUSE. More than 200,000 integrated design evaluations explore capital-cost minimization versus operational reliability while enforcing net electric power, tritium breeding, power exhaust, and flattop constraints. Both shapes can reach similar cost-performance Pareto fronts, but through different risks. Positive-triangularity designs tolerate substantial pedestal degradation by adjusting current and profiles, yet their viable space is squeezed between L-H threshold requirements and divertor power-exhaust limits, favoring larger, lower-field machines. Negative-triangularity designs avoid those constraints, enabling compact high-field solutions that align with HTS magnet development but carry higher baseline capital cost. Sensitivity studies show that uncertainty in L-H threshold scaling or divertor power handling can shift which configuration is favored, making configuration choice a risk-informed design decision.

### Contributions

1. Performed a large constrained optimization study of tokamak power-plant design space.
2. Compared positive- and negative-triangularity concepts under common engineering constraints.
3. Showed that positive triangularity is limited mainly by L-H threshold and exhaust margins.
4. Identified compact high-field negative-triangularity designs enabled by relaxed constraints.
5. Quantified how physics and exhaust uncertainties can change the preferred configuration.
