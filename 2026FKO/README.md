### Summary

The authors compute finite-time optimal growth of small three-dimensional perturbations to an incompressible magnetohydrodynamic $\tanh$ shear layer with a uniform streamwise magnetic field, finite viscosity, and finite resistivity. Singular-value analysis of the linear propagator shows that magnetic tension can suppress Kelvin--Helmholtz eigenmodes while still allowing large transient energy amplification. In sufficiently long domains, the marginal case reaches gain above $10^4$, and even a strongly sub-Alfvénic case with $M_A=0.1$ reaches about $10^3$. Besides the hydrodynamic Orr and lift-up mechanisms, magnetic shear production creates a magnetic analogue of lift-up: cross-stream field perturbations generate streamwise magnetic energy. Optimal perturbations at short times are inherently three-dimensional, while stable-flow optima at longer times approach streamwise-uniform structures with finite spanwise variation. After peak growth, kinetic and magnetic energy exchange through standing Alfvén waves. The results question field-strength bounds inferred solely from Kelvin--Helmholtz stability and show that two-dimensional simulations can omit dominant transient-growth pathways. Reynolds and magnetic Reynolds numbers are fixed at 250, so their scaling remains unresolved.

### Contributions

1. Quantified finite-time optimal perturbation growth in a resistive, viscous MHD shear layer with a streamwise field.
2. Found energy gains above $10^4$ despite Kelvin--Helmholtz stability and about $10^3$ at $M_A=0.1$.
3. Identified magnetic shear production as a magnetic lift-up mechanism analogous to the dynamo omega effect.
4. Showed that dominant transient perturbations can be three-dimensional or nearly streamwise uniform, outside standard two-dimensional analyses.
5. Connected the post-growth kinetic--magnetic energy exchange to standing Alfvén waves and the quarter-Alfvén-crossing-time optimum.
