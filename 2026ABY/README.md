### Summary

This study uses nonlinear MHD–particle-in-cell simulations to examine how fusion-born alpha particles affect a helical core, the saturated $m/n=1/1$ kink or quasi-interchange state expected in low-shear ITER-scale plasmas with $q\gtrsim1$. Increasing alpha pressure enlarges the magnetic-axis displacement. At ITER-relevant central alpha pressure, $\beta_\alpha(0)\lesssim1\%$, the alpha contribution behaves approximately like additional total MHD pressure and does not notably flatten the pressure profiles, so the helical core retains omnigenity. Beyond this range, displacement continues growing until an observed limit near $\beta_\alpha(0)=3\%$ in the reference case, where bulk and alpha profiles partially flatten. The simulations also reveal a secondary resistive pressure-driven instability localized in the compressed-flux region after core formation. Its coherent spectrum of short-wavelength harmonics may promote magnetic chaos, but the authors caution that the present model cannot represent this mode adequately enough for predictive confinement conclusions.
