# 2026LG2

## Claude (September 2026)

### Summary

The authors ask when a Maxwell–Boltzmann (MB) energy distribution becomes a power law (PL), using direct-simulation Monte Carlo of spatially uniform hard-sphere gases with elastic binary collisions, relativistic kinematics, and acceptance weighted by the covariant flux factor; the code reproduces MB, Maxwell–Jüttner, and two-species equipartition equilibria. By Euler's homogeneous-function theorem a scale-free function is a PL. If one injection evolves self-similarly, $dN/dE=g(t)f(E/E_0)$ with characteristic energy $E_0(t)$, superposing steady injections yields an index independent of $f$: $\Gamma=a/b-1$ for $N_p\propto t^a$ and $E_0\propto t^b$, $\alpha/\beta-1$ for exponential growth rates, and $-1$ for linear $N_p$ with exponential $E_0$. Light particles injected steadily into a heavy background give $\Gamma=-0.5$, $0.5$, and $0$ for cross sections $\sigma\propto E^\delta$ with $\delta=0,-1,-\frac12$, as predicted from $E_0\propto t^{2/(1-2\delta)}$, and $\Gamma=-1$ once ultrarelativistic. Cascades of fast particles through a thermal bath give $\Gamma=-2.5$ (nonrelativistic) and $-2$ (ultrarelativistic). The authors conclude that far-from-equilibrium initial conditions, scale-free dynamics, and a scale-free open boundary condition suffice. The PL holds only at intermediate energies, away from the initial, final, and rest-mass scales; the self-similar shapes and the nonrelativistic cascade index are not derived analytically.

### Contributions

1. Showed that mixed time dependences, an exponential $N_p$ with a PL $E_0$ or a PL $N_p$ with $a\neq1$ and an exponential $E_0$, give no PL, so a self-similar distribution need not superpose into a scale-free one.
2. Derived from light–heavy reflection kinematics a constant absolute gain $\Delta E_{kl}\simeq2m_lv_h^2$ per scattering in the nonrelativistic limit and a constant fractional gain $2\gamma_h^2\beta_h^2$ in the relativistic limit, implying $E_0\propto t^2$ and exponential growth, and estimated about 230 scatterings per particle for equipartition at mass ratio 1836, against 480 in the simulation.
3. Found self-similar shapes $f(x)\propto\sqrt{x}\exp(-x^\beta)$ with fitted $\beta=1.4\pm0.1$ and $0.98\pm0.06$ for $\delta=-1$ and $-\frac12$ and $\beta=\frac12$ for $\delta=0$, suggesting $\beta\simeq-\delta+\frac12$, so a steeper decline of the cross section steepens the cutoff while its amplitude only rescales time.
4. Kept the exponentially growing cascade tractable by randomly subsampling the tracked population, for example to $10^4$ particles whenever it reaches $10^5$, while following the true population size, which reaches about $10^{20}$, and showed that energy conservation gives the self-similar scaling $(E_k,F)\to(sE_k,F/s^2)$ and, through $\alpha+\beta=0$, the ultrarelativistic index $-2$.
5. Showed in Appendix C that energy injection into a finite thermal gas yields an MB core, shifted to higher temperature at high injection, with a sharp transition to a $\Gamma\simeq-2.5$ tail rather than a κ distribution, and in Appendix B that relativistic light and nonrelativistic heavy particles in equilibrium share $T$ but differ in $\langle E_k\rangle$ by up to a factor of 2.

### Comments

- §IV A and Figs. 3 and 5: the constant-cross-section acceleration run uses light ($m=1$) and heavy ($M=10^6$) particles with "the same initial $\gamma\beta=0.001$", which gives mean kinetic energies $5\times10^{-7}$ and $0.5$ (Fig. 2, with $m_1=1$ and $\gamma\beta=10^{-3}$, indeed places the light particles at $5\times10^{-7}$), but the initial light-particle MB in Figs. 3 and 5 peaks near $2\times10^{-9}$ and the background MB lies near $10^{-3}$–$10^{-2}$, as the text also states ("At $E_k<10^{-8}$ the DF is set by the initial MB", PL at $10^{-9}\ll E_k\ll10^{-3}$); these energies correspond to $\gamma\beta\approx10^{-4}$ (means $5\times10^{-9}$ and $5\times10^{-3}$), not $10^{-3}$; which value was used is unresolved.
- §II D, Eqs. (38)–(39): substituting $E_0=E/x$ into the factor $E_0^{-\alpha-3/2}$ of Eq. (37) gives $(E/x)^{-\alpha-3/2}$, not the printed $(Ex)^{-\alpha-3/2}$, so the integrand of Eq. (39) should be $x^{\alpha-1/2}e^{-x}$ rather than $x^{-\alpha-7/2}e^{-x}$; the printed integrand diverges as $x\to0$ for every $\alpha>-5/2$, contradicting the statement that the integral "converges to a definite integral at $0-\infty$", whereas the corrected one converges for $\alpha>-\frac12$; the $E^{-1/2}$ scaling of Eq. (40) is unaffected.
- §IV A 4, Eq. (51), and Fig. 9(b): the exponential self-similarity variable is defined as $x=\exp(E/E_0)$, as also in §II B and §VI, so $x\ge1$ for $E,E_0>0$, but Fig. 9(b), described as the three DFs "transformed according to Eq. (51)", plots $f(x)$ over $10^{-10}\lesssim x\lesssim30$, a range the printed transformation cannot produce; which variable is plotted is unresolved.
- Fig. 10 caption: the thermal background is given as "$\langle E_k\rangle=5\times10^{-8}$", but §IV B 1 gives $\langle E_k\rangle=5\times10^{-9}$, §IV B 2 repeats $5\times10^{-9}$ "as in the nonrelativistic simulation", and the "Background energy" marker in Fig. 10 lies near $5\times10^{-9}$; the intended value is $5\times10^{-9}$.
- Fig. 10 caption: the nonrelativistic cascade is said to demonstrate "the formation of a PL DF when the system dynamics leads to $N_p(t)\propto e^{\alpha t}$ and $E_0(t)\propto e^{\beta t}$", but §IV B assigns this exponential evolution to the relativistic regime, the Fig. 11 caption states that the growth "switches to a PL growth in the nonrelativistic regime", the Fig. 10 legend values $\langle E_k\rangle=5.4\times10^{-2}$, $6.2\times10^{-3}$, and $5.0\times10^{-4}$ at $t=5$, 20, and 75 give logarithmic decay rates 0.144 and 0.046 rather than a constant rate, and exponential evolution with conserved energy gives $\Gamma=-2$ by the §IV B 2 argument with Eq. (19), not the plotted $-2.5$; the exponential description applies only to the ultrarelativistic cascade of Fig. 11.
- Appendix B: at $T=10^{11}$ K, with "$kT\simeq10$ MeV", the electrons are said to be relativistic "with a mean $\gamma\simeq20$", but the same appendix states that "in MJ $\langle E_k\rangle=3\times k_BT$" [Eq. (48)], which gives a mean $\gamma\approx59$ for $kT=10$ MeV and 51 for the exact $kT=8.6$ MeV; the quoted 20 is closer to $kT/m_ec^2\approx17$–20; unresolved.
- Appendix C: the injection rate is $Q=ne_i/\Delta t$ with $n=10$ and $e_i=1$, and "$\Delta t$ in the simulations is varied between $10^{-2}$ and 1, always shorter than $t_{\rm th}$" ($t_{\rm th}\sim10$), which gives $10\le Q\le10^3$, but the simulations and Fig. 13(a) include $Q=1$, which requires $\Delta t=10\sim t_{\rm th}$ for the stated $n$ and $e_i$; unresolved.
