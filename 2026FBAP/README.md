### Summary

High-resolution two-dimensional particle-in-cell simulations examine how the nonlinear collisionless Kelvin--Helmholtz instability mixes plasmas across a magnetized shear layer. Particle-origin labels directly measure interpenetration, while a complementary density-based tracer derived from labeled-population densities localizes the same process. Mixing rises during vortex merging but remains inefficient and spatially localized: values near $30\%$ occur only in narrow interfaces, detached parcels, and reconnection regions. Ions mix more than electrons because their weaker magnetization permits more cross-field motion; electrons remain largely tied to magnetic field lines. The growth of mixed regions coincides spatially and temporally with current intensification and clusters of magnetic X-points, quantitatively associating localized reconnection with transport. Larger-domain tests preserve this localization. The conclusions are limited to a symmetric, two-dimensional configuration with reduced ion-to-electron mass ratio and periodic boundaries; realistic asymmetry and three-dimensional secondary instabilities may increase cumulative transport.

### Contributions

1. Combined particle-origin labeling with a density-based tracer to quantify genuine plasma interpenetration.
2. Demonstrated that nonlinear Kelvin--Helmholtz mixing remains confined to narrow interfaces and detached plasma parcels.
3. Quantified species-dependent transport, finding stronger ion mixing and tightly constrained electron mixing.
4. Linked enhanced mixing to the temporal growth and spatial clustering of magnetic-reconnection signatures.
5. Tested domain-size sensitivity and identified dimensionality, mass-ratio, symmetry, and boundary-condition limitations.
