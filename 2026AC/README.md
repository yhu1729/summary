## Claude (September 2026)

### Summary

The authors propose low-rank Anderson acceleration (lrAA) for nonlinear matrix equations $G(X)=X$, $X\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}$, from local-stencil discretizations of two-dimensional PDEs with approximately low-rank solutions, targeting $O(m+n)$ rather than $O(mn)$ cost and storage. As background, Anderson acceleration (AA) combines the previous $\min(k,\hat m)+1$ iterates and residuals with weights minimizing a linearized residual. lrAA keeps iterates, function values, and differences as truncated-SVD factors, solves the least-squares problem on them, and schedules the truncation tolerance as $\epsilon_G=\theta\rho_k$, with $\rho_k$ an approximate residual norm, so ranks grow gradually. Nonlinear terms are compressed by Cross-DEIM, a new adaptive cross approximation (reconstruction from selected rows and columns) alternating discrete-empirical-interpolation index selection with stabilized cross approximation until both the update and an error indicator meet the tolerance. No convergence analysis is given. Tests cover matrix approximation, Laplace's equation, and Bratu, Monge–Ampère, Allen–Cahn, and level-set advection problems; for Laplace, unpreconditioned Bratu, and Monge–Ampère, intermediate ranks stay near the converged rank, and for Laplace lrAA needs fewer iterations than full-rank AA. The authors note that Cross-DEIM can fail on adversarial matrices and its cost depends on initialization under slow singular-value decay, that accurate least squares need a tiny rounding tolerance, and that finer meshes show some rank overshoot.

### Contributions

1. Designed Cross-DEIM (Algorithm 3.1) to merge new QDEIM (pivoted-QR DEIM of Drmač and Gugercin) row and column indices from the current singular vectors into the previous index sets, add one random index to a set that fails to grow, drop rows and columns whose pivoted-QR diagonal entries fall below $10^{-12}$, stop once the change between consecutive approximations and the indicator $\min(\eta_1(1+\eta_2),\eta_2(1+\eta_1))S_{\min}$, built on estimates of Donello et al. with $S_{\min}$ the smallest current singular value, both fall below $\epsilon$, and truncate to the smallest rank whose discarded squared singular values sum below $\epsilon^2$; its stabilized cross step (Algorithm 3.3), a version of that of Donello et al., solves $Q(\mathcal{I},:)W=R$ with $Q$ from a pivoted QR of $C=G(:,\mathcal{J})$, or the transposed system, and then takes the SVD of $W$.
2. Solved the lrAA least-squares problem (Algorithm 2.4) through column-pivoted QR factorizations of the stacked factors $[U_1,\dots,U_s]$ and $[V_1,\dots,V_s]$ of the residual differences with right-hand side $\operatorname{vec}(Q_1^TU_BS_BV_B^TQ_2)$, rounded residual differences at $\epsilon_F=10^{-12}$ because larger values caused ill-conditioning and more iterations, recommended windows $\hat m$ of 3–5 because larger ones raise both iteration counts and least-squares cost, and replaced the rounding of the AA update by Cross-DEIM warm-started with the singular vectors of $X_k$, with every operation other than Cross-DEIM costing $O((m+n)r^2)$.
3. Benchmarked Cross-DEIM against the truncated SVD and adaptive cross approximation (ACA) with partial pivoting: over 100 random rank-one initializations for the $500\times500$ matrix $(|x_i+y_j|/2)^5$, sampled from a $C^4$ function with a nonsmooth feature along $x+y=0$, ACA missed tolerances below about $10^{-3}$ and departed from the SVD above rank 10 while Cross-DEIM stayed close to the SVD, and for solid-body-rotated anisotropic Gaussian and $(|x+y|/2)^5$ matrices of size $500\times500$ at tolerance $10^{-2}$, warm starts roughly halved the Cross-DEIM iterations and maximum intermediate ranks.
4. For Laplace's equation with $m=n=31$ and $\hat m=5$, $\theta=1$ ended at rank 5 against rank 10 of the truncated direct solution, $\theta=0.5$ raised the rank stepwise to 10 in about 330 iterations, and a fixed tolerance $10^{-10}$ drove the rank to nearly full early and needed about 520 iterations; at $m=n=63$ lrAA took about a quarter of the iterations of full-rank AA, and an exponential-sum preconditioner acting only on the factors gave convergence on $1023^2$ and $4096^2$ grids in about ten iterations.
5. Solved the Monge–Ampère equation with $f=1/\sqrt{x^2+y^2}$ and exact solution $u=\frac{2\sqrt2}{3}(x^2+y^2)^{3/4}$ through the relaxed map $G=X+0.9(H-X)$ built on Method 1 of Benamou et al. with $\theta=0.25$: on 21, 61, 101, and 221 points per direction lrAA needed 109, 287, 443, and 675 iterations (final ranks 13, 18, 20, and 26) at tolerance $10^{-10}$, against 1083 to 107388 Gauss–Seidel iterations reported by Benamou et al. at the stricter $10^{-14}$, and 7 to 13 iterations with ranks 4 to 8 at the truncation-error-guided tolerance $0.01h_x$.

### Comments

- Section 2.2: "Lines 9 to 11 find the solution to the least squares minimization problem using Algorithm 2.4 and update the solution as a linear combination of previous $G_k$. The rounding of sums of low-rank matrices used in line 9 is described in Algorithm 2.3", but in Algorithm 2.2 line 9 is $\hat m_k=\min(\hat m,k)$, the rounding $\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{round}}_{\epsilon_F,r_{\max}}$ is applied in line 10, and the update is line 12, while the section's references to lines 4, 7, 12, and 13 match the box; both references are evidently shifted by one line.
- Sections 2.2, 4.2.1, and 4.2.2: Section 2.2 states "In the computations in this paper we take $\theta\approx0.1-0.5$", and Section 4.2.1 finds $0.1\le\theta\le0.5$ effective while $\theta=1$ "does not lead to a robust scheduling", but the Bratu parameters in Section 4.2.2 are "TOL $=10^{-6}$, $\hat m=5$, $\theta=0.9$"; unresolved.
- Algorithms 2.3, 2.4, and 3.3: with $[Q_1,R_1,\Pi_1]=\mathsf{qr}(U)$ and $[Q_2,R_2,\Pi_2]=\mathsf{qr}(V)$ in both 2.3 and 2.4, Algorithm 2.3 truncates the core $R_1\Pi_1S\Pi_2^TR_2^T$ while Algorithm 2.4 uses $a_k=\operatorname{vec}(R_1\Pi_1^TD_k\Pi_2R_2^T)$, which differ for general permutations; the factorization $CP_{\mathrm{C}}=QR_{\mathrm{C}}$ in line 4 of Algorithm 3.3 gives $U=Q_1R_1\Pi_1^T$ and makes Algorithm 2.4 consistent, but line 16 of the same algorithm, $r_{\mathrm{C}}=\operatorname{diag}(P_{\mathrm{C}}^TR_{\mathrm{C}}P_{\mathrm{C}})$, follows the opposite orientation, mapping pivoted diagonal entries back to the original columns only for involutive permutations; the intended convention is unresolved.
- Section 3: "for lrAA applications tested in this paper, we observe that the range of the Cross-DEIM iteration number is from 2 to 4", but the per-step averages in Fig. 18 (level-set advection) are about 5, and up to about 6, for the nonlinear evaluation on the 256- and 512-point meshes, and those in Fig. 16 (Allen–Cahn) fall to about 1.3–1.6 for the rounding in early steps, so individual Cross-DEIM counts outside 2–4 occur.
- Algorithm 3.1, lines 26–30: the loop that removes redundant columns runs "for $l=1,2,\dots,|\mathcal{I}_k|$" while testing $(r_{\mathrm{C}})_l$ and removing "element $l$ from $\mathcal{J}_k$" ("Remove redundant columns in $C=G(:,\mathcal{J}_k)$"), although $r_{\mathrm{C}}=\operatorname{diag}(P_{\mathrm{C}}^TR_{\mathrm{C}}P_{\mathrm{C}})$ of Algorithm 3.3 has one entry per column of $C$; the evident intended bound is $|\mathcal{J}_k|$, mirroring the row loop of lines 21–25.
- Algorithm 3.3: line 1 gives $\mathcal{I},\mathcal{J}$ "with $k$ and $l$ elements, respectively", but line 3 gives $C=G(:,\mathcal{J})\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times k}$ and $R=G(\mathcal{I},:)\in\mathbb{R}^{l\times n}$, which require $|\mathcal{J}|=k$ and $|\mathcal{I}|=l$; only this reading makes "if $k\le l$ then Solve $Q(\mathcal{I},:)W=R$" a square or overdetermined solve (and likewise the else branch with $Z(\mathcal{J},:)$), so line 1 evidently has $k$ and $l$ swapped.
- Section 4.2.1: the Fig. 8 computations are described as "computations with $n=m=32$", but the Fig. 8 caption states "Here all results are for $n=m=31$", and the scheduled run in Fig. 8 reproduces the rank history and the roughly 330 iterations of the $\theta=0.5$, $m=n=31$ panel of Fig. 5; the evident intended value is 31.
- Section 4.2.2: the cumulative average of the Cross-DEIM maximum intermediate rank is said to be "on par with the final rank (that is 10) for the unpreconditioned case", but in Fig. 11 the unpreconditioned rank history ends at 11 and the singular-value panel shows eleven singular values of the unpreconditioned lrAA solution; unresolved.

(4.3): "$2a_2=u_{i,j+1}+u_{i,j+1}$" repeats $u_{i,j+1}$ and omits $u_{i,j-1}$, so $2(a_2-u_{i,j})/h^2$ would not approximate $u_{yy}$, unlike the symmetric $2a_1=u_{i+1,j}+u_{i-1,j}$ and the "second order finite difference scheme" invoked for the tolerance $0.01h_x$; the evident intended definition is $2a_2=u_{i,j+1}+u_{i,j-1}$.

(4.3): the radicand $(a_1-a_2)^2+\frac14(a_3-a_4)^2-h^4f_{i,j}$ has the wrong sign on $h^4f_{i,j}$ for the stated equation $u_{xx}u_{yy}-u_{xy}^2=f$: with $u_{xx}\approx2(a_1-u_{i,j})/h^2$, $u_{yy}\approx2(a_2-u_{i,j})/h^2$, and $u_{xy}\approx(a_3-a_4)/(2h^2)$, the smaller root of $4(a_1-u)(a_2-u)-\frac14(a_3-a_4)^2=h^4f_{i,j}$ has radicand $(a_1-a_2)^2+\frac14(a_3-a_4)^2+h^4f_{i,j}$, and for $u=(x^2+y^2)/2$, where $f=1$, the printed radicand is $-h^4<0$ whereas $+h^4f_{i,j}$ returns $u$ exactly; the evident intended sign is $+$.
