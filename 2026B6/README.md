## Claude (October 2026)

### Summary

Boozer argues that tokamak analysis should use the toroidal flux $\psi_t$ enclosed by a magnetic surface as radial coordinate rather than the internal poloidal flux $\psi_p^{in} = \Psi_p^{ax}(t) - \psi_p$, because Faraday's law fixes the slippage of poloidal relative to toroidal flux, $\partial\psi_p(\psi_t, t)/\partial t = V_\ell$, and because a divertor separatrix makes poloidal-flux definitions of the edge safety factor $q_{95}$ and internal inductance $\ell_i$ depend on the current profile. In axisymmetry $q = \sigma G/I$, with $I$ and $G$ the toroidal and poloidal currents and $\sigma(\psi_t)$ a shape function of the surface geometry. A two-wire model, the plasma current and an equal divertor-coil current at $y = \pm a$ in a slab periodic in $z = R\varphi$, is solved with Jacobi elliptic functions: the surfaces are $k^2 = r^2r_e^2/a^4$, $\sigma = k^2K(k)a^2/(2\pi R^2)$, $\psi_t = a^2B_\varphi[E(k) - (1 - k^2)K(k)]$, and $\sigma = (a^2/\pi R^2)(\psi_t/\Psi_t)\{1 + \Pi_\sigma\}$ with $\Pi_\sigma$ rising from zero to about 0.7 near the separatrix. For a flat current channel, 95% of the poloidal flux lies at 81% to 89% of the toroidal flux depending on channel width, which a fixed toroidal-flux fraction such as 85% would remove. The optimal edge fraction is left open.

### Contributions

1. Derived from the contravariant and covariant forms (14)–(15) of $\vec{B}$ in $(\psi_t, \theta, \varphi)$ coordinates, and from their generalizations (17)–(18) to angles $\theta_*$, $\varphi_*$ with $\varphi = \varphi_* + \nu$, that $\iota\sigma = I/G$ and hence $q = \sigma G/I$ (28)–(29), where $\sigma$ is the ratio of the $J_*^{-1}$-weighted surface averages of $(\partial\vec{x}/\partial\theta_*)^2$ and $(\partial\vec{x}/\partial\varphi_*)^2$ (27); for two-to-one elliptical surfaces with triangular shaping (33) this gives $\sigma = 2.5r^2/R^2 + 0.36r^4/(a^2R^2)$ against $r^2/R^2$ for circular surfaces (36).
2. Built the two-wire divertor model with poloidal flux $\psi_p^{in} = (\mu_0RI_p/2)\ln k^2$, $k^2 = (r_x/a)^4 + 2(r_x/a)^2\cos(2\theta_x) + 1$ in cylindrical coordinates about the X-point (46)–(50), regularized the logarithmic singularity at the axis by a flat current channel of radius $r_I$, giving $\psi_p^{in}(k) = \mu_0RI_p\ln(\sqrt{e}k/k_I)$ for $k \ge k_I = 2r_I/a$ (55), (57), and located the $q_{95}$ surface at $k_{95p} = (k_I/\sqrt{e})^{0.05}$ (58).
3. Parametrized the surfaces by $\cos(2\theta_x) = -\mathrm{dn}(u)$, $r_x^2/a^2 = \mathrm{dn}(u) + k\,\mathrm{cn}(u)$, $\theta_* = 2\pi u/4K(k)$ (60)–(66), showed $(\partial\vec{x}/\partial u)^2 = k^2r_x^2/4$ (81), and obtained $\sigma(k) = k^2K(k)a^2/(2\pi R^2)$ (84), so that $\sigma/\sigma_c = 2K(k)/\pi$ relative to circular surfaces (86) and $\iota = \mu_0RI_p/(B_\varphi a^2k^2K)$ (89), which reproduces $\mu_0RI_p/(2\pi B_\varphi r^2)$ as $k \to 0$.
4. Integrated $d\psi_p^{in}/\iota$ to $\psi_t(k) = a^2B_\varphi[E(k) - (1 - k^2)K(k)]$ (94), so $\Psi_t = a^2B_\varphi$ is the flux inside the separatrix, and wrote $\Pi_\sigma = \tfrac{1}{2}k^2K/[E - (1 - k^2)K] - 1$ (96), with the fit $\Pi_\sigma = 0.2019x^{1.1426}/(1 - x)^{0.4176}$, $x = \psi_t/\Psi_t$ (97), stated to be accurate to within 4%.
5. Using $(\psi_t/\Psi_t)^{(I)} = \pi k_I^2/4$ for the channel width, found that the 95% poloidal-flux surface sits at $\psi_t^{p95}/\Psi_t$ between about 0.81 and 0.89 (Fig. 6), so $(\psi_t^{p95}/\Psi_t)\{1 + \Pi_\sigma\}$ varies between about 1.05 and 1.33 with the current profile, leaving $\sigma$, $q_{95}$, and $\ell_i = \int_0^1 I(s)q(1)/(I(1)q(s))\,ds$ (11) correspondingly uncertain; proposed fixing the edge at a toroidal-flux fraction such as 0.85, noted that the pedestal bootstrap current of a separatrix-bounded plasma lowers $\ell_i$ at fixed edge $q$, and suggested data-driven and linear tearing and kink stability studies to identify disruption-avoidance parameters.
