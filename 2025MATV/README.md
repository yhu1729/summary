### Summary

This paper reports laser wakefield acceleration experiments using a novel non-symmetric, 3D-printed gas nozzle to improve relativistic electron-beam charge and energy. The target is designed to shape the gas-density profile so that ionization injection and downramp injection reinforce each other. Measurements over backing-pressure scans show a tunable source whose charge per energy per shot increases by at least a factor of ten and whose maximum electron energy doubles relative to the authors' earlier symmetric-nozzle experiments. Particle-in-cell simulations support the interpretation that the non-symmetric profile lowers the injection threshold and promotes additional trapping. The optimal operating range is about 45--55 bar, where higher-energy, more beam-like electrons are obtained, although beam divergence remains relatively large. Lower-pressure bunches still fall in the 6--25 MeV range used by conventional clinical linear accelerators, making them relevant for comparative radiobiology. The work positions tailored gas-target geometry as a practical route toward high-dose very-high-energy electron sources for radiotherapy studies.

### Contributions

1. Demonstrated a non-symmetric gas nozzle for increasing laser wakefield electron charge and energy.
2. Measured pressure-dependent beam energy, charge, divergence, and dose profiles for the new target.
3. Achieved at least tenfold charge-per-energy enhancement and a twofold maximum-energy increase relative to symmetric nozzles.
4. Used particle-in-cell simulations to identify combined ionization and downramp injection mechanisms.
5. Assessed the source's relevance to high-dose and radiotherapy-oriented very-high-energy electron applications.
