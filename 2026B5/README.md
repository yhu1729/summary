### Summary

The Pfirsch-Schlüter current flows along magnetic field lines to keep the current density divergence free in a toroidal plasma with a pressure gradient. It can distort magnetic surfaces and limit the pressure attainable in a stellarator. Boozer derives a condition for confining this current to a half or whole stellarator period: $v_s=\int_0^{\ell_s} d\ell/B$ must be the same for every field line on a magnetic surface, where $\ell_s$ is its length through the segment. The integral also measures flux-tube volume per unit magnetic flux. Because $v_s=\sigma_s\ell_s^2/I_s$ and $\sigma_s$ varies weakly between field lines, equal segment lengths closely approximate the exact condition. Using magnetic-field profiles from a published quasi-isodynamic design, the paper estimates accuracy of about four parts per thousand for a half period and two parts per thousand for a full period. It connects equal lengths to stronger inboard helical shaping, achievable with modular coils or potentially a helical central column.
