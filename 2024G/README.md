### Summary

Plasma kinetic theory is reformulated using exterior calculus, which describes geometric quantities independently of coordinates. Distribution functions become differential forms transported by Hamiltonian flow; their Liouville equation is expressed with a Lie derivative. Integrating out particles yields a geometric BBGKY hierarchy coupling successive reduced distributions. Canonical-coordinate expressions and closure assumptions recover Vlasov, Boltzmann, master, Fokker–Planck, fluid, and magnetohydrodynamic descriptions. The collisional reductions distinguish a thermal bath assumption from the additional small-jump approximation needed for diffusion in action space. A generalized variational reaction-rate construction bounds steady flux through surfaces invariant under the unperturbed Hamiltonian motion, using an assumed upper bound on the distribution. Applied to three-body recombination in a strong magnetic field, it separates axial bounce motion from perpendicular drift and estimates their contributions for a stationary ion. Finite ion mass and motion motivate a two-bottleneck scaling model, with field ionization suppressing recombination in specified parameter regimes relevant to antihydrogen formation. The framework supplies geometric interpretation and rate estimates; its weak-correlation closures and approximate finite-mass model do not establish a general strongly coupled plasma theory.
