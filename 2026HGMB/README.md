## Claude (September 2026)

### Summary

The authors present SpCCL, a fork of the NCCLX GPU collective library whose all-gather, reduce-scatter, and all-reduce send only the nonzeros of unstructured sparse payloads, mainly at 1–99% sparsity, where reductions cause fill-in (densification of partial sums). As background, flat-index COO doubles the bytes per nonzero and reaches dense size at 50% sparsity, whereas a bitvector costs 3.125% of fp32 data. Their format, Pici, adds to a bitvector one prefix sum per $64\times64$ virtual tile, about 3.15% overhead in total, with warp-per-tile CUDA kernels that derive column offsets from popcounts and warp shuffles. The ring collectives compress once in all-gather and at every reduce-scatter step, and send dense data once the measured or extrapolated sparsity drops to empirically tuned thresholds that differ between intra- and inter-node links and for the all-gather phase of all-reduce. On Perlmutter A100 GPUs, at 99% sparsity, speedups over NCCL reach 5.25× (all-gather), 2.5× (reduce-scatter), and 2.66× (all-reduce), SparCML is uncompetitive even with dense NCCL, and gradient-pruned data-parallel training is up to 26% faster end to end. The authors note slowdowns for small, near-latency-bound slices, reduced computation–communication overlap from using more channels, offline threshold tuning, and an application study that does not exhaust sparsification strategies.

### Contributions

1. Laid out Pici as a bitvector $B$ whose 64-bit words each hold one column of a row-major $64\times64$ tile, an index array $I$ of exclusive per-tile prefix sums ($k=4096$) closed by the total nonzero count, and values $A$ ordered by tile, column, and row, an index overhead of about 0.78% of the bitvector against 3.125% for the rank structures poppy and pasta and 50% for Coruscant; in compression each lane of a 32-thread warp scans columns $l$ and $l+32$ for coalesced loads and the tile count is reduced in five shuffles, a block-local prefix sum suffices because each CUDA block compresses an independent segment, and compaction gives each lane the adjacent columns $2l$ and $2l+1$, with popcounts, a warp exclusive prefix sum, and find-first-set bit searches that decompression and a scatter-add variant reuse.
2. Extended the Hockney model $T=\alpha+n\beta$ for ring all-gather to $T=(p-1)(\alpha+\beta n'/p)+T_\mathrm{comp}(n/p)+T_\mathrm{decomp}(n'(p-1)/p)$ with $n'=n(1-\sigma+\omega)$ and $\omega=0.0315$, extrapolated post-reduction sparsity as $\sigma_\mathrm{next}=\sigma_\mathrm{prev}\sigma_0$ for independent uniform nonzero positions, also across dense-send steps and recalibrated at each compression, and, from threshold sweeps in steps of 0.1, switched to dense at sparsity $\le0.6$ intra-node, $\le0.5$ inter-node, and $\le0.1$ at the all-gather phase of all-reduce for Pici (0.8, 0.7, and 0.5 for COO), separating link types because a node's four 25 GB/s Slingshot 11 NICs only match the four 25 GB/s NVLink links of one GPU pair.
3. Compressed and decompressed at slice granularity inside NCCLX's `genericOp` for the Simple protocol, prepending a 48-byte header with format, size, and component offsets, enlarged the FIFO buffers to hold a worst-case compressed slice, reused the application send buffer as accumulation scratch and the send FIFO as a staging buffer so that scatter-adds of intra-node data read local memory instead of making uncoalesced reads over NVLink, and raised the channel count, 64 channels being best in 72.5% of sparse all-gather cases and 32 otherwise; in an all-reduce ablation at 16 GPUs, 512 MiB, and 95% sparsity, going from 8 to 64 channels gave 3.80×, reduce-scatter thresholds 1.19×, the all-gather threshold 1.08×, and Pici instead of COO 1.37×, only the final configuration beating NCCLX.
4. Showed on 16 GPUs with 2048 MiB buffers that Pici all-reduce is 1.19× faster than COO at 99% sparsity and 1.33× at 95%, where COO is slower than dense, while COO all-gather stays competitive down to about 90% sparsity owing to its simpler decompression, and found all-gather speedups at a fixed 32 MiB per-GPU input to grow with GPU count, 3.22×, 3.94×, 4.51×, and 4.88× at 99% sparsity on 8 to 64 GPUs, whereas at 256 MiB on 64 GPUs all-reduce and reduce-scatter drop to 0.81× and 0.77×, which the authors attribute to dense 64 KiB slices near the Hockney crossover $\alpha/\beta\approx60.8$ KiB for 2.49 µs latency and 25 GB/s.
5. Pruned data-parallel gradients in Megatron-LM to 99% sparsity by sampling-based top-$k$, estimating the threshold from 0.1% of the gradients, with leaky error feedback in a Triton kernel, for a GPT-2 XL-based 1.5B model on 40 GB nodes and a StarCoder2-3B-based 3.3B model cut to 28 layers on 80 GB nodes, trained on WikiText-103 with sequence length 512 and micro-batch 2; SpCCL was at least 13% faster on eight 40 GB GPUs and 22% on 64 80 GB GPUs, peaking at 16% and 26% on 16 GPUs, and on 32 GPUs over 80 iterations all-reduce was over 2× faster without error feedback, pruning cost 1.5 s and 3 s, all-reduce time fell by over 40% in every case, and error feedback added overhead to both pruning and all-reduce.
