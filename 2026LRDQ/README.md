# 2026LRDQ

## Claude (September 2026)

### Summary

The authors measure carbon-ion stopping in dense plasma at strong ion–plasma coupling, where linear-response and binary-collision theories are expected to fail. Coupling is quantified by $\mathcal{Z}=Z_{eff}/(n_0\lambda_D^3)$, the effective ion charge over the electron number in a Debye sphere; as background, weak coupling ($\mathcal{Z}\ll1$) gives $dE/dx\propto Z_{eff}^2$. Laser-accelerated ions pass a magnetic dipole with slits that selects quasi-monoenergetic pulses of equal momentum-to-charge ratio, separated by time of flight. Incident 7.00 MeV (583 keV/u) C$^{5+}$ ions cross a 1 mm foam heated by hohlraum X-rays to $T_e=17\pm1$ eV and $n_e=(4.0\pm0.3)\times10^{20}$ cm$^{-3}$, while earlier C$^{6+}$ ions probe the cold foam, where their 1.51 MeV loss agrees with SRIM. A Thomson parabola spectrometer resolves exit energy and charge state, so $Z_{eff}$ need not be assumed. The plasma loss is 1.92 MeV at an estimated $\mathcal{Z}\approx3.8$. Hybrid charge-state and stopping simulations show that T-Matrix, Bethe–Bloch, Vlasov, Brown–Preston–Singleton, Li–Petrasso, and Zwicknagel's combined models overestimate the loss and underestimate the mean charge, whereas classical molecular dynamics with quantum corrections agrees within uncertainties. Only C$^{6+}$ and C$^{5+}$ are analysed, as later ions meet expanded plasma of unknown state; the authors call for slower, more highly charged ions and self-consistent first-principles calculations.

### Contributions

1. Drove target normal sheath acceleration with a 131 J, 759 fs laser focused to a 20 µm spot on a 15 µm CH-coated copper foil, trimmed the spectrum with a 0.28 T dipole between 500 µm entrance and exit slits, and heated a porous tricellulose-acetate foam (C$_9$H$_{16}$O$_8$, 2 mg/cm$^3$, 1 mm) through a gold hohlraum irradiated by a 147 J, 2 ns beam, so that on the Fig. 2 time-of-flight axis C$^{6+}$ reaches the foam before the ns-laser trigger, C$^{5+}$ inside the 10 ns window before significant expansion with an interaction time of about 0.26 ns, and the 4.49 MeV C$^{4+}$ and 2.47 MeV C$^{3+}$ pulses after expansion.
2. Converted the magnetic (X) and electric (Y) Thomson-parabola deflections recorded on CR39 into spectra fitted by Gaussians, counting only the unsaturated high- and low-energy tails of C$^{5+}$ and C$^{4+}$, whose central tracks saturate the detector, and found that C$^{6+}$ leaves the cold foam as C$^{5+}$ and C$^{6+}$ centred at 8.45 MeV while C$^{5+}$ leaves the plasma spread over C$^{3+}$–C$^{6+}$ centred at 5.08 MeV, with an energy-loss error bar of about ±0.4 MeV in Fig. 4(a) from statistics, fitting, and spectrometer resolution.
3. Solved charge-state rate equations with Coulomb ionization by bound and free target electrons, capture of bound and free electrons, three-body recombination, and excited states through target-density-effect modifications, coupled to each free-electron stopping model with bound-electron stopping from the Bethe–Bloch terms, over 20 loops across the target; charge equilibrium is reached within about 0.01 mm in every step, so $Z_{eff}(x)=Z_{eq}(v(x))$, C$^{1+}$–C$^{3+}$ fractions stay about seven orders of magnitude lower, and models with stronger stopping give lower mean charges because slower ions favour recombination.
4. For a representative 6 MeV C$^{5+}$ ion, the mean of incident and exit energies, evaluated the Brown–Preston–Singleton projectile–plasma coupling $g^2=\sum_b\beta_b^2(Z_{eff}Z_b/4\pi)^2\kappa_b^2$ of Eq. (1), with Debye wavenumbers $\kappa_b=\sqrt{\beta_be_b^2n_b}$ and $\beta_b=1/T_b$, as $g\sim2.1$ and Zwicknagel's $\langle\eta\rangle=Z_{eff}e^2/(4\pi\varepsilon_0\hbar\langle v_r\rangle)$ of Eq. (2) as about 1.1, violating $g\ll1$ and $\langle\eta\rangle\ll1$, although the plasma itself is ideal with electron coupling $\Gamma_{ee}=e^2/(a_ek_BT)\sim0.09$ and degeneracy $\Theta=k_BT/E_F\sim100$.
5. Attributed the remaining excess of Zwicknagel's combined model, which adds strong-coupling corrections to linear response, to its linear treatment of static and dynamic collective screening, and built the agreeing prediction from the classical molecular-dynamics stopping of Grabowski et al. averaged over the differing quantum corrections of Brown et al. and of Issanova et al., whose deviation sets its uncertainty, motivated by a closest-approach distance comparable to the electron de Broglie wavelength; it gives a mean exit charge of about 4.31 against the measured 4.34 and 4.13–4.21 for the other models in Fig. 4(b).

### Comments

- Fig. 2 and its discussion: the central energies of the incident C$^{3+}$, C$^{4+}$, C$^{5+}$, and C$^{6+}$ ions are given as "2.47 MeV, 4.49 MeV, 7.00 MeV, and 10.01 MeV", but the cold-foam loss is obtained from a downshift "from 9.96 MeV to 8.45 MeV", and in Fig. 2(a) the Gaussian fit to the incident C$^{6+}$ spectrum peaks, and the dashed C$^{6+}$ reference line of Figs. 2(a)–(b) lies, at about 9.96 MeV, whereas the other three fits peak at 2.47, 4.49, and 7.00 MeV; the stated 1.51 MeV cold-foam loss rests on 9.96 MeV.
- Figs. 3(b) and 4(a): the Fig. 3(b) remaining energies at the 1 mm exit, about 3.93 MeV (T-Matrix), 3.97 MeV (Bethe–Bloch), 4.35 MeV (Vlasov), 4.39–4.46 MeV (BPS, LP, Combined), and 4.76 MeV (MD+Quantum), imply losses from 7.00 MeV of about 3.07, 3.03, 2.65, 2.54–2.61, and 2.24 MeV, but Fig. 4(a) plots about 3.26, 3.21, 2.75, 2.62–2.68, and 2.29 MeV, although the exit average charge states of Fig. 3(b) match those of Fig. 4(b) to within about 0.005; unresolved.
