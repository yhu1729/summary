# 2026ZZY

## Claude (September 2026)

### Summary

Electron drift instabilities (EDIs), azimuthal kinetic instabilities driven by the $\mathbf E\times\mathbf B$ electron drift, are linked to anomalous cross-field electron transport in Hall thrusters. The authors study how the magnetic field shapes this transport with Cartesian 3-D particle-in-cell simulations of channel and plume: 0.1 mm cells, $\Delta t=1.5\times10^{-11}$ s, a periodic 0.64 cm azimuthal extent, ionisation-only Monte Carlo collisions, and an axial neutral continuity solver. They compare an analytic radial-only field with FEMM-computed fields having radial and axial components and centreline peaks near 200 G (strong) and 100 G (weak). Earlier runs are restarted at 15 µs with up to tenfold more macro-particles. Averaging $\langle n_eE_y\rangle$ over the azimuth and time gives a correlation-based turbulent mobility $-\langle n_eE_y\rangle/(B_xn_eE_z)$ and a field-perpendicular variant. In the strong field, transport concentrates near the exit in the upper radial region, where the field is weaker. The analytic field places it at the exit and along the channel walls, and the weak field gives collapsed ion density and abnormal discharge. The turbulent mobility follows the spatial trends of the direct average $\langle u_{ez}/E_z\rangle$. Stated limitations include cells possibly exceeding the Debye length, absorbing walls without secondary emission or recycling, and a prescribed cathode current.

### Contributions

1. Compared particle-number scaling factors 1, 3, and 10 in the strong-field case, finding mean downstream ion currents of 0.020–0.024 A across runs and diagnostics but axial electron density and temperature profiles for the unscaled run that differ from the two scaled runs.
2. Initialized the neutral density from the steady axial continuity equation with a cosine ionisation source, giving $n_a=\frac{n_{a0}}2[1-\sin(\pi(z-z_m)/(z_2-z_1))]$ and $n_{a0}=2S_0(z_2-z_1)/(\pi u_z)$, and tripled the implied 304 m/s neutral speed to 912 m/s to accelerate convergence.
3. Showed that the strong-field correlation $\langle n_eE_y\rangle$ changes in magnitude and path over the breathing cycle, falling from about $5\times10^{19}$ to $2\times10^{19}$ V m$^{-4}$ near the ion-density minimum and reaching the $10^{20}$ range near the peak, with the axial electron current density rising with it.
4. Replaced $B_x$ and $E_z$ by the field magnitude and the in-plane electric field perpendicular to the local field, which suppresses the sign-changing line that the axial mobility estimate shows in the upper radial region of the strong-field case.
5. Proposed the fluctuation force $\mathbf R_e^{\rm turb}=q\langle\delta n_e\delta\mathbf E\rangle$, distinct from Coulomb drag, and the rate $\nu^{\rm eff}_{\rm turb}=|R^{\rm turb}_{e,y}|/(mn_e|v_{de,y}|)$ as an MCC-like subgrid closure for reduced radial–axial PIC models, and reported inverse Hall parameters whose axial profiles differ between the centreline and the upper wall.

### Comments

- §2 and §2.4: §2 states that at the restart each macro-particle "is split into $f_{pn}$ macro-particles with the same position, while their velocities are resampled during the restart procedure", whereas §2.4 states that each loaded macro-particle "will be copied $f_{pn}$ times with the same position and velocity"; which procedure was used is unresolved.
- Table 1: the macro-particle weight is listed as $w_0=1955.5$, whereas §2 defines $w_0\approx19\,555$ as the weight of the earlier runs and the reduced weight after the restart as $w_p=w_0/f_{pn}$ in Eq. (2.1); since the note after Table 1 says its macro-particle parameters are for $f_{pn}=10$, the tabulated value is $w_p$.
- Table 1: the final time is $T_{\rm final}=35\times10^{-6}$ s, but the strong-B curves in Figs. 5, 6, and 9, including the $f_{pn}=10$ run to which Table 1 refers, continue to about 37 µs, while the weak-B and analytic-B curves in Fig. 9 end near 33 and 27 µs; unresolved.
- §3.2, Eqs. (3.3)–(3.4): for a purely radial field, Eq. (3.4) gives $E_\perp=-B_xE_z/|B_x|$, so Eq. (3.3) reduces to $\mu_\perp^{turb}=\langle n_eE_y\rangle/(B_xn_eE_z)=-\mu_z^{turb}$ instead of to Eq. (3.2), which it is introduced to generalize; with $B_x>0$ on the centreline (Figs. 3(a) and 4), Eq. (3.4) also gives $\langle E_\perp\rangle\approx-\langle E_z\rangle$ just downstream of the exit, where the field lines are radial, but Fig. 22 shows $\langle E_\perp\rangle$ positive there, like $\langle E_z\rangle$ in Fig. 19, and $\mu_\perp^{turb}$ negative in the plume, like $\mu_z^{turb}$; the plotted quantities correspond to $E_\perp=(B_xE_z-B_zE_x)/B$, the component whose $\mathbf E\times\mathbf B$ drift is along $+y$.
- §3.4: the velocities in Fig. 26 are said to be averaged over a "time period of 12.3 µs as the same as that in figure 24 of the strong-B case", but in Fig. 24 the strong-B panel is labelled $T_{mean}=18.0$ µs and 12.3 µs is the averaging period of the analytic-B panel; the period used for Fig. 26 is unresolved.
