### Summary

This paper computes thermalization times for energetic light ions in tokamak-relevant plasmas, focusing on deuterium-beam heating and $3.5$ MeV alpha particles from D-T fusion. The authors use a quantum dielectric plasma model and derive accurate analytical approximations that separate electronic and ionic stopping contributions. A key improvement over common slowing-down formulas is the use of different logarithmic factors for projectile interactions with plasma electrons and ions, including velocity dependence in the ion term. Calculations span temperatures from $10^7$ to $10^9$ K and electron densities from $10^{13}$ to $10^{15}$ cm$^{-3}$. The results show that plasma ions can significantly reduce thermalization times, while the tritium isotope effect and modest alpha impurities have small effects in the main velocity range because electron stopping dominates. The paper provides scaling laws, fitting formulas, and reference tables for fusion modeling.

### Contributions

1. Computed stopping powers, ranges, and thermalization times for light ions in D and D-T plasmas.
2. Combined quantum electronic stopping with classical ion stopping in an analytical approximation.
3. Improved on single-Coulomb-log treatments by separating electron and ion logarithmic factors.
4. Derived scaling laws for projectile charge, mass, temperature, and plasma density.
5. Supplied fitting formulas and reference tables for deuteron heating beams and fusion alpha particles.
