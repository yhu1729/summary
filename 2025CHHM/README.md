### Summary

This experiment measures energy changes of an initially monoenergetic $\sim450\,\mathrm{MeV}$ chromium-ion beam crossing a magnetized plasma formed by colliding laser-ablated jets. Interferometry, ion deflectometry, time-of-flight detection, and radiation-MHD simulations constrain the plasma and beam interaction. Single-jet shots show little energy shift or broadening, whereas double-jet shots exhibit larger, stochastic acceleration and diffusion, including separate regimes dominated by net energization or spectral broadening with mean-energy loss. Interferometry bounds fluid-scale velocity fluctuations, while deflectometry estimates magnetic fields of roughly $40$--$230\,\mathrm{kG}$. Coulomb stopping and diffusion cannot explain the measurements, and the predicted second-order Fermi energy change is below the beam spread and detector resolution. Short-scale electrostatic turbulence driven by the lower-hybrid drift instability is quantitatively compatible with the observed scale of energy changes, and beam-driven or slowly growing electromagnetic instabilities are disfavored. The experiment does not uniquely identify the wave mode, so other wave--particle interactions remain possible.

### Contributions

1. Measured acceleration and energy diffusion of heavy ions in a laser-driven magnetized plasma.
2. Distinguished weak single-jet responses from stronger stochastic double-jet interactions.
3. Constrained density, flow fluctuations, and magnetic-field strength with complementary diagnostics and simulations.
4. Ruled out Coulomb transport and fluid-scale second-order Fermi acceleration at the measured magnitude.
5. Showed that lower-hybrid-scale electrostatic turbulence is consistent with, but not uniquely proven by, the observations.
