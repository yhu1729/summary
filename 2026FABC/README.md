### Summary

This experiment combines a laser wakefield accelerator with a second, beam-driven plasma stage: the first-stage electron bunch drives the second wake, while a density down-ramp injects a new witness bunch internally. Operating near depletion of the driver simultaneously increases witness energy and improves beam quality relative to the laser-only output. The best witnesses reach $1.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$, with 38--53 pC charge in representative cases, roughly $0.1\,\mathrm{mrad}$ divergence at the spectral peak, and few-percent energy spread. The energy transformer ratio approaches two. From measured driver and witness spectra, about 20% of the initial integrated driver energy transfers to the witness, while a lower-bound estimate assigns at least 39% of the wake energy extracted from the driver to the witness. Particle-in-cell modeling supports the injection and depletion interpretation. The authors report simultaneous high depletion and high plasma-to-witness efficiency beyond previous plasma-wakefield experiments, while noting that future application studies need complete phase-space measurements, including current and emittance.

### Contributions

1. Demonstrated a compact laser-driven source feeding an internally injected beam-driven plasma stage.
2. Operated the second stage near driver depletion while retaining narrow, low-divergence witness bunches.
3. Reached record $1.3\,\mathrm{GeV}$ witness energy for this hybrid scheme.
4. Measured an energy transformer ratio near two and about 20% driver-to-witness efficiency.
5. Connected the observed injection, energy gain, and depletion with particle-in-cell simulations.
