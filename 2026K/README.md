### Summary

This review clarifies the history, terminology, and mathematical scope of axisymmetric equilibrium equations for plasmas with anisotropic pressure. Starting from the canonical Grad--Shafranov equation for scalar pressure, it derives the generalized form for the tensor $\mathbf{P}=p_\perp\mathbf{I}+(p_\parallel-p_\perp)\mathbf{b}\mathbf{b}$. The anisotropy factor $\sigma=1-\mu_0(p_\parallel-p_\perp)/B^2$ and modified current function $F=\sigma i_p$ yield the divergence-form Mercier--Cotsaftis equation; importantly, $F$ is a magnetic-surface function even though $\sigma$ and $i_p$ need not be. The review connects ellipticity and well-posedness conditions to firehose and mirror stability, traces priority to Mercier and Cotsaftis's 1961 work, and documents the equation's later rediscovery and inconsistent naming. For mirror traps without poloidal current, it gives a reduced equation and argues that it can describe intermediate diamagnetic-bubble states, while fully developed diamagnetic traps instead require a nonlocal integro-differential model. The author recommends “Mercier--Cotsaftis equation” as the least ambiguous name for the anisotropic equilibrium equation.
