# 2026MLL

## Claude (October 2026)

### Summary

This preprint uses the global gyrokinetic particle-in-cell code TRIMEG to test how nonlinear thermal-plasma dynamics change energetic-particle (EP) transport by Alfvén eigenmodes in the ITPA toroidal Alfvén eigenmode (TAE) benchmark ($R_0 = 10$ m, $a = 1$ m, $B = 3$ T, $n = -6$, 400 keV deuterons) and in beta-induced (BAE) and reversed-shear (RSAE) cases. Case A pushes thermal ions, electrons, and EPs nonlinearly; Case B pushes only the EPs nonlinearly. Linear results coincide, but in Case A the field energy overshoots and decays, the dominant harmonics split and broaden radially, and the EP flux develops two peaks and is much smaller. Over EP densities from 0.2 to 4 times the reference, the saturated amplitude scales quadratically then linearly with drive in Case B but nearly linearly in Case A, so the flux scaling drops from quartic to quadratic and the flux is overestimated by up to an order of magnitude without thermal nonlinearity. Zonal flows grow at twice the TAE rate but shear far too weakly to matter. BAE and RSAE saturation is overestimated by about 5 and 2 to 4 times. Single-$n$ runs with a reduced mass ratio limit generality.

### Contributions

1. Specified the setup: gyrocenter equations (3)–(6) with $u_\parallel = v_\parallel + (q_s/m_s)\langle\delta A^h_\parallel\rangle$, the $\delta f$ equation (7), the quasi-neutrality equation (8) without electron polarization, the iterative Ampère solver (10)–(13), and pullback (14)–(16); ad hoc equilibria with analytic $\psi$ for $q = q_0 + q_2r^2$ (TAE, BAE) and $q = q_0 + q_2r^2 + q_4r^4$ (RSAE); $n_{e0} = 2 \times 10^{19}$ m$^{-3}$, $T_e = T_i = 1$ keV, $\beta_e \approx 9 \times 10^{-4}$, $\rho_{ti} = 1.52 \times 10^{-3}$ m, $m_i/m_e = 183.6$; EP profile (20) with $c_0 = 0.49123$, $c_1 = 0.298228$, $c_2 = 0.198739$, $c_3 = 0.521298$ and $n_{EP}/n_e = 0.00376$ at $r = 0.5a$; $32 \times 10^6$, $8 \times 10^6$, and $8$ to $16 \times 10^6$ markers on a $64 \times 128$ grid; Table 1 defines Case B by dropping (5)–(6), the $\delta\dot{R}$ and $\delta\dot{u}_\parallel$ terms on the left of (7), and the parallel-velocity pullback (15).
2. Showed that the radial mode width grows with EP density whether or not thermal nonlinearity is included (FWHM from about 0.06 to 0.19 for $n_f/n_{f,ref}$ from 0.2 to 4, Fig. 12), that Case B exhibits a sub-exponential upward drift of the field energy after the linear stage while Case A overshoots and decays with broadened $E_P(r)$ and split $m = 10, 11$ harmonics at $t = 50R_N/v_N$ (Figs. 3, 4), and that the gyro-Bohm-normalized EP flux stays positive with two peaks in Case A, whose thermal density perturbations exceed the EP ones but cancel in net charge, whereas in Case B they are smaller than the EP flattening (Figs. 5, 6).
3. Analyzed $|\delta f|^2$ in $(E/E_{max}, \lambda)$ space against the bounce–precession resonance $n\omega_d + p\omega_b = \omega$ (30) with $\omega \approx 4 \times 10^5$ rad/s: passing EPs resonate at $p = 3/2$, with $p = 5/2$ emerging nonlinearly, consistent with $v_\parallel \approx v_A/3$ and $v_A/5$ from $p\omega_b \approx \omega = k_\parallel v_A$, $k_\parallel \approx 1/(2qR)$; electrons also show $p = 3/2$ with a broader nonlinear structure in Case A, and Case B shows stronger resonance overlap (Fig. 7).
4. Found with the $n = 0$ component retained that the zonal electrostatic energy grows at $\gamma_{P,0} = 0.11744$, close to twice the TAE rate $0.058197$, indicating beat-driven zonal flow, that the zonal to $n = -6$ energy ratio is about 0.6 after saturation, and that the shearing rate $-\partial_r^2\delta\Phi/B$ is much smaller than the TAE frequency and growth rate (Figs. 8, 9), so zonal flows regulate the instability only weakly.
5. Defined transport stiffness as $S_{\Gamma_{EP}} = \partial\Gamma_{EP}/\partial n_{EP}$ (31), distinct from profile stiffness $\partial P_{EP}/\partial\mathrm{Src}_{EP}$ (32); showed the Case B saturation level follows the pendulum-model quadratic scaling up to about $n_{f,ref}$ and then the linear radial-decoupling scaling, with Case A lower by a factor rising to about 5 (Fig. 10), and the flux overestimation exceeding an order of magnitude at $n_{f,ref}$ (Fig. 11); for the BAE the growth rate falls and the frequency rises by about 20% toward near stabilization at the highest shear, with saturation overestimated about 5 times at the lowest shear (Fig. 14), and for the RSAE the growth rate falls from about 0.054 to 0.035 and the frequency roughly halves as $\Delta q = q_{min} - 1.75$ goes from 0 to 0.05, with saturation overestimated 2 to 4 times (Fig. 16); $\delta A_{\parallel,m=12}$ has odd parity for the BAE and even parity for the RSAE.

### Comments

- Section 1: "The results show that nonlinear thermal plasma dynamics can significantly modify the mode structure and lead to a stiff dependence of energetic particle transport", whereas the title, the abstract ("the dependence of the EP flux on the EP drive becomes weaker"), and Section 7 state that thermal nonlinearity reduces the stiffness; a less stiff dependence is evidently intended.
- Section 2.2 and Fig. 1: the RSAE profiles are listed with $\bar{q}_0 = 1.765625, 1.790625, 1.790625, 1.790625, 1.815625$, but the legend of Fig. 1 gives $q_0 = 1.7656, 1.7781, 1.7906, 1.8031, 1.8156$, and Section 9 spans $\Delta q = q_{min} - 1.75$ from 0 to 0.05 with $q_{min} = \bar{q}_0 - 0.015625$ at $r_c = 0.5$; the repeated entries are evidently 1.778125 and 1.803125.
- Section 8 and Fig. 14: the BAE cases are labeled by $dq(r_c)/dr$ from 0.32 to 0.8, but the profiles $\bar{q} = \bar{q}_0 + \bar{q}_2r^2$ of Section 2.2 with $\bar{q}_2 = 0.16, 0.24, 0.32, 0.40$ give $dq/dr = 2\bar{q}_2r_c = \bar{q}_2$, that is 0.16 to 0.40, at $r_c = 0.5a$; the plotted abscissa equals $2\bar{q}_2$; unresolved.
- Section 10: "the case with EP-only nonlinearity exhibits an approximately quadratic scaling of the saturation level and EP flux, the fully nonlinear simulations show a scaling closer to linear", whereas Section 7 and Fig. 11 give quartic and quadratic scalings of the flux for the EP-only and fully nonlinear cases, the quadratic and linear scalings applying to the saturation level alone.
- Section 3: "The left and middle frames show the 2D structures of the density perturbation for $n_{EP}/n_{f,ref} = 0.5$ (left) and $n_{EP}/n_{f,ref} = 2$ (right)", but Fig. 2 has two frames, left and right.
