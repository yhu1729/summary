### Summary

This paper rigorously justifies first-order hyperbolic relaxations of several higher-order partial differential equations. Using relative-energy estimates, it compares weak entropy solutions of the relaxation systems with smooth solutions of the limiting equations and proves convergence as the relaxation parameter vanishes. The analysis treats mixed space--time derivatives typified by Benjamin--Bona--Mahony equations and purely spatial derivatives of arbitrary odd or even order, covering Korteweg--de Vries, KdV--Burgers, Gardner, Kawahara, biharmonic, and Kuramoto--Sivashinsky models. The authors manage the degenerating convexity of the relaxation energy and, where existing hyperbolizations lack a usable energy, propose alternative energy-compatible systems. Structure-preserving summation-by-parts spatial discretizations with additive Runge--Kutta time integration reproduce the predicted asymptotic rates. Numerically, auxiliary derivative variables often converge as rapidly as the primary solution, a stronger behavior left as a conjectured analytical refinement.

### Contributions

1. Applied a unified relative-energy framework requiring only weak entropy solutions of the hyperbolizations and a smooth limiting solution.
2. Proved convergence for BBM-type equations despite degenerating convexity of the relative energy.
3. Extended the construction to broad odd- and even-order spatial PDE classes.
4. Replaced an unsuitable Kuramoto--Sivashinsky hyperbolization with an energy-compatible approximation and proved convergence.
5. Supplied reproducible SBP and additive-Runge--Kutta experiments confirming the theoretical rates.
