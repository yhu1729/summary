### Summary

The authors use two-dimensional first-principles particle-in-cell simulations to determine how ion-acoustic instability affects collisionless magnetic reconnection. Harris-sheet runs compare upstream ion-to-electron temperature ratios $T_i/T_e=1/50$, $1/10$, and $1$. In the cold-ion cases, electron--ion drift in the diffusion region exceeds the ion-acoustic threshold, producing wave activity consistent with linear Vlasov--Poisson theory and nonlinear ion phase-space structures. The instability heats outflowing ions until $T_i\approx T_e/10$ and causes local momentum fluctuations, but the diffusion-region-averaged anomalous terms are nearly zero. Peak reconnection rates vary by less than a factor of two across the temperature ratios, and anomalous resistivity remains negligible, contrary to estimates based on initially supercritical or steady-state assumptions. Ion-acoustic turbulence therefore redistributes reconnection energy mainly through ion heating rather than enabling Petschek-like reconnection. Scaling arguments suggest that analogous instability-driven heating may operate at sub-ion reconnection sites in low-$T_i/T_e$ solar-wind turbulence.

### Contributions

1. Demonstrated self-consistent ion-acoustic instability in cold-ion reconnection diffusion regions.
2. Matched instability onset and wave scales to local linear kinetic theory.
3. Showed that the instability heats ions to approximately one-tenth of the electron temperature.
4. Found negligible diffusion-region-averaged anomalous resistivity and little change in reconnection rate.
5. Derived conditions connecting the mechanism to sub-ion-scale solar-wind reconnection.
