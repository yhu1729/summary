### Summary

This paper studies impurity transport in three projected SPARC H-mode scenarios using the ASTRA+STRAHL workflow. Neoclassical transport is modeled with FACIT, turbulent transport with TGLF-SAT2, and pedestal boundary conditions with a neural network trained on EPED simulations. The calculations benchmark earlier constant-impurity-fraction studies and then scan uncertain assumptions for a low-current scenario. The main result is robust: turbulent transport dominates neoclassical transport for argon and tungsten impurities over broad variations in pedestal concentrations, toroidal rotation, and temperature and density gradients. Tungsten peaking is nearly insensitive to pedestal tungsten concentration, while argon scans show competing changes in pedestal pressure, dilution, density peaking, and fusion gain that largely cancel. Rotation has little impact in the no-neutral-beam SPARC setting. Separate deuterium and tritium treatment finds maximum fusion power near a 55-45% D-T mixture because isotope effects alter impurity transport. The predictions support low tungsten accumulation in low-collisionality next-step tokamaks.

### Contributions

1. Modeled SPARC H-mode impurity transport with coupled ASTRA, STRAHL, FACIT, TGLF-SAT2, and EPED-surrogate tools.
2. Benchmarked variable impurity profiles against earlier constant-fraction SPARC performance predictions.
3. Performed pedestal-source and boundary-condition scans for argon and tungsten impurities.
4. Showed turbulent impurity transport robustly exceeds neoclassical transport across sensitivity studies.
5. Assessed rotation and D-T fuel-composition effects on impurity peaking and fusion performance.
