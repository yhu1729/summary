### Summary

The paper develops a one-dimensional cold, collisionless theory for nonlinear plasma waves driven by a rigid charged-particle beam. In a coordinate moving with the beam, the relativistic fluid and Poisson equations reduce to an energy integral that applies to either beam-charge sign and arbitrary driver velocity up to wave breaking. The resulting analytic expressions give electric-field amplitude, wavelength, peak electron density, and Lorentz factor for both the wake behind the beam and a distinct wave inside it. These interior waves exist for normalized beam charge density $\rho_b>-1/2$; unlike resonantly driven wakes, their properties do not depend on driver length. For dense positive drivers their wavelength contracts as $\lambda_{\mathrm{int}}/\lambda_{pe}\sim(2/\rho_b)^{1/2}/\pi$, permitting localized, short-wavelength structures. Choosing an integer number of interior wavelengths can eliminate the trailing wake. Numerical solutions closely match the analytic results, including the mildly nonlinear regime where earlier asymptotic formulas fail. The model assumes a wide, non-evolving beam, immobile ions, and one-dimensional electrostatic dynamics.

### Contributions

1. Derived nonlinear wake and interior-wave solutions valid for either beam-charge sign and arbitrary driver velocity up to wave breaking.
2. Identified the interior-wave existence condition $\rho_b>-1/2$ and calculated its amplitude, wavelength, and peak density.
3. Showed that interior waves are insensitive to driver length and can be isolated by suppressing the trailing wake.
4. Obtained the positive-driver short-wavelength scaling $\lambda_{\mathrm{int}}/\lambda_{pe}\sim(2/\rho_b)^{1/2}/\pi$.
5. Verified the analytic predictions numerically and derived wave-breaking bounds for wakefield acceleration.
