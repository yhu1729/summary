## Claude (September 2026)

### Summary

The authors couple a plasma fluid model of capacitively coupled plasma (CCP) discharges with an external circuit model (an L-matching network) in a finite-volume solver on ultraMPP. The CCP voltage is not an explicit function of charge or current, and explicit coupling can diverge even for a plasma-free capacitor, so the potential is split into a Laplace part scaled by the electrode voltage and a charge-driven part, giving a vacuum capacitance $C_{CCP}$ and a plasma-induced charge $Q_{pm}$, which alone is extrapolated while the circuit equations are integrated implicitly at second order. Runs stop when relative two-norm differences of plasma fields and electrode voltage between successive radio-frequency (RF) cycles fall below a threshold, linked to the slowest exponential time constant. A time-step size refinement (TSSR) algorithm halves $\Delta t$ from $T/100$ ($T$ the RF period), restarting from the interpolated periodic state, until the discharge power changes by less than 1%. An unignited test matches the analytic circuit solution with errors generally below $10^{-3}$. For a quasi-one-dimensional 20-W, 0.2-torr argon discharge, TSSR reached $T/3200$ after six iterations and 709,700 steps, against over 1.28 million at fixed $T/3200$. The authors note that savings are problem-dependent and small two-norm differences do not guarantee convergence.

### Contributions

1. Split the potential as $\phi=V_{CCP}\phi_{base}+\phi_\rho$, with $\phi_{base}$ the Laplace solution for unit voltage on the powered electrode and $\phi_\rho$ the Poisson solution with both electrodes grounded, so that the powered-electrode charge is $Q_p=C_{CCP}V_{CCP}+Q_{plasma,p}$ with $C_{CCP}=\int\varepsilon\vec E_{base}\,dA$ and $Q_{plasma,p}$ the sum of the $\vec E_\rho$ displacement charge and the conduction charge $\int\sum_jq_j\vec\Gamma_j\,dA$, and kept the Kirchhoff system for $(Q_{CCP},I_{CCP},Q_m)$ in descriptor form with the matrix $A$ containing $L_1$ and $R_s$ rather than premultiplying by $A^{-1}$, which would introduce $1/L$ terms, problematic for circuits without inductors.
2. Blended linear and quadratic extrapolation as $Q^{n+1}_{pm}\approx(1-\omega)(2Q^n_{pm}-Q^{n-1}_{pm})+\omega(3Q^n_{pm}-3Q^{n-1}_{pm}+Q^{n-2}_{pm})$ with $\omega=\min(N_t/500,1)$, since the linear form underestimated and the quadratic form overestimated the discharge power and the quadratic form was unstable for $\Delta t\gtrsim T/500$, after testing backward Euler, midpoint, trapezoidal, Lobatto IIIC, Radau IIA, modified extended BDF, BDF2, and BDF3 circuit integrators and finding BDF2 accuracy sufficient, and matched the target power by updating the supply amplitude once per cycle as $V_{pp}^{(new)}=V_{pp}^{(old)}+0.2(P_{p,tar}V_{pp}^{(old)}/P_p^n-V_{pp}^{(old)})$ whenever the relative power error exceeded 0.1%.
3. Showed for a plasma-free CCP driven through series $R$ and $C$ by explicit coupling with $Q^n_{CCP}=C_{CCP}V^n_{CCP}$ that the amplification polynomial $r^2+(\xi+\zeta)r-\xi=0$, with $\xi=C_{CCP}R/\Delta t$ and $\zeta=C_{CCP}/C$, is stable only if $2\xi<1-\zeta$, and that adding a series $L$ yields the cubic $r^3+(\eta+\xi+\zeta)r^2-(2\eta+\xi)r+\eta=0$ with $\eta=C_{CCP}L/\Delta t^2$, whose root product $-\eta$ makes $\Delta t>\sqrt{C_{CCP}L}$ necessary, a step at the intrinsic LC time scale.
4. Modeled a transient variable as $X=X_{ave,qs}(1-e^{-t/\tau})H$ with $H$ periodic in the RF cycle and bounded the cycle-to-cycle difference by $D_X\lesssim(T/\tau_{max})e^{-t/\tau_{max}}$, giving the 1% threshold $D_X<(T/\tau_{max})\cdot1\%$; in a 2000-cycle run at $\Delta t=T/400$ with $\omega=0$, slopes gave $\tau_1\approx48.64T$ for the electron and $\mathrm{Ar}^+$ densities and $V_{CCP}$ (1% after $223.7T$) and $\tau_2\approx385.5T$ for metastable argon ($1773T$), and the authors recommended stopping at about 474 cycles ($D_{V_{CCP}}\le10^{-5}$) for macroscopic argon quantities and at $D_{V_{CCP}}\le10^{-6}$ for other chemistries or boundary conditions without prior convergence benchmarks.
5. With $R_s=50$ Ω, $C_m=76.10$ pF, $C_1=200$ pF, $L_1=4.181$ μH, $R_1=0.5$ Ω, a nine-reaction argon set with five BOLSIG+ rate coefficients, and 60 nonuniform cells of minimum width 0.2 mm, obtained $Z_{CCP}=17.96-239.4j$ Ω from $V_{CCP,max}=220.2$ V, $I_{CCP,max}=0.9173$ A, and a phase of $-85.71^\circ$, against $5.326-253.2j$ Ω from two 3-mm vacuum sheaths and a resistive bulk with $\sigma_p=0.08366$, and found in the TSSR run that the peak electron density rose from about $2.4\times10^{15}$ to $3.2\times10^{15}$ m$^{-3}$, the sheath electron temperature from 3.9 to 4.4 eV, and the plasma potential from 110 to over 120 V.

### Comments

- Section II-B, Eqs. (36)–(40): with $\vec y=(Q_{CCP},I_{CCP},Q_m)^T$, the first row of $A\,d\vec y/dt=B\vec y+c$ gives $dQ_{CCP}/dt=-I_{CCP}$, contradicting the definition of $Q_{CCP}$ as the time integral of $I_{CCP}$, and the other rows give $L_1\dot I_{CCP}+R_s\dot Q_m=(1/C_1+1/C_{CCP})Q_{CCP}+(R_1+R_s)I_{CCP}+V_p+Q_{pm}/C_{CCP}$ and $R_s\dot Q_m=R_sI_{CCP}+Q_m/C_m+V_p$, whereas (21), (22), and (35) with $Q_p=Q_{CCP}$ give every $B\vec y$ term with the opposite sign and the same $c$; the evident intended system is $A\,d\vec y/dt=-B\vec y+c$, so that $\tilde A=-A^{-1}B$.
- Section II-C: with $f_p^{-1}\approx T/36.3$, the resolution "$\Delta t\lesssim T/100$" is said to correspond "to approximately $3f_p^{-1}$", but $T/100\approx0.36f_p^{-1}$; the evident intended statement is about three time steps per $f_p^{-1}$, that is, $\Delta t\approx f_p^{-1}/3$.
- Section II-D, Eq. (52): $X_{ave,qs}(\vec r)$ is introduced as "The spatially varying quasi-steady-state average" and then as "The spatially averaged quasi-steady-state value", but (52) defines it at fixed $\vec r$ as the limit of the one-period average $\frac1T\int_t^{t+T}X(\vec r,\tilde t)\,d\tilde t$, a cycle average, consistent with $\lim_{t\to\infty}a_0=1$ in (54).
- Section II-D, Eq. (61): with $H(t-T)\approx H(t)$, the denominator $X(\vec r,t-T)$ on the left becomes proportional to $1-e^{-(t-T)/\tau_{max}}$, but the right side has $1-e^{-t/\tau_{max}}$; the difference disappears once the denominator is approximated by 1 for $t\gg\tau_{max}$.
- Section II-D, Eq. (62): the first two lines, $|(1-e^{\frac{t-T}{\tau_{max}}})-(1-e^{\frac{t}{\tau_{max}}})|$ and $T\frac{d}{dt}(1-e^{\frac{t}{\tau_{max}}})$, drop the minus signs of the exponents in (61); with positive exponents the derivative is $-\frac{T}{\tau_{max}}e^{t/\tau_{max}}$, whereas the stated result $\frac{T}{\tau_{max}}e^{-t/\tau_{max}}$ follows from the exponents $-(t-T)/\tau_{max}$ and $-t/\tau_{max}$ of (61).
- Fig. 7 caption: "(c) Electrostatic potential. (d) Electron temperature.", but panel (c) is labeled "Ave. $T_e$ [eV]" and panel (d) "Ave. $\phi$ [V]", as in the text of Section III-A ("Fig. 7(c) demonstrates the transient evolution of the electron temperature", "In Fig. 7(d), the electrostatic potential"); the labels in the caption are swapped.
- Section III-A, discussion of Fig. 8(b): the Ar$^+$ density is stationary because "the ion plasma frequency is typically in the kilohertz range", but Section II-C gives $\omega_p=3.089\times10^9$ rad/s for $n_e=3\times10^{15}$ m$^{-3}$, and scaling by $(m_e/m_{\mathrm{Ar}})^{1/2}\approx3.7\times10^{-3}$ gives an Ar$^+$ plasma frequency of about $1.1\times10^7$ rad/s, or 1.8 MHz, still below but not far below the 13.56-MHz drive.
- Section III-B: "The converged power approaches 9.5 W, while simulations using larger time-steps report values below 8 W", but in Fig. 13 the discharge power at the largest step, 100 time steps per cycle, is about 8.6 W, no plotted value is below 8 W, and the last two points, at 1600 and 3200 steps per cycle, are about 9.8 and 9.7 W; unresolved.
- Appendix A, after Eq. (A7): "when $C_{CCP}>C$, the system is always stable as the stability condition becomes negative", but for $\zeta=C_{CCP}/C>1$ the condition (A6), $2\xi<1-\zeta$ with $\xi=C_{CCP}R/\Delta t>0$, holds for no $\Delta t$, and then $|r_-|>\xi+\zeta>1$, so the explicit coupling is unstable for every time step; (A7) follows from (A6) only for $C_{CCP}<C$, since dividing by the negative $1/C_{CCP}-1/C$ reverses the inequality.
