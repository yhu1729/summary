### Summary

The paper uses multi-species gyrokinetic Vlasov simulations to evaluate steady-burning profile conditions in an ITER-like deuterium--tritium--helium plasma. The model evolves deuterium, tritium, thermalized helium ash, and real-mass kinetic electrons with interspecies collisions, capturing ion-temperature-gradient and trapped-electron-mode turbulence. Unlike zero-dimensional confinement-time balances, the analysis directly evaluates turbulent energy and particle fluxes needed for helium-ash exhaust and inward fuel transport. Scans over fuel composition and helium concentration reveal that deuterium and tritium can have strongly imbalanced particle fluxes even at equal fractions; an effective single-ion model cannot reproduce this behavior. Density- and temperature-gradient scans identify regimes satisfying Reiter's steady-burning inequalities. For the thermal-helium case studied, relatively flat density profiles favor inward fuel pinch, including a regime with $R_{\mathrm{ax}}/L_n\leq1.2$. Suppressing zonal flows removes inward fuel transport, while hotter, nonthermal helium can reduce or reverse helium exhaust. Species-resolved gyrokinetics is therefore necessary for predictive fuel-control and ash-exhaust assessment.

### Contributions

1. Formulated a gyrokinetic flux-based evaluation of Reiter's steady-burning condition.
2. Simulated deuterium, tritium, helium ash, and real-mass electrons with interspecies collisions in an ITER-like plasma.
3. Revealed deuterium--tritium particle-flux imbalance and showed why effective single-ion models miss it.
4. Identified density- and temperature-profile regimes combining helium exhaust with inward fuel transport.
5. Demonstrated that zonal flows and nonthermal helium materially alter whether those steady-burning regimes persist.
