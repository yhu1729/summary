### Summary

Motivated by synchronized core and edge oscillations in KSTAR double-peaked fishbones, this study investigates radial coupling using visco-resistive, single-fluid magnetohydrodynamic simulations with MEGA. Modified KSTAR equilibria contain nearly flat safety-factor profiles that produce low-frequency Alfvén-continuum plateaus, while a spatially localized internal antenna supplies periodic drive. An antenna near the edge excites a coherent core-localized quasi-mode, demonstrating that a local response need not require local drive. Comparisons between central and off-axis receiving plateaus show stronger inward than outward excitation, consistent with energy concentration toward smaller plasma volumes. The core also responds below its continuum-plateau frequency, suggesting a mechanism that could accommodate frequency chirping without an exactly matching continuum. Artificially suppressing pressure and density fluctuations changes the response but preserves remote excitation. Analysis of transients distinguishes rapid magnetoacoustic propagation, poloidal Alfvén-wave spreading, radial structure formation, and slower amplitude saturation. These results establish a possible MHD coupling mechanism; its relevance to experimental fishbones remains conditional because energetic-particle kinetics and differential plasma rotation are omitted, and chirping is not simulated self-consistently.

### Contributions

1. Demonstrated coherent core Alfvénic quasi-modes excited by antennas at distant radii on deliberately constructed low-frequency continuum plateaus.
2. Used two receiving plateaus and an antenna-position scan to establish stronger inward than outward excitation, supporting a volumetric-focusing interpretation.
3. Found substantial central responses to edge drive at 4.5 and 6.75 kHz below an approximately 10 kHz continuum plateau, including configurations without a $q=1$ surface.
4. Verified that remote core excitation survives artificial suppression of density and pressure perturbations, although polarization, continuum frequencies, and amplitudes change.
5. Resolved distinct propagation, radial structure formation, and amplitude saturation stages, with dissipation scans distinguishing the settling time from the final core response.
