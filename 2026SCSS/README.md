### Summary

This preprint organizes cold-fluid dispersion theory for the purely transverse Weibel instability across nonrelativistic and relativistic single- and multispecies counterstreaming plasmas. Known quartic relations are reduced to quadratics in $\omega^2$ and used to map growth rates, characteristic scales, relativistic suppression, and mass-ratio effects. The resulting regime charts quantify when simpler formulas cease to be reliable and where oblique or two-stream modes may compete. Applied to a tabletop laser experiment, the model predicts an ion skin depth of about $31.7\,\mu\mathrm{m}$, close to the reported $31\,\mu\mathrm{m}$ filament spacing; its growth-rate and saturation-field estimates are explicitly treated as cold-fluid upper bounds. Analyses of two MMS bow-shock crossings place perpendicular magnetic spectral breaks near $k d_i=1$. The paper emphasizes that warm-beam kinetics, nonlinear saturation, and background magnetic fields are required for quantitative growth predictions beyond these scale comparisons.

### Contributions

1. Unified cold-fluid Weibel derivations for four velocity and species regimes.
2. Quantified regime-transition errors from velocity and particle-mass ratios.
3. Produced diagnostic maps for relativistic suppression and competing instability domains.
4. Compared the ion-inertial scale prediction with a tabletop laser experiment.
5. Tested $k d_i\approx1$ against two MMS bow-shock magnetic spectra.
