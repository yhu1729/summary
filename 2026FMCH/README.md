### Summary

The paper introduces Coherent Structure Transport (CST), a reduced analytic model of scrape-off-layer blobs coupled to full-orbit deuterium-ion tracking in GEMX. A SOLPS-ITER solution supplies realistic axisymmetric electric and magnetic fields in DIII-D X-point geometry; time-dependent blob potentials are superimposed to study divertor ion heat-flux profiles without an expensive first-principles electron-turbulence calculation. Magnetic-only calculations recover the expected inverse-poloidal-field scaling of the heat-load width. Adding the SOLPS-ITER electric field substantially changes ion orbits and produces a broadened profile with a secondary peak outside the strike point. Blob turbulence further lowers the peak heat flux and broadens the profile approximately linearly with fluctuation amplitude; a few-percent normalized density fluctuation can roughly double the width. For a DIII-D wide-pedestal quiescent-H-mode case, the combined model predicts a $2.9$ mm width consistent with experiment and XGC1. The fast model currently treats ions only and requires future sheath, electron, neutral-collision, and broader parameter-scan work.

### Contributions

1. Defined the CST analytic blob-field model and coupled it to GEMX full-orbit ion tracking in realistic X-point geometry.
2. Integrated SOLPS-ITER equilibrium electric fields and showed that they alter ion drifts and generate a secondary divertor heat-flux peak.
3. Recovered the expected $\lambda_q\propto B_p^{-1}$ magnetic-only scaling and compared it with Eich and Goldston expectations.
4. Quantified heat-load broadening with blob amplitude and size, finding near-linear amplitude dependence but modest size dependence at fixed packing fraction.
5. Produced a fast DIII-D estimate, $\lambda_q=2.9$ mm, comparable to experiment and XGC1 while identifying missing physics.
