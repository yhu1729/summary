### Summary

This paper argues that Farley--Buneman turbulence in the auroral E region self-organizes near marginal stability rather than behaving only as locally forced turbulence. ICEBEAR radar, CHAIN GPS, Swarm, Arase, and SuperMAG observations connect small-scale electrojet irregularities to magnetospheric forcing. Composite radar--GPS spectra repeatedly peak near a kinetic-Alfv\'en-like $k^{-8/3}$ slope across roughly four wavenumber decades, while radar phase speeds saturate near the ion-acoustic speed. Above a critical driving threshold, radar echo density scales nearly linearly with magnetospheric wave power, with fitted exponent $0.98\pm0.09$. The authors formulate a Martin--Siggia--Rose effective field theory in which inertial dynamics become irrelevant under renormalization, leaving an overdamped advective--diffusive fixed point. Saturation electric fields supply anomalous drag, producing a Bohm-limit diffusion coefficient and an Adler-type noise-broadened transition. The resulting closed transport relations offer a proposed parameter-free subgrid closure for anomalous ionospheric currents and heating, although the causal interpretation of matching spectra and the proposed critical-state picture need further substantiation.

### Contributions

1. Combined ICEBEAR, CHAIN, Swarm, Arase, and geomagnetic-index observations to relate auroral E-region turbulence to particle precipitation and magnetospheric driving.
2. Resolved a composite turbulence cascade centered near $k^{-8/3}$, with recurring kilometer- and decameter-scale breaks and ion-acoustic phase-speed saturation.
3. Measured a thresholded response in which turbulent-wave density is flat below critical driving and scales as $\Delta E^{0.98\pm0.09}$ above it.
4. Derived a Martin--Siggia--Rose renormalized effective theory whose macroscopic limit is an overdamped $z=2$ advective--diffusive fixed point.
5. Obtained anomalous-resistivity, Bohm-diffusion, and Adler--Ohmic relations proposed as subgrid closures for ionospheric currents and heating.
