# 2022SKP

## Claude (October 2026)

### Summary

This companion paper treats nonlinear method-of-lines systems $Mu' = \mathcal{N}(u,t)$ and differential-algebraic equations (DAEs) integrated by $s$-stage fully implicit Runge–Kutta (IRK) or DG-in-time methods. After $A_0 \otimes I$ is pulled out of the linearized stage system, the real Schur decomposition $A_0^{-1} = Q_0R_0Q_0^T$ makes the simplified-Newton operator block upper triangular. For stage-dependent linearizations $\hat{\mathcal{L}}_i$, the rotated operator $(Q_0^T \otimes I)\,\mathrm{diag}(\hat{\mathcal{L}}_i)\,(Q_0 \otimes I)$ has off-diagonal blocks whose coefficients sum to zero, motivating three Newton-like approximations: lumping each diagonal block onto its dominant stage operator, keeping the weighted diagonal blocks, or truncating to the pattern of $R_0$. The $2 \times 2$ blocks of conjugate eigenvalue pairs $\eta \pm i\beta$ receive block triangular preconditioners whose Schur complement $\eta I - \hat{\mathcal{L}}_2 + \beta^2(\eta I - \hat{\mathcal{L}}_1)^{-1}$ is replaced by $\gamma_* I - \hat{\mathcal{L}}_2$, $\gamma_* = \eta + \beta^2/\eta$; under field-of-values assumptions its condition number is at most $1 + \beta^2/(2\eta^2)$ for equal operators and at most twice that otherwise, so backward-Euler-type preconditioners suffice. MFEM tests on an isentropic Euler vortex, Reynolds 40,000 flow over a NACA0012 airfoil, and a vorticity–streamfunction double shear layer (an index-1 DAE) reach tenth order; fourth-order Gauss needs about half the preconditioner applications of SDIRK.

### Contributions

1. Cast Newton, Picard, and other fixed-point schemes as linearly preconditioned nonlinear Richardson iterations $x_{k+1} = x_k + \mathcal{P}^{-1}\mathcal{F}(x_k)$ and, using $\sum_i (d_{k,\ell})_i = \delta_{k\ell}$ for $d_{k,\ell}$ the elementwise product of the $k$th row of $Q_0^T$ with the $\ell$th column of $Q_0$, showed that two-stage Gauss has a block-diagonal rotated operator, so exact Newton costs no more than simplified Newton, while two-stage Radau IIA has diagonal blocks $0.985\hat{\mathcal{L}}_1 + 0.015\hat{\mathcal{L}}_2$ and off-diagonal blocks $0.121(\hat{\mathcal{L}}_1 - \hat{\mathcal{L}}_2)$ (Eq. (13)).
2. Proved Theorem 4.4: if $W(\hat{\mathcal{L}}_1), W(\hat{\mathcal{L}}_2) \le 0$ and $\langle\hat{\mathcal{L}}_1 w, \hat{\mathcal{L}}_2 w\rangle \ge 0$, right preconditioning the Schur complement by $(\gamma_* I - \hat{\mathcal{L}}_2)^{-1}$ gives condition number at most $2 + \beta^2/\eta^2$, by choosing $\gamma_*$ so that the identity perturbation $(\eta^2 + \beta^2 - \gamma\eta)I$ in the minimum singular value vanishes; for equal operators Table 1 gives bounds 1.17 to 2.18 (Gauss), 1.25 to 2.60 (Radau IIA), and 1.50 to 3.44 (Lobatto IIIC) for two to five stages, and Remark 4.3 gives the tight bound $\frac{1}{2}(1 + \sqrt{1 + \beta^2/\eta^2})$ for symmetric negative semidefinite operators.
3. Extended the framework to DAEs $Mu_t = \mathcal{N}(u,w,t)$, $0 = \mathcal{G}(u,w,t)$, where the Schur-decomposed blocks become the $4 \times 4$ systems (34); for the Picard-linearized vorticity–streamfunction form with $\mathcal{L}_u = K(\psi^{(i)})$, $\mathcal{L}_w = 0$, $\mathcal{G}_u = M_{mix}$, and $\mathcal{G}_w = A$, reordered (41) into the block triangular (42), so the $2 \times 2$ vorticity block is solved first, with AIR for advection–diffusion blocks, and two independent Poisson problems preconditioned by AMG follow.
4. For the isentropic Euler vortex (degree-4 DG, 532 triangles, Roe flux) observed orders 2 to 7 against an explicit fourth-order Runge–Kutta reference with $\delta t = 5 \times 10^{-5}$ (Table 2); at $\delta t = 2 \times 10^{-2}$ with elementwise block ILU, Gauss(2) and Gauss(4) needed 15 and 36 preconditioner applications against 26 and 59 for second- and fourth-order SDIRK, Radau(3) 44 against 45, and Lobatto methods 66 to 292 (Table 3); $\gamma_*$ instead of $\eta$ reduced average Krylov iterations per $2 \times 2$ system, e.g. from 35.2 to 11.2 for Lobatto(8) (Table 4).
5. For the NACA0012 case at $\delta t = 5 \times 10^{-2}$ (degree-3 compact DG, 3154 triangles), Gauss(2), Radau(3), and Gauss(4) needed 128, 314, and 244 preconditioner applications against 200, 359, and 481 for SDIRK of orders 2 to 4 (Table 5), and Newton-like(3) lowered Gauss(4) runtime from 33.8 s with simplified Newton (9 Newton iterations, 148 matrix-vector products) to 21.6 s (4 iterations, 76 products) (Fig. 2); for the double shear layer with $p = 3$, $h = 0.0025$, $\delta t = 10^{-2}$ on 288 cores, Gauss(10) needed 333 preconditioner applications against 365 for SDIRK(4) at Re $= 10$ and 194 against 177 at Re $= 25{,}000$ (Tables 6, 7), the reordered preconditioner (42) needing up to 1064 AIR and 1204 AMG iterations for Gauss(10).

### Comments

- Section 3.2: "extract the real Schur decomposition as in subsection 3.2" refers to the subsection in which the sentence appears; the decomposition $A_0^{-1} = Q_0R_0Q_0^T$ is introduced in subsection 3.1, the evident intended reference.
- Table 2, Lobatto 4: the errors $2.39 \times 10^{-5}$ at $\delta t = 1.25 \times 10^{-2}$ and $2.39 \times 10^{-6}$ at $\delta t = 6.25 \times 10^{-3}$ give the rate $\log_2 10 \approx 3.32$, not the listed 3.67; unresolved.
- Section 7.2 and Table 8: the long-time accuracy study states "We choose a Reynolds number of 100", whereas the caption of Table 8 reads "double shear layer problem with Re $= 10$"; unresolved.
- Section 7.2: the reference solution is computed "by applying explicit sixth-order SDIRK integration", but SDIRK denotes singly diagonally implicit Runge–Kutta methods (Section 2.1), which are not explicit; whether an explicit sixth-order method or an SDIRK method was used is unresolved.
- Appendix A, Eqs. (45) and (51): (45) gives $\|\mathcal{P}_\gamma\| \le 1 + \beta^2/(\gamma\eta)$, which for $\gamma_* = (\eta^2 + \beta^2)/\eta$ is $1 + \beta^2/(\eta^2 + \beta^2)$, but (51) uses the factor $1 + \eta^2/(\eta^2 + \beta^2)$ to reach $2 + \beta^2/\eta^2$, the bound of Theorem 4.4, Eq. (25); combining (45) with (50) instead gives $1 + 2\beta^2/\eta^2$, which exceeds $2 + \beta^2/\eta^2$ whenever $\beta > \eta$; unresolved.
