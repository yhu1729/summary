### Summary

This paper examines which magnetohydrodynamic invariants remain dynamically relevant in weakly collisional solar-wind-like turbulence. A two-dimensional hybrid simulation treats protons kinetically and electrons as a fluid, starts with strong cross helicity but negligible kinetic and magnetic helicities, and is analyzed through K\'arm\'an--Howarth--Monin scale-by-scale balances. Combined kinetic-plus-magnetic energy decays at large scales, cascades through MHD nonlinearities and then Hall coupling, and transfers to internal energy through resistive and pressure--strain effects. Although Hall MHD suggests a mixed combination of cross and kinetic helicities, the simulation instead finds that cross helicity most closely parallels energy: it decays, cascades through joint MHD--Hall transfer, and is dissipated mainly by its pressure--strain analogue. Hall effects influence cross-helicity transfer even above ion scales. Magnetic helicity is generated only weakly and does not cascade. Spectral-transfer and coarse-graining analyses reproduce these conclusions. The evidence comes from one two-dimensional parameter set, so three-dimensional, fully kinetic, and different-helicity regimes remain open.

### Contributions

1. Derived and evaluated hybrid K\'arm\'an--Howarth--Monin balances for energy and several helicities.
2. Resolved the MHD-to-Hall continuation and small-scale conversion of the combined-energy cascade.
3. Identified cross helicity, rather than mixed helicity, as the energy-like rugged quantity in the simulated regime.
4. Showed that Hall and pressure--strain physics alter cross-helicity transfer across a broad scale range.
5. Confirmed the scale-by-scale findings with independent spectral-transfer and coarse-graining diagnostics.
