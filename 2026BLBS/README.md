## Claude (September 2026)

### Summary

In beam-driven plasma-wakefield accelerators, the efficiency of energy transfer from the wake to a trailing bunch is usually measured with an electron spectrometer, which is invasive and averages over the whole plasma stage. At DESY's FLASHForward facility, the authors instead use the excess light emitted by a 195-mm argon discharge capillary after the beams pass, recorded by a side-view camera. The light yield rises with driver energy loss and falls when a trailing bunch extracts energy, so a linear response curve calibrated on driver-only shots converts light into remaining wake energy and hence efficiency. At a high-efficiency working point, the spectrometer gave $34\pm2\%$ wake-to-trailing-bunch efficiency and $2.2\pm0.1\%$ driver-to-trailing-bunch efficiency. Light-based and spectrometer efficiencies are linearly correlated, but the light-based values are higher, which the authors attribute mainly to trailing-bunch charge loss. Dividing the image into 3-mm segments yields longitudinally resolved efficiencies: the final segment agrees much better with the spectrometer, and an efficiency decline along the density plateau, removed by collimating the bunch tail, is consistent with in-plasma charge loss. Segment calibration assumes local and integrated driver energy losses are proportional; simulations indicate resulting errors of a few percentage points. The light-emission mechanism itself is not fully understood.

### Contributions

1. Demonstrated excess plasma-emission light as a noninvasive, in situ measure of the energy remaining in a plasma wake, including its reduction when a trailing bunch extracts energy.
2. Formulated an efficiency measurement from a calibrated light-versus-wake-energy response curve, with a shot-to-shot driver energy-loss estimate combining light and spectrometer data.
3. Reached a working point with 2.2% driver-to-trailing-bunch efficiency, compared with 0.9% at a previous working point, mainly because a longer plasma quadrupled the trailing-bunch energy gain.
4. Measured the local energy-transfer efficiency along the plasma, revealing a decline that tail collimation removes and that the downstream spectrometer cannot detect.
5. Quantified with HiPACE++ simulations the systematic error of the segment-calibration assumption, reaching 8 percentage points only for an unrealistically high driver emittance.

### Comments

- §V, first paragraph: the stated best fit $\eta_p=0.75\,\eta_s$, with bounds $\eta_p=0.65\,\eta_s$ and $\eta_p=0.81\,\eta_s$, implies $\eta_p<\eta_s$. The preceding sentence (points lie above the line $\eta_p=\eta_s$, and for $\eta_p>30\%$ the spectrometer value $\eta_s$ is on average 9.7 percentage points lower) and Fig. 3 ($\eta_p$ on the vertical axis, $\eta_s$ on the horizontal axis, points above the diagonal) both show $\eta_p>\eta_s$. The intended relations are most likely $\eta_s=0.75\,\eta_p$, $0.65\,\eta_p$, and $0.81\,\eta_p$.
