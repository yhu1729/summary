## Claude (September 2026)

### Summary

The authors build GyroSwin, a neural surrogate that autoregressively advances the 5D distribution function of nonlinear gyrokinetics, the gyrophase-averaged kinetic description of magnetized-plasma turbulence. In the background theory, quasilinear (QL) models weight per-mode linear simulations by saturation rules fitted to nonlinear fluxes, omitting nonlinear physics such as zonal flows. The data are 255 collisionless electrostatic GKW flux-tube simulations of ion-temperature-gradient turbulence with adiabatic electrons on a $32\times8\times16\times85\times32$ grid in $(v_\parallel,\mu,s,x,k_y)$, varying safety factor, magnetic shear, and temperature and density gradients. GyroSwin is a shifted-window (Swin) transformer U-Net on patch tokens of the perturbed ion distribution, with a cross-attention integrator over velocity space giving 3D latents for the electrostatic potential, a heat-flux head, and the zonal ($k_y=0$) mode as separate channels. Trained on 48 simulations, it gives heat-flux RMSEs of 47.35 (in-distribution) and 59.15 (out-of-distribution), against 89.48 and 95.18 for a QL saturation rule and about 120 for other neural surrogates, whose fluxes decay; with 241 simulations errors fall to 16–26, and the largest model's rollouts stay above Pearson correlation 0.1 for over 100 snapshots. Stated limitations are error accumulation from ignoring the distributional nature of chaotic turbulence, the omitted linear phase, adiabatic electrons, and incomplete parameter-space coverage.

### Contributions

1. Built the potential branch as a 3D Swin decoder without an encoder, taking its latents at every resolution level from a learned query attending over all $v_\parallel$ tokens at each spatial location, coupled the $\delta f$ and $\phi$ branches by bidirectional cross-attention at the bottleneck, in every decoder stage, and before unpatching, and conditioned every Swin block through FiLM on sinusoidal embeddings of $(t,R/L_T,R/L_n,\hat s,q)$.
2. Sampled the four parameters by Latin hypercube in two passes, in which about half of 100 runs over $R/L_T\in[3,12]$, $R/L_n\in[1,7]$, $q\in[1,9]$, $\hat s\in[0.5,5]$ and all 200 runs over the less stabilizing $R/L_T\in[6,12]$, $R/L_n\in[0,2]$, $q\in[5,9]$, $\hat s\in[0.5,2]$ became turbulent, dropped the first 80 of 266 snapshots per run as linear phase, and held out six in-distribution runs inside and five out-of-distribution runs outside the convex hull of the training parameters, plus three for validation.
3. Reported time-averaged Pearson correlations with the ground-truth flux spectrum $Q(k_y)$ of 0.51 ± 0.4 (in-distribution) and 0.58 ± 0.33 (out-of-distribution) for QL, 0.59 ± 0.06 and 0.69 ± 0.06 for GyroSwin on 48 simulations, and 0.87 ± 0.11 and 0.91 ± 0.08 for GyroSwin$_\mathrm{Large}$, whose spectra fall off at high $k_y$, attributed to spectral bias, and whose time-averaged zonal-flow profiles are smoothed, overestimated in amplitude, and on some test cases entirely off.
4. Fitted $L(N,D)=E+AN^{-\alpha}+BD^{-\beta}$ to the one-step validation loss of 11 configurations with 45.5M–893.4M parameters and 37–241 simulations, giving $\alpha=0.46$ (95% CI [0.22, 0.79]) and $\beta=0.37$ ([0.32, 0.87]) with the irreducible term $E$ not identifiable, while the single-H100 forward time grew only from 11.8 ms at 90.2M parameters to 15.4 ms at 998.3M, against 569.6 ms, 963.3 ms, and 3103 ms for factorized CNN, factorized FNO, and Transolver.
5. Trained GyroSwin$_\mathrm{Linear}$, a learned saturation rule mapping the last snapshot of each linear simulation to the nonlinear time-averaged flux, which with 241 simulations reached RMSEs of 42.85 (in-distribution) and 23.40 (out-of-distribution), against 56.68 and 56.06 for the QuaLiKiz saturation rule with its normalization fitted by least squares to the training fluxes and 36.95 and 25.89 for Gaussian-process regression on the four parameters.

### Comments

- Section 3: the core layer performs attention "within 5D windows of size $M:=M_{v_\parallel}\times M_{v_\mu}\times M_s\times M_x\times M_y$" and patch merging concatenates "2×2×2×2×2 neighboring patches ... reducing resolution by $2^5$ times", but Appendix D states that "The δf branch operates on a 4D token grid. The µ axis is folded into the channel dimension", with windows $(4,2,9,4)$ over $(v_\parallel,s,x,k_y)$, an $8\times4\times17\times8$ grid of 4,352 tokens, and merging by 2 on each axis with more than two tokens; unresolved.
- Sections 1 and 3, Eq. (8), and Figs. 1–2: GyroSwin is trained with the multitask loss $\mathcal{L}=w_f\mathcal{L}_f(\hat f,f)+w_\phi\mathcal{L}_\phi(\hat\phi,\phi)+w_{\bar Q}\mathcal{L}_{\bar Q}(\hat Q,\bar Q)$ to predict distribution, potential, and fluxes "simultaneously", with the "heat flux at the next timestep" (Fig. 2 caption), whereas Appendix D states "The total loss is $\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\delta f}+w_\phi(\tau)\mathcal{L}_\phi$" and afterwards trains only a flux head attached to "the frozen pretrained backbone", with an $L_1$ loss against "the heat flux averaged over the last 80 snapshots of the trajectory"; unresolved.
- Section 4, Appendix D, and Table 6: 266 snapshots per simulation, of which the first 80 are discarded, give 185 one-step pairs per trajectory, matching subsets XS ($37\times185=6{,}845$) and S ($63\times185=11{,}655$), but the 27,380, 35,705, and 44,400 samples listed for 150, 199, and 241 trajectories equal $148\times185$, $193\times185$, and $240\times185$, whereas $241\times185=44{,}585$; unresolved.
- Section 5, "Scalability": "GyroSwin$_\mathrm{Small}$ is roughly three orders of magnitude faster than the numerical code GKW (4200 vs. 756 GFLOPs)", as also claimed in the abstract and Section 1, but the quoted costs differ by a factor of about 5.6; unresolved.
- Section 5, "5D→1D flux spectrum": "all neural surrogates exhibit higher correlation than the QL approach" and GyroSwin "performs slightly worse on the ID cases than competitors", but in Table 3 PointNet (0.49 ± 0.02, 0.54 ± 0.09) and Transolver (0.50 ± 0.02, 0.53 ± 0.09) fall below QL (0.51 ± 0.4, 0.58 ± 0.33) on both test sets, and GyroSwin's in-distribution 0.59 ± 0.06 is the highest among QL and all 48-simulation surrogates, ViT being next at 0.52 ± 0.05.
- Table 7: the final design GyroSwin$_{f+\phi+\bar Q}$ repeats the four Table 2 results of GyroSwin (Ours) (17.67 ± 2.16, 19.40 ± 1.46, 47.35 ± 6.20, 59.15 ± 15.62) but is listed with 91.1M parameters, whereas Table 3 gives 90.2M for GyroSwin and GyroSwin$_\mathrm{Small}$; unresolved.
- Fig. 4 (right) and Fig. 11(b): the ground-truth zonal-flow profile of the selected OOD simulation in Fig. 4 coincides with that in the upper-right panel of Fig. 11(b), yet the GyroSwin$_\mathrm{Large}$ prediction peaks near $x\approx7$ with its minimum near $x\approx21$ in Fig. 4, close to the ground truth, but near $x\approx10$ and $x\approx26$ in Fig. 11(b), which Appendix G attributes to GyroSwin$_\mathrm{Large}$; unresolved.
- Fig. 5 caption: "The sampled parameter space is evenly distributed", but the plotted histograms concentrate in $\hat s\in[0.5,2]$, $R/L_n\in[0,2]$, and $q\in[5,9]$, the second-pass ranges of Appendix C, with the first-pass runs forming what Appendix C calls "the sparse point cloud in Figure 5".
- Appendix F: Fig. 8 is said to compare predictions "for timesteps {10, 100, 200}" and to show that "the predictions in wavenumber space remain somewhat stable", but its rows are labeled $t_5$, $t_{20}$, $t_{50}$, and $t_{100}$, and its caption places $x$ and $y$ in real space, so no panel is in wavenumber space; unresolved.
- Appendix G: the improvement over QL on $W(k_y)$ "is reflected in the pearson correlation coefficients shown in Table 3", and wrong zonal-flow profiles explain "the rather high variance observed in the main table", but Table 3 reports only $Q(k_y)$ correlations and no table reports $W(k_y)$ or zonal-flow metrics.
- Appendices H and I: the ablation results are said to be reported "in Section H", the section making the statement, and the learned-saturation-rule results "in Equation (5)", the QL saturation rule of Section 2; the results are in Tables 7 and 8.
- Appendix I: "with increasing training data GyroSwin$_\mathrm{Linear}$ outperforms tabular regressors GPR and MLP", but with 241 simulations Table 8 gives GyroSwin$_\mathrm{Linear}$ an in-distribution RMSE of 42.85 ± 12.65 against 36.95 ± 9.24 for GPR; it beats both only out of distribution (23.40 ± 5.89 against 25.89 ± 8.78 and 34.99 ± 12.80).
- Appendix I.1: $O(\text{MSA})=4(xyzhw)^2d^2+2(xyzhw)^2d$ squares the token count also in the projection term, which windowing leaves unchanged and $O(\text{W-MSA})=4\,xyzhw\,d^2+2M^5xyzhw\,d$ gives as $4\,xyzhw\,d^2$, the evident intended term; both formulas also use $w$ for the extent named $k$ in the resolution $x\times y\times z\times h\times k$.
