### Summary

This study characterizes double-peaked fishbones: chirping plasma oscillations with synchronized core and edge activity separated by weaker intermediate fluctuations. A survey of approximately 3000 events in 40 KSTAR discharges relates magnetic fluctuation strength to operating conditions, finding stronger events at higher normalized beta and lower edge safety factor. Events are weakest when applied magnetic perturbations degrade confinement and strongest when optimized perturbations enhance it. Hilbert-transform analysis separates electron-temperature and magnetic signals into amplitude envelopes and phases. As fishbones strengthen, edge temperature envelopes become more correlated with magnetic fluctuations, while core correlations change comparatively little. Phase measurements near the outer midplane show that edge temperature oscillations generally lead core oscillations; the weakest events remain inconclusive because edge signals approach background levels. A case with an L-mode-like temperature profile retains two correlation peaks, showing that steep H-mode edge gradients are not required. Rare radial phase reversals also occur within the edge component. The observations constrain theories of long-range coupling and support investigating an active edge contribution, but do not establish that edge activity causes the core response.

### Contributions

1. Characterized approximately 3000 double-peaked fishbones across 40 discharges and related their magnetic fluctuation strength to normalized beta, edge safety factor, and externally modified confinement.
2. Separated signal envelopes from phases and showed that edge-temperature correlations with magnetic fluctuations increase with fishbone strength while core correlations remain comparatively insensitive.
3. Identified an edge-leading phase relation near the outer midplane using complementary phase diagnostics and a survey of 28 discharges, with explicit limits for weak edge signals.
4. Observed two correlation peaks with an L-mode-like edge temperature profile, showing that steep H-mode gradients alone cannot explain the double-peaked structure.
5. Documented rare approximately $\pi$ phase reversals between neighboring edge channels during strong fishbones, distinguishing internal structure of the outer component from an additional radial activity peak.
