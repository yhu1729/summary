# 2026G3

## Claude (September 2026)

### Summary

The author designs a fully spectral discretization of the linearized Bhatnagar–Gross–Krook (BGK) equation $\partial_t f+v\partial_x f-\partial_x\phi\,\partial_v f=\mathscr{L}(f)$ on $\mathbb{R}_x\times\mathbb{R}_v$, with a confining even polynomial potential $\phi$ and a collision operator conserving mass, momentum, and energy. The perturbation of equilibrium is expanded in Hermite polynomials in velocity and in polynomials orthonormal with respect to $\rho=e^{-\phi}$ in space, truncated at degrees $K$ and $N$, and advanced by implicit Euler, without domain truncation or artificial boundary conditions. For $N\ge\deg\phi$, the semi-discrete and fully discrete schemes conserve mass and total energy and, for the harmonic potential, keep zero the four further moments associated with oscillatory equilibria. Adapting the continuous entropy method of Carrapatoso et al., the author proves the semi-discrete scheme hypocoercive: although collisions damp only Hermite modes of degree three and higher, solutions decay exponentially in the weighted $L^2$ norm, with constants depending on $N$ and $\phi$. Experiments show $N$-independent decay rates, with a fast-then-slow relaxation for a double-well potential. The author notes that the constants could degenerate as $N$ grows unless certain projected operator norms stay bounded, which holds for the harmonic potential and is conjectured in general, and leaves open the closure choice and the two-phase relaxation.

### Contributions

1. Applied the projection $\Pi_{\mathbb{P}_N}$ in the Hermite-moment hierarchy $\partial_tC_k=\sqrt{k+1}\,\partial_x^*C_{k+1}-\sqrt{k}\,\partial_xC_{k-1}-\delta_{k\ge3}C_k$ only after the weighted adjoint $\partial_x^*=-\partial_x+\partial_x\phi$, because polynomials are stable under $\partial_x$ but not under multiplication by $\partial_x\phi$, and evaluated the entries $\int\tilde P_r'\tilde P_n\rho\,dx$, which vanish for $r\le n$, as $\int\tilde P_r\tilde P_n\partial_x\phi\,\rho\,dx$ from the recurrence, in a sparse block-tridiagonal iteration matrix.
2. Built an entropy $\mathscr{H}_1$ adding to $\|\tilde h\|^2$ four cross terms with $\Omega^{-1}$, $\Omega=\partial_x^*\partial_x+1$, weighted by $\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon^{3/2}$, $\varepsilon^{7/4}$, $\varepsilon^{15/8}$, which suffices for the harmonic potential, and $\mathscr{H}_2=\mathscr{H}_1-\varepsilon^{62/32}(b'b+c'c'')$ otherwise, with $b=\langle\tilde C_1\rangle$ and $c=\langle\tilde C_2\rangle$ the spatial means of the momentum and kinetic-energy moments, and with $N$ entering only through a constant $K_N$ bounding four projected operators on $\mathbb{P}_N$.
3. Transferred the decay to the implicit Euler iterates on the invariant-free subspace through a Lyapunov norm, $\|u_i\|_P^2\le(1+2\alpha\Delta t)^{-i}\|u_0\|_P^2$, which tends to $e^{-2\alpha T}\|u_0\|_P^2$ at fixed $T=I\Delta t$ as $I\to\infty$, with $\alpha$ possibly depending on $N$.
4. Computed the three-term recurrence coefficients of the orthonormal polynomials by iterating Freud's equation, which is ill-conditioned and diverges at large index, up to $K_0=10$ and by the Deift et al. asymptotic expansion beyond, after showing on three quartic potentials that the expansion is inaccurate at small index for the non-convex $x^4-2x^2$.
5. Reported, with $K=20$ and $N=15$ or 30, log-scale decay slopes of $-0.14$ for the harmonic potential and of $-0.29$ for $t\le20$ then $-0.05$ for the normalized double well $(x-1)^2(x+1)^2$, and harmonic-case norms $\|\tilde C_0\|$ and $\|\tilde C_1\|$ oscillating with a common period shifted by half a period.

### Comments

- Section 1.1: the oscillatory modes $f_+=(x\cos t+v\sin t)\mathscr{M}$ and $f_-=(v\cos t-x\sin t)\mathscr{M}$ do not satisfy the first equation of (1.3) for the harmonic potential ($\partial_x\phi=x$), which leaves $2(v\cos t-x\sin t)\mathscr{M}$ and $-2(x\cos t+v\sin t)\mathscr{M}$, and $f_+$ gives $\frac{d}{dt}\langle rx\rangle=-\langle m\rangle$, contrary to $\frac{d}{dt}\langle rx\rangle=\langle m\rangle$ in the proof of Proposition 2.1; $g_\pm$ do satisfy (1.3), so the evident intended modes are $(x\cos t-v\sin t)\mathscr{M}$ and $(v\cos t+x\sin t)\mathscr{M}$.
- Section 2.2, proof of Proposition 2.1: part 2 states "It was already proven above that" $\frac{d}{dt}\langle r\phi\rangle=-\langle m\partial_x\phi\rangle$, but part 1 derives $\frac{d}{dt}\langle r\phi\rangle=\langle m\partial_x\phi\rangle$, and only the latter sign gives the next line $\frac{d}{dt}(\frac{1}{\sqrt2}\langle e\rangle-\langle r\phi\rangle)=-2\langle mx\rangle$; the recalled sign would give zero.
- Section 3.1, Eqs. (3.2) and (3.5): the initial data are written $\tilde C_{k,n}(0)=\int h(0,x,v)\tilde P_n(x)\tilde H_n(v)\mathscr{M}\,dx\,dv$ and $\tilde C_k(0,x)=\Pi_{\mathbb{P}_N}\int h(0,x,v)\mu(v)\,dv$, so neither depends on $k$, contrary to $C_{k,n}=\langle C_k,\tilde P_n\rangle$ with $C_k$ the Hermite coefficients of (2.1); the evident intended weight is $\tilde H_k(v)$ in both.
- Section 4.1, Eq. (4.1): with $\alpha_{r,n}:=\int\partial_xP_rP_n\rho\,dx$, the coefficients $\langle\tilde P_r,\partial_x\tilde P_n\rangle$ and $\langle\tilde P_n,\partial_x\tilde P_r\rangle$ of (3.2) are $\alpha_{n,r}$ and $\alpha_{r,n}$, but (4.1), stated as inherited from (3.2) and equivalent to (4.2), writes $\sum_r\alpha_{r,n}\tilde C^{i+1}_{k+1,n}$ and $\sum_r\alpha_{n,r}\tilde C^{i+1}_{k-1,r}$; the evident intended terms are $\sum_r\alpha_{n,r}\tilde C^{i+1}_{k+1,r}$ and $\sum_r\alpha_{r,n}\tilde C^{i+1}_{k-1,r}$.
- Section 4.3: the reduced system is written $\frac{d}{dt}u=Au$ with $A$ "$\alpha$-coercive" in $\|\cdot\|_P$, and its implicit Euler step as $u_{i+1}+\Delta tAu_{i+1}=u_i$; implicit Euler for $\frac{d}{dt}u=Au$ is $u_{i+1}-\Delta tAu_{i+1}=u_i$, and a coercive $A$ would make $\frac{d}{dt}u=Au$ grow rather than be exponentially stable, so the evident intended system is $\frac{d}{dt}u=-Au$.
- Section 5.2, Eq. (5.27) and Lemma 5.6: $z$ is defined with the term $-\sqrt2c(t)b\,\xi_\phi(x)$, and Lemma 5.6 inverts it as $\tilde r=-b'x+c''\xi_2/(2\sqrt2)+\sqrt2c(t)+z$, whereas (5.28), the proof of Lemma 5.8, and Lemma 5.11 ($\tilde C_0=\sqrt2\xi_\phi c+S$) use $z=\tilde r+b'x-c''\xi_2/(2\sqrt2)-\sqrt2c\,\xi_\phi$, which is evidently intended.
- Section 5.2, proof of Lemma 5.9: it starts by "multiplying Equation (5.23) by $\partial_x\rho$" and later injects the expression for $b$ "into (5.24)", but (5.23) and (5.24) are inequalities in the proof of Lemma 5.5; the computations use the master equation (5.29) and its consequence (5.30), from which (5.31) follows.
- Appendix A: $\phi$ is expanded as $\phi(x)=\sum_{i=0}^m\gamma_ix^{2i}$ with leading coefficient $\gamma_m$, as also used in the proof of Lemma 5.9, so $\gamma_2$ multiplies $x^4$ for a quartic, but the quartic evaluation of (A.2) and Freud's equation (A.3), $k+1=2\gamma_2a_k^2+4\gamma_4a_k^2(a_{k-1}^2+a_k^2+a_{k+1}^2)$, use $\gamma_2$ and $\gamma_4$ as the coefficients of $x^2$ and $x^4$, the reading under which (A.3) follows from (A.1).
