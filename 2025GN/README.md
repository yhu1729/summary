### Summary

This dissertation develops global bounds for extreme roots of multivariate real-zero polynomials, whose restriction to every line through the origin has only real zeros. Its central tool is a computationally small spectrahedral relaxation of the polynomial's rigidly convex set, constructed from the degree-three truncation and sized by the number of variables. The thesis analyzes why straightforward attempts to strengthen this relaxation by adding higher-degree monomials or variables fail in general: positive semidefiniteness or the relaxation property can be lost. Eulerian polynomials provide a structured exception. Stability-preserving recurrences embed the univariate family into multivariate Eulerian polynomials, and restricting the resulting relaxation along carefully chosen directions yields exponentially improved asymptotic bounds for the leftmost root relative to the direct univariate relaxation. Comparisons with interlacing, Sobolev estimates, and Dandelin--Lobachevski--Gräffe root transformations refine earlier asymptotics and produce a still tighter combined bound. The work also proves real-zero and mirror-reciprocity properties for multivariate Eulerian polynomials and derives descent-set counting identities. The improvements depend on family-specific stability and experimentally chosen linearization directions, so a general extension theory and optimal directions remain open.

### Contributions

1. Developed and analyzed a degree-three spectrahedral relaxation for bounding extreme roots of multivariate real-zero polynomials.
2. Identified positive-semidefiniteness and real-zero-preservation obstructions to straightforward matrix and variable extensions.
3. Proved that multivariate Eulerian embeddings yield exponentially better asymptotic root bounds than the direct univariate relaxation.
4. Refined Sobolev and Dandelin--Lobachevski--Gräffe estimates and combined them with the relaxation for a stronger bound.
5. Established new structural results for multivariate Eulerian polynomials, including mirror-reciprocity and descent-set identities.
