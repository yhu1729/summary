### Summary

The authors experimentally demonstrate relativistic electron acceleration in a laser wakefield driven by a flying focus, whose intensity maximum moves at an adjustable velocity. An axiparabola, a mirror with an extended focal region, focuses a laser pulse into a helium–nitrogen gas jet, while adjustable pulse-front curvature controls the timing across the beam. Measurements over twenty shots at each of three settings show that faster wakefields produce higher electron cutoff energies, increasing from approximately $350$ to $400\,\mathrm{MeV}$. Optical propagation and particle-in-cell simulations reproduce the energy shift and show reduced electron dephasing, the loss of accelerating phase caused by electrons outrunning the wake. A simplified analytical model predicts the same trend but overestimates the energies. The experiment establishes that this structured wakefield can sustain relativistic acceleration and provides evidence for partial dephasing mitigation. Fully dephasingless operation has not yet been demonstrated and requires improved control and measurement of wake velocity inside plasma.

### Contributions

1. Demonstrates electron acceleration to several hundred $\mathrm{MeV}$, with charges of tens of picocoulombs, using an axiparabola and adjustable pulse-front curvature.
2. Controls electron cutoff energy through wake-velocity tuning, observing an approximately $50\,\mathrm{MeV}$ increase between the slowest and fastest settings over twenty shots per setting.
3. Establishes statistically distinct maximum-energy distributions despite substantial charge fluctuations, including $p=0.0004$ between the fastest and intermediate settings.
4. Reproduces an approximately $45\,\mathrm{MeV}$ energy separation in particle-in-cell simulations and resolves changes in electron position within the wake that support reduced dephasing.
5. Develops a simplified bubble-regime model linking the wake velocity controlled by pulse-front curvature to longitudinal momentum gain and the observed ordering of cutoff energies.
