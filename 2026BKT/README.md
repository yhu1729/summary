### Summary

Three-dimensional particle-in-cell simulations examine high-pressure plasma formation and stability in a compact magnetic mirror, treating both ions and electrons kinetically. An energy- and charge-conserving semi-implicit model follows continuous Maxwellian plasma injection into an initially empty trap. A radial electric field develops alongside the longitudinal ambipolar potential, redistributing azimuthal currents so that electrons carry almost all the current supporting equilibrium. Electron cooling and temperature anisotropy in the magnetic expanders also modify the escaping ion flow relative to an isothermal-electron model. As pressure rises, an azimuthal $m=1$ flute instability displaces the plasma toward the wall before $\beta$, the ratio of plasma pressure to vacuum magnetic pressure, reaches unity. Neither conducting ends nor conducting cylindrical or square sidewalls stabilize this mode in the simulated regime. The authors tentatively associate this failure with rapid ion motion and strong electrostatic fluctuations that disrupt particle attachment to magnetic field lines. The reduced mass ratio $m_i/m_e=100$, collisionless treatment, rapid model injection, and omission of secondary electron emission limit direct experimental extrapolation.

### Contributions

1. Simulated compact high-beta mirror-plasma formation with fully kinetic ions and electrons using a semi-implicit scheme conserving global energy and local charge while resolving electron gyromotion.
2. Quantified how the radial electric field suppresses ion azimuthal current and enhances electron current, with force-balance analysis identifying a significant magnetic-curvature contribution.
3. Resolved electron cooling and anisotropy in magnetic expanders, reducing the ambipolar potential drop and ion outflow speed relative to an isothermal-electron nozzle model.
4. Identified the pressure-limiting $m=1$ flute instability, whose displacement grows at a rate comparable to the inverse ion transit time while higher azimuthal modes saturate at smaller amplitudes.
5. Tested conducting cylindrical and square sidewalls and found no stabilization in the fast-instability regime, documenting plasma displacement relative to weakly distorted magnetic field lines.
