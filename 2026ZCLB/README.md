### Summary

Flux-tube gyrokinetic calculations treat one stellarator field line at a time, but nonaxisymmetry couples field lines across a flux surface. This paper uses the global gyrokinetic particle-in-cell code GTC to study linear electrostatic ion-temperature-gradient modes in quasi-axisymmetric, quasi-helically symmetric, and Wendelstein 7-X configurations. The eigenmodes are nonuniform across field lines and localize downstream of the ion diamagnetic drift, with stronger localization as the normalized gyroradius decreases. A WKB analysis of a reduced surface-global model attributes this behavior to a nonzero imaginary component of the binormal wavenumber: a single global eigenfrequency lies below the growth rate of the most unstable flux tube, so satisfying the dispersion relation away from that line requires analytic continuation to complex wavenumber. The authors then reconstruct localized surface-global modes from local stella and GX calculations by first solving each real-wavenumber dispersion relation and continuing it into the complex plane. Agreement with GTC shows that real-wavenumber local spectra alone cannot recover the cross-field-line envelope. The analysis is limited to linear electrostatic modes with adiabatic electrons, but it provides a mechanism for interpreting symmetry-breaking localization seen in nonlinear stellarator turbulence.

### Contributions

1. Demonstrated surface-global localization of linear ion-temperature-gradient eigenmodes across three stellarator configurations.
2. Identified the downstream ion-diamagnetic-drift direction as the consistent location of peak mode amplitude.
3. Explained localization through the imaginary binormal wavenumber obtained from a WKB surface-global model.
4. Showed that the global growth rate lies below that of the most unstable local flux tube.
5. Reconstructed localized global eigenmodes from stella and GX results by analytically continuing local dispersion relations to complex wavenumbers.
