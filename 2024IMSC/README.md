### Summary

High-resolution two-dimensional particle-in-cell simulations reveal that kinetic plasma turbulence self-organizes into long-lived, magnetically dominated vortices rather than remaining entirely disordered. The structures persist for many dynamical times, merge, and generate larger vortices with similar profiles, making them active mediators of the turbulent evolution. To explain them, the authors construct a self-consistent cylindrical kinetic equilibrium from an explicit particle velocity distribution inspired by the Harris pinch. Its macroscopic limit reduces to a modified force-free Gold–Hoyle configuration, connecting the measured current and magnetic-field profiles to an analytic model. The work therefore links coherent structures observed in fully kinetic turbulence to exact metastable equilibria rather than transient eddies alone. Because the simulations are 2.5D, extrapolation to fully three-dimensional systems remains open, but the structures may inform models of heliospheric coherence and plasmoid formation near compact astrophysical objects.

### Contributions

1. Observed persistent force-free vortices in fully kinetic plasma-turbulence simulations.
2. Showed that vortex mergers produce self-similar, larger metastable structures.
3. Constructed a cylindrical kinetic equilibrium from an explicit velocity distribution.
4. Connected the kinetic solution to a modified Gold–Hoyle force-free state.
5. Identified a possible coherent-structure mechanism relevant to heliospheric and compact-object plasmas.
