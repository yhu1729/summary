### Summary

This study explains deflagration-to-detonation transition in a short closed channel containing stoichiometric hydrogen-air at knocking-relevant conditions of $10$ atm and $1100$ K. An analytical low-Mach-number solution for a planar flame approaching inert end-gas develops a formal finite-time temperature singularity, but reactions in the end-gas determine the physical transition. During autoignition induction, even weak heat release raises the compressed end-gas temperature. Because the induction delay is far more temperature-sensitive than the laminar flame speed, the reaction time scale collapses, invalidating the low-Mach approximation. The resulting flame acceleration emits compression waves that steepen into shocks and reinforce the flame through thermal feedback, destroying its laminar structure and initiating detonation before bulk autoignition. One-dimensional detailed-chemistry simulations support this mechanism: selectively suppressing small induction-period heat release delays or eliminates detonation without changing the flame kinetics. The mechanism differs from long-tube flame acceleration because it originates in thermally sensitive end-gas induction near autoignition.
