### Summary

The authors develop THEMIS, a three-dimensional electromagnetic model for helicon-wave propagation, damping, and antenna coupling in the toroidal Helimak boundary plasma. A finite-temperature dielectric tensor, measured magnetic-field and density profiles, and several damping channels show that fast waves are inaccessible, whereas slow Trivelpiece--Gould modes propagate and deposit power locally. Electron Landau and collisional damping provide more than 95% of absorption under Helimak conditions. Simulations of four experimental antennas explain the initial campaign's null density response: the protruding dielectric window traps fields in the waveguide, and the low-density scrape-off-layer gradient causes a slow-wave cutoff. Recessing the window reduces the evanescent layer and improves plasma access, with absorption depending non-monotonically on recess depth. Geometry scans favor open-circuit termination, longer and wider current straps, suitable inter-turn spacing, and clearance from metallic walls. An optimized racetrack spiral raises simulated absorption by over an order of magnitude relative to the short-circuited rectangular spiral. The results are qualitative design guidance: Helimak differs substantially from tokamak edges, and the linear model omits nonlinear high-power response and engineering constraints.

### Contributions

1. Built a full three-dimensional thermal-dielectric model of helicon coupling in realistic Helimak geometry.
2. Identified slow-wave accessibility and electron Landau plus collisional damping as the dominant absorption physics.
3. Explained poor core penetration in the protruding-window experiment through cutoff and geometric trapping.
4. Quantified how window recess, termination, strap dimensions, turn spacing, and wall clearance affect coupling.
5. Designed a racetrack spiral antenna with more than tenfold simulated absorption improvement.
