### Summary

The paper develops a rapid classical tearing-mode stability workflow by asymptotically matching two complementary calculations. STRIDE supplies the ideal-MHD toroidal outer-region index $\Delta'$, including shaping, while SLAYER solves a two-fluid drift-MHD slab model for the resistive inner-layer response $\Delta(\gamma,\omega)$. A toroidal proxy for thermal-conduction-modified Glasser stabilization enters through $\Delta_{\mathrm{eff}}=\Delta'-\Delta_{\mathrm{crit}}$. SLAYER smoothly connects four analytic growth-rate regimes, and the combined workflow agrees with the TJ code in a low-$\beta$ benchmark. At inverse aspect ratio above about $0.45$, STRIDE+SLAYER and TJ diverge as their geometric approximations approach different ideal limits. Application to a shaped, DIII-D-like synthetic H-mode equilibrium tracks $2/1$ and $3/1$ growth rates as the toroidal-current profile broadens. The model is fast and interpretable but omits inner-layer pressure gradients, parallel flow, rational-surface coupling, and resistive-wall effects; experimental validation of $\Delta_{\mathrm{crit}}$ remains necessary.

### Contributions

1. Coupled toroidal STRIDE outer-region calculations to the two-fluid SLAYER resistive layer.
2. Solved jointly for tearing-mode growth rate and rotation frequency using an effective stability index.
3. Incorporated a toroidal proxy for thermal-conduction-modified Glasser stabilization.
4. Validated the workflow against four analytic regimes and TJ scans in plasma $\beta$ and inverse aspect ratio.
5. Demonstrated stable calculations for multiple rational surfaces in a shaped H-mode-like tokamak equilibrium.
