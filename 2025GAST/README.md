### Summary

The familiar separation of ideal magnetohydrodynamic waves into incompressible Alfvén waves and compressible magnetosonic waves applies cleanly only to a uniform, unbounded plasma. This review shows how equilibrium non-uniformity couples displacement, compression, pressure perturbations, and vorticity so that a single wave can exhibit different character in different regions. Using the displacement formulation for cylindrical plasmas, it identifies total-pressure perturbation and radial displacement as the variables governing the coupled system and examines special cases where partial decoupling survives. Resonant layers are especially important: a wave may become locally Alfvénic while retaining pressure variations that classical uniform-plasma terminology would exclude. The authors therefore argue that wave classification should be based on locally dominant physical properties rather than inherited global labels. The framework consolidates results relevant to structured solar and laboratory plasmas and clarifies why “Alfvénic” is often more precise than “Alfvén wave.”
