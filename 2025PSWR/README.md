### Summary

This paper analyzes isotope production as an economic driver for fusion systems before energy breakeven. The central idea is to place selected feedstock materials in a fusion blanket so neutron-driven transmutation can co-produce high-value isotopes and, at higher plasma gain, electricity. The authors calculate the value of this co-generation and formulate an economic breakeven condition based on net present value rather than only energy balance. For low plasma gain, including $Q_{\mathrm{plas}} \lesssim 1$, the analysis indicates that medical-radioisotope production can be viable in watt-to-megawatt fusion systems; one example is ${}^{102}\mathrm{Ru}\rightarrow{}^{99}\mathrm{Mo}$ production in a 3 MW system. For higher gain, $Q_{\mathrm{plas}}\gtrsim 3$, the paper argues that electricity plus isotope co-production can reduce the gain needed for economic viability. It also discusses asymmetric neutron wall loading as a way to enhance transmutation.

### Contributions

1. Framed neutron-driven isotope production as an early revenue path for fusion systems.
2. Derived an economic breakeven criterion based on net present value.
3. Quantified low-gain medical-radioisotope production scenarios.
4. Analyzed higher-gain co-production of electricity and valuable isotopes.
5. Identified asymmetric neutron wall loading as a strategy for improving transmutation yield.
