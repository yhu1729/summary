### Summary

Strongly driven ion-temperature-gradient turbulence in tokamaks is shown to saturate through a propagating zonal-flow mode termed the toroidal secondary mode (TSM). Nonlinear turbulence creates an up-down pressure asymmetry; together with zonal shearing and magnetic-drift advection, this drives small-scale zonal flows that propagate radially. Above a fluctuation-amplitude threshold, TSMs become unstable and shear turbulent eddies, returning the system toward marginality. Free-energy transfer diagnostics show these flows moving energy from large injection scales toward small dissipative scales. Incorporating TSM marginality into critical-balance arguments replaces perpendicular isotropy with anisotropic outer scales and produces scaling laws for heat flux, fluctuation amplitudes and spectra, correlation lengths, and zonal-flow energy. Nonlinear gyrokinetic simulations with both *stella* and *GX* validate the parameter scalings, including heat flux approximately linear in the normalized temperature gradient rather than the previously predicted cubic dependence. Rapid TSM oscillations still regulate turbulence because their temporally decorrelated advection produces diffusive cross-field displacement.
