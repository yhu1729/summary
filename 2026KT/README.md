### Summary

Kaganovich and Tendler review partially ionized plasma physics with emphasis on low-temperature technological plasmas. The paper contrasts partially ionized systems with fully ionized plasmas by stressing the role of neutrals, molecular reactions, and non-Maxwellian electron energy distribution functions obtained from kinetic Boltzmann analysis. Examples include non-monotonic distributions in argon--NF$_3$ afterglows, explosive cold-electron generation in capacitive discharges, hysteresis in inductively coupled plasmas, spokes in Penning discharges, and anomalous transport in Hall thrusters. The review also discusses modern particle-in-cell, fluid, and hybrid simulation tools used for plasma reactor design, etching, deposition, semiconductor processing, and emerging quantum-device manufacturing. Its conclusion emphasizes that practical reactor optimization needs hybrid kinetic-fluid models: fluid solvers are fast but miss kinetic effects, while fully kinetic models remain too expensive for routine engineering sweeps.
