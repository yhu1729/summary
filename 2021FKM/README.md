# 2021FKM

## Claude (October 2026)

### Summary

Irksome is a Python library that turns Unified Form Language (UFL) descriptions of semidiscrete finite element problems, extended by a time-derivative operator Dt, into UFL for the stage-coupled equations of any Runge–Kutta (RK) method given by its Butcher tableau, solved through Firedrake and PETSc. For a form $G(t, u, u_t; v) = 0$, stage $i$ replaces $u_t$ by $k_i$, $u$ by $u^n + \Delta t\sum_j a_{ij}k_j$, $v$ by the $i$th test component, and $t$ by $t_n + c_i\Delta t$; strongly enforced Dirichlet data $g$ become $k_i|_\Gamma = \partial_t g(t_n + c_i\Delta t)$. A TimeStepper class and tableaux for Gauss–Legendre, RadauIIA, LobattoIIIA, and LobattoIIIC of arbitrary order, and other schemes, are provided. Since the system stays symbolic, block diagonal, block triangular, and monolithic multigrid preconditioners follow from PETSc options, and DIRKs reduce to block forward substitution through field splits. Heat-equation tests show stage-order reduction and a monolithic vertex-star multigrid whose iteration counts are independent of stages, mesh, and time step; the mixed wave equation confirms exact energy conservation for Gauss–Legendre methods; Navier–Stokes drag and lift, Benjamin–Bona–Mahony invariants, and three-dimensional Gross–Pitaevskii dynamics cover nonlinear, DAE, and Sobolev-type problems. The monolithic preconditioner lacks theory, and explicit and IMEX methods are not efficiently supported.

### Contributions

1. Built the transformation on getForm, which takes the semidiscrete UFL form, a Butcher tableau, the time and time step as UFL Constants, and the solution Coefficient, and returns the coupled stage form on the $s$-fold product space together with stage boundary conditions; a pre-order variant of ufl.replace was needed because the post-order traversal substitutes $u$ before $u[i]$ and so indexes the wrong piece of the stage vector, and the implementation assumes Dt acts only on unknown fields, so one writes 2*t*u + t**2*Dt(u) rather than Dt(t**2*u).
2. Verified accuracy on the heat equation with $u = e^{-t}\sin(\pi x)\cos(\pi y)$ and cubic serendipity elements on $N \times N$ meshes, $N = 8$ to $128$: two-stage Gauss–Legendre reaches fourth order in $L^2$ only for small time steps and degenerates to stage order two, LobattoIIIC(3) loses $L^2$ but not $H^1$ order, RadauIIA(2) loses order only in $L^2$, and RadauIIA(3) keeps full accuracy; with third-order Raviart–Thomas elements for the mixed form, Gauss–Legendre(2) again shows order reduction while RadauIIA(3) does not (Figs. 1, 2).
3. Compared, for $Q_2$ elements on a $128 \times 128$ mesh with $\Delta t = 0.078125$ and RadauIIA($s$), $s = 1, 2, 3$, the block diagonal and block triangular preconditioners (2.35), (2.36) with one geometric multigrid V-cycle per block against a monolithic multigrid whose Chebyshev-accelerated vertex-star smoother couples all stages: block diagonal makes RadauIIA(2) about four times and RadauIIA(3) about eight times as expensive as backward Euler, whereas the monolithic variant keeps about five FGMRES iterations per step for every stage count, costing about two and four times the single-stage time, and needs four or five iterations for $N = 16$ to $128$ and $\Delta t = c/N$, $c = 1, 4, 16$ (Table 1); on a $32 \times 32$ mesh RadauIIA(2) with $\Delta t = 1/8$ reaches error $2.9 \times 10^{-4}$ in 2.01 s against $1.1 \times 10^{-3}$ in 7.75 s for RadauIIA(1) with $\Delta t = 1/128$ (Table 2).
4. Showed for the mixed wave equation on a $10 \times 10$ mesh with $RT_2 \times DG_1$ and $\Delta t = 0.1, 0.5, 1.0$ to $t = 10$ that one- and two-stage Gauss–Legendre keep the energy ratio at 1 while LobattoIIIC(2) decays to $3.79 \times 10^{-1}$, $9.75 \times 10^{-18}$, and $1.17 \times 10^{-20}$ and RadauIIA(2) to $9.00 \times 10^{-1}$, $7.10 \times 10^{-4}$, and $3.77 \times 10^{-7}$ (Table 3), and that the symplectic two-stage Qin–Zhang DIRK, solved exactly by a multiplicative field split blocking the $u$ and $\sigma$ unknowns of each stage (Listing 5), reproduces the Gauss–Legendre energy behavior.
5. Reproduced the Schäfer–Turek cylinder drag and lift at Reynolds number 100 with a curvilinear mesh of 14,640 cells, Scott–Vogelius $P_4$/discontinuous $P_3$ elements (about 382k unknowns), and two-stage RadauIIA with $\Delta t = 0.00125$, against a reference using about 667k unknowns and $\Delta t = 0.000625$, with discrete divergence between $10^{-5}$ and $10^{-3}$; for the Benjamin–Bona–Mahony solitary wave with $c = 1/2$ on $[0, 100]$, $h = 0.1$, the two-stage Gauss–Legendre method with $\Delta t = 10h$ gave 0.14% error against 0.15% for the implicit midpoint rule with $\Delta t = h$ and over 10% with $\Delta t = 10h$, conserving $I_1$ and $I_2$ to $10^{-16}$ with cubic-invariant drift $9.6 \times 10^{-7}$ versus $5.1 \times 10^{-3}$ for one stage (Table 4); for the Gross–Pitaevskii equation on $[-6, 6]^3$ with cubic Lagrange elements, Gauss–Legendre with one to three stages and $\Delta t = 0.05$ to $0.25$ needed 4.86 to 6.79 Krylov iterations per Newton step with monolithic multigrid, and the energy drift fell from $7.56 \times 10^{-2}$ to $5.45 \times 10^{-8}$ (Table 5).

### Comments

- Section 2.1, Eqs. (2.3)–(2.4): the stages are said to satisfy (2.4) "for all $1 \le i \le n$", the coefficients are indexed "for $1 \le i, j \le n$", and the sum in (2.4) runs over $i = 1, \dots, s$, although $n$ is the dimension of $y$ and (2.3) and (2.14) index the $s$ stages by $i$ and sum over $j$; the evident intended ranges are $1 \le i, j \le s$ with the sum over $j$.
- Section 2.2.2, Eq. (2.23): the stage equations for the wave equation, introduced as $u_{tt} - \Delta u = 0$ with the homogeneous system (2.22), contain the forcing term $-(f(t_n + c_i\Delta t, \cdot), v_i)$ carried over from the mixed heat equation (2.21); the evident intended form has no forcing term.
- Section 4.1: after discussing the order reduction of the two-stage Gauss–Legendre method in Fig. 2(a), the text concludes that "the extra formal order of accuracy for Gauss–Lobatto methods does not materialize unless one also takes a very small time step", although no Gauss–Lobatto method appears in Fig. 2; Gauss–Legendre is evidently intended.
- Section 4.2, Listing 4, and Table 1: the solver option sets are cited as "Figure 2", "Figure 3", and "Figure 4", whereas they are Listings 2–4 and Figs. 2–4 show the mixed heat-equation errors, the solver performance, and the cylinder domain; the caption of Listing 4 refers to "the monolithic multi-grid method described in Equation (2)" and that of Table 1 to "the Monolithic Multi-grid Options in Figure 4"; Listings 2 and 4 are evidently intended.
