### Summary

Using 1,600 one-dimensional, one-velocity-dimensional Vlasov--Poisson--Fokker--Planck simulations, this paper resolves the evolution of large-amplitude electron plasma waves when the bounce frequency greatly exceeds the electron--electron collision frequency. The dynamics separate into three phases: rapid collisionless trapping and plateau formation; a long quasi-steady detrapping phase governed by collisions and wave--particle interactions; and a return to near-linear Landau damping after the distribution becomes nearly Maxwellian. During the middle phase, collisions counterintuitively strengthen the nonlinear frequency downshift, which can approximately double because the Fokker--Planck contribution to susceptibility is only partly opposed by Vlasov dynamics. Fits quantify the initial shift, shift-growth rate, effective damping, phase lifetime, and final damping across wave amplitude, wavenumber, and collision frequency. The measured middle-phase damping is approximately linear in collision frequency but about an order of magnitude above Zakharov--Karpman theory and scales as $\omega_B^{-1.48}$ rather than $\omega_B^{-3}$.

### Contributions

1. Identified a three-phase kinetic evolution for weakly collisional, large-amplitude electron plasma waves.
2. Isolated the Fokker--Planck susceptibility contribution that enhances the nonlinear frequency downshift during detrapping.
3. Characterized the long-lived quasi-steady phase with empirical damping, frequency-shift, and lifetime laws.
4. Quantified where Zakharov--Karpman damping theory agrees with and departs from the simulations.
5. Reduced the simulated behavior to phase-specific dispersion relations suitable for piecewise wave-evolution models.
