### Summary

This paper relates the Chapman--Enskog asymptotic expansion of kinetic equations to exact hydrodynamic closure on slow spectral modes. For linearized kinetic dynamics, the authors show that the Chapman--Enskog series agrees locally, to every order as the Knudsen number tends to zero, with the eigenvalue and invariant-manifold expansion defining the slow spectral closure. An explicit one-dimensional linear BGK model makes the distinction between local asymptotics and global hydrodynamics concrete. Its slow diffusion eigenvalue has an exact closed form and defines the reduced dynamics for arbitrary Knudsen number up to the relevant spectral continuation, whereas the corresponding Chapman--Enskog power series diverges at every nonzero Knudsen number. The divergence is tied to a critical wave number where the hydrodynamic eigenvalue encounters the kinetic spectrum. Finite Burnett and super-Burnett truncations can therefore develop artifacts such as sign changes and Bobylev instability even though the exact spectral closure remains meaningful. The result clarifies why asymptotic agreement near equilibrium does not imply globally convergent hydrodynamic equations.

### Contributions

1. Proved all-order local equivalence between Chapman--Enskog expansion and slow spectral closure.
2. Expressed hydrodynamic reduction through slow eigenvalues and invariant spectral manifolds.
3. Derived an exact diffusion-type closure for a one-dimensional linear BGK model.
4. Showed that its Chapman--Enskog series diverges for every nonzero Knudsen number.
5. Connected divergence and higher-order instability to the critical wave number of the hydrodynamic eigenvalue.
