## Claude (September 2026)

### Summary

This review sorts numerical differentiation into five situations with recommended methods. Automatic differentiation suits fixed analytic relationships, not evolving simulated fields, except as the gradient engine of differentiable simulators. For noiseless simulation data, finite differences are simple, Fourier and Chebyshev spectral methods are highly accurate but require periodicity or special sampling, and finite elements handle irregular domains and weak derivatives at higher cost. Noise, amplified as $\mathcal O(\Delta x^{-\nu})$ by finite differences of the $\nu$th derivative, is handled through its band separation from the signal: spectral low-pass filtering for periodic data, Kalman filtering and smoothing with robust and nonlinear variants when a model exists, and otherwise smoothing methods such as polynomial, spline, basis, wavelet, total-variation, naive-model Kalman, and local linear-dynamics fits for the ill-posed model-free problem. Hyperparameters are tuned by minimizing a fidelity-plus-total-variation loss, or a Huber variant for outliers, whose weight follows a bandlimit heuristic. Methods from the authors' PyNumDiff package are compared on six simulated signals across outliers, noise type and scale, step size, and bandlimit, with 52 seeds. Most perform similarly, so auxiliary properties decide; the authors recommend RTSDiff, a Rauch–Tung–Striebel smoother, as the default. No method can separate noise that shares the signal's character.

### Contributions

1. Provided decision maps linking analytic structure, simulation complexity, noise, model availability, periodicity, and sampling to preferred differentiation algorithms and their error behavior.
2. Presented Kalman filtering and smoothing as a derivative-estimation framework, including its Bayesian interpretation, forward–backward smoothing, robust generalizations for outliers and non-Gaussian noise, and nonlinear filtering.
3. Generalized the fidelity–smoothness hyperparameter loss to a Huber form scaled by the median absolute deviation, which greatly improved all methods in the presence of outliers.
4. Found that accuracy is governed mainly by problem difficulty, with LinearDiff and RTSDiff best in average RMSE rank, RobustDiff and TVRDiff least biased, and sliding-window fits degrading least at large step sizes.
5. Released PyNumDiff with the comparison notebooks, and described how finite differences, splines, and Kalman smoothing extend to irregularly spaced samples.

(6.8), obtained by taking the negative logarithm and minimizing, require; the intended exponents are $-\frac12\|\cdot\|_2^2$.
- §6.5: the nonlinear form of the cruise-control model of §6.1.1 gives the third state row as $-f_rx_1-mg\,u_0+\frac{k_i}{\Delta t^2}x_2+\frac{k_p}{\Delta t}u_1$, the fourth as $x_4+\Delta t\,u_1$, and the measurement as $[x_1]_{n-1}$, whereas Eq. (6.6) makes these the evident intended forms.
