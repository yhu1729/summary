### Summary

This work develops physics-based objectives for reducing ion-temperature-gradient turbulence in quasi-isodynamic stellarators. An updated model estimates the critical gradient of localized toroidal modes and motivates "mode splitting": separating unfavorable-curvature wells so that unstable modes localize independently and require a steeper temperature gradient to grow. Optimization produces the six-field-period QICG configuration with $a/L_{T,\mathrm{crit}}\approx2.14$, low neoclassical transport, and a vacuum magnetic well, although its above-threshold nonlinear heat flux is excessive. A second, inverse-mirror-like configuration adds a target combining field-line variation of $|\nabla\alpha|$ with kinetic-electron mode inertia. It retains a critical gradient near 2 while shifting unfavorable curvature away from the magnetic-field minimum. Nonlinear gyrokinetic calculations with kinetic electrons show heat flux below or comparable to W7-X high mirror for density gradients through $a/L_n=2$. Fast-particle confinement and tighter quasi-isodynamic quality remain open design constraints.

### Contributions

1. Updated the localized toroidal-mode critical-gradient model for quasi-isodynamic geometry.
2. Introduced mode splitting as a strategy for raising the ion-temperature-gradient threshold.
3. Produced a six-field-period configuration with $a/L_{T,\mathrm{crit}}\approx2.14$ and a vacuum magnetic well.
4. Designed an inverse-mirror target that accounts for kinetic-electron mode inertia above threshold.
5. Demonstrated kinetic-electron heat flux competitive with W7-X high mirror over finite density gradients.
