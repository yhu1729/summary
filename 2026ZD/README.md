### Summary

This paper analyzes a steady, one-dimensional, collisionless plasma sheath containing cold positive ions, wall-emitted secondary electrons, and nonthermal primary electrons described by a Cairns distribution. Substitution of the distribution into the sheath fluid and Poisson equations yields a generalized Bohm criterion and corresponding ion entry speed. Current balance at a floating wall gives a modified wall potential, and the transition between classical and inverse-sheath behavior gives a critical secondary-electron-emission coefficient. All three relations recover their Maxwellian forms when the Cairns nonthermality parameter $\alpha$ vanishes. Parameter sweeps show that increasing $\alpha$ raises the generalized Bohm speed and lowers the floating potential in the adopted normalization. The critical emission threshold also becomes more sensitive to wall potential and ion Mach number as nonthermality increases. These results quantify how a suprathermal primary-electron population changes sheath entry and wall-current balance within the paper's cold-ion, planar, steady-state assumptions.

### Contributions

1. Incorporated Cairns-distributed primary electrons into a secondary-emitting sheath model.
2. Derived a generalized Bohm criterion and nonthermal Bohm speed.
3. Obtained the floating-wall potential from the modified current balance.
4. Derived a nonthermality-dependent critical secondary-electron-emission coefficient.
5. Mapped how $\alpha$, wall potential, emission, and ion Mach number affect the sheath properties.
