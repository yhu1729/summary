### Summary

This paper uses Bayesian optimization and particle-in-cell simulations to improve channel-guided laser wakefield acceleration through spectral pulse shaping. The accelerator model uses a realistic plasma profile based on optical-field-ionized channel formation, ionization injection, and low on-axis density to maximize energy gain in a trailing electron bunch. Spectral shaping changes the laser temporal profile while holding total laser energy fixed, so it can alter injection and acceleration without simply increasing driver energy. Because the combined laser-shape and plasma-channel design space is broad, the authors use numerical optimization to find high-performing configurations. The optimized pulses include additional spectral content and, together with tuned channel parameters, produce electron bunches with roughly an order-of-magnitude higher charge than a baseline Gaussian case while also increasing mean energy. The study demonstrates how machine-learning-guided optimization can expose useful driver profiles in laser-plasma accelerator designs where intuition alone may not identify the best coupled laser and plasma settings.

### Contributions

1. Applied Bayesian optimization to spectral pulse shaping for channel-guided laser wakefield acceleration.
2. Coupled optimization to particle-in-cell simulations with realistic optical-field-ionized plasma-channel profiles.
3. Varied laser temporal structure at fixed laser energy to isolate spectral-shaping effects.
4. Found optimized profiles that increase electron-bunch charge by about an order of magnitude over a Gaussian baseline.
5. Demonstrated simultaneous improvements in bunch charge and mean energy through coupled laser and plasma-channel tuning.
