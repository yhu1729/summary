### Summary

The paper identifies velocity-space signatures of the ion-ion-acoustic instability that could be sought in single-spacecraft measurements of the young solar wind. Fully kinetic one-dimensional Vlasov--Poisson simulations model core protons, drifting beam protons, and electrons over parameter ranges motivated by Parker Solar Probe observations. Simulated frequencies and growth rates agree with linear Landau theory. Global energy accounting shows that the proton beam drives the instability and transfers most of its lost energy to core protons; electron energization is small relative to the electrons' initial energy. Field-particle correlations separate reversible oscillatory exchange from secular transfer and localize the latter near the ion-acoustic resonance velocity: beam protons lose energy, core protons gain it, and electrons mainly show oscillatory exchange. Increasing electron temperature, beam density, or appropriate beam drift strengthens growth and transfer. Probe position affects beam and electron signatures through ion-hole structure but little affects the core signature. Present spacecraft cadence is too slow to resolve the predicted millisecond-scale evolution.

### Contributions

1. Validated fully kinetic instability simulations against linear-theory frequencies and growth rates across three parameter scans.
2. Established that beam-proton energy primarily heats core protons, with the field acting mainly as an intermediary.
3. Identified distinct resonant field-particle-correlation signatures for beam protons, core protons, and electrons.
4. Quantified how electron temperature, beam density, drift speed, and sampling position alter energy-transfer signatures.
5. Defined measurement-cadence requirements by showing that current Parker Solar Probe data cannot resolve the predicted signatures.
