### Summary

This 26-part roadmap surveys warm dense matter, where thermal, Fermi, Coulomb-correlation, and binding energies compete and neither conventional condensed-matter nor ideal-plasma approximations are uniformly reliable. It connects first-principles and reduced theories--path-integral Monte Carlo, finite-temperature density-functional theory, response and Green-function methods, radiation hydrodynamics, molecular dynamics, and average-atom models--to experimental creation and diagnosis using diamond-anvil cells, lasers, particle beams, shocks, X-ray scattering and absorption, and optical techniques. Material-focused chapters treat hydrogen, planetary mixtures, rocky-planet matter, compact stars, inertial-confinement fusion, and high-pressure synthesis. The roadmap identifies cross-cutting bottlenecks: the fermion sign problem, exchange-correlation and equation-of-state uncertainty, theory-dependent thermometry, sparse validation data, and multiscale coupling. It closes with machine learning, interoperable databases, reproducible open software, and future high-repetition-rate facilities.
