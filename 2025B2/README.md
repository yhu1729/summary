### Summary

This review develops physical intuition for magnetic-field-line chaos, cantori, and turnstiles and argues that they organize several otherwise disconnected problems in toroidal plasmas. Magnetic field-line trajectories form a Hamiltonian system; when irrational magnetic surfaces break, their remnants are cantori. Small holes in a cantorus admit paired inward and outward, highly collimated flux tubes called turnstiles. Fourier analysis of long field-line trajectories is proposed as a more informative alternative to conventional Poincaré plots for reconstructing nearly invariant surfaces and locating leakage. The framework explains how chaotic ideal evolution exponentially reduces separations between flux tubes, so weak non-ideal effects can trigger large-scale reconnection on a timescale that depends only logarithmically on resistivity. It also connects turnstile formation to localized runaway-electron wall strikes during tokamak disruptions and to the long connection lengths and resilient strike patterns of non-resonant stellarator divertors. The paper further discusses electromagnetic consequences of nominally electrostatic microinstabilities and Bohm-like transport when pressure varies along chaotic field lines. These are synthesis and theory arguments rather than a new experimental validation, and the review identifies unresolved needs in simulation fidelity, Alfvén-wave dissipation, and controllable non-resonant divertors.

### Contributions

1. Unified magnetic-field-line chaos, cantori, and turnstiles in a Hamiltonian description tailored to toroidal plasma applications.
2. Explained how paired turnstiles transport magnetic flux through holes in broken irrational surfaces while remaining strongly collimated.
3. Proposed Fourier reconstruction of long field-line trajectories to identify near-surfaces, quantify chaos, and locate turnstile leakage.
4. Connected chaotic field evolution to logarithmically fast reconnection, disruption dynamics, and concentrated runaway-electron damage.
5. Applied the same concepts to resilient non-resonant stellarator divertors and identified open transport and simulation questions.
